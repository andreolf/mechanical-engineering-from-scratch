A **transducer** turns the quantity you care about into one you can measure, usually a voltage. A thermocouple turns temperature into microvolts, a piezo crystal turns force into charge, and a **strain gauge** turns stretch into a tiny change of resistance. This lesson takes the strain gauge apart, because the same bridge circuit sits inside load cells, pressure sensors and torque meters.

## The strain gauge

A foil gauge is a zig-zag of thin metal on a plastic backing, glued to the part. Stretch it by strain $\varepsilon$ and its resistance changes:

$$
\frac{\Delta R}{R} = GF\,\varepsilon ,
$$

with the **gauge factor** $GF \approx 2$ for constantan foil. At 1000 µε (a steel part at about 200 MPa) a 350 Ω gauge changes by only 0.74 Ω. You cannot read that with an ohmmeter while it drifts with temperature, so you put the gauge in a bridge.

## The Wheatstone bridge

Four arms $R_1, R_2, R_3, R_4$ form two voltage dividers across the excitation $V$. The output is the difference of the two mid-points:

$$
V_\text{out} = V\left(\frac{R_1}{R_1+R_2} - \frac{R_4}{R_3+R_4}\right) .
$$

With all four equal the bridge is balanced and $V_\text{out} = 0$, whatever $V$ is. Write $R_i = R(1 + x_i)$ with small $x_i$ and expand to first order:

$$
V_\text{out} \approx \frac V4\,(x_1 - x_2 + x_3 - x_4) .
$$

**Opposite arms add and adjacent arms subtract.** That one line explains every bridge configuration:

- **Quarter bridge**: one active gauge, $x_1 = GF\varepsilon$. Exactly, $V_\text{out} = V x/(4 + 2x)$, so $V_\text{out} \approx V\,GF\,\varepsilon/4$ with a non-linearity of about $x/2$.
- **Half bridge** on a bending beam: gauges on top ($+\varepsilon$) and bottom ($-\varepsilon$) in adjacent arms. $V_\text{out} = V\,GF\,\varepsilon/2$ exactly, twice the signal.
- **Full bridge**: four active gauges, $V_\text{out} = V\,GF\,\varepsilon$.

Temperature makes every gauge on the part read the same **apparent strain**. In adjacent arms it cancels, so a half bridge, or a quarter bridge with an unstrained **dummy gauge** in the adjacent arm, is temperature-compensated.

:::example A 350 Ω gauge at 1000 µε
$GF = 2.1$, $V = 5$ V, so $x = GF\varepsilon = 0.0021$.

1. **Quarter bridge.** Exact $V_\text{out} = 5 \times 0.0021/(4 + 0.0042) = 2.62225$ mV. The linear formula gives 2.62500 mV, a non-linearity of 0.105 %. It grows to 0.52 % at 5000 µε and 1.04 % at 10 000 µε.
2. **Half bridge**: 5.25000 mV, **full bridge**: 10.50000 mV, with no non-linearity at all.
3. **Temperature.** Warm the part by 10 K with 1.5 µε/K of apparent strain. The quarter bridge drifts by 39.4 µV, which looks like 15 µε of load. With a dummy gauge or in a half bridge the drift is zero.
4. **Lead wires.** 10 m of 0.14 mm² copper each way adds $R_L = 2.50$ Ω in series with the gauge. That desensitises the quarter bridge by $R_L/(R + R_L) = 0.71$ %. A three-wire connection puts one lead in each adjacent arm and cancels most of it.
:::

```python file=bridge.py
"""Wheatstone bridge with strain gauges: exact output for quarter, half and full bridges,
the quarter-bridge non-linearity, temperature compensation and lead-wire error. numpy only."""
import numpy as np

def bridge(V, R1, R2, R3, R4):
    """Output V_out = V [R1/(R1+R2) - R4/(R3+R4)]. R1 and R2 form one half, R4 and R3 the other.
    Arms R1 and R3 are opposite, so their changes add; adjacent arms (R1, R2) subtract."""
    return V * (R1 / (R1 + R2) - R4 / (R3 + R4))

R, GF, V = 350.0, 2.1, 5.0
def gauges(eps, kind, app=0.0):
    """Arm resistances for a bending beam with surface strain eps. app = apparent strain from temperature
    (the same on every gauge stuck to the beam)."""
    d = lambda e: R * (1 + GF * (e + app))
    if kind == 'quarter': return d(eps), R, R, R                    # one active gauge, three fixed resistors
    if kind == 'quarter+dummy': return d(eps), d(0.0), R, R        # dummy gauge on unstrained steel, adjacent arm
    if kind == 'half': return d(eps), d(-eps), R, R                # top (tension) and bottom (compression), adjacent
    if kind == 'full': return d(eps), d(-eps), d(eps), d(-eps)

eps = 1000e-6
x = GF * eps
print(f"GF eps = {x:.5f}")
for kind, approx in [('quarter', V * x / 4), ('half', V * x / 2), ('full', V * x)]:
    vo = bridge(V, *gauges(eps, kind))
    print(f"{kind:8s}: exact {vo*1e3:.5f} mV, linear formula {approx*1e3:.5f} mV, non-linearity {(approx/vo-1)*100:.4f} %")
vq = bridge(V, *gauges(eps, 'quarter'))
assert abs(vq - V * x / (4 + 2 * x)) < 1e-15                    # the closed form derived in the text
assert abs(vq / (V * x / 4) - 1) < 1.1e-3                         # quarter bridge ~ V GF eps / 4
assert abs(bridge(V, *gauges(eps, 'half')) - V * x / 2) < 1e-15   # half and full bridges are exactly linear
assert abs(bridge(V, *gauges(eps, 'full')) - V * x) < 1e-15

# non-linearity grows with strain: at 1 % strain (a yielding mild-steel coupon)
for e in (1e-3, 5e-3, 1e-2):
    xe = GF * e
    print(f"eps = {e*1e6:6.0f} microstrain: quarter-bridge reads {bridge(V, *gauges(e, 'quarter')) / (V/4*GF):.6f} -> error {(1 - bridge(V, *gauges(e, 'quarter')) / (V*xe/4))*100:.3f} %")

# temperature: 10 K warmer, gauge on steel shows 1.5 microstrain/K of apparent strain
app = 10 * 1.5e-6
for kind in ('quarter', 'quarter+dummy', 'half'):
    v0 = bridge(V, *gauges(0.0, kind, app))
    print(f"{kind:14s}: zero shift from +10 K = {v0*1e6:8.3f} microvolt = {v0 / (V*GF/4) * 1e6:6.2f} microstrain (quarter-bridge scale)")
assert abs(bridge(V, *gauges(0.0, 'half', app))) < 1e-15 and abs(bridge(V, *gauges(0.0, 'quarter+dummy', app))) < 1e-15

# lead wires: 10 m of 0.14 mm^2 copper each way in series with a quarter-bridge gauge
Rl = 2 * 0.0175 * 10 / 0.14                                      # ohm, both leads
vo = bridge(V, R * (1 + x) + Rl, R, R, R) - bridge(V, R + Rl, R, R, R)   # sensitivity after re-zeroing
print(f"lead resistance {Rl:.3f} ohm desensitises the quarter bridge by {(1 - vo / vq)*100:.2f} % (expected R/(R+R_L) -> {Rl/(R+Rl)*100:.2f} %)")
assert abs((1 - vo / vq) - Rl / (R + Rl)) < 2e-4
```

:::exercise Size the amplifier
A full bridge with $GF = 2.0$, $V = 10$ V carries ±500 µε at full load. You want ±2.5 V into an ADC. What gain do you need, and what is the bridge output per µε?
---solution---
1. Full bridge: $V_\text{out} = V\,GF\,\varepsilon = 10 \times 2.0 \times 500\times10^{-6} = 10$ mV at full load.
2. Gain $= 2.5/0.010 = 250$.
3. Sensitivity $10 \times 2.0 \times 10^{-6} = 20$ µV per µε before the amplifier, 5 mV per µε after it.
:::
