A washing machine on spin walks across the floor; a fan with a bent blade rattles its housing; a turbine with a lost blade fragment shakes its bearings. Each has a rotating part whose centre of mass is not on the axis. That small offset creates a force that grows with the square of speed.

## The forcing

An unbalanced mass $m$ at eccentricity $e$ spins at $\omega$ inside a machine of total mass $M$ (including $m$), mounted on a spring $k$ and damper $c$. The lump needs a centripetal force $me\omega^2$, so it pulls the machine with a rotating force of that size. Its vertical component drives the vertical motion:

$$
M\ddot x + c\dot x + kx = me\omega^2\cos\omega t .
$$

This is forced vibration with $F_0 = me\omega^2$, so the amplitude is

$$
\frac{MX}{me} = \frac{r^2}{\sqrt{(1-r^2)^2 + (2\zeta r)^2}} .
$$

## Three regions again

- **Low speed**: the force $me\omega^2$ is small, and so is $X$, growing as $r^2$.
- **Resonance**: $X = \dfrac{me}{M}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2\zeta}$. The machine must pass through quickly, and the damping must be enough to keep this tolerable.
- **High speed**: $X \to me/M$. The rotor and machine now spin about their *combined* centre of mass, which stays still. The amplitude stops growing however fast it spins. That's why a washing machine is noisy as it spins up and calmer at full speed.

The force into the floor is the transmissibility times $me\omega^2$. It keeps growing with speed above $\sqrt2\,\omega_n$, but much more slowly than the unbalance force itself.

:::example A washing machine
Drum assembly $M = 40$ kg, a 0.5 kg lump of wet laundry at $e = 0.25$ m, suspension $k = 40$ kN/m, $\zeta = 0.1$.

1. Resonance at $\omega_n = 31.62$ rad/s, which is 302 rpm. $me/M = 3.125$ mm.
2. Steady amplitudes, checked by an 80-cycle RK4 simulation where shown:

| speed | amplitude | simulated | force to floor | lump's pull $me\omega^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 150 rpm | 1.015 mm | 1.015 | 41 N | 31 N |
| 302 rpm | 15.626 mm | | 637 N | 125 N |
| 600 rpm | 4.148 mm | 4.147 | 179 N | 493 N |
| 1400 rpm | 3.274 mm | 3.274 | 179 N | 2687 N |

3. At resonance the floor feels five times the unbalance force. At 1400 rpm it feels only 7 % of it, while the drum moves about $me/M$. (That 600 and 1400 rpm give the same floor force here is a coincidence of $\zeta = 0.1$.)
4. At very high speed the amplitude is 3.1250 mm, exactly $me/M$.
:::

```python file=unbalance.py
"""Rotating unbalance: response of a washing-machine drum on its suspension."""
import math
import numpy as np


def amplitude(M, m, e, k, zeta, w):
    """Steady amplitude of M x'' + c x' + k x = m e w^2 cos(w t)."""
    wn = math.sqrt(k / M); r = w / wn
    return m * e / M * r**2 / math.sqrt((1 - r**2)**2 + (2 * zeta * r)**2)


def simulate(M, m, e, k, zeta, w, cycles=80, per=100):
    c = 2 * zeta * math.sqrt(k * M); dt = 2 * math.pi / w / per
    f = lambda t, s: np.array([s[1], (m * e * w**2 * math.cos(w * t) - c * s[1] - k * s[0]) / M])
    s = np.zeros(2); t = 0.0; xs = []
    for i in range(cycles * per):
        k1 = f(t, s); k2 = f(t + dt/2, s + dt/2*k1); k3 = f(t + dt/2, s + dt/2*k2); k4 = f(t + dt, s + dt*k3)
        s = s + dt/6*(k1 + 2*k2 + 2*k3 + k4); t += dt
        if i >= (cycles - 5) * per: xs.append(s[0])
    return (max(xs) - min(xs)) / 2


if __name__ == "__main__":
    M, m, e, k, zeta = 40.0, 0.5, 0.25, 40e3, 0.1     # drum assembly (incl. laundry), unbalanced lump, suspension
    wn = math.sqrt(k / M)
    print(f"suspension resonance {wn:.2f} rad/s = {wn*30/math.pi:.0f} rpm; m e / M = {m*e/M*1000:.3f} mm")
    for rpm in (150, 302, 600, 1400):
        w = rpm * math.pi / 30; X = amplitude(M, m, e, k, zeta, w)
        line = f"{rpm:5d} rpm: amplitude {X*1000:.3f} mm"
        if rpm in (150, 600, 1400):
            Xs = simulate(M, m, e, k, zeta, w); line += f" (simulated {Xs*1000:.3f})"; assert abs(Xs / X - 1) < 5e-3
        # force through spring and damper into the floor
        r = w / wn; FT = k * X * math.sqrt(1 + (2 * zeta * r)**2)
        print(line + f", force to the floor {FT:.0f} N (the lump itself pulls with {m*e*w*w:.0f} N)")
    w = 1400 * math.pi / 30
    assert abs(amplitude(M, m, e, k, zeta, w) / (m * e / M) - 1) < 0.06
    hi = amplitude(M, m, e, k, zeta, 1e5)
    print(f"very high speed: {hi*1000:.4f} mm -> m e / M; the drum spins about the combined centre of mass")
    assert abs(hi / (m * e / M) - 1) < 1e-6
```

:::exercise A pump on a frame
A 250 kg pump has a rotor unbalance of $me = 0.002$ kg m. It sits on a frame with $f_n = 12$ Hz and $\zeta = 0.05$. Find the amplitude at its running speed of 2900 rpm, and at resonance during start-up.
---solution---
1. $me/M = 0.002/250 = 8.0\times10^{-6}$ m.
2. Running: $r = (2900/60)/12 = 4.028$. $r^2 = 16.22$, and $r^2/\sqrt{(1-16.22)^2 + (0.4028)^2} = 1.0653$. $X = 8.52\ \mu$m.
3. Resonance: $X = 8\ \mu\text{m}/(2\cdot0.05) = 80\ \mu$m, ten times the steady running value.
:::
