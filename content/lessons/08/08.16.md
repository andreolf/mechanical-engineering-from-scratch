Hit a nail with a hammer and the force doesn't reach the far end instantly. A stress pulse runs down the nail at about 5 km/s, reflects from the point and returns. For slow loads this doesn't matter. For impacts, crashes, pile driving, rock drills and the Hopkinson bar used to test materials at high strain rates, these waves are the whole story.

## Wave speed and impedance

From 08.10, axial waves in a rod obey the wave equation with $c = \sqrt{E/\rho}$. Any shape travels unchanged. A wave moving to the right carries particle velocity $v$ and stress

$$
\sigma = -\rho c\,v \qquad (\text{tension positive}) ,
$$

and a left-moving wave carries $\sigma = +\rho c\,v$. The product $Z = \rho c$ is the **impedance** (per unit area). For steel, $Z \approx 40.6$ MPa per m/s.

## Two rods colliding

A striker of length $L_1$ moving at $v_0$ hits a resting bar of the same material and section. At the contact both faces must share one velocity and one stress. By symmetry the interface moves at $v_0/2$, and a compression pulse of

$$
\sigma = -\frac{\rho c\,v_0}{2}
$$

runs into each rod. Notice that it doesn't depend on the length or mass, only on the impact speed and impedance. A steel bar hit at 5 m/s sees about 100 MPa, whatever its size.

The pulse in the striker reflects from its free end, comes back, and unloads it after $2L_1/c$. At that moment the striker is at rest and the bar carries a compression pulse $2L_1$ long, moving at $v_0/2$. All the momentum and energy have passed across.

## Reflection at ends

- **Fixed end** ($v = 0$): the wave reflects with the same sign of stress. Compression returns as compression, and the stress doubles while the waves overlap.
- **Free end** ($\sigma = 0$): the wave reflects with opposite stress. **Compression returns as tension**, and the end velocity doubles.

That second rule is why concrete piles and brittle rocks can crack in tension near a free end even though they were struck in compression (**spalling**).

:::example A striker hits a bar
Steel rods, striker 1 m and bar 2 m long, impact at 5 m/s. The scheme moves right- and left-going waves exactly one cell per step (Courant number 1), so it is exact on the grid. The contact transmits compression only.

1. $c = 5172.2$ m/s. The predicted impact stress $\rho c v_0/2 = 101.50$ MPa; the simulation gives −101.50 MPa.
2. Contact force lasts 0.3867 ms, exactly $2L_1/c$. The faces part for good at 0.7734 ms, when tension reflected from the bar's far end arrives.
3. Afterwards the striker is at rest (velocity 0.0000 m/s). The bar's momentum is 39,250 kg/(m² s), exactly the striker's original $\rho L_1 v_0$, and the energy 98,125 J/m² is conserved to $10^{-9}$.
4. A probe 0.25 m from the bar's free end sees −101.50 MPa compression from 0.3383 ms. Then, once the 2 m pulse has fully passed and only its reflection remains, +101.50 MPa of tension from 0.7250 ms.
:::

```python file=impact.py
"""Stress waves on impact: a striker rod hits a bar end-on; exact characteristic (leapfrog) scheme at Courant number 1."""
import math
import numpy as np

E, rho = 210e9, 7850.0
c = math.sqrt(E / rho); Z = rho * c           # wave speed and impedance per unit area


def simulate(L1, L2, v0, nx_per_m=200, t_end=2e-3):
    """f: right-going velocity part, g: left-going. Particle velocity v = f + g, stress (tension +) s = Z (g - f).
    Striker cells 0..n1-1, bar cells n1..n1+n2-1. The interface transmits compression only."""
    dx = 1 / nx_per_m; dt = dx / c; n1, n2 = int(L1 * nx_per_m), int(L2 * nx_per_m)
    f = np.zeros(n1 + n2); g = np.zeros(n1 + n2); f[:n1] = g[:n1] = v0 / 2
    probe = n1 + n2 - int(0.25 * nx_per_m)                # 0.25 m from the bar's free end
    hist = []; contact = True; t_sep = None
    for step in range(int(t_end / dt)):
        fa, gb = f[n1 - 1], g[n1]                         # waves arriving at the interface from each side
        sig_if = Z * (gb - fa)
        if contact and sig_if > 0:
            contact = False; t_sep = step * dt
        fn = np.empty_like(f); gn = np.empty_like(g)
        fn[1:] = f[:-1]; gn[:-1] = g[1:]
        fn[0] = g[0]                                       # free left end of striker: s = 0 means f = g
        gn[-1] = f[-1]                                     # free right end of bar
        if not contact:                                    # separated: both interface faces are free
            gn[n1 - 1] = f[n1 - 1]; fn[n1] = g[n1]
        f, g = fn, gn
        hist.append((step * dt, Z * (g[probe] - f[probe]), Z * (g[n1 + 5] - f[n1 + 5]), sig_if if contact else 0.0))
    return np.array(hist), f, g, n1, t_sep, dx


if __name__ == "__main__":
    L1, L2, v0 = 1.0, 2.0, 5.0
    hist, f, g, n1, t_sep, dx = simulate(L1, L2, v0)
    print(f"steel: c = {c:.1f} m/s; impact stress rho c v / 2 = {Z*v0/2/1e6:.2f} MPa")
    smin = hist[:, 2].min()
    print(f"simulated peak compression near the impact face: {smin/1e6:.2f} MPa")
    assert abs(smin + Z * v0 / 2) < 1e-6 * Z * v0
    tf = hist[:, 0][hist[:, 3] < -0.5 * Z * v0 / 2]
    dur = tf.max() - tf.min() + dx / c
    print(f"contact force lasts {dur*1e3:.4f} ms (2 L1 / c = {2*L1/c*1e3:.4f} ms); the faces part for good at {t_sep*1e3:.4f} ms")
    assert abs(dur - 2 * L1 / c) < 2 * dx / c
    v = f + g
    print(f"after the impact: striker velocity {v[:n1].mean():.4f} m/s, bar momentum per area {rho*dx*v[n1:].sum():.2f} kg/(m^2 s) (striker had {rho*L1*v0:.2f})")
    assert abs(v[:n1]).max() < 1e-9 and abs(rho * dx * v[n1:].sum() - rho * L1 * v0) < 1e-6 * rho * L1 * v0
    # the probe near the free end first sees compression, then the reflected pulse as tension
    t, sp = hist[:, 0], hist[:, 1]
    tc = t[np.argmax(sp < -1e6)]; tt = t[np.argmax(sp > 1e6)]
    print(f"probe 0.25 m from the free end: compression {sp.min()/1e6:.2f} MPa from t = {tc*1e3:.4f} ms, tension {sp.max()/1e6:.2f} MPa from t = {tt*1e3:.4f} ms")
    assert sp.max() > 0.99 * Z * v0 / 2 and tt > tc
    # the overlap of incoming compression and reflected tension cancels near the end: the probe is 0.25 m from it, the pulse 2 m long
    E_k = 0.5 * rho * dx * np.sum(v**2) + 0.5 * dx * np.sum((Z * (g - f))**2) / E
    print(f"energy per area after: {E_k:.2f} J/m^2 (before {0.5*rho*L1*v0**2:.2f})")
    assert abs(E_k / (0.5 * rho * L1 * v0**2) - 1) < 1e-9
```

:::exercise A pile-driving hammer
A steel ram hits a steel pile head-on at 4 m/s, with the same cross-section of 0.01 m². What is the stress in the pile, and what force does the pile feel? If the pile is 20 m long and its toe is in soft mud (nearly free), what happens when the pulse reaches the toe?
---solution---
1. $\sigma = \rho c v_0/2 = 7850\cdot5172.2\cdot2 = 81.2$ MPa compression.
2. Force $= 81.2\times10^6\cdot0.01 = 812$ kN.
3. At a nearly free toe the compression reflects as tension of up to 81 MPa. Fine for steel, but a concrete pile driven into soft ground can crack in tension this way. Pile drivers watch for it with strain gauges near the head.
:::
