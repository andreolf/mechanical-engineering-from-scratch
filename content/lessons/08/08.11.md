Twang a ruler held over the edge of a desk. Its pitch rises sharply as you shorten the overhang. Diving boards, aircraft wings, turbine blades, chimneys and the tines of a tuning fork all vibrate in bending, and bending modes behave quite differently from those of strings and rods.

## The beam equation

From the Euler-Bernoulli theory of Phase 06, the bending moment is $EI\,w''$ and the shear force its derivative. A slice of beam, mass $\rho A\,dx$, is accelerated by the change in shear force across it:

$$
EI\frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial x^4} + \rho A\frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial t^2} = 0 .
$$

The fourth derivative changes everything. Try $w = W(x)\cos\omega t$:

$$
W'''' = \beta^4 W, \qquad \beta^4 = \frac{\rho A\,\omega^2}{EI} ,
$$

with general solution $W = a\cos\beta x + b\sin\beta x + c\cosh\beta x + d\sinh\beta x$.

## The cantilever

Clamped at $x = 0$ ($W = W' = 0$) and free at $x = L$ (no moment, $W'' = 0$, and no shear, $W''' = 0$). Four conditions on four constants give a non-zero solution only if

$$
\cos\beta L\,\cosh\beta L = -1 .
$$

This has no closed-form roots. Numerically $\beta_n L$ = 1.8751, 4.6941, 7.8548, 10.9955, … and then

$$
f_n = \frac{(\beta_n L)^2}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{EI}{\rho A L^4}} .
$$

The overtones are 6.27, 17.5 and 34.4 times the fundamental: not harmonics. That's why a struck bar sounds clangy, and why gamelan and marimba makers carve an arch under their bars to pull the overtones toward musical ratios.

Frequency goes as $1/L^2$: halving the overhang quadruples the pitch. For a rectangular section $EI/\rho A \propto t^2$, so frequency is proportional to thickness.

:::example A steel ruler on a desk
300 mm overhang, 25 mm wide, 1 mm thick, $E = 200$ GPa, $\rho = 7850$ kg/m³.

1. Brent's method on $\cos x\cosh x + 1 = 0$: $\beta_n L$ = 1.8751, 4.6941, 7.8548, 10.9955. Frequency ratios 1, 6.267, 17.547, 34.386.
2. $EI = 200\times10^9\cdot0.025\cdot0.001^3/12$ and $\rho A = 0.19625$ kg/m give $f_1$ to $f_4$ = 9.06, 56.78, 158.98 and 311.53 Hz.
3. 40 Hermite beam finite elements agree to within $4\times10^{-6}$ relative for all four modes.
4. Shorten the overhang to 150 mm: $f_1 = 36.24$ Hz, exactly four times.
:::

```python file=beam_modes.py
"""Bending vibration of beams: roots of the cantilever frequency equation cos(bL) cosh(bL) = -1, checked by finite elements."""
import math
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import brentq
from scipy.linalg import eigh


def cantilever_roots(n):
    """beta_n L from cos x cosh x + 1 = 0, bracketed near (2n - 1) pi / 2."""
    f = lambda x: math.cos(x) * math.cosh(x) + 1
    return [brentq(f, (i - 0.5) * math.pi - 1.2 if i > 1 else 1.0, (i - 0.5) * math.pi + 1.2 if i > 1 else 2.5, xtol=1e-14) for i in range(1, n + 1)]


def fe_cantilever(L, EI, rhoA, ne):
    """Euler-Bernoulli Hermite beam elements, consistent mass; clamped at x = 0."""
    h = L / ne
    k = EI / h**3 * np.array([[12, 6*h, -12, 6*h], [6*h, 4*h*h, -6*h, 2*h*h], [-12, -6*h, 12, -6*h], [6*h, 2*h*h, -6*h, 4*h*h]])
    m = rhoA * h / 420 * np.array([[156, 22*h, 54, -13*h], [22*h, 4*h*h, 13*h, -3*h*h], [54, 13*h, 156, -22*h], [-13*h, -3*h*h, -22*h, 4*h*h]])
    n = 2 * (ne + 1); K = np.zeros((n, n)); M = np.zeros((n, n))
    for e in range(ne):
        s = slice(2 * e, 2 * e + 4); K[s, s] += k; M[s, s] += m
    return np.sqrt(eigh(K[2:, 2:], M[2:, 2:], eigvals_only=True)) / (2 * math.pi)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    b = cantilever_roots(4)
    print("beta_n L:", [round(x, 4) for x in b])
    assert abs(b[0] - 1.8751) < 1e-4
    print("ratios f_n / f_1:", [round((x / b[0])**2, 3) for x in b])
    # a steel ruler clamped to a desk: 300 mm overhang, 25 mm wide, 1 mm thick
    E, rho, w, t, L = 200e9, 7850.0, 0.025, 0.001, 0.30
    EI = E * w * t**3 / 12; rhoA = rho * w * t
    f = [x**2 / (2 * math.pi) * math.sqrt(EI / (rhoA * L**4)) for x in b]
    print(f"ruler: f1..f4 = {', '.join(f'{x:.2f}' for x in f)} Hz")
    fe = fe_cantilever(L, EI, rhoA, 40)
    print(f"finite elements (40 elements): {', '.join(f'{x:.2f}' for x in fe[:4])} Hz")
    print("relative differences:", [f"{x:.1e}" for x in np.abs(fe[:4] / np.array(f) - 1)])
    assert np.allclose(fe[:4], f, rtol=1e-5)
    # halve the overhang: frequency x 4
    print(f"150 mm overhang: f1 = {b[0]**2/(2*math.pi)*math.sqrt(EI/(rhoA*0.15**4)):.2f} Hz")
```

:::exercise A turbine blade check
Model a steel turbine blade as a uniform cantilever 0.12 m long and 4 mm thick (bending about the thin direction), with $E = 200$ GPa and $\rho = 7850$ kg/m³. Is its first bending mode clear of an engine running at 3000 rpm and its first 8 multiples (excitation orders)?
---solution---
1. $EI/(\rho A) = Et^2/(12\rho) = 200\times10^9\cdot(0.004)^2/(12\cdot7850) = 33.97$ m⁴/s².
2. $f_1 = 1.8751^2/(2\pi)\cdot\sqrt{33.97}/0.12^2 = 0.5596\cdot5.828/0.0144 = 226.5$ Hz.
3. 3000 rpm is 50 Hz, so the orders lie at 50, 100, …, 400 Hz. $f_1$ = 226.5 Hz sits between orders 4 (200 Hz) and 5 (250 Hz): 13 % above one and 9 % below the other. That's a reasonable margin, though designers would also check the higher modes and the stiffening effect of rotation, which raises blade frequencies at speed.
:::
