Hang two pendulums side by side and join them with a weak spring. Start one swinging and leave the other still. Slowly the first one stops and the second takes over, then they swap back. Nothing is lost; the energy just passes between them. Explaining that needs the idea at the centre of all structural dynamics: **modes**.

## Equations in matrix form

A system with two coordinates $\mathbf x = (x_1, x_2)$ obeys

$$
\mathbf M\ddot{\mathbf x} + \mathbf K\mathbf x = \mathbf 0 ,
$$

with a symmetric mass matrix $\mathbf M$ and stiffness matrix $\mathbf K$. For the chain wall, spring $k_1$, mass $m_1$, spring $k_2$, mass $m_2$:

$$
\mathbf M = \begin{bmatrix} m_1 & 0 \\ 0 & m_2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf K = \begin{bmatrix} k_1 + k_2 & -k_2 \\ -k_2 & k_2 \end{bmatrix} .
$$

## Modes as an eigenproblem

Look for motions where every coordinate moves in step at one frequency: $\mathbf x = \boldsymbol\phi\cos\omega t$. Substituting gives

$$
(\mathbf K - \omega^2\mathbf M)\boldsymbol\phi = \mathbf 0 .
$$

A non-zero $\boldsymbol\phi$ needs $\det(\mathbf K - \omega^2\mathbf M) = 0$, a quadratic in $\omega^2$ with two roots: two natural frequencies, each with its **mode shape** $\boldsymbol\phi_i$. Any free motion is a mix of the two modes.

## Orthogonality

The mode shapes are not perpendicular in the ordinary sense, but they are **M-orthogonal** and **K-orthogonal**:

$$
\boldsymbol\phi_1^T\mathbf M\boldsymbol\phi_2 = 0, \qquad \boldsymbol\phi_1^T\mathbf K\boldsymbol\phi_2 = 0 .
$$

Scaling each so that $\boldsymbol\phi_i^T\mathbf M\boldsymbol\phi_i = 1$ (mass normalisation) makes $\boldsymbol\Phi^T\mathbf M\boldsymbol\Phi = \mathbf I$ and $\boldsymbol\Phi^T\mathbf K\boldsymbol\Phi = \text{diag}(\omega_i^2)$. This is what lets 08.08 split big systems into independent oscillators.

## Beats

For the coupled pendulums the modes are obvious: swinging together at $\omega_1 = \sqrt{g/l}$ (the spring never stretches), and swinging opposite at $\omega_2 = \sqrt{g/l + 2k/m}$. Pulling one pendulum aside starts both modes with equal amplitude. Their sum is

$$
\theta_1 = \tfrac{\theta_0}{2}(\cos\omega_1 t + \cos\omega_2 t) = \theta_0\cos\tfrac{\omega_2-\omega_1}{2}t\,\cos\tfrac{\omega_2+\omega_1}{2}t ,
$$

a fast swing inside a slow envelope. The energy moves to the other pendulum and back once every **beat period** $2\pi/(\omega_2 - \omega_1)$. Two gamelan instruments tuned slightly apart beat in exactly this way, and Balinese tuning uses it on purpose to make the sound shimmer.

:::example Coupled pendulums and a two-mass chain
1. Pendulums: $m = 1$ kg, $l = 1$ m, coupling spring 0.5 N/m between the bobs. Solving the eigenproblem gives 3.1321 rad/s with shape (1, 1) and 3.2879 rad/s with shape (1, −1), matching $\sqrt{g/l}$ and $\sqrt{g/l + 2k/m}$ to machine precision.
2. Mass-normalised: $\boldsymbol\Phi^T\mathbf M\boldsymbol\Phi = \mathbf I$, and $\boldsymbol\Phi^T\mathbf K\boldsymbol\Phi$ has 9.81 and 10.81 on its diagonal, the squared frequencies.
3. Start pendulum 1 at 0.1 rad. Beat period 40.34 s. Half a beat later, at 20.17 s, pendulum 2 swings with the full 0.1000 rad and pendulum 1 is almost still.
4. Chain $m_1 = 2$, $m_2 = 1$ kg, $k_1 = 2000$, $k_2 = 1000$ N/m: frequencies 22.3607 and 44.7214 rad/s with shapes (1, 2) and (1, −1). Their plain dot product is −0.2357 after mass-normalising, but with $\mathbf M$ in between it is $-8\times10^{-17}$, zero to rounding.
:::

```python file=twodof.py
"""Two-DOF systems: modes of coupled pendulums, M-orthogonality and beats."""
import math
import numpy as np


def modes(M, K):
    """Solve (K - w^2 M) phi = 0 via the symmetric form L^-1 K L^-T (M = L L^T); mass-normalised modes."""
    L = np.linalg.cholesky(M); Li = np.linalg.inv(L)
    lam, V = np.linalg.eigh(Li @ K @ Li.T)
    return np.sqrt(lam), Li.T @ V


if __name__ == "__main__":
    g, l, m, ks = 9.81, 1.0, 1.0, 0.5             # two 1 m pendulums joined at the bobs by a soft spring
    M = np.diag([m * l**2, m * l**2])
    K = np.array([[m * g * l + ks * l**2, -ks * l**2], [-ks * l**2, m * g * l + ks * l**2]])
    w, Phi = modes(M, K)
    print(f"mode 1: {w[0]:.4f} rad/s, shape {Phi[:,0]/Phi[0,0]}; mode 2: {w[1]:.4f} rad/s, shape {Phi[:,1]/Phi[0,1]}")
    assert abs(w[0] - math.sqrt(g / l)) < 1e-12 and abs(w[1] - math.sqrt(g / l + 2 * ks / m)) < 1e-12
    print("Phi^T M Phi =\n", np.round(Phi.T @ M @ Phi, 12), "\nPhi^T K Phi diag =", np.round(np.diag(Phi.T @ K @ Phi), 6))
    assert np.allclose(Phi.T @ M @ Phi, np.eye(2), atol=1e-12)
    assert np.allclose(Phi.T @ K @ Phi, np.diag(w**2), atol=1e-10)
    # beats: start pendulum 1 at 0.1 rad, pendulum 2 at rest; exact modal solution
    q0 = Phi.T @ M @ np.array([0.1, 0.0])
    t = np.linspace(0, 60, 60001)
    th = Phi @ (q0[:, None] * np.cos(np.outer(w, t)))
    Tb = 2 * math.pi / (w[1] - w[0])
    i = np.argmin(np.abs(t - Tb / 2))
    print(f"beat period {Tb:.2f} s: at t = {t[i]:.2f} s pendulum 1 swings with envelope {np.max(np.abs(th[0, i-1500:i+1500])):.4f} rad, pendulum 2 {np.max(np.abs(th[1, i-1500:i+1500])):.4f} rad")
    assert np.max(np.abs(th[1, i-1500:i+1500])) > 0.099
    # an unequal 2-DOF chain: wall - k1 - m1 - k2 - m2
    M2 = np.diag([2.0, 1.0]); K2 = np.array([[3000.0, -1000.0], [-1000.0, 1000.0]])
    w2, P2 = modes(M2, K2)
    print(f"chain: {w2[0]:.4f} and {w2[1]:.4f} rad/s, shapes {P2[:,0]/P2[0,0]} and {P2[:,1]/P2[0,1]}")
    assert abs(P2[:, 0] @ M2 @ P2[:, 1]) < 1e-12 and abs(P2[:, 0] @ P2[:, 1]) > 1e-3   # M-orthogonal, not plainly orthogonal
    print(f"plain dot product of the modes {P2[:,0] @ P2[:,1]:.4f}; with M in between {P2[:,0] @ M2 @ P2[:,1]:.1e}")
```

:::exercise Modes by hand
For the chain $m_1 = 2$ kg, $m_2 = 1$ kg, $k_1 = 2000$ N/m, $k_2 = 1000$ N/m, write $\det(\mathbf K - \omega^2\mathbf M) = 0$ and solve it.
---solution---
1. $\mathbf K = \begin{bmatrix} 3000 & -1000 \\ -1000 & 1000 \end{bmatrix}$. With $\lambda = \omega^2$: $(3000 - 2\lambda)(1000 - \lambda) - 10^6 = 0$.
2. Expand: $2\lambda^2 - 5000\lambda + 2\times10^6 = 0$, so $\lambda = 500$ or $2000$.
3. $\omega_1 = 22.36$ rad/s, $\omega_2 = 44.72$ rad/s.
4. Mode 1 from the second row: $-1000\phi_1 + (1000 - 500)\phi_2 = 0$, so $\phi_2 = 2\phi_1$. Mode 2: $\phi_2 = -\phi_1$.
:::
