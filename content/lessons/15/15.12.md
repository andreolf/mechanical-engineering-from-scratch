A bolt is a spring. Tighten it and it stretches a little while the parts it clamps squeeze a little. That **preload** is the whole point: a well-preloaded joint carries most of an external pulling load by *unloading the clamped parts*, not by loading the bolt. This is why a correctly torqued bolt survives fatigue loads that would snap a loose one.

## Stiffness of the bolt and the members

Inside the grip length $l$, the bolt is an unthreaded shank of area $A_d$ and length $l_d$ in series with a threaded part of tensile stress area $A_t$ and length $l_t$:

$$
k_b = \frac{A_d A_t E}{A_d l_t + A_t l_d} .
$$

The clamped members are compressed in a region that spreads out from the washer face roughly as a 30° cone (a **frustum**). For two equal steel members with a washer face of $1.5d$, integrating that cone gives

$$
k_m = \frac{0.5774\,\pi E d}{2\ln\!\left(5\,\dfrac{0.5774\,l + 0.5d}{0.5774\,l + 2.5d}\right)} .
$$

The members are typically three to five times stiffer than the bolt.

## The joint diagram

Preload $F_i$ stretches the bolt and compresses the members. An external load $P$ pulling the joint apart stretches both by the same extra amount $\delta$, so the bolt gains $k_b\delta$ and the members lose $k_m\delta$, with $k_b\delta + k_m\delta = P$:

$$
F_b = F_i + C P, \qquad F_m = F_i - (1 - C)P, \qquad C = \frac{k_b}{k_b + k_m} .
$$

$C$ is the **joint constant**, usually 0.15 to 0.3: the bolt feels only a fraction of the load. This holds until the members' compression reaches zero at the **separation load** $P_0 = F_i/(1 - C)$; beyond it, the joint opens and the bolt takes everything. Typical preload is 75% of the bolt's proof load for reusable joints and 90% for permanent ones, applied with a tightening torque $T \approx K F_i d$, where the nut factor $K \approx 0.2$ for dry steel (and scatters by ±25%, which is why critical joints use angle or stretch control).

:::example An M10 class 8.8 bolt through two steel plates
$d = 10$ mm, $A_t = 58.0$ mm², proof strength 600 MPa; two 15 mm plates (grip 30 mm); a 40 mm long bolt with threaded length $2d + 6 = 26$ mm, so $l_d = 14$ mm and $l_t = 16$ mm in the grip.

1. $k_b = 78.54\cdot58.0\cdot207\,000/(78.54\cdot16 + 58.0\cdot14) = 455.8$ kN/mm.
2. $k_m = 0.5774\pi\cdot207\,000\cdot10/(2\ln(5\cdot22.32/42.32)) = 1936.1$ kN/mm.
3. $C = 455.8/(455.8 + 1936.1) = 0.1906$.
4. Preload $0.75\cdot58.0\cdot600 = 26.10$ kN, from a torque of about $0.2\cdot26\,100\cdot0.010 = 52.2$ N·m.
5. Simulating the bolt and plates as two springs: at $P = 15$ kN the bolt carries 28.959 kN (it gained only 2.859 kN = $C\cdot15$) and the plates still press with 13.959 kN. Bolt stress 499.3 MPa, below the 600 MPa proof.
6. Separation at $P_0 = 26.10/(1 - 0.1906) = 32.24$ kN. At 40 kN the joint is open and the bolt carries all 40 kN.
:::

```python file=bolt.py
"""Bolted joint: bolt and member stiffness, the joint diagram, how an external load is shared, and the separation load. The load share is checked by simulating the bolt and members as two springs."""
import math
import numpy as np


def bolt_stiffness(d, At, ld, lt, E):
    """Unthreaded shank length ld and threaded length lt inside the grip act as springs in series."""
    Ad = math.pi * d**2 / 4
    return Ad * At * E / (Ad * lt + At * ld)


def member_stiffness(d, l, E):
    """Shigley's frustum (30 degree cone) result for two equal steel members, washer face 1.5 d, total grip l."""
    return 0.5774 * math.pi * E * d / (2 * math.log(5 * (0.5774 * l + 0.5 * d) / (0.5774 * l + 2.5 * d)))


def joint_forces(Fi, kb, km, P):
    """Simulate the joint as two springs held at the same extension: find the extension that balances P."""
    # after preload, bolt force = Fi + kb x, member force = Fi - km x (while compressed); equilibrium: Fb - Fm = P
    def residual(x):
        Fb = Fi + kb * x
        Fm = max(Fi - km * x, 0.0)     # members cannot pull: once separated they carry nothing
        return Fb - Fm - P
    lo, hi = 0.0, 10 * (P + Fi) / kb + 1.0
    for _ in range(200):
        mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        if residual(mid) > 0:
            hi = mid
        else:
            lo = mid
    x = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
    return Fi + kb * x, max(Fi - km * x, 0.0)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # M10 x 1.5 property class 8.8 through two 15 mm steel plates, 40 mm long bolt
    d, At, Sp, E = 10.0, 58.0, 600.0, 207e3
    l, L = 30.0, 40.0
    LT = 2 * d + 6                  # threaded length of a short bolt
    ld = L - LT; lt = l - ld
    kb = bolt_stiffness(d, At, ld, lt, E)
    km = member_stiffness(d, l, E)
    C = kb / (kb + km)
    Fi = 0.75 * At * Sp             # preload 75% of proof load for a reusable joint
    print(f"kb = {kb/1000:.1f} kN/mm, km = {km/1000:.1f} kN/mm, joint constant C = {C:.4f}")
    print(f"preload {Fi/1000:.2f} kN, tightening torque about {0.2*Fi*d/1000:.1f} N m (K = 0.2)")
    for P in (5000.0, 15000.0, 30000.0, 40000.0):
        Fb, Fm = joint_forces(Fi, kb, km, P)
        if P < Fi / (1 - C):
            assert abs((Fb - Fi) - C * P) < 1e-6 * P  # the bolt sees only the fraction C of the external load
        else:
            assert Fm == 0 and abs(Fb - P) < 1e-6 * P  # separated: the bolt carries everything
        print(f"P = {P/1000:5.1f} kN: bolt {Fb/1000:.3f} kN, members {Fm/1000:.3f} kN")
    P0 = Fi / (1 - C)
    print(f"separation load {P0/1000:.2f} kN; bolt stress at P = 15 kN: {joint_forces(Fi, kb, km, 15000)[0]/At:.1f} MPa (proof 600)")
```

:::exercise A fatigue load
The same joint sees an external load cycling between 0 and 10 kN. What is the bolt force range, and what would it be without any preload?
---solution---
1. With preload: from $F_i = 26.10$ kN to $26.10 + 0.1906\cdot10 = 28.01$ kN. The alternating force is only $\pm0.95$ kN.
2. Without preload the joint is open from the start: the bolt goes from 0 to 10 kN, an alternating force of $\pm5$ kN, more than five times larger.
3. Preload trades a higher mean stress for a much smaller alternating stress, and alternating stress is what drives fatigue.
:::
