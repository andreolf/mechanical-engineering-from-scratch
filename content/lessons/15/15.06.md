A ball bearing does not wear out like a brake pad. If it is clean and lubricated, it fails by **rolling contact fatigue**: after many millions of stress cycles, a crack starts below the surface of a race and a flake of metal spalls out. The time to that event is random, even for identical bearings under identical loads, so bearing life is a statistical quantity.

## The L10 life

The **basic rating life** $L_{10}$ is the number of revolutions that 90% of a large group of identical bearings survive. Tests show it follows a power law in the load:

$$
L_{10} = \left(\frac{C}{P}\right)^{a} \ \text{million revolutions}, \qquad a = 3 \ \text{(ball)}, \quad a = \tfrac{10}{3} \ \text{(roller)} .
$$

$C$ is the **dynamic load rating** from the catalogue: the load giving $L_{10}$ = one million revolutions. $P$ is the **equivalent load**. For combined radial and axial load the catalogue gives $P = X F_r + Y F_a$, with $X$ and $Y$ depending on the ratio $F_a/F_r$ and the bearing type. Because of the cube law, **doubling the load cuts a ball bearing's life by 8**, and a 10% overload costs about 25% of the life.

Rearranged for selection: $C_{\text{req}} = P\, L^{1/a}$, with $L$ in millions of revolutions.

## Other reliabilities

Fatigue lives scatter roughly as a **Weibull distribution**: the fraction surviving to life $L$ is $\exp[-(L/\eta)^b]$, with slope $b \approx 1.5$ for ball bearings. Anchoring it at the $L_{10}$ point gives the life at any reliability $R$:

$$
L_R = L_{10}\left(\frac{\ln(1/R)}{\ln(1/0.9)}\right)^{1/b} .
$$

So 95% reliability gives $0.62\,L_{10}$, 99% gives $0.21\,L_{10}$, and the median bearing lasts $3.5\,L_{10}$. (ISO 281 tabulates slightly different factors, 0.64 and 0.25, because it adds a minimum life below which no bearing fails.)

:::widget bearinglife
The life curve is a straight line on log-log axes, with slope −3 for balls and −10/3 for rollers.
:::

:::example A scooter wheel bearing
A 6202 deep groove ball bearing has $C = 8060$ N. On a scooter wheel of 450 mm diameter it carries an equivalent load of 1500 N (weight plus a dynamic allowance).

1. $L_{10} = (8060/1500)^3 = 155.1$ million revolutions. The wheel rolls 1.414 m per revolution, so that is about 219 000 km.
2. At 3000 N: $155.1/8 = 19.4$ million revolutions.
3. Combined load $F_r = 1200$ N, $F_a = 400$ N with catalogue factors $X = 0.56$, $Y = 1.8$: $P = 672 + 720 = 1392$ N, $L_{10} = 194.1$ million revolutions.
4. At 95% reliability the life is $0.619\,L_{10} = 96.0$ million revolutions.
5. A simulated fleet of 200 000 bearings with Weibull lives: 10.01% fail before $L_{10}$, and the median is $3.51\,L_{10}$, as the formula says.

The fatigue life is huge, yet scooter wheel bearings in Bali often last only a few years. Real bearings in wet, sandy service fail from **contamination and corrosion** long before fatigue; the catalogue's life modification factors (ISO 281 $a_{\text{ISO}}$) can cut the life tenfold or more for dirty conditions. Seals matter more than $C$ here.
:::

```python file=bearing.py
"""Rolling bearing life: L10 = (C/P)^a, equivalent load, life at other reliabilities from a Weibull model, and a Monte Carlo fleet of bearings."""
import math
import numpy as np

B_WEIBULL = 1.5  # typical Weibull slope for ball bearing fatigue


def L10(C, P, roller=False):
    """Basic rating life in millions of revolutions."""
    a = 10 / 3 if roller else 3
    return (C / P) ** a


def equivalent_load(Fr, Fa, X=1.0, Y=0.0):
    return X * Fr + Y * Fa


def life_at_reliability(L10_life, R, b=B_WEIBULL):
    """Two-parameter Weibull through the L10 point: fraction surviving is exp(-(L/eta)^b)."""
    return L10_life * (math.log(1 / R) / math.log(1 / 0.9)) ** (1 / b)


def required_C(P, life_mrev, roller=False):
    a = 10 / 3 if roller else 3
    return P * life_mrev ** (1 / a)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    C = 8060.0  # deep groove ball bearing 6202, dynamic rating (N)
    P = 1500.0
    L = L10(C, P)
    wheel = math.pi * 0.45  # scooter wheel circumference, m
    print(f"L10 = {L:.1f} million revolutions = {L*1e6*wheel/1000:,.0f} km")
    assert abs(L10(C, 2 * P) - L / 8) < 1e-9  # doubling the load cuts ball bearing life 8 times
    assert abs(L10(C, 2 * P, True) / L10(C, P, True) - 2 ** (-10 / 3)) < 1e-12
    # combined load with catalogue factors
    Pc = equivalent_load(1200, 400, X=0.56, Y=1.8)
    print(f"Fr 1200 N + Fa 400 N -> P = {Pc:.0f} N, L10 = {L10(C, Pc):.1f} Mrev")
    # reliability: 95% and 99% lives, and the median
    for R in (0.95, 0.99, 0.5):
        print(f"life at {100*R:.0f}% reliability: {life_at_reliability(L, R):.1f} Mrev ({life_at_reliability(L, R)/L:.3f} x L10)")
    # Monte Carlo fleet: Weibull lives scaled so 10% fail before L10
    eta = L / (math.log(1 / 0.9)) ** (1 / B_WEIBULL)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(5)
    lives = eta * rng.weibull(B_WEIBULL, 200_000)
    frac = np.mean(lives < L)
    print(f"fleet of 200000: {100*frac:.2f}% fail before L10, median life {np.median(lives)/L:.3f} x L10")
    assert abs(frac - 0.10) < 0.003
    assert abs(np.median(lives) / life_at_reliability(L, 0.5) - 1) < 0.01
    # selection: required rating for 20000 km of scooter riding at 1500 N
    need = required_C(P, 20000e3 / wheel / 1e6)
    print(f"for 20000 km: C >= {need:.0f} N")
```

:::exercise Choosing a bearing
A gearbox shaft runs at 1500 rpm with an equivalent radial load of 2.2 kN, and must reach 20 000 hours with 90% reliability. What dynamic rating is needed for a ball bearing and for a roller bearing?
---solution---
1. Revolutions: $1500\cdot60\cdot20\,000 = 1.8\times10^9 = 1800$ million.
2. Ball: $C = 2200\cdot1800^{1/3} = 2200\cdot12.164 = 26\,760$ N, so about 26.8 kN (a 6306 has 33.2 kN, enough).
3. Roller: $C = 2200\cdot1800^{0.3} = 2200\cdot9.475 = 20\,850$ N, about 20.8 kN.
:::
