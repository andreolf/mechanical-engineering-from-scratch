When two shafts are far apart, gears become heavy and expensive. A **belt** or a **chain** carries the power across the gap instead. Belts grip by friction and are quiet and forgiving; chains mesh positively with sprocket teeth and never slip. Each has one characteristic piece of physics: the belt's **capstan equation** and the chain's **polygonal effect**.

## The polygonal effect

A chain is not a flexible band: it is a string of rigid links of pitch $p$, so it wraps the sprocket as a polygon, not a circle. The pitch radius of an $N$-tooth sprocket is

$$
R = \frac{p}{2\sin(\pi/N)} .
$$

As the sprocket turns at $\omega$, the straight span leaves from the pin at the top. While that pin swings through $\pm\pi/N$ around the top, the chain's speed is $\omega R\cos\theta$: it varies between $\omega R\cos(\pi/N)$ and $\omega R$ once per tooth. The fractional speed fluctuation is

$$
\frac{\Delta v}{v_{\max}} = 1 - \cos\frac{\pi}{N} \approx \frac{\pi^2}{2N^2} .
$$

It falls quickly with tooth count. That pulsing excites vibration and noise and adds dynamic load, which is why chain makers recommend at least 17 or so teeth on the small sprocket for fast drives. Bicycles get away with 11-tooth cogs because they turn slowly.

## The capstan equation

A flat belt wrapped an angle $\theta$ around a pulley can hold a tension ratio of at most

$$
\frac{T_1 - T_c}{T_2 - T_c} = e^{\mu\theta}, \qquad T_c = m' v^2, \qquad P = (T_1 - T_2)\,v ,
$$

where $T_1$ is the tight side, $T_2$ the slack side, $m'$ the belt mass per metre and $T_c$ the **centrifugal tension**, which the belt carries without contributing any grip. A V-belt wedges into a groove of angle $2\beta$, multiplying the effective friction to $\mu' = \mu/\sin\beta$: with a 40° groove, almost three times more. That is why the CVT in a scooter uses a V-belt.

Assumptions: the chain span leaves horizontally from the top pin (no span vibration), the belt is inextensible and slips everywhere at once at the limit (real belts creep over part of the arc).

:::example Bike sprockets and a scooter belt
1. Bicycle chain, pitch 12.7 mm. An 11-tooth cog has pitch radius 22.539 mm; simulating the top pin over two tooth periods gives a speed fluctuation of 4.051%, matching $1 - \cos(\pi/11)$.
2. For 14, 18, 25, 32 and 42 teeth: 2.507%, 1.519%, 0.789%, 0.482% and 0.280%. A big chainring runs almost smoothly.
3. A flat belt carries 2 kW at 12 m/s with $\mu = 0.35$ over 160° of wrap; it weighs 0.12 kg/m. Then $e^{\mu\theta} = 2.6575$, $T_c = 0.12\cdot144 = 17.28$ N and $T_1 - T_2 = 2000/12 = 166.7$ N.
4. Slack side $T_2 = 166.7/(2.6575 - 1) + 17.28 = 117.8$ N, tight side 284.5 N, so the belt must be installed with an initial tension of about $(T_1 + T_2)/2 = 201.2$ N.
5. A V-belt in a 40° groove has $\mu' = 0.35/\sin20° = 1.023$ and $e^{\mu'\theta} = 17.42$.
:::

```python file=chain.py
"""Chain and belt drives: the polygonal (chordal) speed fluctuation of a sprocket, simulated pin by pin, and belt tensions from the capstan equation with centrifugal tension."""
import math
import numpy as np


def pitch_radius(p, N):
    return p / (2 * math.sin(math.pi / N))


def simulate_chain_speed(p, N, omega=1.0, samples=200001):
    """Sprocket turning at omega. The straight top span leaves from whichever pin is nearest the top; the chain
    moves with that pin's horizontal velocity. Sampled over two tooth periods, velocity by central differences."""
    R = pitch_radius(p, N)
    t = np.linspace(0, 2 * (2 * math.pi / N) / omega, samples)
    h = 1e-7
    pins = 2 * math.pi * np.arange(N) / N
    ang = omega * t[:, None] + pins[None, :]                       # pin angles from the top
    wrapped = (ang + math.pi) % (2 * math.pi) - math.pi
    j = np.argmin(np.abs(wrapped), axis=1)                          # the pin at the top carries the span
    a0 = wrapped[np.arange(len(t)), j]
    v = (R * np.sin(a0 + omega * h) - R * np.sin(a0 - omega * h)) / (2 * h)
    return v.min(), v.max()


def capstan(T2, mu, wrap):
    return T2 * math.exp(mu * wrap)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    p = 12.7  # bicycle chain pitch, mm
    prev = None
    for N in (11, 14, 18, 25, 32, 42):
        vmin, vmax = simulate_chain_speed(p, N)
        R = pitch_radius(p, N)
        fluct = 1 - vmin / vmax
        print(f"{N:2d} teeth: pitch radius {R:.3f} mm, speed {vmin:.4f} to {vmax:.4f} (x omega), fluctuation {100*fluct:.3f}% (theory {100*(1-math.cos(math.pi/N)):.3f}%)")
        assert abs(vmax / R - 1) < 1e-6 and abs(fluct - (1 - math.cos(math.pi / N))) < 1e-4
        if prev is not None:
            assert fluct < prev
        prev = fluct
    # flat belt on a scooter-sized pulley: 2 kW at 12 m/s, wrap 160 degrees, mu = 0.35, belt 0.12 kg/m
    P, v, mu, wrap, mass = 2000.0, 12.0, 0.35, math.radians(160), 0.12
    Tc = mass * v**2
    ratio = math.exp(mu * wrap)
    dT = P / v                         # T1 - T2 carried by friction
    T2 = dT / (ratio - 1) + Tc         # capstan acts on the tensions above the centrifugal part
    T1 = T2 + dT
    print(f"e^(mu theta) = {ratio:.4f}; centrifugal {Tc:.2f} N; tight side {T1:.1f} N, slack side {T2:.1f} N; initial tension {(T1+T2)/2:.1f} N")
    assert abs((T1 - Tc) / (T2 - Tc) - ratio) < 1e-12
    # V-belt: the wedge multiplies the friction by 1/sin(half angle)
    mu_v = mu / math.sin(math.radians(20))
    print(f"V-belt with a 40 degree groove: effective mu {mu_v:.4f}, e^(mu' theta) = {math.exp(mu_v*wrap):.2f}")
```

:::exercise A 16-tooth cog
Find the pitch radius and the speed fluctuation of a 16-tooth sprocket with 12.7 mm pitch, and the tension ratio of a flat belt with $\mu = 0.3$ and 180° of wrap.
---solution---
1. $R = 12.7/(2\sin(\pi/16)) = 32.549$ mm.
2. $1 - \cos(\pi/16) = 0.01921$, about 1.92%. The approximation $\pi^2/(2\cdot256) = 1.93\%$ is close.
3. $e^{0.3\pi} = 2.566$ (ignoring centrifugal tension).
:::
