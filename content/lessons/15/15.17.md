A detachable chairlift lets chairs crawl slowly through the stations for boarding and then race up the mountain at 5 m/s. At the station exit each chair's **grip** clamps onto the moving haul rope; at the top it lets go again. Nothing but friction holds a chair full of people to a steel rope on a steep slope. This case study puts several Phase 15 tools together: friction, springs, safety factors and belt-drive tension. It is an **educational model**: real grips are designed and tested to standards such as EN 13796, with values and procedures that differ in detail from the ones used here.

## Will the grip slip?

A grip squeezes the rope between jaws with a clamping force $F_c$ from a stack of springs. With friction coefficient $\mu$ on each of two jaw faces, the largest force along the rope it can resist is

$$
F_{\text{grip}} = 2\mu F_c .
$$

On a rope inclined at $\theta$, a loaded chair of mass $m$ pulls downhill along the rope with $m g\sin\theta$. Requiring a safety factor $SF$ against slipping:

$$
2\mu F_c \ \ge\ SF\cdot m g\sin\theta \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \theta \le \theta_{\max} = \arcsin\frac{2\mu F_c}{SF\, m g} .
$$

The steepest point of the line is usually just after a tower, where the rope bends. Grips are checked at every station pass with a force-testing device, because worn jaws or tired springs reduce $F_c$, and with it the margin.

## Rope tension and the drive

The rope is kept taut by a **tensioning weight** (or hydraulic ram) at one terminal, which sets the bottom tension $T_0$. Climbing a height $H$, the tension rises by the weight per metre times $H$ (friction ignored):

$$
T_{\text{top}} = T_0 + \left(m'_{\text{rope}} + \frac{m_{\text{chair}}}{s}\right) g H ,
$$

with chairs every $s$ metres. The uphill side carries loaded chairs, the downhill side empty ones, so the uphill top tension is larger. The **bullwheel** at the drive terminal transmits the difference by friction, exactly like a belt pulley, so the capstan equation $T_{\text{up}}/T_{\text{down}} \le e^{\mu\pi}$ must hold. The drive power is $(T_{\text{up}} - T_{\text{down}})\,v$.

:::example A detachable quad chair
Chair 250 kg plus four riders of 100 kg: $m = 650$ kg. Grip clamping force 35 kN, $\mu = 0.20$, $SF = 3$.

1. At a 45° rope angle the downhill pull is $650\cdot9.81\cdot\sin45° = 4509$ N, so the required capacity is 13 527 N.
2. Grip capacity $2\cdot0.20\cdot35\,000 = 14\,000$ N: it holds, just. The steepest angle with $SF = 3$ is $\arcsin(14\,000/19\,130) = 47.04°$.
3. The code checks over 179 rope angles that "holds" is exactly "capacity ≥ slope component × SF". For 45° the clamp force must be at least 33.82 kN.
4. Line: 400 m vertical, rope 6.5 kg/m, chairs every 20 m, $T_0 = 200$ kN. Uphill top tension $200 + (6.5 + 32.5)\cdot9.81\cdot400/1000 = 353.0$ kN; downhill, with empty chairs, 274.6 kN.
5. Ratio 1.286, below the bullwheel's limit $e^{0.25\pi} = 2.193$: no slip. Drive power $78.5\ \text{kN}\cdot5\ \text{m/s} = 392$ kW, before friction. A rope with a 1.6 MN breaking load has a factor of 4.53.
:::

```python file=skilift.py
"""Detachable ski-lift grip and haul rope (educational model): friction grip capacity against the downhill pull of a loaded chair, the steepest rope angle a grip can hold, line tensions and the drive bullwheel slip check."""
import math
import numpy as np

G = 9.81


def downhill_force(m, slope):
    return m * G * math.sin(slope)


def grip_capacity(F_clamp, mu, faces=2):
    return mu * F_clamp * faces


def grip_holds(F_clamp, mu, m, slope, SF):
    return grip_capacity(F_clamp, mu) >= SF * downhill_force(m, slope)


def max_slope(F_clamp, mu, m, SF):
    s = grip_capacity(F_clamp, mu) / (SF * m * G)
    return math.asin(min(s, 1.0))


def line_tension(T0, rope_kg_m, carrier_kg, spacing, height):
    """Tension rise over a vertical height for a rope with carriers every `spacing` metres (friction ignored)."""
    w = (rope_kg_m + carrier_kg / spacing) * G
    return T0 + w * height


if __name__ == "__main__":
    m_chair, m_rider, riders = 250.0, 100.0, 4
    m = m_chair + riders * m_rider
    mu, SF, F_clamp = 0.20, 3.0, 35e3
    steep = math.radians(45)
    need = SF * downhill_force(m, steep)
    print(f"loaded chair {m:.0f} kg; downhill pull at 45 deg {downhill_force(m, steep):.0f} N; required capacity with SF {SF}: {need:.0f} N")
    print(f"grip: clamp {F_clamp/1000:.0f} kN, capacity {grip_capacity(F_clamp, mu):.0f} N -> holds: {grip_holds(F_clamp, mu, m, steep, SF)}")
    th = max_slope(F_clamp, mu, m, SF)
    print(f"steepest rope angle it holds with SF {SF}: {math.degrees(th):.2f} deg")
    # the grip holds exactly when friction capacity exceeds the slope component times SF: check across angles
    for deg in np.linspace(0, 89, 179):
        s = math.radians(deg)
        assert grip_holds(F_clamp, mu, m, s, SF) == (grip_capacity(F_clamp, mu) >= SF * m * G * math.sin(s))
        assert grip_holds(F_clamp, mu, m, s, SF) == (s <= th + 1e-12)
    F_min = need / (2 * mu)
    print(f"minimum clamp force for 45 deg: {F_min/1000:.2f} kN")
    # line: 400 m vertical, 6.5 kg/m rope, chairs every 20 m, tension at the bottom 200 kN from the tensioning weight
    H, rope, spacing, T0, v = 400.0, 6.5, 20.0, 200e3, 5.0
    T_up = line_tension(T0, rope, m, spacing, H)          # loaded chairs going up
    T_down = line_tension(T0, rope, m_chair, spacing, H)  # empty chairs coming down
    ratio = T_up / T_down
    capstan = math.exp(0.25 * math.pi)                    # lined bullwheel, 180 degree wrap
    print(f"top tensions: uphill {T_up/1000:.1f} kN, downhill {T_down/1000:.1f} kN; ratio {ratio:.3f} vs capstan limit {capstan:.3f}")
    assert ratio < capstan
    print(f"drive power ignoring friction: {(T_up - T_down) * v / 1000:.0f} kW at {v} m/s")
    breaking = 1.6e6
    print(f"rope safety factor on breaking load: {breaking / T_up:.2f}")
```

:::exercise Worn jaws
After a season the jaw friction drops to $\mu = 0.15$. What is the steepest rope angle the same grip can hold with $SF = 3$? What clamping force would restore 45°?
---solution---
1. $\theta_{\max} = \arcsin(2\cdot0.15\cdot35\,000/(3\cdot650\cdot9.81)) = \arcsin(0.5489) = 33.29°$.
2. For 45°: $F_c = 13\,527/(2\cdot0.15) = 45.09$ kN.
3. That sensitivity is why grip force is tested daily and jaws are replaced on schedule.
:::
