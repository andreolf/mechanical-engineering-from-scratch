A washing machine walking across the floor, a car steering wheel shaking at 110 km/h, a fan humming loudly: all are unbalanced rotors. A few grams off-centre on something spinning at thousands of rpm produce forces of tens or hundreds of newtons, shaking bearings to pieces. Balancing is how every wheel, fan, turbine and crankshaft is made smooth.

## The force from a small unbalance

A mass $m$ at radius $r$ on a shaft spinning at $\omega$ needs a centripetal force $mr\omega^2$, supplied by the bearings. The force rotates with the shaft, so the bearings feel it as a vibration at the running speed. The quantity that matters is $mr$, the **unbalance**, usually in g mm or g m.

For several masses, add the vectors: $\mathbf U = \sum m_i r_i\,\mathbf e_i$. The rotor is **statically balanced** if $\mathbf U = 0$: it then stays put at any angle when resting on knife edges.

## Dynamic unbalance

Static balance is not enough for a long rotor. Two equal masses on opposite sides at different positions along the shaft cancel each other's force, but they make a rotating **couple** $\sum m_ir_iz_i\,\mathbf e_i$ that rocks the rotor in its bearings. A rotor is **dynamically balanced** only if both the force and the moment vanish:

$$
\sum m_ir_i\,\mathbf e_i = 0, \qquad \sum m_ir_iz_i\,\mathbf e_i = 0 .
$$

## Two-plane balancing

Any unbalance can be cancelled with one correction mass in each of two chosen planes, $z_L$ and $z_R$. With unknown correction vectors $\mathbf u_L$, $\mathbf u_R$:

$$
\mathbf u_L + \mathbf u_R = -\mathbf U, \qquad z_L\mathbf u_L + z_R\mathbf u_R = -\mathbf M .
$$

Two vector equations, two vector unknowns. Balancing machines measure the bearing forces and solve exactly this. Car tyre fitters do it with clip-on weights on the inner and outer rim edges.

```python file=balancing.py
"""Two-plane balancing of a rotor: correction masses that cancel both the net force and the net moment."""
import math
import numpy as np


def unbalance(masses):
    """masses: list of (m kg, r m, angle rad, z m). Returns the force vector per omega^2 (kg m) and moment per omega^2 (kg m^2) about z = 0."""
    F = np.zeros(2); M = np.zeros(2)
    for m, r, a, z in masses:
        u = m * r * np.array([math.cos(a), math.sin(a)])
        F += u; M += u * z
    return F, M


def two_plane_correction(masses, zL, zR, rc):
    """Find correction masses at radius rc in planes zL and zR so the total force and moment vanish."""
    F, M = unbalance(masses)
    # unknown vectors uL, uR (m r in each plane): uL + uR = -F,  uL zL + uR zR = -M
    uR = (-M + F * zL) / (zR - zL)
    uL = -F - uR
    out = []
    for u, z in ((uL, zL), (uR, zR)):
        out.append((np.linalg.norm(u) / rc, rc, math.atan2(u[1], u[0]), z))
    return out


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a 0.6 m long fan rotor with three small unbalances (kg, m, angle, axial position)
    rotor = [(0.020, 0.10, math.radians(30), 0.10), (0.015, 0.12, math.radians(160), 0.30), (0.010, 0.08, math.radians(270), 0.50)]
    F, M = unbalance(rotor)
    w = 3000 * 2 * math.pi / 60
    print(f"unbalance: m r = {np.linalg.norm(F)*1e3:.3f} g m, moment m r z = {np.linalg.norm(M)*1e3:.3f} g m^2; at 3000 rpm the bearings feel up to {np.linalg.norm(F)*w**2:.1f} N")
    # static balance only: one mass in the middle plane cancels the force but leaves a moment
    zc = 0.3
    Fs = -F
    static = rotor + [(np.linalg.norm(Fs) / 0.1, 0.1, math.atan2(Fs[1], Fs[0]), zc)]
    F1, M1 = unbalance(static)
    print(f"static balance only: residual force {np.linalg.norm(F1):.1e}, residual moment {np.linalg.norm(M1)*1e3:.3f} g m^2 (dynamic unbalance remains)")
    corr = two_plane_correction(rotor, 0.0, 0.6, 0.1)
    for m, r, a, z in corr:
        print(f"  correction at z = {z:.1f} m: {m*1e3:.2f} g at r = {r} m, angle {math.degrees(a):.1f} deg")
    F2, M2 = unbalance(rotor + corr)
    print(f"after two-plane balancing: force {np.linalg.norm(F2):.1e} kg m, moment {np.linalg.norm(M2):.1e} kg m^2")
    assert np.linalg.norm(F2) < 1e-15 and np.linalg.norm(M2) < 1e-15
```

:::exercise A car wheel
A car wheel spins at 110 km/h with a 0.31 m rolling radius. A 25 g balance weight has fallen off the rim at radius 0.20 m. What rotating force does the missing weight cause?
---solution---
1. $\omega = v/r = 30.56/0.31 = 98.6$ rad/s.
2. $F = mr\omega^2 = 0.025\cdot0.20\cdot98.6^2 = 48.6$ N, rotating at 15.7 Hz.
3. That is enough to shake the steering wheel noticeably: this is the classic "wobble at motorway speed".
:::
