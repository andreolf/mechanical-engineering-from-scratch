A rocket pushes itself forward by throwing mass backward. As it burns fuel it gets lighter, so the same thrust accelerates it harder. Newton's law in its plain form assumes constant mass, so rockets need a careful derivation, and the result explains why rockets are mostly fuel and why they have stages.

## Momentum over a short time

At time $t$ the rocket has mass $m$ and speed $v$. In $dt$ it ejects mass $dm_e$ backwards at speed $v_e$ relative to itself. With no outside forces, total momentum stays the same:

$$
mv = (m - dm_e)(v + dv) + dm_e\,(v - v_e) .
$$

Expanding and dropping the tiny product $dm_e\,dv$ gives $m\,dv = v_e\,dm_e$. The **thrust** is $v_e\dot m_e$. Since $dm_e = -dm$:

$$
dv = -v_e\,\frac{dm}{m} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \Delta v = v_e \ln\frac{m_0}{m_1} .
$$

This is **Tsiolkovsky's rocket equation**. The speed gained depends only on the exhaust speed and the mass ratio, not on how fast the fuel burns.

## Gravity

Flying straight up with gravity, $m\dot v = \dot m_e v_e - mg$. Integrating over the burn time $t_b$ gives $\Delta v = v_e\ln(m_0/m_1) - g\,t_b$. The **gravity loss** $gt_b$ is why rockets burn hard and fast at lift-off.

## Why stages

The logarithm is cruel. Reaching low Earth orbit needs about 9.3 km/s including gravity and drag losses. With a good kerosene engine, $v_e \approx 3$ km/s, so $m_0/m_1 = e^{3.1} \approx 22$: 95 % of the rocket must be propellant. Dropping empty tanks and engines along the way (staging) means the later stages don't have to accelerate dead weight.

:::example A sounding rocket
Lift-off mass 1000 kg, 750 kg of propellant, $v_e = 2500$ m/s, burning 15 kg/s for 50 s.

1. Tsiolkovsky: $\Delta v = 2500\ln(1000/250) = 3465.736$ m/s. RK4 on $m\dot v = \dot m v_e$ gives the same to $10^{-6}$ m/s.
2. With gravity: burnout at 54.98 km and 2975.2 m/s. The difference is exactly $g t_b = 490.5$ m/s.
3. Thrust is 37.5 kN, a thrust-to-weight ratio of 3.82 at lift-off.
4. Staging: drop a 60 kg tank after 500 kg of propellant and the same rocket reaches 3832 m/s instead of 3466 m/s.
5. Coasting without drag would carry it to 506 km. Real sounding rockets of this size reach far less, because of air drag and the fact that real ones carry payloads.
:::

```python file=rocket.py
"""The rocket equation: Tsiolkovsky's delta v, then an ascent integrated with gravity."""
import math
import numpy as np


def tsiolkovsky(ve, m0, m1):
    return ve * math.log(m0 / m1)


def burn(m0, mprop, ve, mdot, g=0.0, dt=1e-3):
    """Vertical burn: m dv/dt = mdot ve - m g, with m(t) = m0 - mdot t. RK4 on (h, v)."""
    tb = mprop / mdot
    f = lambda t, s: np.array([s[1], mdot * ve / (m0 - mdot * t) - g])
    t, s = 0.0, np.zeros(2)
    n = int(round(tb / dt))
    h = tb / n
    for _ in range(n):
        k1 = f(t, s); k2 = f(t + h / 2, s + h / 2 * k1); k3 = f(t + h / 2, s + h / 2 * k2); k4 = f(t + h, s + h * k3)
        s = s + h / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4); t += h
    return s, tb


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a small sounding rocket: 1000 kg at lift-off, 750 kg of propellant, exhaust 2500 m/s, burning 15 kg/s
    m0, mp, ve, mdot = 1000.0, 750.0, 2500.0, 15.0
    dv = tsiolkovsky(ve, m0, m0 - mp)
    (h, v), tb = burn(m0, mp, ve, mdot, g=0.0)
    print(f"Tsiolkovsky: dv = {dv:.3f} m/s; integrated without gravity: {v:.3f} m/s after {tb:.0f} s")
    assert abs(v - dv) < 1e-6
    (h, v), tb = burn(m0, mp, ve, mdot, g=9.81)
    print(f"with gravity: burnout at {h/1e3:.2f} km, {v:.1f} m/s (gravity loss g tb = {9.81*tb:.1f} m/s)")
    assert abs((dv - v) - 9.81 * tb) < 1e-6
    hmax = h + v**2 / (2 * 9.81)
    print(f"coasting (no drag, flat-earth g) to apogee {hmax/1e3:.1f} km")
    print(f"lift-off acceleration {mdot*ve/m0 - 9.81:.2f} m/s^2, thrust/weight {mdot*ve/(m0*9.81):.2f}")
    # staging: the same propellant split into two stages beats one stage
    s1 = tsiolkovsky(ve, 1000, 1000 - 500)                 # stage 1 burns 500 kg, then drops a 60 kg tank
    s2 = tsiolkovsky(ve, 1000 - 500 - 60, 1000 - 500 - 60 - 250)
    one = tsiolkovsky(ve, 1000, 250)
    print(f"single stage {one:.0f} m/s; two stages (dropping 60 kg of empty tank) {s1 + s2:.0f} m/s")
```

:::exercise How much propellant?
A spacecraft of 2000 kg (dry) needs a $\Delta v$ of 1500 m/s for an orbit change. Its engine has $v_e = 3100$ m/s. How much propellant must it carry?
---solution---
1. $m_0/m_1 = e^{1500/3100} = e^{0.4839} = 1.6224$.
2. $m_0 = 1.6224\cdot2000 = 3245$ kg, so it needs 1245 kg of propellant: 38 % of the fuelled mass.
:::
