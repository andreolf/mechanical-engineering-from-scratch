Dynamics starts with describing motion before asking what causes it. A car on a roundabout, a cyclist braking into a bend and a phone logging GPS points are all "particles": objects whose size doesn't matter for the question. Kinematics gives their velocity and acceleration, and the right choice of coordinates makes the physics obvious.

## Cartesian coordinates

Position $\mathbf r(t) = (x, y)$. Velocity and acceleration are time derivatives:

$$
\mathbf v = \dot{\mathbf r}, \qquad \mathbf a = \dot{\mathbf v} = \ddot{\mathbf r} .
$$

Simple, but it hides what a driver feels: braking versus turning.

## Path coordinates

Use a unit tangent $\mathbf e_t$ along the path and a unit normal $\mathbf e_n$ towards the centre of curvature. With speed $v$ and local radius of curvature $\rho$:

$$
\mathbf v = v\,\mathbf e_t, \qquad \mathbf a = \dot v\,\mathbf e_t + \frac{v^2}{\rho}\,\mathbf e_n .
$$

The **tangential** part $a_t = \dot v$ changes the speed. The **normal** part $a_n = v^2/\rho$ changes the direction. On a straight, $\rho \to \infty$ and $a_n = 0$. At steady speed round a circle, $a_t = 0$ but $a_n = v^2/R$ still points inward.

## Polar coordinates

For things that swing or spin (a radar tracking a plane, a bead sliding on a rotating rod), use $r$ and $\theta$:

$$
a_r = \ddot r - r\dot\theta^2, \qquad a_\theta = r\ddot\theta + 2\dot r\dot\theta .
$$

The $-r\dot\theta^2$ term is the centripetal part; $2\dot r\dot\theta$ is the Coriolis part (more in 07.12).

## From measured data

A GPS log gives positions at discrete times. Differentiating once gives velocity, twice gives acceleration. Central differences work, but every differentiation amplifies measurement noise. Smoothing first trades noise for a little lag.

:::example A roundabout and a GPS track
1. A car circles a 50 m roundabout at 10 m/s, logged at 10 Hz. Central differences give $a_t = 5\times10^{-14}$, $a_n = 1.9997$ m/s² against $v^2/R = 2$, and a radius of 50.000 m.
2. A cyclist brakes at 0.3 m/s² from 12 m/s through a 30 m bend, logged once a second. Mid-bend at 12 s: speed 8.29 m/s (true 8.40), $a_t = -0.27$ (true −0.30), $a_n = 2.29$ m/s² (true 2.35). One-second sampling cuts corners.
3. Add 0.5 m of GPS noise and the tangential acceleration even comes out with the wrong sign (+0.56). Accelerations from raw GPS need filtering.
4. Polar check: $r = 2 + 0.5t$, $\theta = 0.8t$ at $t = 1.3$ s gives $a_r = -1.696$ and $a_\theta = 0.800$ m/s², by formula and by numerical differentiation.
:::

```python file=kinematics.py
"""Particle kinematics along a sampled track: velocity, tangential and normal acceleration."""
import math
import numpy as np


def derivatives(t, x, y):
    """Central differences (second order) for velocity and acceleration."""
    vx, vy = np.gradient(x, t, edge_order=2), np.gradient(y, t, edge_order=2)
    ax, ay = np.gradient(vx, t, edge_order=2), np.gradient(vy, t, edge_order=2)
    return vx, vy, ax, ay


def path_components(vx, vy, ax, ay):
    """Split acceleration into tangential (speed change) and normal (direction change) parts."""
    v = np.hypot(vx, vy)
    at = (vx * ax + vy * ay) / v
    an = (vx * ay - vy * ax) / v           # signed: positive when turning left
    rho = v**2 / np.maximum(np.abs(an), 1e-12)   # straight stretches: radius effectively infinite
    return v, at, an, rho


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # 1. a car on a 50 m radius roundabout at a steady 10 m/s, logged at 10 Hz
    R, V = 50.0, 10.0
    t = np.arange(0, 20, 0.1)
    x, y = R * np.cos(V * t / R), R * np.sin(V * t / R)
    v, at, an, rho = path_components(*derivatives(t, x, y))
    print(f"roundabout: speed {v[100]:.4f} m/s, a_t {at[100]:+.2e}, a_n {an[100]:.4f} m/s^2 (v^2/R = {V**2/R:.4f}), radius {rho[100]:.3f} m")
    assert abs(an[100] - V**2 / R) < 1e-3 and abs(at[100]) < 1e-3
    # 2. a cyclist's GPS track: a straight, a 30 m bend while braking, then a straight, with 0.5 m GPS noise
    dt = 1.0
    s_of_t = lambda tt: 12 * tt - 0.15 * tt**2         # braking at 0.3 m/s^2
    tt = np.arange(0, 30, dt)
    s = s_of_t(tt)
    def place(s):
        L1, Rb = 100.0, 30.0
        if s < L1: return s, 0.0
        if s < L1 + math.pi / 2 * Rb:
            ph = (s - L1) / Rb; return L1 + Rb * math.sin(ph), Rb * (1 - math.cos(ph))
        return L1 + Rb, Rb + (s - L1 - math.pi / 2 * Rb)
    xy = np.array([place(si) for si in s])
    rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
    noisy = xy + rng.normal(0, 0.5, xy.shape)
    for name, P in (("clean", xy), ("noisy", noisy)):
        v, at, an, rho = path_components(*derivatives(tt, P[:, 0], P[:, 1]))
        i = 12                                           # mid-bend (s = 122 m)
        print(f"GPS {name}: at t = {tt[i]:.0f} s speed {v[i]:.2f} m/s (true {12 - 0.3*tt[i]:.2f}), a_t {at[i]:+.2f} (true -0.30), a_n {an[i]:.2f} (true {(12-0.3*tt[i])**2/30:.2f})")
    # smoothing helps: a 5-point moving average before differencing
    k = np.ones(5) / 5
    sm = np.column_stack([np.convolve(noisy[:, j], k, mode="same") for j in range(2)])
    v, at, an, rho = path_components(*derivatives(tt, sm[:, 0], sm[:, 1]))
    print(f"smoothed noisy: speed {v[12]:.2f} m/s, a_n {an[12]:.2f} m/s^2 (differentiating noise amplifies it; smoothing trades noise for lag)")
    # 3. polar coordinates: a_r = r'' - r th'^2, a_th = r th'' + 2 r' th'
    r = lambda t: 2 + 0.5 * t; th = lambda t: 0.8 * t
    tq, h = 1.3, 1e-4
    X = lambda t: np.array([r(t) * math.cos(th(t)), r(t) * math.sin(th(t))])
    a = (X(tq + h) - 2 * X(tq) + X(tq - h)) / h**2
    er = np.array([math.cos(th(tq)), math.sin(th(tq))]); et = np.array([-er[1], er[0]])
    ar, ath = 0 - r(tq) * 0.8**2, 0 + 2 * 0.5 * 0.8
    print(f"polar: a_r numeric {a @ er:.4f} vs formula {ar:.4f}; a_theta numeric {a @ et:.4f} vs formula {ath:.4f}")
    assert abs(a @ er - ar) < 1e-5 and abs(a @ et - ath) < 1e-5
```

:::exercise A motorway exit
A car leaves a motorway at 100 km/h on an exit ramp with a 120 m radius and brakes at 2 m/s². What is its total acceleration as it enters the curve? How fast could it go round the curve if the tyres can deliver 0.6 g in total and it stops braking?
---solution---
1. $v = 27.78$ m/s, so $a_n = 27.78^2/120 = 6.43$ m/s².
2. Total: $\sqrt{6.43^2 + 2^2} = 6.73$ m/s², about 0.69 g. Too much for 0.6 g of grip: the driver should have braked before the curve.
3. Without braking: $v^2/120 = 0.6\cdot9.81$ gives $v = 26.6$ m/s $= 95.7$ km/h.
:::
