Toss a phone or a tennis racket in the air spinning about each of its three axes in turn. About the long axis and the flat axis it spins cleanly. About the third, the intermediate axis, it flips over every time, however carefully you throw it. In 1985 a Soviet cosmonaut saw a wing nut do the same in orbit. Euler's equations explain why.

## The equations

In body-fixed principal axes, $\mathbf H = (I_1\omega_1, I_2\omega_2, I_3\omega_3)$. The axes rotate with the body, so differentiating $\mathbf H$ needs the rotating-frame rule of 07.12: $\dot{\mathbf H} = \dot{\mathbf H}_{rel} + \boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf H = \mathbf M$. In components:

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1\dot\omega_1 - (I_2 - I_3)\,\omega_2\omega_3 &= M_1 \\
I_2\dot\omega_2 - (I_3 - I_1)\,\omega_3\omega_1 &= M_2 \\
I_3\dot\omega_3 - (I_1 - I_2)\,\omega_1\omega_2 &= M_3
\end{aligned}
$$

They are nonlinear: the spin about one axis feeds the others. Even with no moment at all, $\boldsymbol\omega$ can change in body axes.

## Stability of steady spin

Take $M = 0$, spin $\Omega$ about axis 2 and small wobbles $\omega_1$, $\omega_3$. Linearising:

$$
\ddot\omega_1 = \frac{(I_2 - I_1)(I_3 - I_2)}{I_1I_3}\,\Omega^2\,\omega_1 .
$$

With $I_1 < I_2 < I_3$ the coefficient is positive: the wobble grows exponentially at rate $\lambda = \Omega\sqrt{(I_2 - I_1)(I_3 - I_2)/(I_1I_3)}$. Spin is **unstable about the intermediate axis**. About axis 1 or axis 3 the coefficient is negative and the wobble just oscillates: stable. This is the tennis racket theorem.

Kinetic energy $\tfrac12\sum I_i\omega_i^2$ and the size of $\mathbf H$ are both conserved, a good check on any simulation.

:::widget racket
A phone-shaped body ($I_1 : I_2 : I_3 = 1 : 2.5 : 3.3$) spinning at 10 rad/s with a tiny wobble. Pick the spin axis. About the intermediate axis it flips over and back, again and again.
:::

:::example A tumbling phone
$I = (1.0, 2.5, 3.3)\times10^{-4}$ kg m², spin 10 rad/s, initial wobble $10^{-3}$ rad/s on the other two axes, 10 s of RK4 with a 1 ms step.

1. About the smallest axis: the wobble grows only 1.7 times (it oscillates). About the largest: 1.5 times.
2. About the intermediate axis: the wobble grows 9325 times and the main spin reverses 3 times in 10 s. The body flips.
3. Linear theory: $\lambda = 10\sqrt{1.5\cdot0.8/(1.0\cdot3.3)} = 6.03$ s⁻¹, so the wobble doubles every 0.115 s.
4. Energy and $|\mathbf H|$ stay constant to better than $10^{-12}$ in all three runs.
:::

```python file=euler_eq.py
"""Euler's equations for torque-free rotation: the intermediate-axis (tennis racket) instability."""
import numpy as np


def euler_rhs(w, I):
    I1, I2, I3 = I
    w1, w2, w3 = w
    return np.array([(I2 - I3) * w2 * w3 / I1, (I3 - I1) * w3 * w1 / I2, (I1 - I2) * w1 * w2 / I3])


def spin(w0, I, t_end, dt=1e-3):
    w = np.array(w0, float); out = [w.copy()]
    for _ in range(int(round(t_end / dt))):
        k1 = euler_rhs(w, I); k2 = euler_rhs(w + dt/2*k1, I); k3 = euler_rhs(w + dt/2*k2, I); k4 = euler_rhs(w + dt*k3, I)
        w = w + dt/6*(k1 + 2*k2 + 2*k3 + k4); out.append(w.copy())
    return np.array(out)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a phone (or tennis racket): I1 < I2 < I3 about its principal axes (kg m^2, phone-like proportions)
    I = np.array([1.0, 2.5, 3.3]) * 1e-4
    eps = 1e-3
    for k, name in enumerate(("smallest", "intermediate", "largest")):
        w0 = np.full(3, eps); w0[k] = 10.0             # 10 rad/s about one axis plus a tiny wobble
        tr = spin(w0, I, 10.0)
        others = np.delete(tr, k, axis=1)
        growth = np.max(np.abs(others)) / eps
        flips = np.sum(np.diff(np.sign(tr[:, k])) != 0)
        T = 0.5 * np.sum(I * tr**2, axis=1); H = np.linalg.norm(I * tr, axis=1)
        print(f"spin about the {name:12s} axis: wobble grows x{growth:9.1f}, main spin changes sign {flips} times; energy drift {abs(T[-1]/T[0]-1):.1e}, |H| drift {abs(H[-1]/H[0]-1):.1e}")
        assert abs(T[-1] / T[0] - 1) < 1e-9 and abs(H[-1] / H[0] - 1) < 1e-9
        if k == 1:
            assert growth > 1000 and flips >= 2
        else:
            assert growth < 10
    # linearised growth rate about axis 2: lambda = w2 sqrt((I2 - I1)(I3 - I2) / (I1 I3))
    lam = 10.0 * np.sqrt((I[1] - I[0]) * (I[2] - I[1]) / (I[0] * I[2]))
    print(f"linear theory: the wobble about the intermediate axis grows like exp({lam:.3f} t), doubling every {np.log(2)/lam:.3f} s")
```

:::exercise A spinning satellite
A satellite has principal moments 800, 1000 and 1200 kg m². It spins at 0.5 rad/s about the 1000 kg m² axis. How fast does a small wobble grow? What would you change?
---solution---
1. Intermediate axis: $\lambda = 0.5\sqrt{(1000 - 800)(1200 - 1000)/(800\cdot1200)} = 0.5\sqrt{40000/960000} = 0.102$ s⁻¹.
2. A wobble doubles every $\ln2/0.102 = 6.8$ s: in a few minutes the satellite tumbles.
3. Spin it about the 1200 kg m² axis (the largest) instead. With some energy dissipation (flexing antennas, fuel slosh), only the largest axis is stable in the long run, as Explorer 1 showed in 1958.
:::
