A train can't lean into a curve the way a cyclist does, and its wheels are held on by flanges only a few centimetres high. So the track does the leaning. The outer rail is raised (the **cant**, or superelevation) so that gravity supplies part of the centripetal force. Getting the cant right sets the comfortable speed, the wear on rails and wheels and the safety margin against overturning.

## Balance speed

The track is tilted by angle $\phi$, with $\sin\phi = h/S$ for cant $h$ and rail-centre spacing $S \approx 1.5$ m on standard gauge. A vehicle at speed $v$ on radius $R$ needs centripetal acceleration $v^2/R$ toward the curve centre. Resolve along the tilted floor: the passengers feel a sideways acceleration

$$
a_{lat} = \frac{v^2}{R}\cos\phi - g\sin\phi .
$$

It vanishes at the **balance speed**

$$
v_b = \sqrt{gR\tan\phi} .
$$

At $v_b$ the resultant of gravity and centrifugal effect is square to the floor. Wheels, rails and passengers feel no sideways force.

## Cant deficiency and excess

Mixed traffic can't all run at one speed. A fast passenger train runs above $v_b$ and has **cant deficiency**: it would need more cant than is there, and its passengers feel pushed outward. A slow freight train runs below $v_b$ with **cant excess**, which loads the inner rail and wears it. Railways set limits, typically about 1.0 m/s² of uncompensated lateral acceleration for conventional trains (roughly 150 mm of deficiency) and up to 2 m/s² measured on the track for tilting trains, whose bodies lean further to keep passengers comfortable.

## Overturning

At very high speed the resultant force moves past the outer rail, and the vehicle tips. The limit depends on the centre of mass height. It is far above the comfort limit, which is what actually sets the line speed.

:::

:::example A main-line curve
$R = 1200$ m, cant 150 mm, $S = 1.5$ m.

1. Tilt $\phi = \arcsin(0.150/1.5) = 5.739°$.
2. Balance speed $v_b = \sqrt{9.81\cdot1200\cdot\tan5.739°} = 34.40$ m/s $= 123.8$ km/h, where the in-floor acceleration is exactly zero.
3. Lateral acceleration and cant deficiency (negative means excess): 80 km/h, −0.572 m/s² (−87 mm); 120 km/h, −0.060 (−8 mm); 160 km/h, +0.657 (+102 mm); 200 km/h, +1.578 (+243 mm).
4. Speed limits: 176.0 km/h for a conventional train at 1.0 m/s², 215.9 km/h for a tilting train at 2.0 m/s².
:::

```python file=railcurve.py
"""Trains in curves: cant (superelevation), balance speed and cant deficiency."""
import math


GAUGE = 1.435          # m, standard gauge
S = 1.5                # m, distance between rail-head centre lines used for cant


def lateral_accel_in_plane(v, R, cant_mm, g=9.81):
    """Acceleration felt by passengers along the (tilted) floor: v^2/R cos(phi) - g sin(phi), phi = asin(cant / S)."""
    phi = math.asin(cant_mm / 1000 / S)
    return v**2 / R * math.cos(phi) - g * math.sin(phi), phi


def balance_speed(R, cant_mm, g=9.81):
    phi = math.asin(cant_mm / 1000 / S)
    return math.sqrt(g * R * math.tan(phi))


def cant_deficiency(v, R, cant_mm, g=9.81):
    """Extra cant (mm) that would be needed to balance speed v: S v^2 / (g R) - applied cant (small-angle form)."""
    return S * 1000 * v**2 / (g * R) - cant_mm


if __name__ == "__main__":
    R, cant = 1200.0, 150.0                                       # a 1200 m curve with 150 mm cant
    vb = balance_speed(R, cant)
    a, phi = lateral_accel_in_plane(vb, R, cant)
    print(f"R = {R:.0f} m, cant {cant:.0f} mm (tilt {math.degrees(phi):.3f} deg): balance speed {vb:.3f} m/s = {vb * 3.6:.1f} km/h")
    print(f"  at balance speed the sideways acceleration on the floor is {a:.1e} m/s^2: no lateral force on wheels or passengers")
    assert abs(a) < 1e-12
    for kmh in (80, 120, 160, 200):
        v = kmh / 3.6
        a, _ = lateral_accel_in_plane(v, R, cant)
        print(f"  {kmh:3d} km/h: uncompensated lateral acceleration {a:+.3f} m/s^2, cant deficiency {cant_deficiency(v, R, cant):+.0f} mm")
    # a typical limit: 1.0 m/s^2 uncompensated (about 150 mm deficiency); tilting trains allow about 2.0
    for lim, name in ((1.0, "conventional"), (2.0, "tilting")):
        lo, hi = vb, 150.0
        for _ in range(100):
            mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
            lo, hi = (mid, hi) if lateral_accel_in_plane(mid, R, cant)[0] < lim else (lo, mid)
        print(f"  {name} train limit {lim} m/s^2: {lo * 3.6:.1f} km/h")
```

:::exercise Setting the cant
A new curve of radius 800 m will carry trains at 100 km/h. What cant gives balance at that speed? The maximum allowed cant is 160 mm. Is it within the limit?
---solution---
1. $v = 27.78$ m/s, so $\tan\phi = v^2/(gR) = 771.6/7848 = 0.09832$ and $\phi = 5.615°$.
2. Cant $h = S\sin\phi = 1.5\cdot0.09785 = 0.1468$ m $= 147$ mm.
3. That is within 160 mm. In practice the cant is often set a little lower so slow trains don't overload the inner rail.
:::
