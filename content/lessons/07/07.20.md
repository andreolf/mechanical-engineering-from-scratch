Car suspensions, robot arms, landing gear and engines are all collections of rigid bodies joined by pins, sliders and contacts. Multibody software (Adams, Simpack, the dynamics in many CAD packages) builds and solves their equations automatically. This lesson builds a small one for a slider-crank, the mechanism inside every piston engine and compressor.

## Bodies and coordinates

Give each planar body three coordinates: the position of its centre of mass and its rotation, $(x_i, y_i, \theta_i)$. A slider-crank has three moving bodies (crank, connecting rod, piston), so $\mathbf q$ has 9 entries. That is more than the one degree of freedom the mechanism really has. The extra coordinates are tied together by constraints.

## Constraint equations

Each joint gives algebraic equations $\mathbf\Phi(\mathbf q) = \mathbf 0$. A pin joint makes two points coincide (2 equations), a slider keeps a point on a line and stops rotation (2 equations), and pinning the crank to the ground takes 2 more. Here there are 8 equations for 9 coordinates, leaving 1 degree of freedom. Differentiating twice gives the acceleration-level constraint

$$
\mathbf\Phi_q\,\ddot{\mathbf q} = \boldsymbol\gamma, \qquad \boldsymbol\gamma = -(\mathbf\Phi_q\dot{\mathbf q})_q\,\dot{\mathbf q},
$$

where $\mathbf\Phi_q$ is the Jacobian of the constraints.

## Lagrange multipliers

The joints apply reaction forces $\mathbf\Phi_q^T\boldsymbol\lambda$, one multiplier per constraint equation. Newton's laws for all bodies plus the constraints form one linear system:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf M & \mathbf\Phi_q^T \\ \mathbf\Phi_q & \mathbf 0 \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} \ddot{\mathbf q} \\ \boldsymbol\lambda \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf Q \\ \boldsymbol\gamma \end{bmatrix} .
$$

Solve it at every time step, then integrate $\ddot{\mathbf q}$. As a bonus, $\boldsymbol\lambda$ gives the bearing loads directly.

## Drift and Baumgarte stabilisation

Integrating accelerations only enforces the constraints at acceleration level. Numerical error slowly lets the joints come apart. Baumgarte's fix replaces $\boldsymbol\gamma$ by $\boldsymbol\gamma - 2\alpha\dot{\mathbf\Phi} - \beta^2\mathbf\Phi$, which pulls any violation back toward zero like a stiff damped spring.

:::example Simulating the slider-crank
$r = 0.05$ m, $l = 0.15$ m, masses 2, 1 and 0.5 kg, no gravity, no applied torque. Start at crank angle 0.3 rad and 600 rpm.

1. Initial positions and velocities are solved to satisfy the constraints exactly: piston at $x = 197.037$ mm, kinetic energy 8.2176 J. The analytic Jacobian agrees with finite differences to better than $10^{-9}$.
2. 0.5 s with RK4 at $\Delta t = 0.5$ ms: with no stabilisation the joints drift apart by up to $7.4\times10^{-8}$ m; with Baumgarte ($\alpha = \beta = 20$) the worst is $2.1\times10^{-9}$ m. Kinetic energy is conserved to $2.2\times10^{-7}$.
3. Even with no load, the crank speed is not constant. The effective inertia changes with angle, so at constant energy the speed must change: after 0.05 s the crank is at 3.0653 rad turning at 66.126 rad/s, up from 62.832. Engines carry flywheels partly to smooth this out.
:::

```python file=slidercrank_mb.py
"""A slider-crank as a multibody system: bodies, constraint equations and Lagrange multipliers, with Baumgarte stabilisation."""
import math
import numpy as np

# bodies: 1 crank (pinned to ground at the origin), 2 connecting rod, 3 piston (slides on the x axis)
r, l = 0.05, 0.15                      # crank radius and rod length (m)
m = np.array([2.0, 1.0, 0.5])          # masses (kg)
J = np.array([2.0 * r**2 / 3, 1.0 * l**2 / 12, 0.0])   # crank as a uniform bar about its centre, rod about its centre
# coordinates q = (x1, y1, th1, x2, y2, th2, x3, y3, th3); body centres at the middle of crank and rod


def constraints(q):
    x1, y1, t1, x2, y2, t2, x3, y3, t3 = q
    A = np.array([x1 - r / 2 * math.cos(t1), y1 - r / 2 * math.sin(t1)])          # crank end at the ground pin
    Bc = np.array([x1 + r / 2 * math.cos(t1), y1 + r / 2 * math.sin(t1)])          # crank pin
    Br = np.array([x2 - l / 2 * math.cos(t2), y2 - l / 2 * math.sin(t2)])          # rod big end
    Cr = np.array([x2 + l / 2 * math.cos(t2), y2 + l / 2 * math.sin(t2)])          # rod small end
    return np.r_[A, Bc - Br, Cr - np.array([x3, y3]), y3, t3]                      # 8 equations, 9 coordinates: 1 DOF


def jacobian(q):
    """Analytic constraint Jacobian dC/dq (8 x 9)."""
    t1, t2 = q[2], q[5]
    s1, c1, s2, c2 = math.sin(t1), math.cos(t1), math.sin(t2), math.cos(t2)
    Cq = np.zeros((8, 9))
    Cq[0, [0, 2]] = [1, r / 2 * s1]
    Cq[1, [1, 2]] = [1, -r / 2 * c1]
    Cq[2, [0, 2, 3, 5]] = [1, -r / 2 * s1, -1, -l / 2 * s2]
    Cq[3, [1, 2, 4, 5]] = [1, r / 2 * c1, -1, l / 2 * c2]
    Cq[4, [3, 5, 6]] = [1, -l / 2 * s2, -1]
    Cq[5, [4, 5, 7]] = [1, l / 2 * c2, -1]
    Cq[6, 7] = 1; Cq[7, 8] = 1
    return Cq


def gamma(q, qd):
    """Right-hand side -(dCq/dt) qd: the velocity-squared terms of the constraint accelerations."""
    t1, t2, w1, w2 = q[2], q[5], qd[2], qd[5]
    s1, c1, s2, c2 = math.sin(t1), math.cos(t1), math.sin(t2), math.cos(t2)
    return np.array([-r / 2 * c1 * w1**2, -r / 2 * s1 * w1**2,
                     r / 2 * c1 * w1**2 + l / 2 * c2 * w2**2, r / 2 * s1 * w1**2 + l / 2 * s2 * w2**2,
                     l / 2 * c2 * w2**2, l / 2 * s2 * w2**2, 0.0, 0.0])


def initial(th1, w1):
    """Consistent positions and velocities for a crank angle and speed."""
    B = r * np.array([math.cos(th1), math.sin(th1)])
    t2 = -math.asin(B[1] / l)
    C = B + l * np.array([math.cos(t2), math.sin(t2)])
    q = np.array([B[0] / 2, B[1] / 2, th1, (B[0] + C[0]) / 2, (B[1] + C[1]) / 2, t2, C[0], 0.0, 0.0])
    # velocities: solve Cq qd = 0 with crank speed fixed
    Cq = jacobian(q)
    A = np.vstack([Cq, np.eye(9)[2]]); b = np.r_[np.zeros(8), w1]
    return q, np.linalg.solve(A, b)


def simulate(t_end, dt, alpha=0.0, beta=0.0, torque=0.0):
    M = np.diag([m[0], m[0], J[0], m[1], m[1], J[1], m[2], m[2], 1.0])
    q, qd = initial(0.3, 2 * math.pi * 10)                  # 600 rpm
    Q = np.zeros(9); Q[2] = torque
    drift = []
    def acc(q, qd):
        Cq = jacobian(q); C = constraints(q)
        rhs = gamma(q, qd) - 2 * alpha * (Cq @ qd) - beta**2 * C
        K = np.block([[M, Cq.T], [Cq, np.zeros((8, 8))]])
        sol = np.linalg.solve(K, np.r_[Q, rhs])
        return sol[:9], sol[9:]
    for _ in range(int(round(t_end / dt))):
        def f(s):
            a, _ = acc(s[:9], s[9:]); return np.r_[s[9:], a]
        s = np.r_[q, qd]
        k1 = f(s); k2 = f(s + dt/2*k1); k3 = f(s + dt/2*k2); k4 = f(s + dt*k3)
        s = s + dt/6*(k1 + 2*k2 + 2*k3 + k4); q, qd = s[:9], s[9:]
        drift.append(np.max(np.abs(constraints(q))))
    T = 0.5 * qd @ M @ qd
    return q, qd, np.array(drift), T


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # check the analytic Jacobian against finite differences
    qa, _ = initial(0.7, 1.0); h = 1e-6
    Jfd = np.column_stack([(constraints(qa + h * e) - constraints(qa - h * e)) / (2 * h) for e in np.eye(9)])
    assert np.max(np.abs(Jfd - jacobian(qa))) < 1e-9
    q0, qd0 = initial(0.3, 2 * math.pi * 10)
    M = np.diag([m[0], m[0], J[0], m[1], m[1], J[1], m[2], m[2], 1.0])
    T0 = 0.5 * qd0 @ M @ qd0
    print(f"start: crank 0.3 rad at 600 rpm, piston at x = {q0[6]*1000:.3f} mm, kinetic energy {T0:.4f} J")
    for alpha, beta, name in ((0.0, 0.0, "no stabilisation"), (20.0, 20.0, "Baumgarte (alpha = beta = 20)")):
        q, qd, drift, T = simulate(0.5, 5e-4, alpha, beta)
        print(f"{name:30s}: worst constraint violation {drift.max():.1e} m, final {drift[-1]:.1e}; energy change {abs(T / T0 - 1):.1e}")
    assert drift.max() < 1e-8
    # the free mechanism (no torque, no gravity) keeps its kinetic energy, so it speeds up and slows down as the
    # effective inertia changes with crank angle: crank speed is not constant even with no load
    q, qd, _, _ = simulate(0.05, 5e-4, 20.0, 20.0)
    print(f"after 0.05 s: crank angle {q[2]:.4f} rad, crank speed {qd[2]:.3f} rad/s (started at {2 * math.pi * 10:.3f})")
```

:::exercise Piston acceleration
Using $x = r\cos\theta + \sqrt{l^2 - r^2\sin^2\theta}$, the piston acceleration at top dead centre ($\theta = 0$) and constant crank speed $\omega$ is $-r\omega^2(1 + r/l)$. For $r = 0.05$ m, $l = 0.15$ m and 6000 rpm, find it, and the inertia force on a 0.5 kg piston.
---solution---
1. $\omega = 6000\cdot2\pi/60 = 628.3$ rad/s.
2. $a = -0.05\cdot628.3^2\cdot(1 + 1/3) = -26{,}319$ m/s², about 2700 g.
3. $F = 0.5\cdot26{,}319 = 13.2$ kN. That load goes through the wrist pin and rod on every revolution, 100 times a second.
:::
