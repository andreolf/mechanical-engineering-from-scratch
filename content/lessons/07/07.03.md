Newton's law gives the acceleration at every instant. Often you only want to know how fast something is going at a certain place: a roller coaster at the bottom of a drop, a car after braking over 30 m, a hammer as it hits a nail. Integrating $F = ma$ along the path once and for all gives a shortcut that skips time entirely: work and energy.

## The work–energy theorem

Dot $m\dot{\mathbf v} = \mathbf F$ with $\mathbf v\,dt = d\mathbf r$ and integrate along the path from 1 to 2:

$$
\tfrac12 mv_2^2 - \tfrac12 mv_1^2 = \int_1^2 \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = W_{12} .
$$

The change in kinetic energy equals the work done by all forces. Forces perpendicular to the motion (the normal force from a track, the tension in a pendulum string) do no work.

## Conservative forces and potential energy

Some forces do work that depends only on the start and end points, not the path: gravity, springs. Their work can be stored as potential energy, $V_g = mgh$ and $V_s = \tfrac12 kx^2$. If only conservative forces do work:

$$
T + V = \text{constant} .
$$

Friction and drag are not conservative. They take energy out, usually as heat: $T_2 + V_2 = T_1 + V_1 - W_{loss}$.

## Roller coasters

A frictionless car released at height $h_0$ with speed $v_0$ has, at height $h$,

$$
v = \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2g(h_0 - h)} .
$$

Mass cancels and the shape of the track between doesn't matter. The car can never climb above $h_0 + v_0^2/2g$. A test: simulate the motion along the track with $\ddot s = -g\sin\theta$ and the speeds must agree with energy everywhere.

:::widget coaster
Change the heights of the bump and the last hill and the speed at the top. Speed is coloured along the track. Set the last hill too high and the car stops and rolls back.
:::

:::example The coaster, two ways
Track profile: from 40 m down towards 10 m, a 12 m bump near $x = 80$ m, a 17 m hill near $x = 150$ m; 1 m/s at the top.

1. RK4 on the path equation with a 1 ms step, 14,458 steps: largest difference from the energy speed $1.5\times10^{-9}$ m/s.
2. Speeds: 16.79 m/s at $x = 30$ m (25 m high), 23.82 m/s = 85.8 km/h at the dip ($h = 10.44$ m), 15.57 m/s over the last crest ($h = 27$ m).
3. Add rolling friction $\mu = 0.015$ and the car reaches the last crest at 14.09 m/s instead: the friction work has eaten into its energy.
:::

```python file=coaster.py
"""Roller-coaster speed from energy, checked against a direct simulation of the motion along the track."""
import math
import numpy as np


def track(x):
    """Height (m) of a smooth track profile: a drop from 40 m towards 10 m, a bump near x = 80 m and a 27 m hill at x = 150 m."""
    return 40 - 30 * (1 / (1 + np.exp(-(x - 30) / 8))) + 12 * np.exp(-((x - 80) / 15) ** 2) + 17 * np.exp(-((x - 150) / 18) ** 2)


def dtrack(x, h=1e-5):
    return (track(x + h) - track(x - h)) / (2 * h)


def speed_from_energy(x, v0=1.0, g=9.81):
    return np.sqrt(np.maximum(v0**2 + 2 * g * (track(0) - track(x)), 0))


def simulate(x_end, v0=1.0, g=9.81, dt=1e-3, mu=0.0):
    """Motion along the path coordinate s: s'' = -g sin(theta) - mu g cos(theta) sign(v). Uses RK4 in (x, s')."""
    def f(state):
        x, v = state
        slope = dtrack(x); c = 1 / math.sqrt(1 + slope**2); s = slope * c
        return np.array([v * c, -g * s - mu * g * c * np.sign(v)])
    st = np.array([0.0, v0]); xs, vs = [0.0], [v0]
    while st[0] < x_end and st[1] > 0:
        k1 = f(st); k2 = f(st + dt / 2 * k1); k3 = f(st + dt / 2 * k2); k4 = f(st + dt * k3)
        st = st + dt / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4); xs.append(st[0]); vs.append(st[1])
    return np.array(xs), np.array(vs)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    xs, vs = simulate(190.0)
    ve = speed_from_energy(xs)
    print(f"max |v_sim - v_energy| = {np.max(np.abs(vs - ve)):.2e} m/s over {len(xs)} steps")
    assert np.max(np.abs(vs - ve)) < 1e-6
    for xq in (30, 80, 115, 150):
        i = int(np.argmin(np.abs(xs - xq)))
        print(f"  x = {xs[i]:6.1f} m, height {track(xs[i]):5.2f} m: {vs[i]:5.2f} m/s = {vs[i]*3.6:5.1f} km/h")
    # mass doesn't appear anywhere: every car, full or empty, has the same speed at the same height
    # with rolling friction (mu = 0.015) the car loses speed and may stall on the last hill
    xs2, vs2 = simulate(190.0, mu=0.015)
    i = int(np.argmin(np.abs(xs2 - 150))) if xs2[-1] > 150 else -1
    print(f"with mu = 0.015: reaches x = {xs2[-1]:.1f} m; speed at the last crest {vs2[i]:.2f} m/s (frictionless {vs[int(np.argmin(np.abs(xs - 150)))]:.2f})")
```

:::exercise A car brakes to a stop
A 1400 kg car brakes from 90 km/h on a level road with a constant friction force of 0.8 times its weight. How far does it travel? Then on a 10 % downhill grade?
---solution---
1. Level: $\tfrac12 mv^2 = \mu mg\,d$, so $d = v^2/(2\mu g) = 25^2/(2\cdot0.8\cdot9.81) = 39.8$ m. Mass cancels.
2. Downhill, slope angle $\theta = \arctan 0.1 = 5.71°$: gravity does positive work $mg\sin\theta\,d$, friction $\mu mg\cos\theta\,d$.
3. $d = v^2/(2g(\mu\cos\theta - \sin\theta)) = 625/(2\cdot9.81\cdot(0.796 - 0.0995)) = 45.7$ m.
:::
