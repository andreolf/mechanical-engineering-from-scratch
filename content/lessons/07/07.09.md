Roll a solid disc and a hoop of the same mass and radius down a ramp, and the disc wins every time. Mass and radius don't matter at all. What matters is how the mass is spread around the axis. Explaining this needs Newton's law for rotation as well as translation.

## Equations of motion in the plane

A rigid body in plane motion has three degrees of freedom: two for the centre of mass G and one for rotation. Three equations:

$$
\sum F_x = m\,a_{Gx}, \qquad \sum F_y = m\,a_{Gy}, \qquad \sum M_G = I_G\,\alpha .
$$

$I_G$ is the moment of inertia about G (next lesson). Moments must be taken about G, or about a fixed point, or else extra terms appear.

## Rolling without slipping

A wheel of radius $r$ rolling without slipping has its contact point momentarily at rest, so $v_G = \omega r$ and $a_G = \alpha r$. That is the **rolling constraint**. Friction at the contact supplies the moment that spins the wheel up, and does no work, since the contact point doesn't move.

## Down a ramp

Write $I_G = kmr^2$. Along the ramp: $ma = mg\sin\theta - F$. About G: $Fr = I_G\alpha = kmr^2\cdot a/r$. Eliminate $F$:

$$
a = \frac{g\sin\theta}{1 + k}, \qquad F = \frac{k}{1 + k}\,mg\sin\theta .
$$

Solid sphere ($k = 2/5$): $a = \tfrac57 g\sin\theta$. Solid disc ($k = 1/2$): $\tfrac23 g\sin\theta$. Thin ring ($k = 1$): $\tfrac12 g\sin\theta$. The bigger $k$, the more of the energy goes into spinning rather than moving.

To roll without slipping, the friction needed must be available: $F \le \mu N = \mu mg\cos\theta$, which gives $\mu \ge \dfrac{k}{1 + k}\tan\theta$.

:::example The race at 10°
A 2 m ramp at 10°, $g\sin\theta = 1.7035$ m/s².

1. Sliding block (no friction): 1.7035 m/s², 1.532 s.
2. Solving the two equations for each shape: sphere 1.2168 m/s² (1.813 s), disc 1.1357 m/s² (1.877 s), ring 0.8517 m/s² (2.167 s). The disc's acceleration is exactly $\tfrac23 g\sin\theta$, the ring's $\tfrac12 g\sin\theta$.
3. Energy check for the disc: $mgh = \tfrac12 mv^2 + \tfrac12 I\omega^2 = \tfrac34 mv^2$ gives $v = \sqrt{4gh/3} = 2.1313$ m/s, the same as the simulation.
4. Friction needed per kg: disc 0.568 N, ring 0.852 N. The disc rolls without slipping if $\mu \ge \tan10°/3 = 0.0588$.
:::

```python file=rolling.py
"""Rolling race down a ramp: a = g sin(theta) / (1 + I / (m r^2)), checked by simulating the rigid-body equations."""
import math
import numpy as np


def race(k, theta, L, g=9.81, dt=1e-4):
    """k = I / (m r^2). Unknowns: friction F and acceleration; equations m a = m g sin - F, I alpha = F r, a = alpha r.
    Integrates x'' = a with semi-implicit Euler and returns the time to roll L metres."""
    A = np.array([[1.0, 1.0], [1.0, -1.0 / k]])           # rows: m a + F = m g sin;  a - F / (k m) = 0  (m = 1)
    a, F = np.linalg.solve(A, [g * math.sin(theta), 0.0])
    x, v, t = 0.0, 0.0, 0.0
    while x < L:
        v += a * dt; x += v * dt; t += dt
    return a, F, t


if __name__ == "__main__":
    th, L, g = math.radians(10), 2.0, 9.81
    shapes = {"sliding block (no friction)": None, "solid sphere": 2 / 5, "solid disk": 1 / 2, "thin ring": 1.0}
    for name, k in shapes.items():
        if k is None:
            a = g * math.sin(th); t = math.sqrt(2 * L / a); F = 0.0
        else:
            a, F, t = race(k, th, L)
        print(f"{name:28s} a = {a:.4f} m/s^2 = {a / (g * math.sin(th)):.4f} g sin(theta), friction {F:.4f} N per kg, time {t:.3f} s")
    a_disk, _, _ = race(0.5, th, L); a_ring, _, _ = race(1.0, th, L)
    assert abs(a_disk - 2 / 3 * g * math.sin(th)) < 1e-12 and abs(a_ring - 0.5 * g * math.sin(th)) < 1e-12
    # energy check for the disk: m g h = m v^2 / 2 + I w^2 / 2 = (3/4) m v^2
    v = math.sqrt(2 * a_disk * L)
    print(f"disk at the bottom: v = {v:.4f} m/s; energy says sqrt(4 g h / 3) = {math.sqrt(4 * g * L * math.sin(th) / 3):.4f}")
    assert abs(v - math.sqrt(4 * g * L * math.sin(th) / 3)) < 1e-12
    # the disk rolls without slipping only if friction mu >= F / N = tan(theta) / 3
    print(f"disk needs mu >= tan(theta)/3 = {math.tan(th) / 3:.4f}; ring needs tan(theta)/2 = {math.tan(th) / 2:.4f}")
```

:::exercise A yo-yo
A yo-yo is a solid disc of mass $m$ and radius $R$ with its string wound round an axle of radius $r = R/4$. Released from rest with the string held fixed, what is its downward acceleration and the string tension?
---solution---
1. Down: $ma = mg - T$. About G: $Tr = \tfrac12 mR^2\,\alpha$, with the constraint $a = \alpha r$.
2. So $T = \tfrac12 mR^2 a/r^2 = 8ma$ (using $R/r = 4$).
3. $ma = mg - 8ma$ gives $a = g/9 = 1.09$ m/s², and $T = \tfrac89 mg$.
:::
