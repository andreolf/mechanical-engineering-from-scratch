A collision lasts milliseconds and the forces during it are huge and hard to measure. Yet what happens afterwards is easy to predict, because Newton's law integrated over time says the total momentum can't change. Car crash reconstruction, billiards, railway couplings and a hammer driving a nail all use this.

## Impulse

Integrate $\mathbf F = m\dot{\mathbf v}$ over a time interval:

$$
\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \mathbf F\,dt = m\mathbf v_2 - m\mathbf v_1 .
$$

The left side is the **impulse**: force times time. A small force for a long time does the same as a big force for a short time. That is why airbags and crumple zones save lives: they stretch the stopping time, so the force on the body drops.

## Conservation of momentum

When two bodies collide, the forces they exert on each other are equal and opposite (Newton's third law), so their impulses cancel. Without outside forces:

$$
m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = m_1v_1' + m_2v_2' .
$$

## Restitution

Momentum alone gives one equation for two unknowns. The second comes from how bouncy the impact is, described by the **coefficient of restitution** $e$:

$$
v_2' - v_1' = -e\,(v_2 - v_1) .
$$

$e = 1$ is perfectly elastic (no energy lost), $e = 0$ is perfectly plastic (the bodies stick together). Real values: steel balls about 0.9, a tennis ball 0.75, a car crash near 0.1. The kinetic energy lost is

$$
\Delta T = \tfrac12(1 - e^2)\,\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,(v_1 - v_2)^2 .
$$

:::example Two lab carts
Cart 1, 0.5 kg at 2 m/s, hits cart 2, 1.0 kg at rest.

1. $e = 1$: $v_1' = -0.667$ m/s (bounces back), $v_2' = 1.333$ m/s, no energy lost.
2. $e = 0.6$: $v_1' = -0.133$, $v_2' = 1.067$ m/s; 0.427 J lost, matching $\tfrac12(1 - 0.36)\cdot\tfrac13\cdot4$.
3. $e = 0$: both move off at 0.667 m/s; 0.667 J lost, two thirds of the original energy.
4. Momentum before and after agrees to machine precision in 10,000 random collisions (worst relative error $5\times10^{-16}$).
5. A ball with $e = 0.8$ dropped from 1 m bounces to 0.64, 0.41, 0.26 m: each height is $e^2$ times the last.
:::

```python file=collision.py
"""One-dimensional collisions with a coefficient of restitution."""
import numpy as np


def collide(m1, v1, m2, v2, e):
    """Velocities after impact. Momentum is conserved; e sets the separation speed: v2' - v1' = -e (v2 - v1)."""
    p = m1 * v1 + m2 * v2
    v1n = (p - m2 * e * (v1 - v2)) / (m1 + m2)
    v2n = (p + m1 * e * (v1 - v2)) / (m1 + m2)
    return v1n, v2n


def ke(m, v):
    return 0.5 * m * v * v


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # two lab carts: 0.5 kg at 2 m/s hits 1.0 kg at rest
    m1, v1, m2, v2 = 0.5, 2.0, 1.0, 0.0
    for e in (1.0, 0.6, 0.0):
        a, b = collide(m1, v1, m2, v2, e)
        dp = (m1 * a + m2 * b) - (m1 * v1 + m2 * v2)
        loss = ke(m1, v1) + ke(m2, v2) - ke(m1, a) - ke(m2, b)
        print(f"e = {e:.1f}: v1' = {a:+.4f}, v2' = {b:+.4f} m/s, momentum change {dp:+.1e}, energy lost {loss:.4f} J")
        assert abs(dp) < 1e-12
    # energy loss formula: (1 - e^2) * reduced mass * closing speed^2 / 2
    mu = m1 * m2 / (m1 + m2)
    a, b = collide(m1, v1, m2, v2, 0.6)
    assert abs((ke(m1, v1) - ke(m1, a) - ke(m2, b)) - 0.5 * (1 - 0.36) * mu * 4) < 1e-12
    # random checks of momentum conservation
    rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
    worst = 0.0
    for _ in range(10000):
        m1, m2 = rng.uniform(0.1, 10, 2); v1, v2 = rng.uniform(-5, 5, 2); e = rng.uniform(0, 1)
        a, b = collide(m1, v1, m2, v2, e)
        worst = max(worst, abs(m1 * a + m2 * b - m1 * v1 - m2 * v2) / (abs(m1 * v1) + abs(m2 * v2)))
    print(f"10000 random collisions: worst relative momentum error {worst:.1e}")
    assert worst < 1e-12
    # a bouncing ball: each bounce keeps a fraction e of the speed, so rebound height scales with e^2
    e, h0 = 0.8, 1.0
    print("bouncing ball heights (m):", [round(h0 * e ** (2 * k), 4) for k in range(5)])
    # Newton's cradle: equal masses, e = 1, exchange velocities
    print("equal masses, e = 1:", collide(1.0, 1.0, 1.0, 0.0, 1.0))
```

:::exercise A railway shunting impact
A 30 t wagon rolling at 1.5 m/s hits a stationary 20 t wagon and they couple ($e = 0$). Find the common speed, the energy absorbed by the buffers and coupling, and the average force if the impact lasts 0.4 s.
---solution---
1. Common speed: $30\cdot1.5/(30 + 20) = 0.9$ m/s.
2. Energy lost: $\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{30\cdot20}{50}\cdot10^3\cdot1.5^2 = 13.5$ kJ.
3. Impulse on the second wagon: $20000\cdot0.9 = 18{,}000$ N s, so the average force is $18000/0.4 = 45$ kN.
:::
