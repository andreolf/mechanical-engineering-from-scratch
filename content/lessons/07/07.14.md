In plane motion one number, $I$, describes how a body resists spinning. In 3D the body can spin about any axis, and a single number can't capture that. A tilted block, a crankshaft or a satellite with solar panels all need the **inertia tensor**: a 3 × 3 matrix whose eigenvectors are the body's natural spin axes.

## Angular momentum in 3D

For a rigid body spinning at $\boldsymbol\omega$, summing $\mathbf r\times m(\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r)$ over all its mass gives

$$
\mathbf H_G = \mathbf I\,\boldsymbol\omega, \qquad \mathbf I = \int\left(|\mathbf r|^2\,\mathbf 1 - \mathbf r\mathbf r^T\right)dm .
$$

Written out:

$$
\mathbf I = \begin{pmatrix} I_{xx} & -I_{xy} & -I_{xz} \\ -I_{xy} & I_{yy} & -I_{yz} \\ -I_{xz} & -I_{yz} & I_{zz} \end{pmatrix}, \quad I_{xx} = \int(y^2 + z^2)\,dm, \quad I_{xy} = \int xy\,dm .
$$

The off-diagonal **products of inertia** mean $\mathbf H$ is generally not parallel to $\boldsymbol\omega$. Spin a lopsided wheel and its angular momentum points off-axis. Keeping it turning then needs a rotating moment from the bearings: the dynamic unbalance of 07.11.

## Principal axes

$\mathbf I$ is symmetric, so it has three real eigenvalues and three perpendicular eigenvectors. Along those **principal axes** the products of inertia vanish and $\mathbf H$ is parallel to $\boldsymbol\omega$. The eigenvalues are the **principal moments** $I_1 \le I_2 \le I_3$. Turning the body changes every entry of $\mathbf I$ in a fixed frame, but not the eigenvalues. Axes of symmetry are always principal axes.

1. A solid block 0.4 × 0.2 × 0.1 m of 8 kg as a cloud of 200,000 random points: diagonal of $\mathbf I$ = (0.03336, 0.11358, 0.13361) kg m², against the exact $\tfrac{m}{12}(b^2 + c^2)$ etc. = (0.03333, 0.11333, 0.13333). The cloud is within 0.25 %.
2. Turn the block by 30° about z: $I_{xx} = 0.05346$, $I_{yy} = 0.09348$ and an off-diagonal entry of −0.03476 kg m² appear.
3. The eigenvalues are unchanged, (0.03336, 0.11358, 0.13361), and the axis of least inertia points at 30.04° (the block's long edge, within sampling noise).
4. Three point masses (1, 2, 1.5 kg) forming an L-shaped bracket: centre of mass (0.1333, 0.0667, 0.0333) m, principal moments (0.02377, 0.12623, 0.15000) kg m².
:::

```python file=inertia_tensor.py
"""Inertia tensor of a body made of point masses; principal axes as its eigenvectors."""
import math
import numpy as np


def inertia_tensor(pts, m):
    """I = sum m (|r|^2 1 - r r^T) about the centre of mass."""
    c = (m[:, None] * pts).sum(0) / m.sum()
    r = pts - c
    I = np.sum(m * (r**2).sum(1)) * np.eye(3) - (r * m[:, None]).T @ r
    return I, c


def box_cloud(a, b, c, n, mass, rng):
    """Uniform random point cloud filling an a x b x c box (an approximation of a solid block)."""
    pts = rng.uniform(-0.5, 0.5, (n, 3)) * np.array([a, b, c])
    return pts, np.full(n, mass / n)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    np.set_printoptions(suppress=True)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(11)
    # 1. a solid block 0.4 x 0.2 x 0.1 m of 8 kg, as 200,000 points: compare with m (b^2 + c^2) / 12 etc.
    a, b, c, M = 0.4, 0.2, 0.1, 8.0
    pts, m = box_cloud(a, b, c, 200000, M, rng)
    I, cg = inertia_tensor(pts, m)
    exact = M / 12 * np.array([b*b + c*c, a*a + c*c, a*a + b*b])
    print("block, diagonal of I:", np.round(np.diag(I), 5), " exact:", np.round(exact, 5))
    assert np.allclose(np.diag(I), exact, rtol=0.01)
    # 2. tilt the block by a known rotation: off-diagonal products of inertia appear, but the eigenvalues don't change
    th = math.radians(30)
    Rz = np.array([[math.cos(th), -math.sin(th), 0], [math.sin(th), math.cos(th), 0], [0, 0, 1]])
    I_t, _ = inertia_tensor(pts @ Rz.T, m)
    print("tilted block I (kg m^2):\n", np.round(I_t, 5))
    lam, V = np.linalg.eigh(I_t)
    print("principal moments (eigenvalues):", np.round(lam, 5), " untilted diagonal sorted:", np.round(np.sort(np.diag(I)), 5))
    assert np.allclose(lam, np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(I)), atol=1e-12)
    # the principal axis with the smallest moment lies along the block's long edge, now turned by 30 degrees
    ax = V[:, 0] * np.sign(V[0, 0])
    print(f"axis of least inertia at {math.degrees(math.atan2(ax[1], ax[0])):.2f} deg in the x-y plane (block turned 30 deg)")
    # 3. an L-shaped bracket from three point masses: no symmetry, so the axes must be computed
    P = np.array([[0, 0, 0], [0.3, 0, 0], [0, 0.2, 0.1]], float); mm = np.array([1.0, 2.0, 1.5])
    IL, cL = inertia_tensor(P, mm)
    lamL, VL = np.linalg.eigh(IL)
    print(f"three-mass bracket: centre {np.round(cL, 4)}, principal moments {np.round(lamL, 5)}")
    assert np.allclose(IL @ VL, VL * lamL)
```

:::exercise A dumbbell at an angle
Two 1 kg point masses sit at $(0.3, 0.3, 0)$ and $(-0.3, -0.3, 0)$ m. Write down $\mathbf I$ about the origin and find its principal moments and axes.
---solution---
1. $I_{xx} = \sum m(y^2 + z^2) = 2\cdot0.09 = 0.18$, $I_{yy} = 0.18$, $I_{zz} = \sum m(x^2 + y^2) = 0.36$, $I_{xy} = \sum mxy = 0.18$, so the matrix entry is $-0.18$.
2. In the x-y block, $\begin{pmatrix}0.18 & -0.18\\-0.18 & 0.18\end{pmatrix}$ has eigenvalues 0 and 0.36.
3. The eigenvalue 0 belongs to $(1, 1, 0)/\sqrt2$, the dumbbell's own axis (point masses on it have no inertia about it). The others are 0.36 about $(1, -1, 0)/\sqrt2$ and 0.36 about z.
:::
