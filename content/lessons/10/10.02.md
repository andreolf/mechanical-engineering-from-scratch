Pump up a scooter tyre and the pump barrel gets warm. Leave the tyre in the Bali sun and its pressure rises. Both follow from the simplest and most used equation in thermodynamics, the ideal-gas law. It holds well for air, nitrogen, helium and combustion gases at ordinary pressures, and you can derive it by imagining molecules as tiny balls bouncing off the walls.

## The equation of state

$$
pV = nR_uT = mRT, \qquad pv = RT ,
$$

with $R_u = 8.314$ J/(mol K) the universal gas constant and $R = R_u/M$ the specific gas constant (287 J/(kg K) for air, $M = 28.97$ g/mol). In molecular terms, $pV = Nk_BT$ with $k_B = 1.381\times10^{-23}$ J/K.

## Where it comes from

Consider $N$ molecules of mass $m$ in a cube of side $L$. A molecule with velocity component $v_x$ hits the right wall, reverses, and delivers momentum $2mv_x$. It returns every $2L/v_x$ seconds, so its average force on the wall is $mv_x^2/L$. Summing over all molecules and dividing by the wall area $L^2$:

$$
p = \frac{Nm\langle v_x^2\rangle}{V} = \frac{Nm\langle v^2\rangle}{3V} .
$$

Comparing with $pV = Nk_BT$ shows what temperature *is*: the average translational kinetic energy of the molecules,

$$
\tfrac12 m\langle v^2\rangle = \tfrac32 k_BT .
$$

The rms speed is $\sqrt{3k_BT/m}$: about 517 m/s for nitrogen at room temperature, faster than sound.

## When is a gas ideal?

The derivation assumed molecules take up no space and don't attract each other. That's good when the gas is far from condensing: low pressure, high temperature compared with its critical point. Steam near saturation and refrigerants are not ideal; 10.06 and 10.16 cover them.

:::example Pressure from collisions
2000 nitrogen molecules in a cube of side 100 nm, with Maxwell-Boltzmann velocities, bounce elastically off the walls for 4000 time steps. The momentum delivered to the walls, divided by wall area and time, is the pressure.

1. At 300 K (the sample's kinetic temperature is 293.7 K): simulated 8111.6 Pa against $Nk_BT/V = 8111.0$ Pa, ratio 1.0001.
2. At 200 K and 600 K the ratio is also 1.0001: pressure is proportional to temperature.
3. The rms speed of N₂ (molecular mass $4.652\times10^{-26}$ kg) at 300 K is $\sqrt{3k_BT/m} = 517$ m/s.
:::

```python file=kinetic.py
"""Kinetic theory by simulation: point molecules bouncing elastically in a box, pressure measured from momentum delivered to the walls, compared with N k T / V."""
import numpy as np

K_B = 1.380649e-23
M_N2 = 28.0134e-3 / 6.02214076e23      # kg per molecule


def simulate(N=2000, T=300.0, L=1e-7, steps=4000, seed=1):
    """Return measured pressure (Pa) and the ideal-gas value for N molecules at temperature T in a cube of side L."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    x = rng.uniform(0, L, (N, 3))
    v = rng.normal(0, np.sqrt(K_B * T / M_N2), (N, 3))     # Maxwell-Boltzmann components
    dt = 0.02 * L / np.sqrt(K_B * T / M_N2)
    impulse = 0.0
    for _ in range(steps):
        x += v * dt
        lo, hi = x < 0, x > L
        hit = lo | hi
        impulse += 2 * M_N2 * np.abs(v[hit]).sum()
        x[lo] = -x[lo]; x[hi] = 2 * L - x[hi]; v[hit] = -v[hit]
    area_time = 6 * L * L * steps * dt
    T_kin = M_N2 * (v**2).sum() / (3 * N * K_B)
    return impulse / area_time, N * K_B * T_kin / L**3, T_kin


if __name__ == "__main__":
    for T in (200.0, 300.0, 600.0):
        p, p_ideal, Tk = simulate(T=T)
        print(f"T = {T:.0f} K (kinetic {Tk:.1f} K): simulated p = {p:.4e} Pa, NkT/V = {p_ideal:.4e} Pa, ratio {p/p_ideal:.4f}")
        assert abs(p / p_ideal - 1) < 0.02
    v_rms = np.sqrt(3 * K_B * 300 / M_N2)
    print(f"rms speed of N2 at 300 K: {v_rms:.0f} m/s")
```

:::exercise A scooter tyre in the sun
A tyre is inflated to 200 kPa gauge (301 kPa absolute) at 25 °C. In the sun it reaches 55 °C at constant volume. What does the gauge read?
---solution---
1. At constant $V$ and $m$, $p \propto T$: $p_2 = 301\cdot328.15/298.15 = 331.3$ kPa absolute.
2. Gauge: $331.3 - 101 = 230$ kPa.
3. Always use absolute temperature and pressure in the gas law.
:::
