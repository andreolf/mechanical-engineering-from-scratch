31 °C in Bali feels oppressive; 31 °C on a dry Zurich summer day feels pleasant. The difference is water vapour. **Psychrometrics**, the thermodynamics of moist air, tells an engineer how much water the air carries, when it will condense on a cold surface, and how much of an air conditioner's work goes into drying the air rather than cooling it. In the tropics that's most of it.

## Describing moist air

Treat moist air as a mixture of dry air and water vapour, both ideal gases at total pressure $p$, with vapour partial pressure $p_v$.

- **Relative humidity** $\phi = p_v/p_\text{sat}(T)$: how close the air is to saturation.
- **Humidity ratio** $\omega = 0.622\,p_v/(p - p_v)$: kg of water per kg of dry air. It depends on the total pressure, which is lower in Zurich at 408 m than in Bali at sea level.
- **Dew point** $T_d$: the temperature at which $p_\text{sat}(T_d) = p_v$, where water starts to condense.
- **Enthalpy** per kg of dry air: $h = 1.006\,T + \omega(2501 + 1.86\,T)$ kJ/kg, with $T$ in °C. The 2501 is the latent heat of vaporisation at 0 °C, and it dominates.

The saturation pressure here comes from the **Magnus formula**, $p_\text{sat} = 610.94\exp\left(\frac{17.625\,T}{T + 243.04}\right)$ Pa (over liquid water), accurate to about 0.1 % between −40 and 50 °C. It inverts exactly, which gives the dew point.

## Air conditioning in the tropics

To bring humid air to a comfortable state, an air conditioner cools it below the target's dew point, so the excess water condenses on the coil and drains away, then (in good systems) reheats it slightly. The latent load, the water removed, often exceeds the sensible load.

:::example Bali against Zurich
1. Denpasar afternoon, 31 °C, 75 %, 101.3 kPa: $\omega = 21.37$ g/kg, dew point 26.05 °C, $h = 85.86$ kJ/kg.
2. Zurich July afternoon, 25 °C, 50 %, 96.6 kPa: 10.35 g/kg, dew point 13.86 °C, $h = 51.51$ kJ/kg.
3. Zurich January morning, 0 °C, 85 %: 3.36 g/kg, dew point −2.22 °C (over liquid water).
4. The code checks that at 100 % humidity the dew point equals the air temperature.
5. Air conditioning in Bali from 31 °C, 75 % to 24 °C, 50 %: cool to 12.94 °C and condense 12.09 g of water per kg of air. Cooling 48.76 kJ/kg and reheat 11.32 kJ/kg, against 7.04 kJ/kg if the air were dry.
:::

```python file=psychro.py
"""Psychrometrics: humidity ratio, dew point and enthalpy of moist air, Bali against Zurich, and the water an air conditioner condenses."""
import math

A_, B_, C_ = 610.94, 17.625, 243.04       # Magnus formula (Alduchov and Eskridge), Pa and C


def p_sat(T):
    return A_ * math.exp(B_ * T / (T + C_))


def dew_point(T, RH):
    g = math.log(RH * p_sat(T) / A_)          # exact inverse of the Magnus formula
    return C_ * g / (B_ - g)


def humidity_ratio(T, RH, p):
    pv = RH * p_sat(T)
    return 0.622 * pv / (p - pv)


def enthalpy(T, w):
    """kJ per kg of dry air, relative to dry air and liquid water at 0 C."""
    return 1.006 * T + w * (2501 + 1.86 * T)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    places = {"Bali, Denpasar (sea level, 101.3 kPa), afternoon": (31.0, 0.75, 101.3e3),
              "Zurich (408 m, 96.6 kPa), July afternoon": (25.0, 0.50, 96.6e3),
              "Zurich, January morning": (0.0, 0.85, 96.6e3)}
    for name, (T, RH, p) in places.items():
        w = humidity_ratio(T, RH, p)
        print(f"{name}: {T} C, RH {RH:.0%}: w = {w*1000:.2f} g/kg, dew point {dew_point(T, RH):.2f} C, h = {enthalpy(T, w):.2f} kJ/kg")
    for T in (-10, 0, 15, 31, 45):
        assert abs(dew_point(T, 1.0) - T) < 1e-9          # at saturation the dew point is the air temperature
    # air conditioning in Bali: 31 C 75 % -> 24 C 50 %: cool to the target's dew point, condense, reheat
    p = 101.3e3
    w1, w2 = humidity_ratio(31, 0.75, p), humidity_ratio(24, 0.50, p)
    Td = dew_point(24, 0.50)
    h1, h_cold, h2 = enthalpy(31, w1), enthalpy(Td, w2), enthalpy(24, w2)
    water = w1 - w2; q_cool = h1 - h_cold - water * 4.186 * Td
    print(f"AC: cool to {Td:.2f} C, condense {water*1000:.2f} g of water per kg of air; cooling {q_cool:.2f} kJ/kg, reheat {h2-h_cold:.2f} kJ/kg")
    print(f"  sensible part only (31 -> 24 C dry): {1.006*7:.2f} kJ/kg, so most of the load is the humidity")
```

:::exercise Dry winter air in Zurich
Outdoor air at 0 °C and 85 % is heated to 21 °C indoors with no moisture added. What's the indoor relative humidity? At what surface temperature does a window start to fog?
---solution---
1. Vapour pressure: $0.85\cdot610.94 = 519.3$ Pa. Heating doesn't change it.
2. At 21 °C, $p_\text{sat} = 2481.9$ Pa, so RH $= 519.3/2481.9 = 20.9$ %. That's why heated rooms feel dry in winter.
3. The dew point stays at −2.22 °C, so the window fogs only below that. With people, cooking and showers adding moisture, real indoor dew points are much higher, and cold window frames do fog.
:::
