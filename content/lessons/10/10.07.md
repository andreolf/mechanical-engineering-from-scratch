Solar panels produce most at midday in summer; demand peaks on winter evenings. Storing electricity at grid scale is hard, and over 90 % of the world's grid storage is one old idea: pump water uphill when power is cheap, let it run back through a turbine when power is scarce. Switzerland, with lakes hundreds of metres above each other, has some of the largest schemes in Europe. The thermodynamics is the first law with gravitational potential energy.

## Energy stored

Lifting a volume $V$ of water through a head $h$ stores

$$
E = \rho g h V .
$$

It's linear in both head and volume. One kilowatt-hour, $3.6\times10^6$ J, needs $3.6\times10^6/(\rho g h)$ m³ of water: 1.22 m³ falling 300 m, or 367 m³ falling 1 m. That's why pumped storage needs mountains. A flat-land scheme would need a lake the size of a city.

Power is the flow rate times the head: $P = \eta \rho g Q h$, so a 900 MW plant at 300 m head moves about 340 m³ of water every second.

## Round-trip efficiency

Every conversion loses something.

- **Generating:** turbine (about 92 %), generator (98 %), and penstock friction, which takes a head $h_f$ off the available head.
- **Pumping:** pump (about 91 %), motor (98 %), and the pump must deliver $h + h_f$.

Friction head follows Darcy-Weisbach, $h_f = f\frac{L}{D}\frac{V^2}{2g}$, so penstocks are built several metres in diameter to keep the speed low. Multiplying the efficiencies gives the **round trip**, typically 75–82 %. About a fifth of the electricity is lost, but storing it is worth far more when the price difference between night and peak, or summer and winter, is large enough.

Change the reservoir level, head and capacity. The stored energy scales linearly with each.
:::

:::example An Alpine plant
Assumed round numbers loosely modelled on a large Alpine scheme: 25 million m³ upper reservoir, 300 m mean head, 900 MW, two penstocks 1.4 km long and 7 m in diameter.

1. Stored energy: $1000\cdot9.81\cdot300\cdot25\times10^6 = 7.358\times10^{13}$ J $= 20.44$ GWh.
2. Flow at full power: $Q = P/(\rho g h\cdot0.90) = 340$ m³/s, 170 m³/s per penstock, about 4.4 m/s.
3. Friction head: 2.38 m, only 0.79 % of the head.
4. Generating efficiency $0.92\cdot0.98\cdot(1 - 0.0079) = 0.894$; pumping $0.91\cdot0.98/(1 + 0.0079) = 0.885$; round trip 0.791.
5. It returns 18.28 GWh for 23.10 GWh pumped in: 20.3 hours at full power.
:::

```python file=pumpedhydro.py
"""Pumped-storage hydropower: stored energy rho g h V, penstock friction, and the round-trip efficiency for an Alpine plant (round, clearly assumed numbers)."""
import math

RHO, G = 1000.0, 9.81


def stored_energy(V, h):
    """Gravitational energy (J) of volume V (m^3) lifted through head h (m)."""
    return RHO * G * h * V


def head_loss(Q, L, D, f=0.012):
    """Darcy-Weisbach friction head (m) for flow Q (m^3/s) in a penstock of length L and diameter D."""
    V = Q / (math.pi * D * D / 4)
    return f * L / D * V * V / (2 * G)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # assumed plant, loosely modelled on a large Alpine scheme: 25 million m^3, 300 m mean head,
    # 900 MW, two penstocks 1.4 km long and 7 m in diameter
    V, h, P = 25e6, 300.0, 900e6
    E = stored_energy(V, h)
    print(f"stored: {E:.3e} J = {E/3.6e12:.2f} GWh")
    assert abs(stored_energy(2 * V, h) - 2 * E) < 1e-3 and abs(stored_energy(V, 2 * h) - 2 * E) < 1e-3
    Q = P / (RHO * G * h * 0.90)                 # flow at full turbine power (turbine x generator 0.90)
    hf = head_loss(Q / 2, 1400, 7.0)               # same loss used for pumping (assumes similar flow)
    print(f"flow at 900 MW: {Q:.0f} m^3/s; friction head per penstock {hf:.2f} m ({hf/h*100:.2f} % of the head)")
    eta_t = 0.92 * 0.98 * (1 - hf / h)          # turbine, generator, penstock
    eta_p = 0.91 * 0.98 / (1 + hf / h)          # pump, motor, penstock (extra head to overcome)
    rt = eta_t * eta_p
    print(f"generating efficiency {eta_t:.3f}, pumping efficiency {eta_p:.3f}, round trip {rt:.3f}")
    print(f"electricity out {E*eta_t/3.6e12:.2f} GWh for {E/eta_p/3.6e12:.2f} GWh in")
    print(f"hours at full power: {E*eta_t/P/3600:.1f} h")
    print(f"1 kWh needs {3.6e6/(RHO*G*h):.2f} m^3 of water falling {h:.0f} m")
```

:::exercise Is it worth it?
A plant buys electricity at 40 CHF/MWh at night and sells at 120 CHF/MWh at the evening peak, with a round-trip efficiency of 0.79. What's the margin per MWh sold, and what's the minimum price ratio that breaks even? Also find the energy stored in a smaller reservoir of 2 million m³ at 500 m head.
---solution---
1. Each MWh sold needs $1/0.79 = 1.266$ MWh bought, costing $40/0.79 = 50.63$ CHF.
2. Margin: $120 - 50.63 = 69.37$ CHF per MWh, before maintenance and grid fees.
3. Break-even when sell/buy price $= 1/0.79 = 1.266$.
4. Smaller reservoir: $1000\cdot9.81\cdot500\cdot2\times10^6 = 9.81\times10^{12}$ J $= 2.725$ GWh. The higher head partly makes up for the much smaller volume.
:::
