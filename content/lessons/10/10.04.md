Energy can't be created or destroyed, only converted. In mechanics that meant kinetic plus potential energy stays constant without friction. Thermodynamics extends the bookkeeping to heat and internal energy, the energy of the molecules' random motion. It's the **first law**, and every energy calculation you'll ever do, from a pressure cooker to a power station, is an application of it.

## The first law

For a closed system going from state 1 to state 2:

$$
Q - W = \Delta U \;(+\,\Delta KE + \Delta PE) ,
$$

with $Q$ the heat added to the system and $W$ the work done by it. In differential form, $\delta Q - \delta W = dU$.

$U$ is a property: its change depends only on the end states. $Q$ and $W$ depend on the path, but their **difference** doesn't. Over a full cycle the system returns to its start, $\Delta U = 0$, so

$$
\oint\delta Q = \oint\delta W :
$$

a cycle's net work equals its net heat input.

## Internal energy and enthalpy of ideal gases

For an ideal gas, $u$ depends only on temperature: $du = c_v\,dT$. The **enthalpy** $h = u + pv$ is also a function of $T$ only, $dh = c_p\,dT$, with $c_p - c_v = R$. For air near room temperature $c_v = 718$ and $c_p = 1005$ J/(kg K).

At constant pressure, heat added equals the change in enthalpy: $Q = \Delta U + p\Delta V = \Delta H$. That's why enthalpy appears whenever pressure is held fixed, or whenever fluid flows (10.05).

## Solving problems

1. Choose the system and draw the boundary.
2. Identify the processes and what's fixed in each ($p$, $V$, $T$, or adiabatic).
3. Find the end states from the equation of state.
4. Compute $W$ along each path and $\Delta U$ from the end states.
5. Get $Q$ from the first law, and check that the residual $Q - W - \Delta U$ is zero.

:::example Heating air under a piston with stops
2 L of air at 150 kPa and 300 K (3.4843 g) sits under a free piston. It's heated at constant pressure to 500 K, when the piston reaches stops, then heated at constant volume to 700 K.

1. Constant pressure: $V_2 = 2\cdot500/300 = 3.333$ L. $W = 150\,000\cdot0.001333 = 200.0$ J. $\Delta U = 0.0034843\cdot718\cdot200 = 500.3$ J. $Q = 700.3$ J.
2. Constant volume: $W = 0$, $\Delta U = Q = 500.3$ J. Final pressure 210.0 kPa.
3. Totals: $Q = 1200.7$ J, $W = 200.0$ J, $\Delta U = 1000.7$ J $= mc_v(700 - 300)$. The residual is zero in every step.
4. An Otto-like cycle (adiabatic compression to 0.5 L, constant-volume heating by 400 K, adiabatic expansion, constant-volume cooling): net $Q$ = net $W$ = 425.73 J and $\Delta U = 0$ around the cycle.
:::

```python file=firstlaw.py
"""First law for a closed system: a piston-cylinder solver that tracks Q, W and the change in internal energy through a sequence of processes, checking the energy residual."""
import math

R, CV = 287.0, 718.0         # air, J/(kg K)


def process(state, kind, value, m):
    """Advance an ideal-gas state (p, V, T) through one process; return new state and (Q, W, dU) in J.
    kind: 'p' constant pressure to temperature value, 'v' constant volume to temperature value,
          'T' isothermal to volume value, 's' reversible adiabatic to volume value (k = 1.4)."""
    p, V, T = state
    if kind == "p":
        T2 = value; V2 = m * R * T2 / p; p2 = p; W = p * (V2 - V)
    elif kind == "v":
        T2 = value; V2 = V; p2 = m * R * T2 / V; W = 0.0
    elif kind == "T":
        V2 = value; T2 = T; p2 = m * R * T / V2; W = m * R * T * math.log(V2 / V)
    elif kind == "s":
        k = (CV + R) / CV; V2 = value; p2 = p * (V / V2) ** k; T2 = p2 * V2 / (m * R); W = (p2 * V2 - p * V) / (1 - k)
    dU = m * CV * (T2 - T)
    Q = dU + W                   # first law: Q - W = dU
    return (p2, V2, T2), (Q, W, dU)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # 2 L of air at 150 kPa and 300 K under a free piston; heated at constant pressure to 500 K,
    # where the piston hits stops; then heated at constant volume to 700 K.
    p1, V1, T1 = 150e3, 2e-3, 300.0
    m = p1 * V1 / (R * T1)
    st = (p1, V1, T1); totals = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    for kind, val, label in (("p", 500.0, "constant pressure to 500 K"), ("v", 700.0, "constant volume to 700 K")):
        st, (Q, W, dU) = process(st, kind, val, m)
        totals = [a + b for a, b in zip(totals, (Q, W, dU))]
        print(f"{label}: Q = {Q:.1f} J, W = {W:.1f} J, dU = {dU:.1f} J -> p = {st[0]/1e3:.1f} kPa, V = {st[1]*1e3:.3f} L")
        assert abs(Q - W - dU) < 1e-9
    print(f"mass {m*1000:.4f} g; totals Q = {totals[0]:.1f} J, W = {totals[1]:.1f} J, dU = {totals[2]:.1f} J")
    assert abs(totals[2] - m * CV * (700 - 300)) < 1e-9
    # a cycle returns to its start: net Q equals net W
    st = (p1, V1, T1); net = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
    for kind, val in (("s", 0.5e-3), ("v", None), ("s", 2e-3), ("v", 300.0)):
        if val is None:
            val = st[2] + 400.0      # heat addition raising T by 400 K at minimum volume
        st, (Q, W, dU) = process(st, kind, val, m)
        net = [a + b for a, b in zip(net, (Q, W, dU))]
    print(f"Otto-like cycle: net Q = {net[0]:.2f} J, net W = {net[1]:.2f} J, net dU = {net[2]:.2e} J; back to T = {st[2]:.1f} K")
    assert abs(net[2]) < 1e-9 and abs(net[0] - net[1]) < 1e-9
```

:::exercise A rigid tank
A rigid 50 L tank holds air at 100 kPa and 20 °C. A 200 W heater runs for 2 minutes and the tank loses 4 kJ of heat to the room meanwhile. Find the final temperature and pressure.
---solution---
1. $m = pV/(RT) = 100\,000\cdot0.05/(287\cdot293.15) = 0.05943$ kg.
2. Net heat: $200\cdot120 - 4000 = 20\,000$ J. Rigid tank, no boundary work. The electric heater's input counts as electrical work done *on* the system, $W = -24$ kJ, with $Q = -4$ kJ. Either way $\Delta U = 20$ kJ.
3. $\Delta T = 20000/(0.05943\cdot718) = 468.7$ K, so $T_2 = 761.8$ K.
4. $p_2 = 100\cdot761.8/293.15 = 259.9$ kPa.
:::
