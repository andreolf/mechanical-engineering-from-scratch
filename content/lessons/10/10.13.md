Every jet engine and every gas turbine power station runs the same loop: squeeze air, burn fuel in it, expand the hot gas through a turbine that drives the compressor, and use what's left as thrust or shaft power. The ideal version is the **Brayton cycle**. Its main lesson is how much the compressor costs: often half of what the turbine makes, so component efficiencies matter enormously.

## The ideal cycle

1. 1→2: isentropic compression by the pressure ratio $r_p$.
2. 2→3: heat added at constant pressure (the combustor).
3. 3→4: isentropic expansion.
4. 4→1: heat rejected at constant pressure (exhaust).

With air as an ideal gas with constant $c_p$,

$$
\eta_\text{Brayton} = 1 - r_p^{(1-k)/k} .
$$

Like Otto, it rises with pressure ratio only.

## Real components

With isentropic efficiencies $\eta_c$ and $\eta_t$ the compressor needs more work and the turbine gives less:

$$
w_\text{net} = c_p(T_3 - T_4) - c_p(T_2 - T_1) .
$$

The **back-work ratio** $w_c/w_t$ is 40–60 % for gas turbines, compared with 1–2 % for steam plants, where the pump only pushes liquid. A few points of component efficiency therefore swing the net output a lot. Net work peaks at a moderate pressure ratio and efficiency at a higher one. Hotter turbine inlets (with blade cooling and single-crystal superalloys) raise both.

:::example A simple gas turbine
Air at 300 K, $r_p = 8$, turbine inlet 1400 K, $\eta_c = 0.85$, $\eta_t = 0.90$, $c_p = 1.005$ kJ/(kg K), $k = 1.3997$.

1. Ideal efficiency: $1 - 8^{-0.2856} = 0.4478$.
2. Real: $T_2 = 586.20$ K, $T_4 = 835.78$ K.
3. Compressor 287.64, turbine 567.04, net 279.40 kJ/kg; heat in 817.86 kJ/kg.
4. Efficiency 0.3416, back-work ratio 0.507. The energy balance closes: heat in minus net work equals heat rejected.
5. Sweeping $r_p$: net work peaks at $r_p = 9.3$ (280.6 kJ/kg); efficiency peaks at 22.6 (0.3977). For the ideal cycle, maximum work is at $(T_3/T_1)^{k/(2(k-1))} = 14.84$.
:::

```python file=brayton.py
"""Brayton cycle: ideal efficiency, and a real cycle with component efficiencies, net work and the best pressure ratio."""
CP, R = 1.005, 0.287
K = CP / (CP - R)


def ideal_eff(rp):
    return 1 - rp ** ((1 - K) / K)


def real_cycle(rp, T1=300.0, T3=1400.0, eta_c=0.85, eta_t=0.90):
    e = rp ** ((K - 1) / K)
    T2 = T1 + T1 * (e - 1) / eta_c
    T4 = T3 - eta_t * (T3 - T3 / e)
    wc, wt, q = CP * (T2 - T1), CP * (T3 - T4), CP * (T3 - T2)
    return dict(T2=T2, T4=T4, wc=wc, wt=wt, w=wt - wc, q=q, eta=(wt - wc) / q, bwr=wc / wt)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    c = real_cycle(8)
    print(f"ideal rp 8: {ideal_eff(8):.4f}")
    print(f"real rp 8: T2 {c['T2']:.2f} K, T4 {c['T4']:.2f} K, net {c['w']:.2f} kJ/kg, efficiency {c['eta']:.4f}, back-work ratio {c['bwr']:.3f}")
    assert abs(real_cycle(8, eta_c=1, eta_t=1)['eta'] - ideal_eff(8)) < 1e-12        # perfect parts give the ideal
    assert abs(c['q'] - c['w'] - CP * (c['T4'] - 300.0)) < 1e-9                      # energy balance closes
    rps = [1.5 + 0.1 * i for i in range(600)]
    best_w = max(rps, key=lambda r: real_cycle(r)['w']); best_e = max(rps, key=lambda r: real_cycle(r)['eta'])
    print(f"max net work at rp {best_w:.1f} ({real_cycle(best_w)['w']:.1f} kJ/kg); max efficiency at rp {best_e:.1f} ({real_cycle(best_e)['eta']:.4f})")
    print(f"ideal cycle, rp for max work: (T3/T1)^(k/(2(k-1))) = {(1400/300)**(K/(2*(K-1))):.2f}")
```

:::exercise A hotter turbine
Raise the turbine inlet temperature of the example to 1600 K and keep everything else. Find the efficiency, net work and back-work ratio, and the power from 100 kg/s of air in both cases.
---solution---
1. Compressor work is unchanged at 287.64 kJ/kg.
2. Turbine: $T_4 = 1600 - 0.9(1600 - 1600/1.8115) = 955.2$ K; $w_t = 648.05$ kJ/kg; net 360.41 kJ/kg.
3. Heat in $1.005(1600 - 586.20) = 1018.86$ kJ/kg; efficiency 0.3537; back-work ratio 0.444.
4. At 100 kg/s: 27.94 MW at 1400 K, 36.04 MW at 1600 K, 29 % more power from the same machine size.
:::
