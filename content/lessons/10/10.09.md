Mix hot and cold water in a shower and you get warm water. Energy is perfectly conserved, yet something has been lost: you can't unmix them, and you've thrown away the chance to run an engine between the hot and cold supplies. **Entropy** is the property that measures that loss. It increases in every real process and stays constant only in ideal reversible ones.

## Definition

For a reversible process, entropy changes by

$$
dS = \frac{\delta Q_\text{rev}}{T} .
$$

Entropy is a **property**: its change between two states is the same whatever the process, reversible or not. To calculate it, invent any convenient reversible path. For an incompressible liquid or solid with specific heat $c$,

$$
\Delta s = c\ln\frac{T_2}{T_1} .
$$

## Entropy generation

For a real process the balance gains a generation term that can't be negative:

$$
\Delta S_\text{system} = \int\frac{\delta Q}{T_b} + S_\text{gen}, \qquad S_\text{gen} \ge 0 ,
$$

where $T_b$ is the temperature at the boundary where the heat crosses. For a steady-flow device, $\sum\dot m_\text{out}s_\text{out} - \sum\dot m_\text{in}s_\text{in} = \sum\dot Q/T_b + \dot S_\text{gen}$.

Sources of generation: heat transfer across a finite temperature difference, friction, mixing, unrestrained expansion, electrical resistance. Engineering for efficiency is largely hunting these down.

## Mixing two streams

In an insulated mixer, the energy balance gives the exit temperature $T_3 = (\dot m_1T_1 + \dot m_2T_2)/(\dot m_1 + \dot m_2)$. The hot stream loses entropy, the cold one gains more than that, and

$$
\dot S_\text{gen} = \dot m_1c\ln\frac{T_3}{T_1} + \dot m_2c\ln\frac{T_3}{T_2} \ge 0 ,
$$

zero only when $T_1 = T_2$. Because $\ln$ is concave, the weighted log of the mean temperature is always larger than the weighted mean of the logs.

A shower mixer. The total bar, entropy generation, is never below zero.
:::

:::example A shower
0.08 kg/s of hot water at 60 °C mixes with 0.04 kg/s of cold at 15 °C; $c = 4.186$ kJ/(kg K).

1. Exit temperature: $(0.08\cdot333.15 + 0.04\cdot288.15)/0.12 = 318.15$ K $= 45.00$ °C.
2. Hot stream: $0.08\cdot4.186\ln(318.15/333.15) = -0.015428$ kW/K. Cold: $0.04\cdot4.186\ln(318.15/288.15) = +0.016584$ kW/K.
3. $\dot S_\text{gen} = 1.1557$ W/K. With surroundings at $T_0 = 288.15$ K the lost work potential is $T_0\dot S_\text{gen} = 333.01$ W.
4. 100 000 random mixings never give negative generation.
5. Heating 1 kg of water from 15 to 60 °C changes its entropy by 0.60744 kJ/K whatever the process. From a 60 °C reservoir, the reservoir loses $4.186\cdot45/333.15$, so 0.04202 kJ/K is generated.
:::

```python file=mixing.py
"""Entropy generation when two water streams mix (a shower mixer), and a check that it is never negative."""
import math
import numpy as np

C = 4.186   # kJ/(kg K), liquid water treated as incompressible


def mix(m1, T1, m2, T2):
    """Adiabatic steady mixing: exit temperature (K) and entropy generation rate (kW/K) for flows in kg/s."""
    T3 = (m1 * T1 + m2 * T2) / (m1 + m2)
    Sgen = m1 * C * math.log(T3 / T1) + m2 * C * math.log(T3 / T2)
    return T3, Sgen


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a shower: 0.08 kg/s hot at 60 C and 0.04 kg/s cold at 15 C
    T3, S = mix(0.08, 333.15, 0.04, 288.15)
    print(f"shower: mixed at {T3-273.15:.2f} C, entropy generated {S*1000:.4f} W/K")
    T0 = 288.15
    print(f"  lost work potential T0 x Sgen = {T0*S*1000:.2f} W")
    # the second law: never negative, zero only when T1 = T2
    rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
    for _ in range(100000):
        m1, m2 = rng.uniform(0.01, 5, 2); T1, T2 = rng.uniform(275, 370, 2)
        assert mix(m1, T1, m2, T2)[1] >= -1e-15
    assert abs(mix(1, 300, 2, 300)[1]) < 1e-15
    print("100 000 random mixings: S_gen >= 0 every time")
    # entropy is a property: heating 1 kg of water 15 -> 60 C reversibly or by stirring gives the same Delta S
    dS = C * math.log(333.15 / 288.15)
    print(f"heating 1 kg of water from 15 to 60 C: delta S = {dS:.5f} kJ/K, whatever the process")
    # but stirring (work turned to heat) generates all of it; reversible heating from a reservoir at each T generates none
    print(f"from a 60 C reservoir instead: S_gen = {dS - C*45/333.15:.5f} kJ/K")
    assert dS - C * 45 / 333.15 > 0
```

:::exercise An insulated bucket
Pour 1 kg of water at 80 °C into 2 kg at 20 °C in an insulated bucket. Find the final temperature, the entropy generated and the lost work with surroundings at 20 °C.
---solution---
1. $T_3 = (353.15 + 2\cdot293.15)/3 = 313.15$ K $= 40$ °C.
2. Hot: $4.186\ln(313.15/353.15) = -0.50320$ kJ/K. Cold: $2\cdot4.186\ln(313.15/293.15) = +0.55253$ kJ/K.
3. $S_\text{gen} = 0.04933$ kJ/K, positive as it must be.
4. Lost work $T_0S_\text{gen} = 293.15\cdot0.04933 = 14.46$ kJ: an ideal engine running between the two buckets could have produced that before they reached 40 °C.
:::
