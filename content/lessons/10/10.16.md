The ideal gas law works beautifully for air at room conditions. Squeeze CO2 into a fire extinguisher or a supercritical-CO2 power loop and it fails badly: molecules take up space and attract each other. In 1873 Johannes van der Waals added two corrections to $pv = RT$. His equation is too crude for design work, but it's the simplest model that predicts condensation and a critical point, and the ideas behind modern cubic equations of state come straight from it.

## The equation

Per mole,

$$
p = \frac{RT}{v - b} - \frac{a}{v^2} .
$$

- $b$ is the volume the molecules themselves occupy, so the free volume is $v - b$.
- $a/v^2$ is the pressure lost to attraction between molecules, which grows as they crowd together.

For CO2, $a = 0.3640$ Pa m⁶/mol² and $b = 4.267\times10^{-5}$ m³/mol. The **compressibility factor** $Z = pv/RT$ measures departure from ideal behaviour. As $p \to 0$, $v$ grows huge, both corrections vanish and $Z \to 1$.

## Solving for volume

Given $p$ and $T$, the equation is a **cubic** in $v$:

$$
p v^3 - (pb + RT)v^2 + a v - ab = 0 .
$$

Above the critical temperature it has one real root. Below it, in a pressure range, it has three: liquid (smallest), vapour (largest), and an unstable middle one.

## The critical point and Maxwell's construction

The critical isotherm has a horizontal inflection: $T_c = 8a/(27Rb)$, $p_c = a/(27b^2)$. Below $T_c$ the isotherm wiggles, which a real fluid never does. Maxwell showed that the real saturation pressure is the horizontal line that cuts off **equal areas** above and below the loop, because liquid and vapour in equilibrium must have equal Gibbs energy.

:::example CO2
1. Critical point from $a$ and $b$: 304.00 K and 7.404 MPa (real CO2: 304.13 K, 7.377 MPa). The model predicts $Z_c = 3/8 = 0.375$; real CO2 has 0.274, one sign of its limits.
2. At 350 K: $Z$ = 0.99997 at 1 kPa, 0.99716 at 0.1 MPa, 0.97107 at 1 MPa and 0.83972 at 5 MPa, where $v = 0.48873$ L/mol against 0.58201 for an ideal gas.
3. Maxwell construction at 280 K: $p_\text{sat} = 5.2843$ MPa (real CO2: 4.160 MPa), liquid 80.88 and vapour 265.4 cm³/mol.
:::

```python file=vdw.py
"""Van der Waals gas: solve the cubic for molar volume, compressibility, and the Maxwell equal-area construction for CO2."""
import numpy as np

R = 8.314462618
A, B = 0.3640, 4.267e-5          # CO2: Pa m^6/mol^2, m^3/mol


def p_vdw(v, T):
    return R * T / (v - B) - A / v ** 2


def volumes(p, T):
    """Real roots v > b of p v^3 - (p b + R T) v^2 + a v - a b = 0, sorted."""
    r = np.roots([p, -(p * B + R * T), A, -A * B])
    return sorted(x.real for x in r if abs(x.imag) < 1e-9 * abs(x) and x.real > B)


def area_mismatch(p, T):
    """Integral of (p_vdw - p) dv between the liquid and vapour roots; zero at the saturation pressure."""
    vl, _, vg = volumes(p, T)
    integral = R * T * np.log((vg - B) / (vl - B)) + A * (1 / vg - 1 / vl)
    return integral - p * (vg - vl)


def p_sat(T, lo, hi):
    for _ in range(100):                         # bisection on the equal-area condition
        mid = (lo + hi) / 2
        if area_mismatch(mid, T) > 0: lo = mid
        else: hi = mid
    return (lo + hi) / 2


if __name__ == "__main__":
    Tc, pc, vc = 8 * A / (27 * R * B), A / (27 * B * B), 3 * B
    print(f"vdW critical point of CO2: {Tc:.2f} K, {pc/1e6:.3f} MPa, Zc = {pc*vc/(R*Tc):.4f} (real CO2: 304.13 K, 7.377 MPa, 0.274)")
    for p in (1e3, 1e5, 1e6, 5e6):
        v = volumes(p, 350.0)[-1]
        print(f"350 K, {p/1e6:8.3f} MPa: v = {v*1e3:.5f} L/mol, Z = {p*v/(R*350):.5f}")
    assert abs(p_vdw(volumes(5e6, 350.0)[-1], 350.0) - 5e6) < 1e-3
    assert abs(1e3 * volumes(1e3, 350.0)[-1] / (R * 350) - 1) < 1e-4                  # Z -> 1 at low pressure
    T = 280.0
    # bracket: the spinodal pressures (local min and max of the isotherm)
    vs = np.linspace(1.05 * B, 30 * B, 50000); ps = p_vdw(vs, T)
    turn = np.where(np.diff(np.sign(np.diff(ps))) != 0)[0] + 1        # local minimum, then local maximum
    lo, hi = max(ps[turn[0]], 1.0) * 1.0001, ps[turn[1]] * 0.9999
    ps_ = p_sat(T, lo, hi)
    vl, _, vg = volumes(ps_, T)
    print(f"Maxwell construction at {T:.0f} K: p_sat = {ps_/1e6:.4f} MPa (real CO2: 4.160 MPa); liquid {vl*1e6:.2f}, vapour {vg*1e6:.1f} cm^3/mol")
    assert abs(area_mismatch(ps_, T)) < 1e-6
```

:::exercise A CO2 cylinder
A 10 litre cylinder holds 2 kg of CO2 at 350 K (above critical, so one phase). Find the pressure from the ideal gas law and from van der Waals.
---solution---
1. Moles: $2/0.04401 = 45.44$ mol, so $v = 0.010/45.44 = 2.2005\times10^{-4}$ m³/mol.
2. Ideal: $p = RT/v = 13.22$ MPa.
3. Van der Waals: $p = \dfrac{8.3145\cdot350}{2.2005\times10^{-4} - 4.267\times10^{-5}} - \dfrac{0.3640}{(2.2005\times10^{-4})^2} = 8.89$ MPa.
4. The ideal gas law overestimates by about 49 %. For a pressure vessel design you'd use an accurate equation of state such as Span-Wagner, but van der Waals points the right way.
:::
