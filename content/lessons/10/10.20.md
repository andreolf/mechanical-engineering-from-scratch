Switzerland gets much of its electricity from water, and the water arrives at the wrong time: snowmelt fills rivers and lakes in summer, while demand peaks in winter. Phasing out nuclear and adding solar, which also peaks in summer, widens that gap. This lesson puts the thermodynamics of the phase together, energy balances, storage and conversion efficiencies, into a national-scale monthly model. **All numbers here are assumed round values of roughly the right size, for illustration, not official statistics.**

## The model

Each source and the demand get an annual total, in TWh, and a seasonal shape: twelve monthly weights $1 + A\cos\big(2\pi(m - m_\text{peak})/12\big)$, which average 1.

| Item (assumed) | TWh/year | Seasonal swing | Peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand | 60 | ±15 % | January |
| Run-of-river hydro | 18 | ±45 % | July |
| Natural inflow to storage lakes | 20 | ±80 % | July |
| Nuclear | 18 or 0 | ±10 % | January |
| Other (biomass, waste, wind) | 4 | none | |
| Solar PV | variable | ±60 % | July |

## Balances

For each month, net = supply − demand. The annual sum is the net export (or, if negative, import). The **seasonal storage** needed is the largest swing in the cumulative level after taking out the annual average:

$$
L_m = \sum_{k \le m}\left(\text{net}_k - \frac{\text{annual}}{12}\right), \qquad \text{storage} = \max_m L_m - \min_m L_m .
$$

The storage lakes, worth roughly 8.8 TWh, already do exactly this with the natural inflow. Anything beyond them has to come from imports, new storage, or winter-heavy production.

:::widget swissseason
Monthly surplus and deficit with the seasonal storage level. Add solar or switch nuclear off and watch the storage requirement.
:::

:::example Four scenarios
1. Today-like (nuclear on, 6 TWh PV): +6.00 TWh a year but −7.85 TWh in the winter half; storage needed 10.85 TWh, 2.05 beyond the lakes.
2. Nuclear off, 6 TWh PV: −12.00 TWh a year, −17.41 in winter.
3. Nuclear off, 24 TWh PV: the year is back to +6.00 TWh, but winter is −11.77 and storage needed rises to 14.77 TWh, 5.97 beyond the lakes.
4. Nuclear off, 34 TWh PV: +16.00 TWh a year, yet still 7.84 TWh beyond the lakes.
5. In every case supply minus net export equals demand, and the storage level returns to its starting value.
:::

```python file=swissbalance.py
"""Swiss seasonal electricity balance with clearly ASSUMED round-number data: monthly demand and supply, the winter gap,
and how much seasonal storage would close it. Illustrative only, not official statistics."""
import math

MONTHS = "Jan Feb Mar Apr May Jun Jul Aug Sep Oct Nov Dec".split()


def shape(amplitude, peak_month):
    """Twelve monthly weights averaging 1, with a cosine peak at peak_month (0 = January)."""
    return [1 + amplitude * math.cos(2 * math.pi * (m - peak_month) / 12) for m in range(12)]


def monthly(annual, amplitude, peak):
    return [annual / 12 * w for w in shape(amplitude, peak)]


# ASSUMED annual values, TWh (round numbers of roughly the right size for Switzerland)
DEMAND = monthly(60.0, 0.15, 0)           # winter peak
RUN_OF_RIVER = monthly(18.0, 0.45, 6)     # snowmelt in early summer
LAKE_INFLOW = monthly(20.0, 0.80, 6)      # natural inflow to storage lakes, mostly summer
NUCLEAR = monthly(18.0, 0.10, 0)          # refuelling outages in summer
OTHER = monthly(4.0, 0.0, 0)              # biomass, waste, wind


def balance(pv_twh, nuclear=True, lake_capacity=8.8):
    pv = monthly(pv_twh, 0.60, 6)
    nuc = NUCLEAR if nuclear else [0.0] * 12
    supply = [RUN_OF_RIVER[m] + LAKE_INFLOW[m] + nuc[m] + OTHER[m] + pv[m] for m in range(12)]
    net = [supply[m] - DEMAND[m] for m in range(12)]
    annual = sum(net)
    # storage that would shift every month's surplus to cover the deficits, with any annual shortfall imported evenly
    level, levels = 0.0, []
    for m in range(12):
        level += net[m] - annual / 12
        levels.append(level)
    storage_needed = max(levels) - min(levels)
    winter = sum(net[m] for m in (0, 1, 2, 9, 10, 11))
    return dict(net=net, annual=annual, storage=storage_needed, winter=winter, beyond_lakes=max(0.0, storage_needed - lake_capacity),
                supply=supply, levels=levels)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    assert abs(sum(DEMAND) - 60.0) < 1e-9 and abs(sum(LAKE_INFLOW) - 20.0) < 1e-9
    for label, kw in (("today-like: nuclear, 6 TWh PV", dict(pv_twh=6)), ("no nuclear, 6 TWh PV", dict(pv_twh=6, nuclear=False)),
                      ("no nuclear, 24 TWh PV", dict(pv_twh=24, nuclear=False)), ("no nuclear, 34 TWh PV", dict(pv_twh=34, nuclear=False))):
        r = balance(**kw)
        print(f"{label}: annual net {r['annual']:+.2f} TWh, winter half {r['winter']:+.2f} TWh, "
              f"seasonal storage needed {r['storage']:.2f} TWh ({r['beyond_lakes']:.2f} beyond the 8.8 TWh of lakes)")
        # the balance closes: supply + imports - exports = demand, and the storage level returns to its start
        assert abs(sum(r['supply']) - r['annual'] - sum(DEMAND)) < 1e-9 and abs(r['levels'][-1]) < 1e-9
    r = balance(24, nuclear=False)
    print("month   net TWh   storage level TWh")
    for m in range(12):
        print(f"  {MONTHS[m]}  {r['net'][m]:+6.2f}   {r['levels'][m]:+6.2f}")
```

:::exercise Hydrogen as seasonal storage
In scenario 3 the extra 5.97 TWh of storage beyond the lakes is to come from summer electricity turned into hydrogen and back into winter electricity, with a 35 % round trip. How much summer electricity does that take, and how does it compare with pumped storage?
---solution---
1. Summer input: $5.97/0.35 = 17.06$ TWh, nearly as much as the extra solar added between scenarios 2 and 3.
2. Losses: $17.06 - 5.97 = 11.09$ TWh, mostly as heat in electrolysers and fuel cells or turbines. That heat could go into district heating to raise the useful output.
3. Pumped storage returns about 79 % (lesson 10.07), but storing 6 TWh needs huge lakes: with the 300 m head of that example, about 7.3 km³ of water. That's why the real Swiss plan mixes more winter production (Alpine solar, wind), raised dams, imports and some power-to-gas.
:::
