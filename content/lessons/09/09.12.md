On 16 January 1943 the tanker SS Schenectady, moored in calm water, split in two with a bang heard a mile away. The welded steel was well below its yield stress. The cause was a small crack in a brittle weld on a cold night. Strength alone doesn't tell you whether a part will break: you need to know how it tolerates cracks. That's fracture mechanics.

## Griffith's energy balance

A crack of length $2a$ in a plate under stress $\sigma$ releases stored elastic energy roughly from a region of size $a$ around it, $\pi\sigma^2a^2/E$ per unit thickness. Making the crack costs surface energy $4a\gamma$ (two faces, two tips). The total

$$
U(a) = 4a\gamma - \frac{\pi\sigma^2 a^2}{E}
$$

rises, peaks, and falls. Beyond the peak, growing the crack releases more energy than it costs, so it runs. The peak is at

$$
a^* = \frac{2E\gamma}{\pi\sigma^2} .
$$

## Stress intensity

Near any crack tip the stresses go as $1/\sqrt r$ with a strength set by one number, the **stress intensity factor**:

$$
K = Y\sigma\sqrt{\pi a} ,
$$

with $Y$ a geometry factor (1 for a central crack in a wide plate, 1.12 for an edge crack). Fast fracture happens when $K$ reaches the material's **fracture toughness** $K_{IC}$. So the critical crack size is

$$
a_c = \frac1\pi\left(\frac{K_{IC}}{Y\sigma}\right)^2 .
$$

Some typical values of $K_{IC}$ in MPa√m: glass 0.75, aluminium 7075-T6 about 25, structural steel 50 or more (much less when cold), tough pressure-vessel steel 150.

## Using it

- Inspection: the smallest crack you can reliably find must be well below $a_c$.
- Halving the stress quadruples the tolerable crack.
- **Leak before break**: a pressure vessel should be tough enough that a crack grows right through the wall, and leaks visibly, while still subcritical.

:::example Glass and steel
1. Glass with $E = 70$ GPa and $\gamma = 1$ J/m² at 50 MPa: scanning $U(a)$ numerically puts the peak at a half-length of 17.83 µm, matching $2E\gamma/(\pi\sigma^2)$. The equivalent $K_{IC} = \sqrt{2E\gamma} = 0.374$ MPa√m. Real glass is about twice that.
2. Edge cracks at 300 MPa ($Y = 1.12$): critical depth 7.05 mm in structural steel ($K_{IC} = 50$), 63.4 mm in pressure-vessel steel (150) and 1.76 mm in 7075-T6 aluminium (25).
3. A vessel with a 12 mm wall and $K_{IC} = 150$: a through crack ($a = 6$ mm) stays subcritical up to $150/\sqrt{\pi\cdot0.006} = 1093$ MPa. That's far above the 400 MPa yield, so it leaks before it breaks.
:::

```python file=fracture.py
"""Fracture mechanics: Griffith's energy balance, stress intensity, critical crack size and leak-before-break."""
import math
import numpy as np


def K(sigma, a, Y=1.0):
    return Y * sigma * math.sqrt(math.pi * a)


def a_crit(KIC, sigma, Y=1.0):
    return (KIC / (Y * sigma))**2 / math.pi


def griffith_energy(a, sigma, E, gamma):
    """Energy per unit thickness of a through crack of length 2a in a large plate (plane stress)."""
    return -math.pi * sigma**2 * a**2 / E + 4 * a * gamma


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # glass: Griffith balance found by scanning the energy curve
    E, gamma, sigma = 70e9, 1.0, 50e6
    a = np.linspace(1e-9, 1e-4, 200001); U = -math.pi * sigma**2 * a**2 / E + 4 * a * gamma
    a_num = a[np.argmax(U)]; a_formula = 2 * E * gamma / (math.pi * sigma**2)
    print(f"glass at 50 MPa: energy peaks at half-length {a_num*1e6:.2f} um (formula 2 E gamma / (pi sigma^2) = {a_formula*1e6:.2f} um)")
    assert abs(a_num - a_formula) < 1e-9
    print(f"  equivalent K_IC = sqrt(2 E gamma) = {math.sqrt(2*E*gamma)/1e6:.3f} MPa sqrt(m)")
    assert abs(K(sigma, a_formula) - math.sqrt(2 * E * gamma)) < 1e-6
    # steel plate with an edge crack
    for KIC, name in ((50e6, "structural steel"), (150e6, "tough pressure-vessel steel"), (25e6, "7075-T6 aluminium")):
        ac = a_crit(KIC, 300e6, Y=1.12)
        print(f"{name} (K_IC {KIC/1e6:.0f}) at 300 MPa, edge crack: critical depth {ac*1e3:.2f} mm")
    assert abs(K(300e6, a_crit(50e6, 300e6, 1.12), 1.12) - 50e6) < 1e-3
    # leak-before-break: a crack through the wall t must still be subcritical (2a = t)
    t, KIC, sy = 0.012, 150e6, 400e6
    sigma_max = KIC / math.sqrt(math.pi * t / 2)
    print(f"vessel wall 12 mm, K_IC 150: leaks before it breaks while hoop stress < {sigma_max/1e6:.0f} MPa (yield {sy/1e6:.0f})")
    assert sigma_max > sy
```

:::exercise A cold weld
A steel's toughness falls to 20 MPa√m at −20 °C. A plate carries 150 MPa and has an edge crack ($Y = 1.12$). What crack depth causes fracture?
---solution---
1. $a_c = \frac1\pi\left(\frac{20}{1.12\cdot150}\right)^2 = \frac1\pi(0.1190)^2$.
2. $a_c = 0.01417/\pi = 4.51\times10^{-3}$ m, so 4.5 mm.
3. A crack that size in a weld is easy to miss. That's how the Liberty ships failed, and why modern steels are specified with toughness at the lowest service temperature.
:::
