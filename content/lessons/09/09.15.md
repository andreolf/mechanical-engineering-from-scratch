Why are bike frames made of steel, aluminium, titanium, carbon fibre and even bamboo, but never concrete? Why are aircraft wing skins aluminium or composite, and roof beams wood or steel? Choosing a material isn't about finding the "strongest". It's about which material does a specific job at the lowest mass or cost. Mike Ashby's method turns that into a ranking with one number.

## Performance indices

Write the quantity to minimise (mass) in terms of the design requirement (stiffness), the fixed geometry (length) and the free variable (cross-section size). Then eliminate the free variable.

**A light stiff tie**, length $L$, needing axial stiffness $S = EA/L$: the area must be $A = SL/E$, so

$$
m = \rho A L = S L^2\frac{\rho}{E} .
$$

Maximise $E/\rho$.

**A light stiff beam** with a square section of side $b$, needing bending stiffness $\propto EI/L^3$ with $I = b^4/12$: $b^2 \propto (S L^3/E)^{1/2}$, so

$$
m = \rho b^2 L \propto \frac{\rho}{E^{1/2}} .
$$

Maximise $E^{1/2}/\rho$. Low density counts for more, because a lighter material can be made thicker, and stiffness grows faster than area.

**A light stiff panel** (fixed width and length, free thickness): maximise $E^{1/3}/\rho$.

For strength-limited beams the index is $\sigma_f^{2/3}/\rho$; for cost, replace $\rho$ by $\rho C_m$ (price per kg).

## Ashby charts

On log-log axes of $E$ against $\rho$, each index is a straight line: $E/\rho = C$ has slope 1, $E^{1/2}/\rho = C$ has slope 2, $E^{1/3}/\rho = C$ has slope 3. All materials on one line perform equally. Slide the line up: the materials still above it are the best.

An Ashby chart of modulus against density. Pick the design and move the guideline: materials above it (lime) outperform those below.
:::

:::example A 1 m cantilever
A 1 m square-section cantilever must deflect no more than 5 mm under 1 kN at the tip. Size it in each material ($I = PL^3/(3E\delta)$, $b = (12I)^{1/4}$) and weigh it:

1. Spruce 3.67 kg, bamboo 4.67, CFRP (quasi-isotropic, 70 GPa) 5.27, aluminium 9.13, GFRP 10.75, titanium 11.87, concrete 12.39, steel 15.32, ABS 19.58.
2. The order is exactly the ranking by $E^{1/2}/\rho$, and mass times index is the same constant for every material to $10^{-12}$.
3. By cost (rough prices per kg): bamboo 4.67 USD, spruce 5.51, steel 12.26, aluminium 22.82, CFRP 211.
4. Ties ($E/\rho$) rank differently: CFRP, then steel, spruce and aluminium almost tied. Panels ($E^{1/3}/\rho$): spruce, bamboo, CFRP, GFRP.
5. Concrete looks fine for a stiff beam but its tensile strength is only 4 MPa, so it fails the strength check. Real selection always screens several requirements.
:::

```python file=ashby.py
"""Material selection: performance indices for light, stiff and strong parts, checked by actually sizing a beam in each material."""
import math

DB = {   # E (GPa), density (kg/m^3), yield or failure strength (MPa), price (USD/kg, rough)
    "steel":        (210, 7850, 350, 0.8),
    "aluminium":    (70, 2700, 250, 2.5),
    "titanium":     (115, 4500, 900, 20.0),
    "CFRP":         (70, 1560, 600, 40.0),
    "GFRP":         (25, 1900, 300, 6.0),
    "bamboo":       (18, 700, 120, 1.0),
    "spruce":       (12, 450, 70, 1.5),
    "concrete":     (30, 2400, 4, 0.1),
    "ABS":          (2.3, 1050, 40, 2.5),
}


def index(name, kind):
    E, rho, s, _ = DB[name]; E *= 1e9; s *= 1e6
    return {"tie": E / rho, "beam": math.sqrt(E) / rho, "plate": E ** (1 / 3) / rho, "strong beam": s ** (2 / 3) / rho}[kind]


def beam_mass(name, L=1.0, P=1000.0, delta=0.005):
    """Square-section cantilever of length L sized so the tip deflects delta under P. Returns mass in kg."""
    E, rho, _, _ = DB[name]; E *= 1e9
    I = P * L**3 / (3 * E * delta)       # needed second moment
    b = (12 * I) ** 0.25                 # square side
    return rho * b * b * L


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("light stiff beam, E^1/2 / rho, and the mass of a 1 m cantilever deflecting 5 mm under 1 kN:")
    order = sorted(DB, key=lambda m: -index(m, "beam"))
    for m in order:
        print(f"  {m:10s} index {index(m, 'beam'):.2f}   mass {beam_mass(m):.2f} kg   cost {beam_mass(m)*DB[m][3]:.2f} USD")
    # mass is exactly proportional to 1/index
    k = [beam_mass(m) * index(m, "beam") for m in DB]
    assert max(k) / min(k) - 1 < 1e-12
    assert order == sorted(DB, key=beam_mass)
    print("ties ranked by E/rho: " + ", ".join(sorted(DB, key=lambda m: -index(m, "tie"))[:4]))
    print("panels ranked by E^1/3/rho: " + ", ".join(sorted(DB, key=lambda m: -index(m, "plate"))[:4]))
```

:::exercise Steel against aluminium
For a light stiff beam, how much heavier is steel ($E = 210$ GPa, $\rho = 7850$ kg/m³) than aluminium ($E = 70$ GPa, $\rho = 2700$ kg/m³)? And for a tie?
---solution---
1. Beam: mass ratio $= (\rho_s/\rho_a)(E_a/E_s)^{1/2} = 2.907\cdot0.5774 = 1.68$.
2. Tie: $(\rho_s/\rho_a)(E_a/E_s) = 2.907/3 = 0.969$. Steel is slightly lighter as a tie.
3. Bending favours low density, which is why aluminium wins for frames and panels, while steel cables are fine.
:::
