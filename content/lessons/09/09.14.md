Leave a steel bolt on a beach in Bali and in a few months it's orange and flaking. Corrosion costs industrial countries around 3 % of GDP a year: rusted bridges, leaking pipes, failed fasteners. It's electrochemistry. Metal atoms give up electrons and dissolve as ions at an **anode**; somewhere nearby, a **cathode** consumes those electrons, usually by reducing oxygen dissolved in water. Salt water makes a good electrolyte to carry the ions, which is why the coast is so harsh.

## Faraday's law

Each dissolved atom of valence $n$ releases $n$ electrons, so a current $I$ flowing for time $t$ removes a mass

$$
m = \frac{I t M}{n F} ,
$$

with $M$ the molar mass and $F = 96\,485$ C/mol. Divide by density and area and you get the **penetration rate** from the corrosion current density $i$:

$$
\dot x = \frac{i M}{n F \rho} .
$$

For steel, 1 µA/cm² means about 0.012 mm per year.

## Galvanic corrosion

Two different metals in electrical contact in an electrolyte form a battery. The more active one (lower in the **galvanic series**: magnesium, zinc, aluminium, steel, then copper, stainless steel, titanium, gold) becomes the anode and corrodes faster. The cathode is protected.

The current is limited mostly by how much oxygen the cathode can reduce, so a **large cathode and small anode** is the worst case: the whole current crowds into a little anode. Steel rivets in a copper sheet fail quickly; copper rivets in a steel sheet are fine.

## Protection

- Barriers: paint, galvanising (zinc coating), anodising aluminium.
- **Sacrificial anodes**: blocks of zinc or aluminium bolted to a boat hull or buried pipeline. They corrode in place of the steel and are replaced periodically.
- Impressed current from a power supply, for large structures.
- Design: avoid crevices and water traps, insulate dissimilar metals, choose stainless steel or aluminium where appropriate.

:::widget galvanic
A steel anode coupled to a large copper cathode. The cathode fixes the current; shrink the anode and watch the wall disappear.
:::

:::example Rates and anode sizing
1. 1 A for a year dissolves $1\cdot3.156\times10^7\cdot55.845/(2\cdot96485) = 9.13$ kg of iron.
2. At 1 µA/cm²: iron thins by 11.6 µm per year, zinc by 15.0, aluminium by 10.9, copper by 11.6.
3. A 2 mA galvanic current into a 1000 cm² steel anode is 2 µA/cm², 0.023 mm per year. The same current into a 10 cm² anode is 200 µA/cm², 2.32 mm per year: 100 times faster.
4. A boat hull needs 0.3 A of protection for 2 years. Zinc consumed is $0.3\cdot6.31\times10^7\cdot65.38/(2\cdot96485) = 6.42$ kg. Allowing 90 % current efficiency and using only 85 % of the anode before replacement, fit 8.4 kg.
:::

```python file=corrosion.py
"""Corrosion: Faraday's law for metal loss, penetration rate from current density, galvanic area effects and sacrificial anode sizing."""
F = 96485.0   # C/mol

METALS = {   # molar mass g/mol, valence, density kg/m^3
    "iron": (55.845, 2, 7870.0),
    "zinc": (65.38, 2, 7140.0),
    "aluminium": (26.98, 3, 2700.0),
    "copper": (63.55, 2, 8960.0),
}


def mass_loss(I, t, metal):
    """Grams dissolved by current I (A) flowing for t seconds."""
    M, n, _ = METALS[metal]
    return I * t * M / (n * F)


def penetration_mm_per_year(i_uA_cm2, metal):
    """Uniform thinning rate from a corrosion current density in microamps per square centimetre."""
    M, n, rho = METALS[metal]
    i = i_uA_cm2 * 1e-6 / 1e-4                  # A/m^2
    v = i * (M / 1000) / (n * F * rho)          # m/s
    return v * 3600 * 24 * 365.25 * 1000


if __name__ == "__main__":
    yr = 365.25 * 24 * 3600
    print(f"1 A for a year dissolves {mass_loss(1, yr, 'iron')/1000:.2f} kg of iron")
    for m in METALS:
        print(f"  {m}: 1 uA/cm^2 thins it by {penetration_mm_per_year(1, m)*1000:.2f} um per year")
    assert abs(penetration_mm_per_year(1, "iron") - 0.0116) < 2e-4
    # galvanic couple: the same total current flows through anode and cathode, so a small anode corrodes fast
    I = 2e-3   # A, set by the cathode's oxygen reduction
    for anode_cm2 in (1000, 10):
        i = I / anode_cm2 * 1e6
        print(f"steel anode of {anode_cm2} cm^2 coupled to a big copper cathode: {i:.0f} uA/cm^2, {penetration_mm_per_year(i, 'iron'):.3f} mm/year")
    # sacrificial zinc anode for a boat hull drawing 0.3 A, 2 years, 90 % efficiency, use 85 % of the anode
    need = mass_loss(0.3, 2 * yr, "zinc") / 0.90 / 0.85 / 1000
    print(f"zinc anode for 0.3 A over 2 years: {need:.1f} kg")
    assert abs(mass_loss(1, F * 2, "iron") - 55.845) < 1e-9   # 2 faradays dissolve one mole of Fe2+
```

:::exercise An aluminium hull
An aluminium boat hull is protected by sacrificial aluminium-zinc-indium alloy anodes, which have a capacity of about 2500 A h/kg. How many kilograms are needed for 0.2 A over 3 years?
---solution---
1. Charge: $0.2\cdot3\cdot8766 = 5260$ A h.
2. Mass: $5260/2500 = 2.1$ kg.
3. Compare zinc: about 780 A h/kg in practice, so 6.7 kg. Aluminium alloy anodes are lighter for the same protection, which is why they're popular on boats.
:::
