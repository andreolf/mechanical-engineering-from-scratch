Every kilogram of material has already cost energy before you use it: mining, refining, smelting, rolling. Making primary aluminium from bauxite takes around 200 MJ/kg, mostly electricity for electrolysis. Recycling it takes about 30. Steel takes about 25, sawn timber under 10. As grids decarbonise and emissions get priced, engineers increasingly have to choose materials for their whole life, not just their performance.

## Embodied energy and carbon

The **embodied energy** $H_m$ (MJ/kg) and **embodied carbon** $c_m$ (kg CO₂ per kg) of a material are the totals from cradle to factory gate. Typical rough values:

| Material | MJ/kg | kg CO₂/kg |
|---|---|---|
| Steel | 25 | 2.0 |
| Primary aluminium | 200 | 12 |
| Recycled aluminium | 30 | 2 |
| Titanium | 600 | 40 |
| CFRP | 270 | 25 |
| Bamboo | 3 | 0.4 |
| Spruce | 8 | 0.5 |
| Concrete | 1.1 | 0.13 |

They vary with the source and the electricity mix, so treat them as factors of two, not three significant figures.

## Selecting for low energy

Replace density by $\rho H_m$ in the performance index (09.15). For a light stiff beam, **minimum energy** means maximising

$$
\frac{E^{1/2}}{\rho H_m} .
$$

## The use phase

For anything that moves, mass costs energy throughout its life. A car consumes roughly 0.35 L of fuel per 100 km for every extra 100 kg. Over 200 000 km that's about 240 MJ for every kilogram it carries. Lightweighting pays back when the fuel saved exceeds the extra production energy:

$$
\Delta m\cdot E_\text{use} > m_\text{new}H_\text{new} - m_\text{old}H_\text{old} .
$$

A full life-cycle assessment adds manufacturing, transport and end of life (recycling credit or landfill).

:::example The cantilever and a car panel
1. Cantilever (09.15) energies: steel 15.32 kg and 383 MJ; primary aluminium 9.13 kg but 1826 MJ; recycled aluminium 274 MJ; titanium 7121 MJ; CFRP 1424 MJ; bamboo 14 MJ; spruce 29 MJ.
2. Carbon: steel 30.6 kg CO₂, primary aluminium 110, CFRP 132, spruce 1.8, bamboo 1.9.
3. Energy is exactly proportional to $\rho H_m/E^{1/2}$ across all the materials, as the index predicts.
4. Car panel: 10 kg of steel replaced by aluminium sized for equal panel stiffness, $10/2.016 = 4.96$ kg. Production goes from 250 to 992 MJ (742 MJ more). Fuel saved over 200 000 km: $5.04\cdot239 = 1206$ MJ, a net gain of 464 MJ. Payback after 123 000 km.
5. With recycled aluminium, production drops to 149 MJ, 101 MJ less than steel, so the lighter panel is ahead from the first kilometre.
:::

```python file=embodied.py
"""Embodied energy and carbon: the 09.15 cantilever in each material, and when lightweighting a car part pays back its production energy."""
import math

#            E GPa, rho, MJ/kg, kg CO2/kg (rough cradle-to-gate values, primary production)
DB = {
    "steel":     (210, 7850, 25.0, 2.0),
    "aluminium": (70, 2700, 200.0, 12.0),
    "recycled Al": (70, 2700, 30.0, 2.0),
    "titanium":  (115, 4500, 600.0, 40.0),
    "CFRP":      (70, 1560, 270.0, 25.0),
    "bamboo":    (18, 700, 3.0, 0.4),
    "spruce":    (12, 450, 8.0, 0.5),
}


def beam_mass(name, L=1.0, P=1000.0, delta=0.005):
    E, rho, _, _ = DB[name]; I = P * L**3 / (3 * E * 1e9 * delta); b = (12 * I) ** 0.25
    return rho * b * b * L


def use_phase_MJ_per_kg(km=200000, L_per_100km_per_100kg=0.35, MJ_per_L=34.2):
    """Fuel energy to carry one extra kilogram over the car's life."""
    return km / 100 * L_per_100km_per_100kg / 100 * MJ_per_L


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("1 m cantilever, 5 mm under 1 kN:")
    res = {}
    for m, (E, rho, H, co2) in DB.items():
        mass = beam_mass(m); res[m] = (mass, mass * H, mass * co2)
        print(f"  {m:12s} {mass:6.2f} kg  {mass*H:7.1f} MJ  {mass*co2:6.2f} kg CO2")
    # stiffness-limited beam: energy index is E^1/2 / (rho H)
    for m in DB:
        E, rho, H, _ = DB[m]
        assert abs(res[m][1] * math.sqrt(E * 1e9) / (rho * H) / (res["steel"][1] * math.sqrt(210e9) / (7850 * 25)) - 1) < 1e-12
    u = use_phase_MJ_per_kg()
    print(f"carrying 1 kg in a car for 200 000 km costs {u:.0f} MJ of fuel")
    # replace a 10 kg steel panel by aluminium with 1.68x less mass (09.15 beam ratio)... panel index ratio is 2.02
    ms = 10.0; ma = ms / ((7850 / 2700) * (70 / 210) ** (1 / 3))
    extra = ma * 200 - ms * 25; saved = (ms - ma) * u
    print(f"steel panel 10 kg -> primary aluminium {ma:.2f} kg: +{extra:.0f} MJ to make, saves {saved:.0f} MJ of fuel; net {saved-extra:+.0f} MJ")
    km_even = extra / ((ms - ma) * use_phase_MJ_per_kg(km=1))
    print(f"  pays back after {km_even:.0f} km")
    rec = ma * 30 - ms * 25
    print(f"  recycled aluminium: {rec:+.0f} MJ to make, so it is ahead from the first kilometre")
    assert rec < 0
    assert km_even > 0 and saved > extra
```

:::exercise A carbon bike frame
A steel bike frame weighs 2.2 kg; a carbon one 1.0 kg. Using the table, what is the embodied energy of each? A rider has a mass of 75 kg; does the lighter frame save meaningful energy over its life?
---solution---
1. Steel: $2.2\cdot25 = 55$ MJ. Carbon: $1.0\cdot270 = 270$ MJ.
2. The carbon frame costs 215 MJ more to make, about the energy in 6 litres of petrol.
3. A bicycle is human powered and 1.2 kg is about 1.5 % of rider plus bike, so the energy saving over its life is tiny. The choice is about performance and feel, not energy. For a car or aircraft the picture is reversed.
:::
