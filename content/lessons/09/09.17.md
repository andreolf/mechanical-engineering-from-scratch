You've designed an aluminium bracket. Should it be machined from a block, sand cast, die cast or 3D printed? Each process makes it with different tolerances and finishes, but usually the decision comes down to **how many** you need. A die-casting mould might cost 40 000 USD and then turn out a part every 30 seconds for pennies; a CNC machine needs almost no tooling but takes 15 minutes per part.

## Screening

First rule out processes that can't make the part at all:

- **Material**: you can't die cast steel or injection mould titanium.
- **Shape**: hollow, thin-walled, undercuts, overhangs.
- **Size and mass**: from grams (die casting, moulding) to tonnes (sand casting).
- **Tolerance and roughness**: machining reaches ±0.01 mm and Ra 0.8 µm; sand casting about ±1 mm and Ra 12 µm.

## Cost model

Among the survivors, Ashby's cost model splits the unit cost into three parts:

$$
C = \frac{m\,C_m}{1-f} + \frac{C_t}{n} + \frac{\dot C_L}{\dot n} ,
$$

- material: part mass $m$, price $C_m$ per kg, scrap fraction $f$;
- **tooling** $C_t$ (moulds, dies, fixtures) shared over a batch of $n$;
- **overhead rate** $\dot C_L$ (machine, labour, energy per hour) divided by the production rate $\dot n$.

At small batches tooling dominates; at large batches the cost falls to a floor of material plus time. Two processes cost the same when

$$
n^* = \frac{C_{t,b} - C_{t,a}}{(\text{floor})_a - (\text{floor})_b} .
$$

Rough guide: 3D print 1 to 10, machine 10 to a few hundred, sand cast hundreds to thousands, die cast or mould above 10 000.

:::

:::example A 250 g aluminium bracket
Aluminium at 3 USD/kg. Processes (scrap fraction, tooling, rate per hour, parts per hour): CNC (0.60, 300, 80, 4); sand casting (0.30, 2000, 50, 10); die casting (0.10, 40 000, 120, 120); metal 3D print (0.05, 0, 60, 0.5).

1. One part: printing 120.79 USD, CNC 321.88, sand 2006, die casting 40 002.
2. 100 parts: CNC 24.88, sand 26.07, die casting 401.83, printing 120.79.
3. 10 000 parts: die casting 5.83, sand 6.27, CNC 21.91.
4. Crossovers: CNC beats printing above 3 parts, sand casting beats CNC above 108, die casting beats sand casting above 8966. At each crossover the two unit costs agree to $10^{-9}$.
5. Printing never wins beyond a couple of parts here. It wins when the shape is impossible otherwise, such as internal channels or lattices.
:::

```python file=process_cost.py
"""Process selection by cost: Ashby's cost model for an aluminium bracket made by four processes, and the batch sizes where each one wins."""
import math

MASS = 0.25        # kg finished part
CM = 3.0           # USD/kg aluminium
#  name: (material scrap fraction, dedicated tooling USD, overhead rate USD/h, parts per hour)
PROC = {
    "CNC machining":  (0.60, 300.0, 80.0, 4.0),
    "sand casting":   (0.30, 2000.0, 50.0, 10.0),
    "die casting":    (0.10, 40000.0, 120.0, 120.0),
    "metal 3D print": (0.05, 0.0, 60.0, 0.5),
}


def unit_cost(proc, n):
    f, tool, rate, nd = PROC[proc]
    return MASS * CM / (1 - f) + tool / n + rate / nd


def crossover(a, b):
    """Batch size where processes a and b cost the same: equate the 1/n terms."""
    fa, ta, ra, na = PROC[a]; fb, tb, rb, nb = PROC[b]
    fixed = lambda f, r, nd: MASS * CM / (1 - f) + r / nd
    return (tb - ta) / (fixed(fa, ra, na) - fixed(fb, rb, nb))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    for n in (1, 10, 100, 1000, 10000, 100000):
        costs = {p: unit_cost(p, n) for p in PROC}
        best = min(costs, key=costs.get)
        print(f"n = {n:6d}: " + ", ".join(f"{p} {c:.2f}" for p, c in costs.items()) + f"  -> {best}")
    for a, b in (("metal 3D print", "CNC machining"), ("CNC machining", "sand casting"), ("sand casting", "die casting")):
        nx = crossover(a, b)
        print(f"{b} beats {a} above {nx:.0f} parts")
        assert abs(unit_cost(a, nx) - unit_cost(b, nx)) < 1e-9
    # each crossover is where the cheaper-tooled process stops winning
    assert unit_cost("CNC machining", 10) < unit_cost("die casting", 10) and unit_cost("die casting", 1e5) < unit_cost("CNC machining", 1e5)
```

:::exercise Injection moulding a plastic part
An ABS housing can be printed (0 tooling, 6 USD per part in material and time) or injection moulded (mould 15 000 USD, 0.40 USD per part). Above what batch size is moulding cheaper?
---solution---
1. Equal cost: $6 = 15000/n + 0.40$.
2. $n = 15000/5.60 = 2679$.
3. Above about 2700 parts, mould. Many products start with printed or machined prototypes and switch once the design is frozen and volume justifies the tool.
:::
