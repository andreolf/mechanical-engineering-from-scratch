Watch a lathe turning steel and a hot blue ribbon curls off the tool. That chip isn't torn off: the metal ahead of the tool is sheared intensely in a narrow zone, the **shear plane**, then slides up the tool's face. Nearly all the power goes into that shearing and into friction, and ends up as heat. Merchant's 1945 model of this zone still gives good estimates of cutting forces, which you need to size machine tools, fixtures and workholding.

## Orthogonal cutting

Model the tool as a straight edge perpendicular to the motion, removing a layer of thickness $t_0$ (the feed) and width $w$ (the depth of cut).

- The **rake angle** $\alpha$ is the tilt of the tool face from vertical. Positive rake is a sharper tool.
- The **shear angle** $\phi$ is the angle of the shear plane from the cutting direction.
- Friction on the rake face, coefficient $\mu = \tan\beta$.

The chip comes off thicker than the cut. Volume conservation gives the **chip ratio**

$$
r = \frac{t_0}{t_c} = \frac{\sin\phi}{\cos(\phi - \alpha)} .
$$

## Forces

The force to shear the material is $F_s = \tau_s\, t_0 w/\sin\phi$, with $\tau_s$ the shear flow stress. Resolving the resultant (which lies at angle $\beta - \alpha$ from the cutting direction):

$$
F_c = \frac{\tau_s t_0 w\cos(\beta - \alpha)}{\sin\phi\cos(\phi + \beta - \alpha)}, \qquad F_t = F_c\tan(\beta - \alpha) .
$$

Merchant assumed the shear plane sits where $F_c$ is smallest. Setting $dF_c/d\phi = 0$:

$$
\phi = 45^\circ + \frac\alpha2 - \frac\beta2 .
$$

The cutting power is $P = F_c V$, so the **specific cutting energy** $u = F_c/(t_0 w)$ (in J/mm³ or N/mm²) multiplied by the removal rate gives the spindle power.

:::example Turning medium-carbon steel
Rake 10°, $\mu = 0.5$ ($\beta = 26.57°$), $\tau_s = 550$ MPa, feed 0.2 mm, depth 2 mm, 200 m/min.

1. $\phi = 45 + 5 - 13.28 = 36.717°$. Brute-force minimisation of $F_c$ over $\phi$ gives 36.718°, within the 0.0001° grid.
2. Chip ratio $\sin36.72°/\cos26.72° = 0.669$: the chip is 0.299 mm thick.
3. $F_c = 590$ N, $F_t = 175$ N. Power $590\cdot3.33 = 1.97$ kW.
4. Specific cutting energy 1475 N/mm². Handbooks give 1.5 to 2.5 J/mm³ for this steel; real tools add edge rounding and ploughing.
5. Rake 0°: $F_c = 712$ N. Rake 20°: 494 N. More rake cuts more easily but weakens the edge, so carbide tools for steel run near 0 to 10°.
:::

```python file=merchant.py
"""Orthogonal cutting: Merchant's shear-angle model, cutting forces and spindle power, with the shear angle found by minimising the cutting force."""
import math


def forces(phi, alpha, beta, tau_s, t0, w):
    """Cutting and thrust force for shear angle phi, rake alpha, friction angle beta (rad)."""
    Fs = tau_s * t0 * w / math.sin(phi)
    R = Fs / math.cos(phi + beta - alpha)
    return R * math.cos(beta - alpha), R * math.sin(beta - alpha)


def merchant_phi(alpha, beta):
    return math.pi / 4 + alpha / 2 - beta / 2


if __name__ == "__main__":
    alpha, mu = math.radians(10), 0.5
    beta = math.atan(mu)
    tau_s, t0, w, V = 550e6, 0.2e-3, 2e-3, 200 / 60      # medium-carbon steel, feed 0.2 mm, depth 2 mm, 200 m/min
    phi = merchant_phi(alpha, beta)
    # brute-force minimum of the cutting force over the shear angle
    grid = [math.radians(5 + 0.0001 * k) for k in range(500000)]
    phi_num = min(grid, key=lambda p: forces(p, alpha, beta, tau_s, t0, w)[0])
    print(f"Merchant shear angle {math.degrees(phi):.3f} deg; numerical minimum of Fc at {math.degrees(phi_num):.3f} deg")
    assert abs(phi - phi_num) < math.radians(0.001)
    Fc, Ft = forces(phi, alpha, beta, tau_s, t0, w)
    r = math.sin(phi) / math.cos(phi - alpha)
    print(f"chip ratio t0/tc = {r:.3f} (chip {t0/r*1e3:.3f} mm thick)")
    print(f"cutting force {Fc:.0f} N, thrust {Ft:.0f} N; power {Fc*V/1000:.2f} kW")
    print(f"specific cutting energy {Fc/(t0*w)/1e6:.0f} MJ/m^3 (= N/mm^2)")
    # removal rate x specific energy = power
    assert abs(Fc / (t0 * w) * (t0 * w * V) - Fc * V) < 1e-9
    for a in (0, 10, 20):
        p = merchant_phi(math.radians(a), beta)
        print(f"  rake {a:2d} deg: phi {math.degrees(p):.1f} deg, Fc {forces(p, math.radians(a), beta, tau_s, t0, w)[0]:.0f} N")
```

:::exercise Removal rate and power
A 5 kW lathe cuts the same steel with $u = 2.0$ J/mm³ (a realistic value). At depth 3 mm and feed 0.25 mm, what is the highest cutting speed (m/min) the motor allows at 80 % efficiency?
---solution---
1. Available cutting power: $0.8\cdot5 = 4$ kW.
2. Removal rate: $4000/2.0 = 2000$ mm³/s.
3. $V = 2000/(3\cdot0.25) = 2667$ mm/s, which is 160 m/min.
:::
