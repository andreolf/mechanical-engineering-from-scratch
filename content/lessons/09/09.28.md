A micrometer reads 25.005 mm. Is the shaft inside a tolerance of 25.000 ± 0.005? It depends on how much you trust the reading. Every measurement has an **uncertainty**: a range around the reading that very probably contains the true value. In precision work the uncertainty decides whether a part passes, so engineers build an **uncertainty budget** following the international Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM).

## Sources and their standard uncertainties

Express each contribution as a standard uncertainty $u$, the equivalent of one standard deviation:

- **Type A**, from repeated readings: the standard deviation of the mean, $u = s/\sqrt n$.
- **Type B**, from other knowledge:
  - resolution $d$ (rounding, uniform distribution): $u = d/\sqrt{12}$;
  - a calibration certificate stating $U$ at coverage factor $k$: $u = U/k$;
  - a quantity known only to lie within $\pm a$ (rectangular): $u = a/\sqrt3$.

Temperature matters: steel grows 11.5 µm per metre per °C, and the reference temperature for length is 20 °C.

## Combining

For independent sources the variances add:

$$
u_c = \sqrt{\sum_i (c_i u_i)^2} ,
$$

where $c_i = \partial f/\partial x_i$ is the **sensitivity coefficient** when the result is a function $f$ of several inputs. Relative uncertainties add in quadrature for products and quotients, weighted by the exponent. The **expanded uncertainty** $U = k u_c$, with $k = 2$, gives about 95 % coverage.

## Abbe's principle

Ernst Abbe noted that the scale should be in line with the dimension being measured. If the scale is offset by a distance $L$ and the moving jaw tilts by an angle $\varepsilon$, the reading is wrong by

$$
\delta = L\,\varepsilon .
$$

A caliper violates the principle (its scale is beside the jaws), so jaw play matters. A micrometer obeys it (spindle in line with the measurement), which is one reason it's more accurate.

:::example A shaft and a density
1. Ten readings: mean 25.0049 mm, $s = 1.197$ µm, so repeatability $u = 0.379$ µm.
2. Resolution 1 µm: $0.289$ µm. Calibration 2 µm at $k = 2$: 1.000 µm. Temperature within ±2 °C, uncompensated: $25\cdot11.5\times10^{-6}\cdot2/\sqrt3 = 0.332$ µm.
3. Combined: $u_c = 1.156$ µm, and calibration is 75 % of the variance. Result: $25.0049 \pm 0.0023$ mm ($k = 2$).
4. Density of a cylinder, $\rho = 4m/(\pi d^2h)$, with $m = 125.0 \pm 0.1$ g, $d = 20.00 \pm 0.01$ mm, $h = 51.00 \pm 0.02$ mm: $\rho = 7801.7$ kg/m³. Propagation, $u_\rho/\rho = \sqrt{(u_m/m)^2 + (2u_d/d)^2 + (u_h/h)^2}$, gives 10.45 kg/m³. A million-sample Monte Carlo gives 10.44.
5. Abbe: a 30 mm offset with 50 µrad of tilt gives 1.50 µm, larger than the whole repeatability term.
:::

```python file=uncertainty.py
"""Measurement uncertainty: a GUM budget for a micrometer reading, propagation through a formula checked by Monte Carlo, and the Abbe error."""
import math
import numpy as np


def combine(*u):
    return math.sqrt(sum(x * x for x in u))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # ten readings of a nominal 25 mm steel shaft (mm)
    r = np.array([25.004, 25.006, 25.003, 25.005, 25.004, 25.007, 25.005, 25.004, 25.006, 25.005])
    mean, s = r.mean(), r.std(ddof=1)
    u_rep = s / math.sqrt(len(r))                 # type A
    u_res = 0.001 / math.sqrt(12)                 # rectangular, 1 um resolution
    u_cal = 0.002 / 2                             # certificate: 2 um at k = 2
    u_th = 25 * 11.5e-6 * 2 / math.sqrt(3)        # +-2 C from 20 C, rectangular, no compensation
    uc = combine(u_rep, u_res, u_cal, u_th)
    print(f"mean {mean:.4f} mm, s = {s*1000:.3f} um")
    for name, u in (("repeatability", u_rep), ("resolution", u_res), ("calibration", u_cal), ("temperature", u_th)):
        print(f"  {name:13s} {u*1000:.3f} um ({u*u/uc/uc*100:.0f} % of variance)")
    print(f"combined {uc*1000:.3f} um; expanded (k = 2): {mean:.4f} +- {2*uc*1000:.1f} um")
    # density of a cylinder rho = 4 m / (pi d^2 h): sensitivity coefficients vs Monte Carlo
    m, um = 0.1250, 0.0001          # kg
    d, ud = 0.0200, 0.00001         # m
    h, uh = 0.0510, 0.00002
    rho = 4 * m / (math.pi * d * d * h)
    rel = math.sqrt((um / m) ** 2 + (2 * ud / d) ** 2 + (uh / h) ** 2)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(7); N = 1_000_000
    sm = rng.normal(m, um, N); sd = rng.normal(d, ud, N); sh = rng.normal(h, uh, N)
    mc = 4 * sm / (math.pi * sd**2 * sh)
    print(f"density {rho:.1f} kg/m^3, u = {rho*rel:.2f} by propagation, {mc.std():.2f} by Monte Carlo")
    assert abs(mc.std() / (rho * rel) - 1) < 0.01
    # Abbe: scale 30 mm away from the measuring line, carriage pitches by 50 microradians
    print(f"Abbe error: 30 mm offset x 50 urad = {30e-3*50e-6*1e6:.2f} um")
```

:::exercise Is it in tolerance?
The shaft must be 25.000 ± 0.005 mm. With the reading 25.0049 mm and $U = 2.3$ µm, can you say it conforms?
---solution---
1. The interval is 25.0026 to 25.0072 mm.
2. The upper tolerance limit, 25.005, lies inside the interval, so the measurement can't prove conformance.
3. Under ISO 14253-1 the supplier must show conformance by staying inside the tolerance reduced by $U$: readings up to $25.005 - 0.0023 = 25.0027$ mm are clearly accepted. This one would need a better measurement, for example temperature-controlled and with a gauge block comparison.
:::
