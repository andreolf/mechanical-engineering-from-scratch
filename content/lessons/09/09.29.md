A CNC lathe turns thousands of shafts a day. No two are exactly the same: tiny variations in material, temperature and tool wear spread the diameters around a mean. That's normal, and if the spread is small compared with the tolerance it doesn't matter. What matters is noticing when something **changes**: a worn tool, a new batch of bar stock, a loose fixture. Walter Shewhart's control charts, invented at Bell Labs in the 1920s, separate those special causes from everyday random variation.

## Control charts

Every so often, measure a small **subgroup** (say 5 consecutive parts). Plot its mean $\bar x$ and range $R$.

From 20 to 25 subgroups taken while the process runs well, compute the grand mean $\bar{\bar x}$ and mean range $\bar R$. For subgroups of 5, the process standard deviation is estimated as $\hat\sigma = \bar R/d_2$ with $d_2 = 2.326$, and the control limits for the means are

$$
\bar{\bar x} \pm A_2\bar R, \qquad A_2 = 0.577 ,
$$

which is $\pm 3\hat\sigma/\sqrt5$. The range chart's upper limit is $D_4\bar R$ with $D_4 = 2.114$.

A point beyond a limit, or a pattern such as 7 points in a row on one side of the centre, signals a special cause. **Control limits are not tolerances**: they describe what the process does, not what the drawing wants.

## Process capability

Is the process good enough for the tolerance (LSL to USL)?

$$
C_p = \frac{USL - LSL}{6\sigma}, \qquad C_{pk} = \frac{\min(USL - \mu,\ \mu - LSL)}{3\sigma} .
$$

$C_p$ compares the spread with the tolerance; $C_{pk}$ also penalises an off-centre mean. With $C_{pk} = 1$, 0.27 % of parts fall outside. Automotive suppliers typically need $C_{pk} \ge 1.33$ (63 ppm at most) or 1.67 (under 1 ppm).

:::example Shafts at 25.000 ± 0.020 mm
Simulated process: mean 25.004 mm, $\sigma = 4$ µm. 25 subgroups of 5.

1. Grand mean 25.0041 mm, $\bar R = 8.43$ µm, $\hat\sigma = 8.43/2.326 = 3.63$ µm.
2. X̄ limits 24.9993 to 25.0090 mm. R chart upper limit 17.83 µm. Check: $A_2 d_2\sqrt5 = 3.00$.
3. $C_p = 0.040/(6\cdot0.00363) = 1.84$ and $C_{pk} = (25.020 - 25.0041)/(3\cdot0.00363) = 1.46$: 6 ppm expected out of tolerance.
4. For the true process ($\sigma = 4$ µm, mean 25.004): the normal formula gives 32 ppm, and 4 million simulated parts give 30 ppm.
5. Centring the process at 25.000 would make $C_p = C_{pk} = 1.67$ and 0.57 ppm.
6. A 4 µm shift from tool wear: each subgroup then has a 29 % chance of falling above the limit, an average run length of 3.4 subgroups.
:::

```python file=spc.py
"""Statistical process control: X-bar and R charts from subgroups, process capability Cp and Cpk, and catching a shift."""
import math
import numpy as np

A2, D3, D4, D2 = 0.577, 0.0, 2.114, 2.326     # constants for subgroups of 5
LSL, USL = 24.980, 25.020                     # shaft tolerance 25.000 +- 0.020 mm


def phi(z):
    return 0.5 * (1 + math.erf(z / math.sqrt(2)))


def capability(mu, sigma):
    cp = (USL - LSL) / (6 * sigma)
    cpk = min(USL - mu, mu - LSL) / (3 * sigma)
    ppm = 1e6 * (phi((LSL - mu) / sigma) + 1 - phi((USL - mu) / sigma))
    return cp, cpk, ppm


if __name__ == "__main__":
    rng = np.random.default_rng(11)
    mu, sigma = 25.004, 0.004
    groups = rng.normal(mu, sigma, (25, 5))
    xbar, R = groups.mean(axis=1), np.ptp(groups, axis=1)
    xbb, Rb = xbar.mean(), R.mean()
    s_hat = Rb / D2
    print(f"grand mean {xbb:.4f} mm, mean range {Rb*1000:.2f} um, sigma estimate {s_hat*1000:.2f} um (true 4.00)")
    assert abs(s_hat / sigma - 1) < 0.15
    ucl, lcl = xbb + A2 * Rb, xbb - A2 * Rb
    print(f"X-bar limits {lcl:.4f} to {ucl:.4f}; R chart upper limit {D4*Rb*1000:.2f} um")
    # A2 R-bar is 3 sigma of the subgroup mean
    assert abs(A2 * D2 * math.sqrt(5) - 3) < 0.01
    cp, cpk, ppm = capability(xbb, s_hat)
    print(f"Cp = {cp:.2f}, Cpk = {cpk:.2f}, expected {ppm:.0f} ppm out of tolerance")
    cp0, cpk0, ppm0 = capability(25.000, sigma)
    print(f"centred at 25.000 with sigma 4 um: Cp = Cpk = {cp0:.2f}, {ppm0:.2f} ppm")
    # simulated check of the ppm for the true process
    big = rng.normal(mu, sigma, 4_000_000)
    sim = 1e6 * np.mean((big < LSL) | (big > USL))
    print(f"true process: formula {capability(mu, sigma)[2]:.0f} ppm, simulation {sim:.0f} ppm")
    assert abs(sim - capability(mu, sigma)[2]) < 0.1 * capability(mu, sigma)[2]
    # tool wear shifts the mean by 4 um after group 25: how soon does a point fall outside?
    later = rng.normal(mu + 0.004, sigma, (20, 5)).mean(axis=1)
    first = next((i + 1 for i, x in enumerate(later) if x > ucl or x < lcl), None)
    p_out = 1 - phi((ucl - mu - 0.004) / (sigma / math.sqrt(5)))
    print(f"after a 4 um shift: first point outside at subgroup {first}; chance per subgroup {p_out:.2f}, average run length {1/p_out:.1f}")
```

:::exercise Capability of a drilling process
Hole diameters have mean 10.012 mm and $\sigma = 0.006$ mm; tolerance 10.000 to 10.040 mm. Find $C_p$ and $C_{pk}$. Is it capable at 1.33?
---solution---
1. $C_p = 0.040/0.036 = 1.11$.
2. $C_{pk} = \min(0.028, 0.012)/0.018 = 0.67$.
3. Not capable: the mean sits too close to the lower limit, and even centred ($C_p = 1.11$) the spread is too wide. Centre it first, then reduce variation.
:::
