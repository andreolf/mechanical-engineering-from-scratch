An arc welder melts a small pool of steel that travels along the joint at a few millimetres per second. Behind it the metal freezes and cools, and how fast it cools matters a lot. Cool too fast and the heat-affected zone next to the weld turns into hard, brittle martensite (09.07) that can crack hours later as hydrogen diffuses in. Welding procedures specify current, voltage, speed and preheat to control that cooling. Rosenthal's 1941 solution for a moving heat source tells you why.

## Heat input

An arc with voltage $U$ and current $I$, of which a fraction $\eta$ (about 0.8 for MIG or stick) reaches the work, delivers net power $q = \eta U I$. Travelling at speed $v$, the **heat input** per unit length is $q/v$, usually quoted in kJ/mm.

## Rosenthal's solution

For a point source moving at speed $v$ over a thick plate, in coordinates moving with the arc ($\xi$ along the travel direction, $r$ the distance from the arc), the steady temperature field is

$$
T - T_0 = \frac{q}{2\pi k r}\exp\left[-\frac{v(\xi + r)}{2\alpha}\right] .
$$

Ahead of the arc ($\xi = r$), the exponential falls off fast: the metal hasn't heated up yet. Behind it ($\xi = -r$), the exponent is zero and the temperature falls only as $1/r$. That's why the weld pool and isotherms are teardrops trailing behind.

## Cooling time

On the centreline behind the arc, $T - T_0 = q/(2\pi k |\xi|)$, and a point passes $|\xi| = vt$ after time $t$. So the time to cool from 800 to 500 °C, which decides whether martensite forms, is

$$
t_{8/5} = \frac{q/v}{2\pi k}\left(\frac{1}{500 - T_0} - \frac{1}{800 - T_0}\right) .
$$

Higher heat input or **preheat** ($T_0$) slows cooling. For thin plates, where heat can only flow sideways, a 2D version applies and $t_{8/5}$ goes as $(q/v)^2$.

:::example A MIG weld on thick steel
25 V, 200 A, efficiency 0.8, 5 mm/s. Steel: $k = 40$ W/(m K), $\alpha = 8.49\times10^{-6}$ m²/s, melting at 1510 °C.

1. $q = 0.8\cdot25\cdot200 = 4000$ W; heat input 0.80 kJ/mm.
2. Bisection on the centreline: the surface pool extends 10.68 mm behind the arc and 2.48 mm ahead. (A point source overestimates temperatures right at the arc; real arcs spread their heat.)
3. Behind the arc the solution reduces to $q/(2\pi k|\xi|)$ to $10^{-9}$.
4. $t_{8/5} = \frac{800}{2\pi\cdot40}\left(\frac1{480} - \frac1{780}\right) = 2.55$ s. Reading the 800 and 500 °C crossings off the centreline history gives the same.
5. Preheat to 150 °C: 4.20 s. Doubling the speed halves the heat input: 1.28 s, likely too fast for a hardenable steel.
:::

```python file=rosenthal.py
"""Welding heat flow: Rosenthal's moving point source on a thick plate, the weld pool size, and the 800 to 500 C cooling time that sets the weld's hardness."""
import math
import numpy as np

K, RHO, CP = 40.0, 7850.0, 600.0     # steel, W/(m K), kg/m^3, J/(kg K)
ALPHA = K / (RHO * CP)
TM = 1510.0


def T(xi, y, z, q, v, T0=20.0):
    """Temperature in the frame moving with the arc (xi along the travel direction, positive ahead)."""
    r = math.sqrt(xi * xi + y * y + z * z)
    return T0 + q / (2 * math.pi * K * r) * math.exp(-v * (xi + r) / (2 * ALPHA))


def pool_length(q, v, T0=20.0):
    """Melt-pool extent behind and ahead of the arc on the centreline (bisection on T = TM)."""
    def find(sign):
        lo, hi = 1e-7, 0.2
        for _ in range(100):
            m = (lo + hi) / 2
            if T(sign * m, 0, 0, q, v, T0) > TM: lo = m
            else: hi = m
        return (lo + hi) / 2
    return find(-1), find(1)


def t85(q, v, T0=20.0):
    """Thick-plate cooling time from 800 to 500 C on the weld centreline."""
    return q / v / (2 * math.pi * K) * (1 / (500 - T0) - 1 / (800 - T0))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    q, v = 0.8 * 25 * 200, 0.005          # arc efficiency 0.8, 25 V, 200 A; 5 mm/s
    print(f"net heat input {q:.0f} W, {q/v/1e6:.2f} kJ/mm of weld")
    back, ahead = pool_length(q, v)
    print(f"melt pool on the surface: {back*1000:.2f} mm behind the arc, {ahead*1000:.3f} mm ahead")
    assert abs(T(-back, 0, 0, q, v) - TM) < 1e-3
    # behind the arc on the centreline the solution reduces to q / (2 pi k |xi|): check
    assert abs(T(-0.03, 0, 0, q, v) - 20 - q / (2 * math.pi * K * 0.03)) < 1e-9
    # cooling time: compare the formula with the temperature history at a point on the centreline far behind
    xs = -np.linspace(1e-4, 0.5, 400001); Ts = 20 + q / (2 * math.pi * K * (-xs))
    t800 = -xs[np.argmin(abs(Ts - 800))] / v; t500 = -xs[np.argmin(abs(Ts - 500))] / v
    print(f"t8/5 = {t85(q, v):.2f} s (from the centreline history {t500 - t800:.2f} s)")
    assert abs((t500 - t800) - t85(q, v)) < 0.01
    print(f"preheat to 150 C: t8/5 = {t85(q, v, 150):.2f} s; double speed: {t85(q, 2*v):.2f} s")
```

:::exercise Choosing a preheat
A steel needs $t_{8/5} \ge 5$ s to avoid cracking. With heat input 1.0 kJ/mm on thick plate, is preheat needed, and if so how much (try 100 °C and 150 °C)?
---solution---
1. $\frac{q/v}{2\pi k} = 1\times10^6/(2\pi\cdot40) = 3979$ K s.
2. No preheat (20 °C): $3979\,(1/480 - 1/780) = 3.19$ s. Too fast.
3. 100 °C: $3979\,(1/400 - 1/700) = 4.26$ s. 150 °C: $3979\,(1/350 - 1/650) = 5.25$ s.
4. Preheat to about 150 °C.
:::
