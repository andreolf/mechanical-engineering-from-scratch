Engine blocks, manhole covers, bronze statues and pump housings are cast: molten metal poured into a mould shaped like the part. Liquid metal shrinks as it freezes, by about 3 to 7 % in volume. If the last region to freeze is inside the casting, it can't draw in more liquid and a shrinkage cavity forms. Foundry engineers prevent this by adding a **riser**, a reservoir of liquid that freezes after the casting and feeds it. To size one you need to know how long things take to freeze.

## Chvorinov's rule

In a sand mould, heat flows out slowly through the sand, which is the main resistance. The heat to remove scales with the casting's volume $V$, and the rate with its surface area $A$. Solving the heat conduction into a semi-infinite mould (Phase 07) gives

$$
t = B\left(\frac{V}{A}\right)^2 .
$$

The ratio $M = V/A$ is the **casting modulus**. The mould constant $B$ depends on the metal and the mould:

$$
B = \frac\pi4\left(\frac{\rho_\text{metal}L}{T_m - T_0}\right)^2\frac{1}{k\rho c\,\big|_\text{mould}} ,
$$

with $L$ the latent heat (plus superheat), $T_m$ the melting point and $k\rho c$ the mould's thermal properties. A metal mould has a much larger $k\rho c$, so die castings freeze in seconds.

Shapes with the same modulus freeze in the same time. A sphere and a cube of the same diameter both have $M = D/6$, so they freeze together. A thin plate freezes fast; a chunky boss slowly. That's why castings should have even wall thicknesses.

## Riser design

The riser must freeze after the section it feeds. A common rule is $M_\text{riser} \ge 1.2\,M_\text{casting}$. A cylinder with height equal to diameter has modulus $D/6$, which is a good balance of modulus against volume. The riser must also hold enough liquid to make up the casting's shrinkage.

Freezing time against modulus for steel in sand. The riser (lime when large enough) must freeze after the plate it feeds.
:::

:::example A steel plate casting
Steel in green sand: $\rho = 7300$ kg/m³, $L = 300$ kJ/kg including superheat, $T_m - T_0 = 1475$ K; sand $k = 0.52$ W/(m K), $\rho = 1600$ kg/m³, $c = 1170$ J/(kg K).

1. $B = \frac\pi4\left(\frac{7300\cdot300000}{1475}\right)^2/(0.52\cdot1600\cdot1170) = 1.779\times10^6$ s/m², or 2.96 min/cm². Handbook values are 2 to 4.
2. Plate 200 × 100 × 20 mm: $M = 7.69$ mm, freezing in 1.8 min. Bar 300 × 40 × 40 mm: 9.38 mm, 2.6 min. Cube and sphere of 100 mm: both 16.67 mm, 8.2 min.
3. Riser for the plate, $H = D$: bisection on $M = 1.2\cdot7.69$ mm gives $D = 55.4$ mm, exactly $6\cdot1.2\cdot7.69$. It freezes in 2.5 min.
4. The riser holds 133 cm³, a third of the plate's 400 cm³, so it easily covers 5 % shrinkage (20 cm³).
:::

```python file=chvorinov.py
"""Casting: Chvorinov's rule t = B (V/A)^2 with B from mould properties, solidification times of simple shapes, and sizing a riser that freezes last."""
import math


def mould_constant(rho_metal=7300.0, L=300e3, Tm=1500.0, T0=25.0, k=0.52, rho_m=1600.0, c_m=1170.0):
    """B in s/m^2 for a metal freezing against a semi-infinite sand mould (Chvorinov's derivation)."""
    return math.pi / 4 * (rho_metal * L / (Tm - T0)) ** 2 / (k * rho_m * c_m)


def modulus_box(a, b, c):
    return a * b * c / (2 * (a * b + b * c + a * c))


def modulus_cylinder(D, H):
    return (math.pi * D**2 / 4 * H) / (math.pi * D * H + 2 * math.pi * D**2 / 4)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    B = mould_constant()
    print(f"steel in green sand: B = {B:.3e} s/m^2 = {B*1e-4/60:.2f} min/cm^2")
    shapes = {"plate 200x100x20 mm": modulus_box(0.2, 0.1, 0.02), "cube 100 mm": modulus_box(0.1, 0.1, 0.1),
              "sphere 100 mm": 0.1 / 6, "bar 300x40x40 mm": modulus_box(0.3, 0.04, 0.04)}
    for name, M in shapes.items():
        print(f"  {name}: V/A = {M*1000:.2f} mm, freezes in {B*M*M/60:.1f} min")
    # a cube and a sphere of the same diameter have the same modulus D/6
    assert abs(modulus_box(0.1, 0.1, 0.1) - 0.1 / 6) < 1e-15
    # riser for the plate: cylinder H = D with modulus 1.2 times the casting's
    Mc = shapes["plate 200x100x20 mm"]
    lo, hi = 1e-3, 0.5
    for _ in range(80):
        mid = (lo + hi) / 2
        if modulus_cylinder(mid, mid) < 1.2 * Mc: lo = mid
        else: hi = mid
    D = (lo + hi) / 2
    print(f"riser (H = D) for the plate: D = {D*1000:.1f} mm, freezes in {B*modulus_cylinder(D, D)**2/60:.1f} min vs plate {B*Mc**2/60:.1f} min")
    assert abs(D - 6 * 1.2 * Mc) < 1e-9          # H = D cylinder has modulus D/6
    print(f"riser volume {math.pi*D**3/4*1e6:.0f} cm^3 vs plate {0.2*0.1*0.02*1e6:.0f} cm^3")
```

:::exercise A cube riser
A 60 mm steel cube is cast in the same sand. How long does it take to freeze, and what diameter of cylindrical riser ($H = D$) does it need?
---solution---
1. $M = 60/6 = 10$ mm $= 1$ cm, so $t = 2.96\cdot1^2 = 2.96$ min.
2. Riser modulus $\ge 1.2$ cm, so $D/6 \ge 12$ mm and $D \ge 72$ mm.
3. The riser is larger than the cube: chunky shapes are expensive to feed, which is another reason to design castings with thin, even walls.
:::
