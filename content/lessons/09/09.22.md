Computer cases, brackets, car body panels and electrical enclosures start as flat sheet, cut by laser or punch, then folded on a press brake. The question every sheet-metal designer faces: how long must the flat blank be so that after bending the part has the right outside dimensions? Bending stretches the outside of the sheet and compresses the inside, and the metal springs back a little when released.

## The neutral axis

In a bend, the outer fibres lengthen and the inner fibres shorten. Somewhere in between is a **neutral axis** whose length doesn't change. For thin sheet bent around a generous radius it sits at mid-thickness. In tight bends it shifts toward the inside. The **K-factor** gives its position as a fraction of the thickness from the inside surface, typically 0.3 to 0.5.

The flat length of the bend region is the neutral arc length, the **bend allowance**:

$$
BA = \theta\,(R + K t) ,
$$

with $\theta$ the bend angle in radians, $R$ the inside radius and $t$ the thickness.

## Blank length

Add up the straight flats and the bend allowances. With outside dimensions, each flat stops at a distance $R + t$ from the outside corner (for 90° bends). Equivalently, subtract a **bend deduction** $BD = 2(R + t) - BA$ per bend from the sum of the outside dimensions.

## Springback

When the punch lifts, the elastic part of the bending strain recovers and the bend opens slightly. For a material with yield stress $Y$ and modulus $E$:

$$
\frac{R_i}{R_f} = 4x^3 - 3x + 1, \qquad x = \frac{R_i Y}{E t} .
$$

The mid-plane length stays the same, so $(R_i + t/2)\theta_i = (R_f + t/2)\theta_f$. Strong, low-modulus materials with large radii spring back most. The press brake overbends to compensate.

Minimum bend radius: bend too tightly and the outer surface cracks, especially across the rolling direction in hard tempers.

:::example A U-channel in aluminium
2 mm 5052-H32 ($Y = 193$ MPa, $E = 70$ GPa), inside radius 3 mm, $K = 0.33$. Outside dimensions 30 × 50 × 30 mm.

1. $BA = (\pi/2)(3 + 0.33\cdot2) = 5.749$ mm. A numerical polyline along the neutral arc gives the same to $10^{-6}$.
2. Flats: $(30 - 5) + (50 - 10) + (30 - 5) = 90$ mm. Blank: $90 + 2\cdot5.749 = 101.50$ mm.
3. Bend deduction $2\cdot5 - 5.749 = 4.251$ mm per bend: $110 - 2\cdot4.251 = 101.50$ mm, the same.
4. Springback: $x = 3\cdot193/(70000\cdot2) = 0.00414$, so $R_i/R_f = 0.98759$. The radius opens to 3.038 mm and a 90° bend to 89.16°. Overbend to 90.85°.
5. Mild steel opens to 89.49°, spring steel ($Y = 1000$ MPa) to 88.55°.
:::

```python file=sheetbend.py
"""Sheet-metal bending: bend allowance from the neutral axis, the flat blank for a bracket, and springback with how far to overbend."""
import math


def bend_allowance(theta_deg, R, t, K=0.33):
    """Arc length of the neutral axis through a bend of angle theta, inside radius R, thickness t, K-factor K."""
    return math.radians(theta_deg) * (R + K * t)


def springback_ratio(R, t, Y, E):
    """Inside radius after release divided by before: Ri/Rf = 4 x^3 - 3 x + 1 with x = R Y / (E t)."""
    x = R * Y / (E * t)
    return 4 * x**3 - 3 * x + 1


if __name__ == "__main__":
    t, R, K = 2.0, 3.0, 0.33
    BA = bend_allowance(90, R, t, K)
    print(f"90 deg bend, R = {R} mm, t = {t} mm, K = {K}: bend allowance {BA:.3f} mm")
    # check against the length of a polyline traced along the neutral axis
    rn = R + K * t; n = 100000
    poly = sum(math.dist((rn * math.cos(a0), rn * math.sin(a0)), (rn * math.cos(a1), rn * math.sin(a1)))
               for a0, a1 in ((math.pi / 2 * i / n, math.pi / 2 * (i + 1) / n) for i in range(n)))
    assert abs(poly - BA) < 1e-6
    # U-channel bracket: outside dimensions 30 x 50 x 30 mm, two 90 deg bends
    setback = R + t                      # from outside mould line to the start of the bend
    flats = (30 - setback) + (50 - 2 * setback) + (30 - setback)
    L = flats + 2 * BA
    print(f"U bracket 30 x 50 x 30 (outside): flats {flats:.2f} mm + 2 bends {2*BA:.3f} mm = blank {L:.2f} mm")
    print(f"  bend deduction per bend: {2*setback - BA:.3f} mm (sum of outside dimensions {30+50+30} minus blank {L:.2f} = {110-L:.3f})")
    assert abs((110 - L) - 2 * (2 * setback - BA)) < 1e-12
    # springback of 5052-H32 aluminium
    Y, E = 193.0, 70000.0
    ratio = springback_ratio(R, t, Y, E)
    Rf = R / ratio
    # neutral (mid-plane) length is conserved: (Ri + t/2) theta_i = (Rf + t/2) theta_f
    theta_f = 90 * (R + t / 2) / (Rf + t / 2)
    theta_i = 90 * 90 / theta_f
    print(f"5052-H32: Ri/Rf = {ratio:.5f}, released radius {Rf:.3f} mm, a 90 deg bend opens to {theta_f:.2f} deg; overbend to {theta_i:.2f} deg")
    for steel in ((350.0, 210000.0, "mild steel"), (1000.0, 210000.0, "spring steel")):
        r = springback_ratio(R, t, steel[0], steel[1])
        print(f"  {steel[2]}: Ri/Rf = {r:.5f}, opens to {90*(R+t/2)/(R/r+t/2):.2f} deg")
```

:::exercise An L-bracket
An L-bracket from 1.5 mm steel has outside legs of 40 mm and 25 mm with one 90° bend, $R = 2$ mm, $K = 0.4$. What is the blank length?
---solution---
1. $BA = (\pi/2)(2 + 0.4\cdot1.5) = (\pi/2)(2.6) = 4.084$ mm.
2. Flats: $(40 - 3.5) + (25 - 3.5) = 58$ mm.
3. Blank: $58 + 4.084 = 62.08$ mm.
:::
