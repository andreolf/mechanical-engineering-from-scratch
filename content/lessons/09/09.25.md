Your phone case, a LEGO brick and a car's dashboard were all injection moulded. Plastic pellets are melted and forced at high pressure into a steel mould, held while the part cools, then ejected. One mould can make millions of identical parts, each in seconds. The step that sets the cycle time, and so the cost per part, is cooling: you can't open the mould until the plastic at the centre of the thickest wall is stiff enough to eject.

## The process

1. **Plasticise**: a rotating screw melts and meters the pellets.
2. **Inject**: the screw rams forward and fills the cavity in about a second, at pressures up to 100 MPa or more.
3. **Pack and hold**: extra material is pushed in to make up shrinkage as the plastic cools.
4. **Cool**: the part cools against the water-cooled mould.
5. **Eject**: the mould opens and pins push the part out.

## Cooling time

A wall of thickness $s$ between two mould faces at $T_w$ is a slab cooling from both sides, the same conduction problem as Phase 07. The centre temperature is a Fourier series whose first term dominates after a short time:

$$
\frac{T_c - T_w}{T_m - T_w} \approx \frac4\pi\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^2\alpha t}{s^2}\right) .
$$

Solve for the time to reach the ejection temperature $T_e$:

$$
t_c = \frac{s^2}{\pi^2\alpha}\ln\left(\frac4\pi\,\frac{T_m - T_w}{T_e - T_w}\right) .
$$

Plastics have tiny thermal diffusivities, around $10^{-7}$ m²/s, and the time goes as $s^2$. Doubling a wall quadruples the cooling time.

## Design rules

- Keep walls **thin and uniform** (1 to 3 mm for most parts). A thick section cools slowly and **sinks** or forms voids.
- Use ribs instead of thick walls for stiffness, with rib thickness about 0.6 times the wall.
- Add **draft** (1 to 2°) on walls so the part releases from the mould, and avoid undercuts or pay for side actions.

:::example ABS walls
ABS: $k = 0.17$ W/(m K), $\rho = 1050$ kg/m³, $c = 2000$ J/(kg K), so $\alpha = 8.10\times10^{-8}$ m²/s. Melt at 240 °C, mould at 60 °C, eject at 90 °C.

1. 2 mm wall: $t_c = \frac{(0.002)^2}{\pi^2\cdot8.10\times10^{-8}}\ln\left(\frac4\pi\cdot\frac{180}{30}\right) = 5.01\cdot2.033 = 10.18$ s.
2. 1 mm: 2.54 s. 3 mm: 22.90 s. 4 mm: 40.72 s, exactly 4 times the 2 mm value.
3. An explicit finite-difference solution agrees with the formula to better than 1 % for all four thicknesses.
4. With 1 s fill and 4 s to open, eject and close, the 2 mm part cycles in 15.2 s, 237 shots per hour. A 4 mm wall gives only 79.
5. A 40 °C mould cools the 2 mm wall in 8.15 s, but the faster freezing locks in more stress and gives a duller surface.
:::

```python file=moulding.py
"""Injection moulding: cooling time of a plastic wall from the one-term slab solution, checked with finite differences, and why it grows with thickness squared."""
import math
import numpy as np

K, RHO, CP = 0.17, 1050.0, 2000.0      # ABS
ALPHA = K / (RHO * CP)
TM, TW, TE = 240.0, 60.0, 90.0         # melt, mould wall and ejection (centre) temperatures, C


def cooling_time(s, a=ALPHA, Tm=TM, Tw=TW, Te=TE):
    """Time for the centre of a wall of thickness s to cool to Te (one-term Fourier series)."""
    return s * s / (math.pi**2 * a) * math.log(4 / math.pi * (Tm - Tw) / (Te - Tw))


def fdm_cooling_time(s, n=81):
    x = np.linspace(0, s, n); dx = x[1] - x[0]; dt = 0.4 * dx * dx / ALPHA
    T = np.full(n, TM); T[0] = T[-1] = TW; t = 0.0
    while T[n // 2] > TE:
        T[1:-1] += ALPHA * dt / dx**2 * (T[2:] - 2 * T[1:-1] + T[:-2]); t += dt
    return t


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(f"ABS thermal diffusivity {ALPHA:.3e} m^2/s")
    for s_mm in (1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0):
        tc = cooling_time(s_mm / 1000); tf = fdm_cooling_time(s_mm / 1000)
        print(f"  wall {s_mm:.0f} mm: formula {tc:.2f} s, finite differences {tf:.2f} s")
        assert abs(tc - tf) / tc < 0.01
    assert abs(cooling_time(0.004) / cooling_time(0.002) - 4) < 1e-12
    # cycle: fill 1 s, hold included in cooling, open/eject/close 4 s
    cyc = 1 + cooling_time(0.002) + 4
    print(f"2 mm part cycle about {cyc:.1f} s: {3600/cyc:.0f} shots per hour; with a 4 mm wall {3600/(5+cooling_time(0.004)):.0f}")
    # colder mould: faster but more stress and worse surface
    print(f"mould at 40 C: {cooling_time(0.002, Tw=40.0):.2f} s for the 2 mm wall")
```

:::exercise A polypropylene lid
A PP lid has 1.5 mm walls; $\alpha = 7\times10^{-8}$ m²/s, melt 230 °C, mould 40 °C, eject 80 °C. Find the cooling time.
---solution---
1. $s^2/(\pi^2\alpha) = (0.0015)^2/(9.8696\cdot7\times10^{-8}) = 3.257$ s.
2. $\ln\left(\frac4\pi\cdot\frac{190}{40}\right) = \ln(6.048) = 1.800$.
3. $t_c = 3.257\cdot1.800 = 5.86$ s.
:::
