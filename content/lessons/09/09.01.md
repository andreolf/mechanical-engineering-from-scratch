Why is steel about 200 times stiffer than rubber and 3 times stiffer than aluminium? Young's modulus is not a property you can change by heat treatment or cold work, unlike strength. It's set by the bonds between atoms: how steeply the energy rises when you pull two atoms apart.

## The pair potential

Two atoms attract at long range and repel strongly when their electron clouds overlap. A classic model is the **Lennard-Jones** potential:

$$
V(r) = 4\varepsilon\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right] .
$$

It has a minimum of depth $\varepsilon$ at $r_0 = 2^{1/6}\sigma$, the equilibrium spacing. The force between the atoms is $F = -dV/dr$, which is zero at $r_0$.

## Stiffness is curvature

Expand $V$ around $r_0$:

$$
V(r) \approx -\varepsilon + \tfrac12 S\,(r - r_0)^2, \qquad S = \left.\frac{d^2V}{dr^2}\right|_{r_0} .
$$

So for small stretches each bond is a linear spring of stiffness $S$: Hooke's law, straight from the shape of the energy well. For Lennard-Jones, $S = 72\varepsilon/(2^{1/3}\sigma^2) = 57.1\,\varepsilon/\sigma^2$.

## From bonds to Young's modulus

In a simple cubic packing, each bond carries the load on an area of about $r_0^2$, and a strain $\epsilon$ stretches it by $\epsilon r_0$. Stress $= S\epsilon r_0/r_0^2$, so

$$
E \approx \frac{S}{r_0} .
$$

Covalent and metallic bonds have $S$ of about 20 to 200 N/m at $r_0 \approx 0.25$ nm, giving $E$ of 80 to 800 GPa: metals and ceramics. Weak van der Waals bonds, as in solid argon or between polymer chains, have $S \approx 1$ N/m and give $E$ of a few GPa. Rubber is softer still because it doesn't stretch bonds at all, it uncoils chains.

## Two bonus results

- The attraction is largest at $1.109\,r_0$. That maximum force sets a theoretical strength of roughly $E/10$ to $E/30$. Real materials fail far below it, for reasons covered in 09.03 and 09.12.
- The well is steeper on the inside than the outside. A vibrating (hot) pair spends its time further apart on average: **thermal expansion**.

:::example Argon, from potential to modulus
$\varepsilon = 1.65\times10^{-21}$ J (10.3 meV), $\sigma = 0.340$ nm.

1. $r_0 = 2^{1/6}\cdot0.340 = 0.3816$ nm.
2. Bond stiffness: the analytic formula gives 0.8157 N/m, and a central finite difference of $V$ gives 0.8157 N/m. The slope of the force at $r_0$ is $-S$, as it should be.
3. $E \approx S/r_0 = 2.14$ GPa. Measured values for solid argon are about 1 to 3 GPa.
4. Peak attraction 11.63 pN at $1.1087\,r_0$ (strain 0.109). Stress estimate 80 MPa, which is $E/26.8$.
5. Vibrating with 5 % of the well depth, the turning points are 0.9669 and 1.0431 $r_0$, so the midpoint moves out to 1.0050 $r_0$. At 20 %, 1.0220 $r_0$. That growth is thermal expansion.
6. A metal-like bond with $S = 50$ N/m at 0.25 nm gives $E \approx 200$ GPa, about right for steel.
:::

```python file=bonding.py
"""Atomic bonding and stiffness: from a Lennard-Jones pair potential to a bond stiffness and an estimate of Young's modulus."""
import math
import numpy as np


def lj(r, eps, sig):
    return 4 * eps * ((sig / r)**12 - (sig / r)**6)


def lj_force(r, eps, sig):
    """F = -dV/dr (positive = repulsive)."""
    return 24 * eps / r * (2 * (sig / r)**12 - (sig / r)**6)


def lj_stiffness_analytic(eps, sig):
    """V''(r0) at r0 = 2^(1/6) sigma: 72 eps / (2^(1/3) sigma^2)."""
    return 72 * eps / (2**(1 / 3) * sig**2)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    eps, sig = 1.65e-21, 3.40e-10          # argon
    r0 = 2**(1 / 6) * sig
    h = 1e-13
    S_fd = (lj(r0 + h, eps, sig) - 2 * lj(r0, eps, sig) + lj(r0 - h, eps, sig)) / h**2
    S = lj_stiffness_analytic(eps, sig)
    print(f"argon: r0 = {r0*1e9:.4f} nm, well depth {eps/1.602e-19*1000:.2f} meV")
    print(f"bond stiffness: analytic {S:.4f} N/m, finite-difference second derivative {S_fd:.4f} N/m")
    assert abs(S_fd / S - 1) < 1e-4
    # the force is zero at r0 and its slope there is -S
    assert abs(lj_force(r0, eps, sig)) < 1e-20
    slope = (lj_force(r0 + 1e-14, eps, sig) - lj_force(r0 - 1e-14, eps, sig)) / 2e-14
    assert abs(-slope / S - 1) < 1e-4
    # E ~ S / r0 (one bond per r0^2 area, stretching by strain * r0)
    print(f"E estimate S / r0 = {S/r0/1e9:.2f} GPa (solid argon is measured at roughly 1 to 3 GPa)")
    # largest attractive force: the theoretical tensile strength of a bond
    rs = np.linspace(r0, 2 * sig, 200001); F = lj_force(rs, eps, sig)
    i = np.argmin(F)
    print(f"maximum bond force {-F[i]*1e12:.3f} pN at r = {rs[i]/r0:.4f} r0 (strain {rs[i]/r0-1:.3f}); stress estimate {-F[i]/r0**2/1e6:.0f} MPa = E / {S/r0/(-F[i]/r0**2):.1f}")
    assert abs(rs[i] / r0 - (26 / 7)**(1 / 6) / 2**(1 / 6)) < 1e-5
    # asymmetry of the well: the mean separation grows with vibration energy, which is thermal expansion
    for Eth in (0.05, 0.2):
        lo = r0 - 1e-12; hi = r0 + 1e-12
        while lj(lo, eps, sig) < -eps * (1 - Eth): lo -= 1e-14
        while lj(hi, eps, sig) < -eps * (1 - Eth): hi += 1e-14
        print(f"vibrating at {Eth*100:.0f} % of the well depth: turning points {lo/r0:.4f} and {hi/r0:.4f} r0, midpoint {(lo+hi)/2/r0:.4f} r0")
    # a stiff metallic-type bond: S = 50 N/m at r0 = 0.25 nm
    print(f"metal-like bond, S = 50 N/m, r0 = 0.25 nm: E ~ {50/0.25e-9/1e9:.0f} GPa")
```

:::exercise A stiffer bond
A diamond C–C bond has a stiffness of about 470 N/m at $r_0 = 0.154$ nm. Estimate Young's modulus with $E \approx S/r_0$ and compare it with the measured 1050 to 1200 GPa.
---solution---
1. $E \approx 470/0.154\times10^{-9} = 3.05\times10^{12}$ Pa, about 3000 GPa.
2. That is about 2.7 times the measured value. The simple-cubic picture assumes every bond lines up with the load. In diamond's tetrahedral network the bonds are tilted, so they also bend and rotate, and fewer bonds cross each square metre. The estimate gets the order of magnitude right, which is what it is for.
:::
