Aluminium window frames, heat sinks, bike rack rails and the T-slot profiles used in machine frames are all made the same way: a hot billet, often 100 to 300 mm across, is pushed through a steel die with the profile's shape cut through it, like toothpaste from a tube. Any constant cross-section is possible, with internal cavities too, and die costs are modest. The engineering question is force: will the press manage this profile?

## The extrusion ratio

The **extrusion ratio** is $R = A_0/A_f$, billet area over profile area. Every bit of metal gets a true strain of

$$
\varepsilon = \ln R .
$$

## Ideal work and real pressure

With no friction or redundant shearing, the work per unit volume equals the plastic work $\bar\sigma\varepsilon$, so the ideal ram pressure is

$$
p_\text{ideal} = \bar\sigma \ln R ,
$$

with $\bar\sigma$ the mean flow stress over the strain. For cold work with $\sigma = K\varepsilon^n$, $\bar\sigma = K\varepsilon^n/(n+1)$. Hot aluminium barely hardens, so $\bar\sigma$ is its flow stress at temperature.

Real dies add friction and the extra shearing of metal turning the corner into the die. Johnson's empirical formula covers both:

$$
p = \bar\sigma\,(0.8 + 1.5\ln R) .
$$

In **direct extrusion** the billet also slides along the container wall. With sticking friction this adds roughly $m\,k\,\pi D_0 L$, where $k = \bar\sigma/\sqrt3$ is the shear flow stress and $L$ the remaining billet length. The force peaks at the start and falls as the billet shortens. **Indirect extrusion** pushes the die into a stationary billet and avoids this friction.

## Temperature

Aluminium is extruded at 450 to 500 °C, where its flow stress drops to around 40 MPa. Too hot and the surface tears; too cold and the press stalls. Ram speed matters too, because deformation heating raises the profile's temperature as it exits.

:::example A 6063 profile
Billet 100 mm diameter, 300 mm long, flow stress 40 MPa at 480 °C, sticking friction ($m = 1$).

1. Container friction at the start: $(40/\sqrt3)\cdot\pi\cdot0.1\cdot0.3 = 2.18$ MN.
2. $R = 10$: ideal pressure 92 MPa, Johnson 170 MPa; die force 1.34 MN, total 3.51 MN.
3. $R = 40$ (a 196 mm² profile): Johnson 253 MPa, die 1.99 MN, total 4.17 MN.
4. $R = 80$: total 4.49 MN. Going from $R = 10$ to 80 raises the force only 28 %, because the pressure grows with $\ln R$.
5. Cold steel ($K = 700$ MPa, $n = 0.2$) with $R = 4$: strain 1.386, mean flow stress 622.7 MPa. Numerical integration of $\int\sigma\,d\varepsilon$ gives 863.3 MJ/m³, equal to $\bar\sigma\varepsilon$. The Johnson pressure is 1793 MPa, 2.08 times the ideal value.
:::

```python file=extrusion.py
"""Extrusion: ideal work, Johnson's empirical die pressure and container friction, for hot aluminium and cold steel."""
import math
import numpy as np


def mean_flow_stress(K, n, eps):
    """Average of K e^n over 0..eps (strain hardening, cold work)."""
    return K * eps**n / (n + 1)


def ram_force(D0, Lb, R, sigma_f, m=1.0):
    """Die force by Johnson's formula p = sigma (0.8 + 1.5 ln R) plus container friction m k over the billet surface."""
    A0 = math.pi * D0**2 / 4
    p_die = sigma_f * (0.8 + 1.5 * math.log(R))
    F_die = p_die * A0
    F_fric = m * sigma_f / math.sqrt(3) * math.pi * D0 * Lb
    return F_die, F_fric


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # hot extrusion of a 6063 aluminium profile from a 100 mm billet, 300 mm long, at about 480 C
    D0, Lb, sig = 0.100, 0.300, 40e6
    A0 = math.pi * D0**2 / 4
    for R in (10, 40, 80):
        Fd, Ff = ram_force(D0, Lb, R, sig)
        print(f"R = {R:3d}: ideal pressure {sig*math.log(R)/1e6:.0f} MPa, Johnson {sig*(0.8+1.5*math.log(R))/1e6:.0f} MPa; die {Fd/1e6:.2f} MN + friction {Ff/1e6:.2f} MN = {(Fd+Ff)/1e6:.2f} MN")
    Fd, Ff = ram_force(D0, Lb, 40, sig)
    print(f"  profile area at R = 40: {A0/40*1e6:.0f} mm^2; the friction falls to zero as the billet is used up")
    # cold forward extrusion of a steel slug: K = 700 MPa, n = 0.2, R = 4
    K, n, R = 700e6, 0.2, 4.0
    eps = math.log(R)
    sbar = mean_flow_stress(K, n, eps)
    e = np.linspace(0, eps, 200001); s = K * e**n
    w = float(np.sum((s[1:] + s[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(e)))
    print(f"cold steel R = 4: strain {eps:.3f}, mean flow stress {sbar/1e6:.1f} MPa, ideal work {w/1e6:.1f} MJ/m^3")
    assert abs(w - sbar * eps) / w < 1e-4
    p = sbar * (0.8 + 1.5 * eps)
    print(f"  Johnson die pressure {p/1e6:.0f} MPa ({p/(sbar*eps):.2f} x ideal)")
    assert 0.8 + 1.5 * eps > eps
```

:::exercise Choosing a press
An aluminium heat-sink profile has an area of 1500 mm² and is extruded from a 178 mm (7 inch) billet, 600 mm long, at a flow stress of 35 MPa. Estimate the peak force with sticking container friction.
---solution---
1. $A_0 = \pi\cdot0.178^2/4 = 0.02488$ m², so $R = 24885/1500 = 16.6$.
2. Die: $35\,(0.8 + 1.5\ln16.6)\cdot0.02488 = 35\cdot5.012\cdot0.02488 = 4.36$ MN.
3. Friction: $(35/\sqrt3)\cdot\pi\cdot0.178\cdot0.6 = 6.78$ MN.
4. Peak about 11.1 MN, so a 12 MN (1200 t) press. Indirect extrusion would need only about 4.4 MN.
:::
