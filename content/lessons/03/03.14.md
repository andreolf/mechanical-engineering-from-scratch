Take a hot steel rod out of a furnace and clamp both ends in cold blocks. How long until the middle is safe to touch? The heat equation answers it, and its solution by separation of variables shows a pattern you'll meet again and again: the answer is a sum of modes, each decaying at its own rate, and after a while only the slowest one matters.

## Derivation in one paragraph

Energy balance on a slice of rod, area $A$, thickness $dx$: the rate of change of stored heat equals the net conducted inflow. Stored heat is $\rho c\,T\,A\,dx$; conducted flux is $q = -k\,\partial T/\partial x$ (Fourier, lesson 03.02). The divergence argument of lesson 03.08 in 1D gives

$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \alpha\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial x^2}, \qquad \alpha = \frac{k}{\rho c} .
$$

$\alpha$ is the **thermal diffusivity**: about $1.2\times10^{-5}$ m²/s for steel, $9.7\times10^{-5}$ for aluminium, $1.1\times10^{-4}$ for copper.

## Separation of variables

Rod of length $L$, ends held at $T = 0$, initial temperature $T_0(x)$. Try $T = X(x)\,\Theta(t)$:

$$
\frac{\Theta'}{\alpha\Theta} = \frac{X''}{X} = -\lambda .
$$

The left side depends only on $t$ and the right only on $x$, so both equal a constant. With $X(0) = X(L) = 0$, the solutions are $X_n = \sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$ with $\lambda_n = (n\pi/L)^2$, and $\Theta_n = e^{-\alpha\lambda_nt}$. Superpose and match the initial condition with a Fourier sine series (lesson 03.11):

$$
T(x, t) = \sum_n b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,e^{-\alpha(n\pi/L)^2t} .
$$

For a uniform initial temperature $T_0$, $b_n = 4T_0/(n\pi)$ for odd $n$.

## Mode $n$ decays like $e^{-n^2t/\tau}$

The time constant of the slowest mode is

$$
\tau_1 = \frac{L^2}{\pi^2\alpha} .
$$

Mode 3 decays 9 times faster, mode 5 25 times faster. After about $0.2\tau_1$ the profile is essentially a single sine arch shrinking exponentially. Doubling the length quadruples the time: diffusion time scales with length squared.

:::example When can you touch the rod?
A 0.5 m steel rod ($\alpha = 1.2\times10^{-5}$ m²/s) at 100 °C has its ends clamped at 0 °C (above the room temperature baseline). Treat the sides as insulated.

1. Time constant $\tau_1 = 0.25/(9.87\cdot1.2\times10^{-5}) = 2111$ s $= 35.2$ min.
2. Centre temperature from the series: 92.6 °C after 10 min, 54.3 °C after 30 min, 23.1 °C after 60 min, 4.2 °C after 120 min.
3. One-mode estimate for late times: $T_c \approx \frac{400}{\pi}e^{-t/\tau_1}$. At 60 min: $127.3\,e^{-1.706} = 23.1$ °C, matching the series to 3 digits.
4. To reach 45 °C (comfortable to touch): $t = \tau_1\ln(127.3/45) = 36.6$ min.
5. A copper rod ($\alpha = 1.11\times10^{-4}$) has $\tau_1 = 3.8$ min and gets there in under 4 minutes.
:::

```python file=heat_series.py
"""Heat equation in a rod: Fourier series solution checked against an explicit finite-difference march."""
import math
import numpy as np


def series(x, t, L, alpha, T0=100.0, terms=2001):
    s = np.zeros_like(x, dtype=float)
    for n in range(1, terms, 2):
        s += 4 * T0 / (n * math.pi) * np.sin(n * math.pi * x / L) * math.exp(-alpha * (n * math.pi / L) ** 2 * t)
    return s


def explicit_fd(L, alpha, t_end, nx=101, T0=100.0, r=0.4):
    x = np.linspace(0, L, nx); dx = x[1] - x[0]
    dt = r * dx * dx / alpha                    # r = alpha dt / dx^2 must stay below 1/2 (lesson 04.14)
    steps = int(round(t_end / dt)); dt = t_end / steps
    T = np.full(nx, T0); T[0] = T[-1] = 0.0
    for _ in range(steps):
        T[1:-1] += alpha * dt / dx ** 2 * (T[2:] - 2 * T[1:-1] + T[:-2])
    return x, T


if __name__ == "__main__":
    L, a = 0.5, 1.2e-5
    tau = L * L / (math.pi ** 2 * a)
    print(f"tau_1 = {tau:.0f} s = {tau/60:.1f} min")
    centre = {m: series(np.array([L / 2]), m * 60, L, a)[0] for m in (10, 30, 60, 120)}
    for m, v in centre.items():
        print(f"centre after {m:3d} min: {v:6.2f} C")
    assert abs(centre[60] - 400 / math.pi * math.exp(-3600 / tau)) < 0.01
    assert abs(centre[10] - 92.56) < 0.01 and abs(centre[30] - 54.25) < 0.01
    t45 = tau * math.log(400 / math.pi / 45)
    print(f"time to 45 C: {t45/60:.1f} min")
    # the finite-difference march agrees with the series
    x, T = explicit_fd(L, a, 1800)
    assert np.max(np.abs(T - series(x, 1800, L, a))) < 0.05
    # energy decays monotonically and the maximum principle holds
    profiles = [series(x, m * 60, L, a) for m in range(1, 121, 10)]
    assert all(p.max() <= 100 + 1e-9 and p.min() >= -1e-9 for p in profiles)
    assert all(np.sum(p2) < np.sum(p1) for p1, p2 in zip(profiles, profiles[1:]))
```

:::exercise Scale it up
A 2 m aluminium bar ($\alpha = 9.7\times10^{-5}$ m²/s) has the same end conditions. What's its slowest time constant, and how does it compare with the 0.5 m steel rod?
---solution---
1. $\tau_1 = L^2/(\pi^2\alpha) = 4/(9.87\cdot9.7\times10^{-5}) = 4178$ s $= 69.6$ min.
2. The steel rod's $\tau_1$ was 35.2 min.
3. Aluminium diffuses heat 8 times faster, but the bar is 4 times longer, so it takes $16/8 = 2$ times longer. Length squared wins.
:::
