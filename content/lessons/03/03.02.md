The gradient packs all the partial derivatives into one vector, and that vector has a meaning you can see: it points uphill, as steeply as possible, perpendicular to the contours. Heat flows against the temperature gradient, fluid accelerates down the pressure gradient, and optimisers walk against the gradient of the cost. This lesson builds all three ideas from one formula.

## Definition and directional derivatives

$$
\nabla f = \Big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\Big) .
$$

The rate of change of $f$ in the direction of a unit vector $\mathbf u$ is the **directional derivative**

$$
D_{\mathbf u}f = \nabla f\cdot\mathbf u = |\nabla f|\cos\theta .
$$

It's largest when $\mathbf u$ points along $\nabla f$ (rate $|\nabla f|$), zero when $\mathbf u$ is perpendicular to it (moving along a contour), and most negative straight against it.

## Gradients in physics

- **Fourier's law**: heat flux $\mathbf q = -k\nabla T$. Heat runs downhill in temperature, fastest where the isotherms crowd together.
- **Pressure force**: a fluid parcel feels $-\nabla p$ per unit volume.
- **Conservative forces**: $\mathbf F = -\nabla V$ for a potential energy $V$ (more in lesson 03.07).

## Gradient descent

To minimise $f$, step against the gradient: $\mathbf x_{k+1} = \mathbf x_k - \eta\nabla f(\mathbf x_k)$. For the quadratic bowl $f = \tfrac12\mathbf x^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf x$, the gradient is $A\mathbf x$ and each step multiplies the error in eigen-direction $i$ by $1 - \eta\lambda_i$. So:

- The step must satisfy $\eta < 2/\lambda_{\max}$, or the steepest direction blows up.
- With the best fixed step, the error shrinks by $\frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1}$ per step, where $\kappa = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$. Long, narrow valleys (large $\kappa$) make gradient descent zigzag and crawl.

That's the same condition number from Phase 02, and it's why CG (lesson 02.15) and Newton's method (03.03) beat plain descent.

:::widget gradient
Cyan arrows are the gradient, always perpendicular to the contours. Raise the elongation κ and the path zigzags; push the step past the stable limit and it diverges.
:::

:::example Heat flow on a plate
A plate has temperature $T(x, y) = 100 - 2x^2 - y^2$ °C, with $x, y$ in metres, and conductivity $k = 50$ W/(m K).

1. $\nabla T = (-4x, -2y)$. At $(1, 2)$: $\nabla T = (-4, -4)$ °C/m.
2. Rate of change heading in direction $(3, 4)/5$: $D_{\mathbf u}T = -4\cdot0.6 - 4\cdot0.8 = -5.6$ °C/m.
3. Steepest increase: along $(-1, -1)/\sqrt2$, towards the hot centre, at $|\nabla T| = 4\sqrt2 = 5.66$ °C/m.
4. Heat flux: $\mathbf q = -k\nabla T = (200, 200)$ W/m², magnitude 283 W/m², pointing away from the centre.
5. Check perpendicularity: the isotherm through $(1, 2)$ is $2x^2 + y^2 = 6$, with tangent direction $(-y, 2x) = (-2, 2)$. Its dot product with $\nabla T = (-4, -4)$ is $8 - 8 = 0$.
:::

```python file=gradient.py
"""Gradients, directional derivatives and gradient descent on quadratic bowls."""
import math
import numpy as np


def num_grad(f, x, h=1e-6):
    x = np.asarray(x, float); g = np.zeros_like(x)
    for i in range(len(x)):
        e = np.zeros_like(x); e[i] = h
        g[i] = (f(x + e) - f(x - e)) / (2 * h)
    return g


def descend(grad, x0, eta, tol=1e-8, max_iter=100000):
    x = np.asarray(x0, float)
    for k in range(max_iter):
        g = grad(x)
        if np.linalg.norm(g) < tol:
            return x, k
        x = x - eta * g
        if not np.all(np.isfinite(x)) or np.linalg.norm(x) > 1e12:
            return x, -1                          # diverged
    return x, max_iter


if __name__ == "__main__":
    T = lambda p: 100 - 2 * p[0] ** 2 - p[1] ** 2
    g = num_grad(T, [1, 2])
    assert np.allclose(g, [-4, -4], atol=1e-6)
    u = np.array([3, 4]) / 5
    assert abs(g @ u + 5.6) < 1e-6 and abs(np.linalg.norm(g) - 4 * math.sqrt(2)) < 1e-6
    assert abs(g @ np.array([-2, 2])) < 1e-6                 # perpendicular to the isotherm
    q = -50 * g
    print(f"heat flux at (1, 2): {np.round(q, 3)} W/m^2, |q| = {np.linalg.norm(q):.1f}")
    # gradient descent on bowls with growing condition number
    print(" kappa  steps (best fixed step)  theory")
    for kappa in (2, 10, 100):
        A = np.diag([1.0, kappa])
        eta = 2 / (1 + kappa)                                # optimal fixed step
        x, k = descend(lambda x: A @ x, [1.0, 1.0], eta)
        rate = (kappa - 1) / (kappa + 1)
        theory = math.log(1e-8 / math.sqrt(1 + kappa ** 2)) / math.log(rate)
        print(f"{kappa:5d}  {k:8d}  {theory:10.0f}")
        assert k > 0 and abs(k - theory) < 0.1 * theory + 3
    # a step beyond 2 / lambda_max diverges
    assert descend(lambda x: np.diag([1.0, 10.0]) @ x, [1.0, 1.0], 2.05 / 10)[1] == -1
```

:::exercise The fastest way down
A hill has height $z = 300 - 0.002x^2 - 0.001y^2$ m. At the point $(100, 200)$, in which direction does water run, and how steep is the slope there in percent?
---solution---
1. $\nabla z = (-0.004x, -0.002y) = (-0.4, -0.4)$.
2. Water runs along $-\nabla z = (0.4, 0.4)$, that is towards $(1, 1)/\sqrt2$, away from the summit.
3. Slope $= |\nabla z| = 0.4\sqrt2 = 0.566$, a 56.6% grade (about $29.5^\circ$). Steep: that's a black ski run.
:::
