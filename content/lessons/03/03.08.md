How much water flows through a pipe cross-section? How much heat leaves a hot electronics box? Both are **fluxes**: a field integrated over a surface. The divergence theorem says that the total flux out of a closed surface equals what's produced inside, and applying it to a tiny box gives the conservation laws of fluid mechanics and heat transfer.

## Flux through a surface

For a vector field $\mathbf F$ and a surface $S$ with unit normal $\mathbf n$,

$$
\Phi = \iint_S\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dA .
$$

If $\mathbf F = \rho\mathbf u$ is a mass flux, $\Phi$ is kg/s through $S$. If $\mathbf F = \mathbf q$ is a heat flux, $\Phi$ is watts.

## Divergence

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf F = \frac{\partial F_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_z}{\partial z} .
$$

It's the net outflow per unit volume from an infinitesimal box: the flux out of the right face minus the flux into the left face is $\frac{\partial F_x}{\partial x}\,dx\cdot dy\,dz$, and the same in $y$ and $z$. Positive divergence is a source, negative a sink, zero means what flows in flows out.

## The divergence theorem

For a closed surface $S$ bounding a volume $V$:

$$
\oiint_S\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dA = \iiint_V\nabla\cdot\mathbf F\,dV .
$$

## From the theorem to conservation laws

Mass in a fixed volume changes only by flux through its boundary: $\frac{d}{dt}\iiint\rho\,dV = -\oiint\rho\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf n\,dA$. Turn the surface integral into a volume integral and, because the volume is arbitrary,

$$
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf u) = 0 .
$$

That's the continuity equation. For an incompressible fluid it reduces to $\nabla\cdot\mathbf u = 0$. The same argument with energy gives the heat equation (lesson 03.14).

:::example Flow through a pipe
Laminar flow in a pipe of radius $R = 10$ mm has the parabolic profile $u(r) = u_{\max}(1 - r^2/R^2)$ with $u_{\max} = 0.4$ m/s.

1. Flux through the cross-section: $Q = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^R u_{\max}(1 - r^2/R^2)\,r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi u_{\max}\big(\tfrac{R^2}{2} - \tfrac{R^2}{4}\big) = \tfrac\pi2u_{\max}R^2$.
2. Numbers: $Q = \tfrac\pi2\cdot0.4\cdot0.01^2 = 6.28\times10^{-5}$ m³/s $= 3.77$ L/min.
3. Mean velocity: $Q/(\pi R^2) = u_{\max}/2 = 0.2$ m/s.
4. Divergence theorem check on the field $\mathbf F = (x, y, z)$: $\nabla\cdot\mathbf F = 3$, so the flux out of a sphere of radius 2 is $3\cdot\tfrac43\pi\cdot8 = 32\pi = 100.5$. Directly: on the sphere $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n = |\mathbf r| = 2$, times area $16\pi$, gives the same $32\pi$.
:::

```python file=divergence.py
"""Flux through surfaces, numerical divergence, and the divergence theorem."""
import math
import numpy as np


def divergence(F, p, h=1e-5):
    p = np.asarray(p, float)
    return sum((F(*(p + h * e))[i] - F(*(p - h * e))[i]) / (2 * h) for i, e in enumerate(np.eye(len(p))))


def flux_sphere(F, R, n=400):
    """Midpoint rule on (phi, theta) with dA = R^2 sin(phi) dphi dtheta."""
    hp, ht = math.pi / n, 2 * math.pi / n
    phi = hp * (np.arange(n) + 0.5); th = ht * (np.arange(n) + 0.5)
    P, T = np.meshgrid(phi, th)
    nx, ny, nz = np.sin(P) * np.cos(T), np.sin(P) * np.sin(T), np.cos(P)
    Fx, Fy, Fz = F(R * nx, R * ny, R * nz)
    return np.sum((Fx * nx + Fy * ny + Fz * nz) * R ** 2 * np.sin(P)) * hp * ht


if __name__ == "__main__":
    F = lambda x, y, z: (x, y, z)
    flux = flux_sphere(F, 2.0)
    print(f"flux of (x, y, z) out of a sphere of radius 2: {flux:.5f}, 32 pi = {32 * math.pi:.5f}")
    assert abs(flux - 32 * math.pi) < 1e-3 and abs(divergence(F, [0.3, -1, 2]) - 3) < 1e-8
    # a field with varying divergence: G = (x^3, y^3, z^3), div = 3 r^2, total = 3 * 4 pi R^5 / 5
    G = lambda x, y, z: (x ** 3, y ** 3, z ** 3)
    assert abs(flux_sphere(G, 1.5) - 12 * math.pi * 1.5 ** 5 / 5) < 1e-2
    # pipe flow rate: integrate the parabolic profile in polar coordinates
    R, umax = 0.01, 0.4
    r = np.linspace(0, R, 100001); u = umax * (1 - (r / R) ** 2)
    Q = 2 * math.pi * np.trapezoid(u * r, r)
    print(f"pipe flow Q = {Q*6e4:.3f} L/min, mean velocity {Q/(math.pi*R**2):.4f} m/s")
    assert abs(Q - math.pi / 2 * umax * R ** 2) < 1e-12
    # an incompressible 2D flow has zero divergence: stagnation flow u = (x, -y, 0)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(13)
    for p in rng.uniform(-1, 1, (20, 3)):
        assert abs(divergence(lambda x, y, z: (x, -y, 0 * z), p)) < 1e-8
```

:::exercise Heat leaving a box
An electronics enclosure is a cube of side 0.2 m. Inside, components dissipate 40 W uniformly. At steady state, what's the average heat flux through the walls? If one face is insulated, what's the average flux through the other five?
---solution---
1. Steady state: the total flux out equals the heat generated, 40 W (the divergence theorem with $\nabla\cdot\mathbf q$ equal to the volumetric heat source).
2. Total wall area $6\cdot0.04 = 0.24$ m², so the average flux is $40/0.24 = 167$ W/m².
3. With one face insulated: $40/0.2 = 200$ W/m² through the other five. The total can't change, so the remaining faces work harder.
:::
