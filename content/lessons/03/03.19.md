A pendulum's state is two numbers, angle and angular velocity. Plot one against the other and every possible motion becomes a curve in the **phase plane**. Equilibria are points, oscillations are loops, and linear algebra (the eigenvalues of a Jacobian) tells you whether nearby motions spiral in, spiral out, or escape. It's the qualitative toolkit for nonlinear mechanics.

## First-order form

Any ODE system can be written as $\dot{\mathbf y} = \mathbf f(\mathbf y)$. The pendulum $\ddot\theta + 2\zeta\omega_0\dot\theta + \omega_0^2\sin\theta = 0$ becomes, with $\mathbf y = (\theta, \omega)$,

$$
\dot\theta = \omega, \qquad \dot\omega = -\omega_0^2\sin\theta - 2\zeta\omega_0\,\omega .
$$

At every point of the plane, $\mathbf f$ is an arrow: the direction field. Trajectories follow the arrows and never cross (uniqueness).

## Equilibria and linearisation

Equilibria are where $\mathbf f = \mathbf 0$: for the pendulum, $\theta = 0$ (hanging) and $\theta = \pi$ (inverted). Near an equilibrium $\mathbf y^*$, let $\mathbf y = \mathbf y^* + \boldsymbol\eta$:

$$
\dot{\boldsymbol\eta} \approx J\,\boldsymbol\eta, \qquad J = \frac{\partial\mathbf f}{\partial\mathbf y}\Big|_{\mathbf y^*} .
$$

The solution is a combination of $e^{\lambda t}$ terms, with $\lambda$ the eigenvalues of $J$ (lesson 02.09):

| Eigenvalues | Picture | Stability |
|---|---|---|
| real, both negative | node | stable |
| real, opposite signs | saddle | unstable |
| complex, negative real part | spiral in | stable |
| complex, positive real part | spiral out | unstable |
| purely imaginary | centre | neutral (linear test inconclusive) |

## The pendulum's two equilibria

- At $\theta = 0$: $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -\omega_0^2 & -2\zeta\omega_0 \end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $-\zeta\omega_0 \pm i\omega_0\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$. A stable spiral for $0 < \zeta < 1$.
- At $\theta = \pi$: $\cos\pi = -1$ flips the sign: $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ \omega_0^2 & -2\zeta\omega_0 \end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues real with opposite signs. A saddle: balance a broomstick and it falls.

Without damping, energy is conserved and trajectories are level curves of $E = \tfrac12\omega^2 - \omega_0^2\cos\theta$. The curve through the saddle, the **separatrix**, divides swinging from going over the top: $\omega = \pm2\omega_0\cos(\theta/2)$.

A damped pendulum (ω₀ = 1). Start with enough speed and it whirls over the top before damping wins and it spirals into a hanging rest position, possibly a whole turn away from where it started.
:::

:::example Will the swing go over the top?
A pendulum of length $L = 1$ m hangs at rest. You hit it to give the bob a speed $v_0$.

1. $\omega_0 = \sqrt{g/L} = 3.132$ rad/s.
2. Undamped separatrix at $\theta = 0$: $\omega = 2\omega_0 = 6.264$ rad/s, so $v_0 = 6.26$ m/s at the bob.
3. Energy check: to reach the top the bob climbs $2L = 2$ m, needing $\tfrac12v_0^2 = 2gL$, so $v_0 = \sqrt{4gL} = 6.26$ m/s. The phase-plane and energy answers agree.
4. Linearised at the bottom with $\zeta = 0.1$: eigenvalues $-0.313 \pm 3.116i$. Each swing period ($2\pi/3.116 = 2.02$ s) the amplitude shrinks by $e^{-0.313\cdot2.02} = 0.53$.
5. At the top: eigenvalues of $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 9.81 & -0.626 \end{pmatrix}$ are $2.84$ and $-3.46$. A small tilt grows by a factor $e$ every 0.35 s.
:::

```python file=phase_plane.py
"""Equilibria, Jacobians and phase-plane trajectories for the damped pendulum."""
import math
import numpy as np


def jacobian(f, y, h=1e-6):
    y = np.asarray(y, float)
    return np.column_stack([(f(y + h * e) - f(y - h * e)) / (2 * h) for e in np.eye(len(y))])


def classify(J, tol=1e-9):
    lam = np.linalg.eigvals(J)
    re, im = lam.real, lam.imag
    if np.all(np.abs(im) > tol):
        return "centre" if np.all(np.abs(re) < tol) else ("stable spiral" if np.all(re < 0) else "unstable spiral")
    if re.min() < -tol and re.max() > tol:
        return "saddle"
    return "stable node" if np.all(re < 0) else "unstable node"


def rk4_path(f, y0, h, n):
    y = np.array(y0, float); out = [y.copy()]
    for _ in range(n):
        k1 = f(y); k2 = f(y + h / 2 * k1); k3 = f(y + h / 2 * k2); k4 = f(y + h * k3)
        y = y + h / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4); out.append(y.copy())
    return np.array(out)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    g, L, z = 9.81, 1.0, 0.1
    w0 = math.sqrt(g / L)
    f = lambda y: np.array([y[1], -w0 ** 2 * math.sin(y[0]) - 2 * z * w0 * y[1]])
    J0, Jpi = jacobian(f, [0, 0]), jacobian(f, [math.pi, 0])
    print("bottom:", classify(J0), np.round(np.linalg.eigvals(J0), 3))
    print("top:   ", classify(Jpi), np.round(np.linalg.eigvals(Jpi), 3))
    assert classify(J0) == "stable spiral" and classify(Jpi) == "saddle"
    assert classify(jacobian(lambda y: np.array([y[1], -w0 ** 2 * math.sin(y[0])]), [0, 0])) == "centre"
    # undamped: just below the separatrix speed it swings back, just above it goes over
    fu = lambda y: np.array([y[1], -w0 ** 2 * math.sin(y[0])])
    below = rk4_path(fu, [0, 0.99 * 2 * w0], 1e-3, 6000)
    above = rk4_path(fu, [0, 1.01 * 2 * w0], 1e-3, 6000)
    assert below[:, 0].max() < math.pi and above[:, 0].max() > 2 * math.pi
    E = 0.5 * below[:, 1] ** 2 - w0 ** 2 * np.cos(below[:, 0])
    assert np.ptp(E) < 1e-8 * abs(E[0])                       # energy conserved to RK4 accuracy
    # damped: a strong kick goes over the top, then settles a whole turn away
    path = rk4_path(f, [0, 8.0], 1e-3, 40000)
    turns = round(path[-1, 0] / (2 * math.pi))
    print(f"kick of 8 rad/s with zeta = 0.1: settles {turns} turn(s) from the start")
    assert turns >= 1 and abs(path[-1, 0] - 2 * math.pi * turns) < 1e-3
```

:::exercise Classify a predator-prey model
For $\dot x = x(1 - y)$, $\dot y = y(x - 1)$, find the equilibria and classify them.
---solution---
1. Equilibria: $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$.
2. $J = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - y & -x \\ y & x - 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. At $(0, 0)$: $J = \operatorname{diag}(1, -1)$, eigenvalues $\pm1$, a saddle.
4. At $(1, 1)$: $J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$, eigenvalues $\pm i$, a linear centre. The linear test is inconclusive, but this system has a conserved quantity $x - \ln x + y - \ln y$, so the orbits really are closed loops.
:::
