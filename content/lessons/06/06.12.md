Most real beams have more supports than statics needs: a floor joist built into a wall at one end, a bridge girder running continuously over several piers. These are indeterminate (05.08), and lesson 06.05 showed the recipe: add compatibility. For beams, the standard deflection formulas plus superposition make it quick.

## Superposition

For linear elastic beams with small deflections, the response to several loads is the sum of the responses to each one. That lets you build complicated cases from a table of standard ones.

## The method: release, then restore

1. **Release** enough supports to make the beam determinate.
2. Compute the deflection at each released support under the real loads.
3. Apply the unknown support reaction as a load on the released beam and compute its deflection.
4. **Compatibility**: the total deflection at the support must be zero. Solve for the reaction.

## Propped cantilever

Clamp at one end, prop at the other, uniform load $q$. Release the prop. The cantilever tip drops $qL^4/(8EI)$; the prop force $R$ lifts it by $RL^3/(3EI)$. Setting the sum to zero:

$$
R = \frac{3}{8}qL .
$$

The wall takes the other $5qL/8$. The bending moment changes completely: from $-qL^2/2$ at the wall of a plain cantilever to $-qL^2/8$, with a sagging peak of $9qL^2/128$ at $3L/8$ from the prop. That's **moment redistribution**, and it's why continuous beams are more efficient than chains of simple spans.

## Two equal spans

Remove the middle support of a continuous beam over two spans $L$: the $2L$ simple span sags $5q(2L)^4/(384EI)$ at the centre. A central point load $R$ lifts it $R(2L)^3/(48EI)$. So the middle support carries $R = 5qL/4$, more than the $qL$ it would carry if the spans were simply supported, and each end carries $3qL/8$.

:::example A propped cantilever floor beam
$L = 4$ m, $q = 5$ kN/m, the I-section from 06.10.

1. Released cantilever tip: $qL^4/(8EI) = 41.28$ mm down.
2. Prop: $R = 41.28/(L^3/3EI) = 7.5$ kN $= 3qL/8$. Wall: 12.5 kN.
3. With $R$ applied, the computed tip deflection is $-1.6\times10^{-7}$ mm, zero to numerical accuracy.
4. Wall moment $-10$ kN m $= -qL^2/8$; largest sagging moment 5.625 kN m $= 9qL^2/128$ at 2.5 m from the wall.
5. Two spans of 4 m: middle reaction $25$ kN $= 5qL/4$, ends 7.5 kN.
:::

```python file=propped.py
"""Indeterminate beams by superposition: a propped cantilever (R = 3qL/8) and a two-span continuous beam (5qL/4)."""
import numpy as np


def cumtrapz(f, x):
    return np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum((f[1:] + f[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(x))])


def cantilever_w(x, M, EI):
    return cumtrapz(cumtrapz(M / EI, x), x)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    EI, L, q = 210e3 * 1.8456e7, 4000.0, 5.0       # N mm^2, mm, N/mm
    # superposition at the propped end: cantilever under q drops q L^4 / 8EI, the prop R lifts R L^3 / 3EI
    R = (q * L**4 / (8 * EI)) / (L**3 / (3 * EI))
    print(f"propped cantilever: prop reaction {R / 1e3:.4f} kN (3qL/8 = {3 * q * L / 8 / 1e3:.4f}), wall reaction {(q * L - R) / 1e3:.4f} kN")
    assert abs(R - 3 * q * L / 8) < 1e-9
    # check numerically: with that prop force the tip deflection of the cantilever is zero
    x = np.linspace(0, L, 8001)
    M = -q * (L - x) ** 2 / 2 + R * (L - x)
    w = cantilever_w(x, M, EI)
    print(f"  tip deflection with the prop: {w[-1]:.2e} mm; without it: {cantilever_w(x, -q * (L - x) ** 2 / 2, EI)[-1]:.4f} mm")
    assert abs(w[-1]) < 1e-6
    # moment redistribution: wall moment -qL^2/8, largest sagging 9qL^2/128 at 5L/8 from the wall
    print(f"  wall moment {M[0] / 1e6:.4f} kN m (-qL^2/8 = {-q * L**2 / 8 / 1e6:.4f}); max sagging {M.max() / 1e6:.4f} kN m (9qL^2/128 = {9 * q * L**2 / 128 / 1e6:.4f}) at x = {x[M.argmax()]:.0f} mm")
    assert abs(M[0] + q * L**2 / 8) < 1e-6 and abs(M.max() - 9 * q * L**2 / 128) < 1e-3
    # two equal spans L on three supports: remove the middle support, compute the gap, push it back with R
    gap = 5 * q * (2 * L) ** 4 / (384 * EI)
    Rm = gap / ((2 * L) ** 3 / (48 * EI))
    print(f"two-span beam: middle reaction {Rm / 1e3:.4f} kN (5qL/4 = {5 * q * L / 4 / 1e3:.4f}), ends {(2 * q * L - Rm) / 2 / 1e3:.4f} kN each (3qL/8)")
    assert abs(Rm - 5 * q * L / 4) < 1e-9
```

:::exercise A prop under a point load
A cantilever of length $L$ carries a point load $P$ at its free end, which also rests on a prop. What does the prop carry? What if the load is at mid-span instead? (A tip load $F$ at $L$ gives tip deflection $FL^3/3EI$; a load $P$ at $L/2$ gives a tip deflection of $5PL^3/48EI$.)
---solution---
1. Load at the tip, right on the prop: the prop carries all of it, $R = P$. The beam isn't bent at all.
2. Load at mid-span: compatibility $RL^3/(3EI) = 5PL^3/(48EI)$, so $R = 5P/16 = 0.3125P$. The wall takes $11P/16$.
:::
