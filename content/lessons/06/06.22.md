Cracks in aircraft skins start at rivet holes. Shafts break at the shoulder where the diameter changes. The de Havilland Comet, the first jet airliner, lost aircraft to fatigue cracks that grew from the corners of square windows. The average stress in a part tells you little about where it fails: geometry concentrates stress, and the peak is what counts.

## A hole in a plate

A wide plate carries a uniform tension $\sigma$. Drill a circular hole of radius $a$. The force lines must flow round the hole, and they crowd together at its sides. In 1898 Kirsch solved the elasticity equations exactly. On the hole edge ($r = a$), with $\theta$ measured from the loading direction, the hoop stress is

$$
\sigma_{\theta\theta}(a, \theta) = \sigma\,(1 - 2\cos2\theta) .
$$

- At $\theta = 90°$ (the sides of the hole, across the load): $3\sigma$.
- At $\theta = 0°$ (on the load axis): $-\sigma$, compression in a plate that is only being pulled.

The **stress concentration factor** is $K_t = \sigma_{max}/\sigma_{nominal} = 3$, whatever the size of the hole. Small holes are not safer than large ones; they just affect a smaller region.

## How fast it fades

Across the plate from the hole's side, $\sigma_x = \sigma\left(1 + \tfrac12(a/y)^2 + \tfrac32(a/y)^4\right)$: 1.22σ at two radii, 1.07σ at three. The peak is very local, which is why it matters most for fatigue and brittle materials (Phase 09) and less for a single overload on ductile steel, which simply yields locally.

## Sharper is worse

An elliptical hole with semi-axis $a$ across the load and $b$ along it has $K_t = 1 + 2a/b$. A crack is an ellipse with $b \to 0$, and the stress at its tip goes to infinity in theory. That is where fracture mechanics begins. In practice: round the corners, use generous fillets, drill a small hole at the tip of a crack to blunt it, and avoid sudden changes of section.

:::example Peak and decay round a 10 mm hole
Remote tension 100 MPa, $a = 5$ mm.

1. The code evaluates Kirsch's solution round the hole edge: maximum 300.00 MPa at 90°, minimum −100.00 MPa at 0°. The radial and shear stresses on the edge are zero, as a free surface requires.
2. Moving across the plate from the side of the hole: 300.0, 151.9, 121.9, 107.4 and 102.2 MPa at 1, 1.5, 2, 3 and 5 radii.
3. Far away, 400 radii out, the field is back to $\sigma_x = 100$, $\sigma_y = \tau_{xy} = 0$ within 0.01 MPa.
4. Elliptical holes: $a/b$ = 2, 5 and 20 give $K_t$ = 5, 11 and 41.
:::

```python file=kirsch.py
"""Kirsch's solution: stresses round a circular hole in a wide plate under remote tension s (along x)."""
import math
import numpy as np


def kirsch_polar(s, a, r, th):
    """Return sigma_rr, sigma_tt, tau_rt at (r, theta), theta measured from the loading direction."""
    q = (a / r) ** 2
    srr = s / 2 * (1 - q) + s / 2 * (1 - 4 * q + 3 * q**2) * np.cos(2 * th)
    stt = s / 2 * (1 + q) - s / 2 * (1 + 3 * q**2) * np.cos(2 * th)
    trt = -s / 2 * (1 + 2 * q - 3 * q**2) * np.sin(2 * th)
    return srr, stt, trt


def kirsch_xy(s, a, x, y):
    r, th = np.hypot(x, y), np.arctan2(y, x)
    srr, stt, trt = kirsch_polar(s, a, r, th)
    c, sn = np.cos(th), np.sin(th)
    sxx = srr * c**2 + stt * sn**2 - 2 * trt * sn * c
    syy = srr * sn**2 + stt * c**2 + 2 * trt * sn * c
    txy = (srr - stt) * sn * c + trt * (c**2 - sn**2)
    return sxx, syy, txy


if __name__ == "__main__":
    s, a = 100.0, 5.0                                  # 100 MPa remote, 10 mm hole
    th = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, 3601)
    _, stt, _ = kirsch_polar(s, a, a, th)
    i = int(np.argmax(stt))
    print(f"hoop stress on the hole edge: max {stt[i]:.2f} MPa at {math.degrees(th[i]):.1f} deg, min {stt.min():.2f} MPa at 0 deg")
    assert abs(stt.max() / s - 3) < 1e-9 and abs(stt.min() / s + 1) < 1e-9
    # the boundary is traction free
    srr, _, trt = kirsch_polar(s, a, a, th)
    assert np.allclose(srr, 0, atol=1e-9) and np.allclose(trt, 0, atol=1e-9)
    # decay along the line across the load through the hole centre (x = 0)
    for k in (1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5):
        sxx, _, _ = kirsch_xy(s, a, 0.0, k * a)
        print(f"  sigma_xx at y = {k:.1f} a: {sxx:.2f} MPa")
    sxx2, _, _ = kirsch_xy(s, a, 0.0, 2 * a)
    assert abs(sxx2 - s * (1 + 1 / 8 + 3 / 32)) < 1e-9
    # far away the plate doesn't notice the hole
    sxx, syy, txy = kirsch_xy(s, a, 400 * a, 300 * a)
    assert abs(sxx - s) < 0.01 and abs(syy) < 0.01 and abs(txy) < 0.01
    # map the field: grid of sigma_xx, its peak sits on the hole edge at 90 degrees
    X, Y = np.meshgrid(np.linspace(-20, 20, 401), np.linspace(-20, 20, 401))
    mask = np.hypot(X, Y) >= a
    Xs, Ys = np.where(mask, X, 2 * a), np.where(mask, Y, 0.0)     # dummy points inside the hole, masked out below
    F = np.where(mask, kirsch_xy(s, a, Xs, Ys)[0], np.nan)
    j = np.nanargmax(F)
    print(f"field peak {np.nanmax(F):.2f} MPa at ({X.flat[j]:.1f}, {Y.flat[j]:.1f}) mm")
    # an elliptical hole: Kt = 1 + 2 a/b (semi-axis a across the load)
    for ab in (1, 2, 5, 20):
        print(f"  ellipse a/b = {ab:2d}: Kt = {1 + 2 * ab}")
```

:::exercise A bolt hole in a bracket
A steel strap, 60 mm wide and 8 mm thick, carries 40 kN. A 12 mm bolt hole is drilled in the middle. Find the nominal stress on the net section and the peak stress at the hole, taking $K_t \approx 3$ on the gross stress. (For a hole this size relative to the width, the measured $K_t$ on the net section is about 2.5.)
---solution---
1. Gross stress: $40000/(60\cdot8) = 83.3$ MPa. Peak with $K_t = 3$: 250 MPa.
2. Net section: $40000/((60 - 12)\cdot8) = 104.2$ MPa. Peak with $K_{t,net} = 2.5$: 260 MPa.
3. The two estimates agree within a few percent. For a 235 MPa steel the hole edge would yield locally, which is harmless once but would start fatigue cracks under repeated load.
:::
