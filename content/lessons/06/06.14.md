Every engine, motor and pedal drives something through a twisted shaft: the propeller shaft under a car, the axle of a wind turbine, a screwdriver, a bike's bottom bracket. Circular shafts are the one torsion problem with a clean exact answer, and it explains why drive shafts are hollow.

## Geometry of the twist

Twist a circular shaft by an angle $\varphi$ over length $L$. By symmetry, plane cross-sections stay plane and radii stay straight; each section just rotates. A line drawn along the surface becomes a helix. At radius $r$, the shear strain is

$$
\gamma = r\,\frac{d\varphi}{dx} ,
$$

growing linearly from zero at the centre. With Hooke's law in shear $\tau = G\gamma$ (06.03), the stress grows linearly too.

## Torque and stress

The torque is the sum of $\tau\,r$ over the section: $T = \int \tau r\,dA = G\,\varphi'\int r^2\,dA = GJ\,\varphi'$, with the **polar second moment of area** $J = \int r^2\,dA$. So:

$$
\tau = \frac{T\,r}{J}, \qquad \varphi = \frac{TL}{GJ}, \qquad J_{solid} = \frac{\pi D^4}{32}, \quad J_{tube} = \frac{\pi(D^4 - d^4)}{32}.
$$

$GJ$ is the torsional stiffness, the twin of $EI$ in bending. Stepped shafts add their twists segment by segment.

## Why drive shafts are hollow

The material near the centre is barely stressed and adds little to $J$. Removing it saves weight with only a small loss of strength. Power and torque are linked by $P = T\omega$ (Phase 02), so a fast-spinning shaft carries a modest torque for a large power.

:::example A car's propeller shaft
A hollow steel shaft, 50 mm outside and 42 mm inside, 1.4 m long, carries 1.2 kN m. $G = 80$ GPa.

1. $J = \pi(50^4 - 42^4)/32 = 3.081\times10^5$ mm⁴ (the code checks it by integrating $2\pi r^3\,dr$).
2. $\tau_{max} = 1.2\times10^6\cdot25/J = 97.37$ MPa.
3. Twist: $\varphi = TL/(GJ) = 0.0682$ rad $= 3.905°$.
4. A solid shaft with the same peak stress needs $D = (16T/\pi\tau)^{1/3} = 39.74$ mm, and is 2.15 times heavier.
5. Stress in that solid shaft at $r = 0, R/4, R/2, 3R/4, R$: 0, 24.3, 48.7, 73.0, 97.4 MPa.
:::

```python file=torsion.py
"""Torsion of circular shafts: tau = T r / J, phi = T L / (G J); solid against hollow."""
import math
import numpy as np


def J_tube(D, d=0.0):
    return math.pi * (D**4 - d**4) / 32


def J_numeric(D, d=0.0, n=200001):
    """J = int r^2 dA = int 2 pi r^3 dr."""
    r = np.linspace(d / 2, D / 2, n)
    return np.trapezoid(2 * math.pi * r**3, r)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    G = 80e3                                       # MPa, steel
    # a car propeller shaft: hollow steel 50 x 42 mm, 1.4 m long, 1.2 kN m
    D, d, L, T = 50.0, 42.0, 1400.0, 1.2e6
    J = J_tube(D, d)
    assert abs(J_numeric(D, d) - J) < 1e-6 * J
    tau, phi = T * (D / 2) / J, T * L / (G * J)
    print(f"hollow shaft: J = {J:.1f} mm^4 (numeric {J_numeric(D, d):.1f}), tau_max = {tau:.3f} MPa, twist {math.degrees(phi):.3f} deg")
    # a solid shaft with the same maximum stress
    Ds = (16 * T / (math.pi * tau)) ** (1 / 3)
    A_h, A_s = math.pi * (D**2 - d**2) / 4, math.pi * Ds**2 / 4
    print(f"solid shaft with the same tau_max: D = {Ds:.3f} mm, mass ratio solid / hollow = {A_s / A_h:.3f}")
    assert abs(T * (Ds / 2) / J_tube(Ds) - tau) < 1e-9
    # shear stress grows linearly with radius, so the core of a solid shaft does little work
    r = np.linspace(0, Ds / 2, 5)
    print("solid shaft tau at r = 0, R/4, R/2, 3R/4, R:", np.round(T * r / J_tube(Ds), 2), "MPa")
    # twist of the hollow shaft by integrating d(phi)/dx = T / (G J) along a stepped shaft: 0.9 m hollow + 0.5 m solid 40 mm
    phi2 = T * 900 / (G * J) + T * 500 / (G * J_tube(40.0))
    print(f"stepped shaft (0.9 m hollow + 0.5 m solid 40 mm): twist {math.degrees(phi2):.3f} deg")
```

:::exercise A wind turbine low-speed shaft
A small turbine produces 30 kW at 30 rpm. Its hollow shaft has 120 mm outside diameter. If the allowable shear stress is 60 MPa, what is the largest bore?
---solution---
1. $\omega = 30\cdot2\pi/60 = 3.1416$ rad/s, so $T = 30000/3.1416 = 9549$ N m $= 9.549\times10^6$ N mm.
2. Need $J \ge T\cdot60/60 = 9.549\times10^6$ mm⁴ (with $r = 60$ mm and $\tau = 60$ MPa).
3. $\pi(120^4 - d^4)/32 \ge 9.549\times10^6$ gives $d^4 \le 2.0736\times10^8 - 9.727\times10^7 = 1.1009\times10^8$, so $d \le 102.4$ mm.
:::
