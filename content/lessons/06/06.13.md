Bending stress gets most of the attention, but beams also carry shear. Short, heavily loaded beams, timber joists near their supports, thin webs of steel girders and glued joints in laminated beams can all fail in shear first. The shear stress is not uniform over the section, and finding where it peaks takes one elegant formula.

## Where shear stress comes from

Cut a short slice $dx$ out of a beam where the moment changes from $M$ to $M + dM$. The bending stresses on the two faces differ slightly. Now cut that slice again horizontally at height $y$ and look at the part above the cut. The unbalanced bending force on it must be carried by a horizontal shear stress on the cut. Working it through (with $dM/dx = V$) gives **Jourawski's formula**:

$$
\tau(y) = \frac{V\,Q(y)}{I\,b(y)}, \qquad Q(y) = \int_{y}^{top} y'\,dA ,
$$

where $Q$ is the first moment, about the neutral axis, of the area above the cut, and $b(y)$ the width at the cut. By the symmetry of shear stresses (06.01), the same $\tau$ acts vertically on the cross-section.

## Rectangles and I-sections

- **Rectangle**: $\tau$ is parabolic, zero at the top and bottom, maximum at the neutral axis: $\tau_{max} = \dfrac{3}{2}\dfrac{V}{A}$, 50 % above the average.
- **I-section**: $Q$ changes little across the web while $b$ is small, so the web carries nearly all the shear at almost uniform stress, about $V/(h_w t_w)$. At the junction with the flange $b$ jumps and $\tau$ drops by the ratio of widths.

That's the division of labour in an I-beam: flanges carry the bending moment (06.10), the web carries the shear.

:::widget shearprofile
Shear stress over the height of a rectangle and an I-section with the same shear force. The rectangle's profile is a parabola; the I-section's web carries almost everything.
:::

:::example A joist and an I-beam under 20 kN of shear
1. Rectangle 50 × 200 mm: $\tau_{max} = 1.5\cdot20000/10000 = 3.0$ MPa at mid-depth. The code integrates $\tau b$ over the height and gets back exactly 20 kN.
2. I-section ($h = 200$, flanges $100\times8.5$, web 5.6 mm): $\tau_{max} = 20.29$ MPa in the web, 15.75 MPa at the web–flange junction, dropping to 0.88 MPa just inside the flange ($15.75\cdot5.6/100$).
3. The simple estimate $V/(h_wt_w) = 20000/(183\cdot5.6) = 19.52$ MPa is within 4 %, and the web carries 96.2 % of the shear force.
:::

```python file=shear_stress.py
"""Shear stress in beams by Jourawski's formula tau = V Q / (I b), for a rectangle and an I-section."""
import numpy as np


def jourawski(y, b_of_y, V):
    """y from bottom to top (centroid at 0 assumed). Q(y) = first moment of the area above y. Returns tau(y), I."""
    b = b_of_y(y)
    I = np.trapezoid(b * y**2, y)
    # Q(y) = int_y^top b(s) s ds, accumulated from the top down
    f = b * y
    Q = np.concatenate([np.cumsum(((f[1:] + f[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(y))[::-1])[::-1], [0.0]])
    return V * Q / (I * b), I, Q


if __name__ == "__main__":
    V = 20e3                                             # N
    # rectangle 50 x 200
    b, h = 50.0, 200.0
    y = np.linspace(-h / 2, h / 2, 20001)
    tau, I, Q = jourawski(y, lambda y: b + 0 * y, V)
    print(f"rectangle: max tau {tau.max():.4f} MPa at y = {y[tau.argmax()]:.2f} mm (1.5 V/A = {1.5 * V / (b * h):.4f}); I = {I:.4e}")
    assert abs(tau.max() - 1.5 * V / (b * h)) < 1e-4 and abs(I - b * h**3 / 12) < 1e-3 * I
    assert abs(np.trapezoid(tau * b, y) - V) < 1e-3 * V          # the shear stresses add up to V
    # I-section from 06.10: flanges 100 x 8.5, web 5.6, depth 200
    H, B, tf, tw = 200.0, 100.0, 8.5, 5.6
    bI = lambda y: np.where(np.abs(y) > H / 2 - tf, B, tw)
    y = np.linspace(-H / 2, H / 2, 40001)
    tau, I, Q = jourawski(y, bI, V)
    web = np.abs(y) < H / 2 - tf
    k = np.argmin(np.abs(y - (H / 2 - tf + 0.01)))        # just inside the top flange
    print(f"I-section: max tau in the web {tau[web].max():.4f} MPa, at the web-flange junction {tau[web][-1]:.4f} MPa, just inside the flange {tau[k]:.4f} MPa")
    assert abs(tau[k] - tau[web][-1] * tw / B) < 0.05      # same Q, but spread over the flange width
    print(f"  average over the web V / (h_w t_w) = {V / ((H - 2 * tf) * tw):.4f} MPa; I = {I:.4e} mm^4")
    share = np.trapezoid(np.where(web, tau * tw, 0), y) / V
    print(f"  the web carries {share * 100:.1f} % of the shear force")
    assert share > 0.95 and abs(np.trapezoid(tau * bI(y), y) - V) < 2e-3 * V
```

:::exercise A glued laminated beam
A glulam beam is 120 mm wide and 400 mm deep, made of 40 mm laminations glued together. The shear force near a support is 60 kN. What shear stress does the glue line at mid-depth carry, and the one 40 mm below the top?
---solution---
1. At mid-depth: $\tau = 1.5\cdot60000/(120\cdot400) = 1.875$ MPa.
2. 40 mm below the top: the area above is $120\times40$ with its centroid 180 mm above the neutral axis, so $Q = 4800\cdot180 = 8.64\times10^5$ mm³. $I = 120\cdot400^3/12 = 6.4\times10^8$ mm⁴.
3. $\tau = 60000\cdot8.64\times10^5/(6.4\times10^8\cdot120) = 0.675$ MPa: only 36 % of the mid-depth value.
:::
