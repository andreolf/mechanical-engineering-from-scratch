Real parts rarely carry just one kind of load. A bike crank is bent, twisted and squeezed all at once; a drive shaft carries torque and bending from the belt pull; a sign post bends in the wind and twists when the gust hits one side of the sign. Because everything so far is linear, the recipe is simple: compute each stress separately, add them at each point, then judge the combined state with a yield criterion.

## The recipe

1. Find the internal forces at the critical section: axial force $N$, shear $V$, bending moment $M$, torque $T$ (Phase 05).
2. Compute each stress at candidate points of the section:
   - axial: $\sigma = N/A$ (06.01)
   - bending: $\sigma = My/I$ (06.09)
   - torsion: $\tau = Tr/J$ (06.14)
   - transverse shear: $\tau = VQ/(Ib)$ (06.13)
3. **Superpose**: add normal stresses together and shear stresses together, taking care with directions.
4. Combine with **von Mises** (06.08). For one normal and one shear stress: $\sigma_{vm} = \sqrt{\sigma^2 + 3\tau^2}$.
5. The hotspot is where $\sigma_{vm}$ peaks. It is usually on the surface, where bending and torsion stresses are both largest, but which side depends on the signs.

## The crank arm

Push down with force $F$ on a pedal at distance $L$ along the crank and $a$ out to the side. At the spindle, the crank arm carries:

- bending $M = FL\cos\theta$ in the plane of rotation, $\theta$ being the crank angle above horizontal;
- axial force $N = -F\sin\theta$, compressive when the crank points up;
- torque $T = Fa$ from the pedal's offset.

Transverse shear peaks at the neutral axis, where bending stress is zero, and is small for a slender arm, so we leave it out.

:::example A strong rider on a steel crank
$F = 1000$ N, $L = 170$ mm, $a = 60$ mm, a tube 24 × 16 mm: $A = 251.3$ mm², $I = 13{,}069$ mm⁴, $J = 26{,}138$ mm⁴.

1. Crank horizontal: $M = 170$ N m, $T = 60$ N m, $N = 0$.
2. Bending at the top fibre: $\sigma = 170000\cdot12/13069 = 156.09$ MPa. Torsion: $\tau = 60000\cdot12/26138 = 27.55$ MPa.
3. von Mises: $\sqrt{156.09^2 + 3\cdot27.55^2} = 163.22$ MPa. The code scans 720 points around the surface and finds the same hotspot at the top of the arm.
4. Crank at 30° up: $N = -500$ N adds $-1.99$ MPa everywhere, and $M$ drops to 147.22 N m. Now the compressed bottom is worst: $\sqrt{(1.99 + 135.18)^2 + 3\cdot27.55^2} = 145.23$ MPa.
5. Sweeping the crank round confirms the horizontal position is the worst, with a safety factor of 2.17 against 355 MPa steel. Fatigue (Phase 09) is what really sizes cranks, because this happens at every pedal stroke.
:::

```python file=combined.py
"""Combined loading of a bicycle crank arm: axial + bending + torsion, then von Mises around the section."""
import math
import numpy as np


def crank_root_stresses(F, crank_deg, L=170.0, a=60.0, D=24.0, d=16.0, n=721):
    """Stresses round the circumference of a tubular crank arm at the spindle.

    The pedal force F (N) acts straight down at the pedal, L mm along the crank and a mm out
    to the side. crank_deg is the crank angle from horizontal-forward. Returns the angles,
    von Mises stress round the outer surface, and the individual parts.
    """
    th = math.radians(crank_deg)
    A = math.pi * (D**2 - d**2) / 4
    I = math.pi * (D**4 - d**4) / 64
    J = 2 * I
    # resolve F into components along the crank (axial) and across it (bending)
    N = -F * math.sin(th)                  # compressive when the crank points up and you push down
    M = F * math.cos(th) * L               # bending moment at the root (in the plane of rotation)
    T = F * a                              # torque about the crank axis from the pedal offset
    phi = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, n)     # phi = 0 at the top of the section (tension side when M > 0)
    c = D / 2
    sigma = N / A + M * c * np.cos(phi) / I
    tau = T * c / J * np.ones_like(phi)
    vm = np.sqrt(sigma**2 + 3 * tau**2)
    return phi, vm, sigma, tau, dict(N=N, M=M, T=T, A=A, I=I, J=J)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    F = 1000.0                                             # N, a strong rider standing on the pedal
    phi, vm, s, t, k = crank_root_stresses(F, 0.0)
    sb = k["M"] * 12 / k["I"]; st = k["T"] * 12 / k["J"]
    print(f"crank horizontal: M = {k['M']/1e3:.0f} N m, T = {k['T']/1e3:.0f} N m, I = {k['I']:.1f} mm^4")
    print(f"  bending {sb:.2f} MPa, torsion {st:.2f} MPa, hand von Mises {math.hypot(sb, math.sqrt(3) * st):.2f} MPa")
    i = int(np.argmax(vm))
    print(f"  numerical hotspot {vm[i]:.2f} MPa at phi = {math.degrees(phi[i]):.1f} deg (top of the arm)")
    assert abs(vm[i] - math.hypot(sb, math.sqrt(3) * st)) < 1e-9 and math.degrees(phi[i]) % 360 < 1e-9
    # crank at 30 degrees above horizontal: axial compression joins in, and the hotspot moves to the compressed side
    phi, vm, s, t, k = crank_root_stresses(F, 30.0)
    i = int(np.argmax(vm))
    hand = math.hypot(abs(k["N"]) / k["A"] + k["M"] * 12 / k["I"], math.sqrt(3) * st)
    print(f"crank at 30 deg: N = {k['N']:.0f} N, M = {k['M']/1e3:.2f} N m; hotspot {vm[i]:.2f} MPa at phi = {math.degrees(phi[i]):.0f} deg (hand {hand:.2f})")
    assert abs(vm[i] - hand) < 1e-9 and abs(math.degrees(phi[i]) - 180) < 1e-9
    # sweep the crank round: the worst position is horizontal
    angs = np.arange(-90, 91, 5)
    peak = [crank_root_stresses(F, a)[1].max() for a in angs]
    print(f"worst crank angle {angs[int(np.argmax(peak))]} deg, {max(peak):.2f} MPa; safety factor on 355 MPa steel {355 / max(peak):.2f}")
```

:::exercise A sign post in the wind
A round steel post, 114 mm outside diameter with 4 mm wall, holds a sign 2.5 m up. Wind puts 1.2 kN on the sign, with its centre 0.4 m to the side of the post's axis. Find the bending and torsional stresses at the base, and the von Mises stress.
---solution---
1. $I = \pi(114^4 - 106^4)/64 = 2.093\times10^6$ mm⁴, $J = 4.187\times10^6$ mm⁴.
2. $M = 1200\cdot2500 = 3\times10^6$ N mm: $\sigma = 3\times10^6\cdot57/2.093\times10^6 = 81.7$ MPa.
3. $T = 1200\cdot400 = 4.8\times10^5$ N mm: $\tau = 4.8\times10^5\cdot57/4.187\times10^6 = 6.53$ MPa.
4. $\sigma_{vm} = \sqrt{81.7^2 + 3\cdot6.53^2} = 82.5$ MPa. Torsion matters little here: bending dominates.
:::
