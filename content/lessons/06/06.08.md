A tensile test gives one number: the yield stress $f_y$, where a bar under pure tension starts to deform permanently. A real part, like a shaft under bending and torsion, has a combination of stresses. Is 120 MPa of tension plus 70 MPa of shear safe for a steel with $f_y = 355$ MPa? A failure criterion turns any stress state into one equivalent stress you can compare with $f_y$.

## Why shear matters for metals

Metals yield by crystal planes slipping over each other, which is driven by shear. Pure hydrostatic pressure, however large, causes no slip and no yield. So a good criterion for ductile metals must ignore the hydrostatic part of the stress and depend only on the **deviatoric** part, $\mathbf s = \boldsymbol\sigma - \tfrac13\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol\sigma)\mathbf I$.

## Tresca: maximum shear

Yield starts when the largest shear stress reaches its value in the tensile test, $f_y/2$:

$$
\sigma_{Tresca} = \sigma_1 - \sigma_3 \le f_y .
$$

## von Mises: distortion energy

Split the strain energy into a volume-change part and a shape-change (distortion) part. Yield starts when the distortion energy reaches its value in the tensile test:

$$
\sigma_{vM} = \sqrt{\tfrac12\big[(\sigma_1 - \sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2 - \sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3 - \sigma_1)^2\big]} \le f_y .
$$

For a shaft surface with normal stress $\sigma$ and shear $\tau$, these become $\sqrt{\sigma^2 + 3\tau^2}$ (von Mises) and $\sqrt{\sigma^2 + 4\tau^2}$ (Tresca). In **pure shear**, von Mises predicts yield at $\tau = f_y/\sqrt3 = 0.577f_y$ and Tresca at $0.5f_y$. Experiments on steel usually land closer to von Mises, which is why most design codes use it. Tresca is a little conservative and easier by hand.

In the $\sigma_1$–$\sigma_2$ plane (with $\sigma_3 = 0$), von Mises is an ellipse and Tresca a hexagon inscribed in it. Brittle materials like cast iron or glass need different criteria based on the largest tensile stress.

:::example A drive shaft surface in S355
$\sigma = 120$ MPa from bending, $\tau = 70$ MPa from torsion, $f_y = 355$ MPa.

1. von Mises: $\sqrt{120^2 + 3\cdot70^2} = 170.59$ MPa. Safety factor $355/170.59 = 2.08$.
2. Tresca: $\sqrt{120^2 + 4\cdot70^2} = 184.39$ MPa. Safety factor 1.93.
3. Pure shear in S355: yield at $\tau = 204.96$ MPa (von Mises) or 177.5 MPa (Tresca).
4. The code checks that the stress state and a tensile bar at 170.59 MPa store exactly the same distortion energy, and that 500 MPa of hydrostatic pressure gives an equivalent stress of zero.
:::

```python file=failure.py
"""Yield criteria: von Mises (distortion energy) and Tresca (maximum shear), with safety factors."""
import math
import numpy as np


def principal(sig):
    return np.sort(np.linalg.eigvalsh(np.asarray(sig, float)))[::-1]


def von_mises(sig):
    s1, s2, s3 = principal(sig)
    return math.sqrt(((s1 - s2) ** 2 + (s2 - s3) ** 2 + (s3 - s1) ** 2) / 2)


def tresca(sig):
    s = principal(sig)
    return s[0] - s[2]                           # twice the maximum shear stress


def distortion_energy(sig, E, nu):
    """Strain energy density minus its volumetric part."""
    sig = np.asarray(sig, float); G = E / (2 * (1 + nu))
    dev = sig - np.trace(sig) / 3 * np.eye(3)
    return (dev * dev).sum() / (4 * G)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    fy, E, nu = 355.0, 210e3, 0.3               # S355 structural steel
    # pure shear: von Mises yields at fy / sqrt(3), Tresca at fy / 2
    for name, f, limit in (("von Mises", von_mises, fy / math.sqrt(3)), ("Tresca", tresca, fy / 2)):
        tau = limit; sig = [[0, tau, 0], [tau, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0]]
        print(f"pure shear, {name}: yields at tau = {tau:.4f} MPa (equivalent stress {f(sig):.4f})")
        assert abs(f(sig) - fy) < 1e-9
    # a shaft surface: 120 MPa bending stress plus 70 MPa torsional shear
    sig = [[120, 70, 0], [70, 0, 0], [0, 0, 0]]
    vm, tr = von_mises(sig), tresca(sig)
    print(f"shaft: von Mises {vm:.4f} MPa (sqrt(s^2 + 3 t^2) = {math.sqrt(120**2 + 3 * 70**2):.4f}), Tresca {tr:.4f} MPa (sqrt(s^2 + 4 t^2) = {math.sqrt(120**2 + 4 * 70**2):.4f})")
    print(f"  safety factors against yield: {fy / vm:.3f} (von Mises), {fy / tr:.3f} (Tresca)")
    assert abs(vm - math.sqrt(120**2 + 3 * 70**2)) < 1e-9 and tr >= vm
    # von Mises = equal distortion energy: compare with a uniaxial bar at the same equivalent stress
    assert abs(distortion_energy(sig, E, nu) - distortion_energy(np.diag([vm, 0, 0]), E, nu)) < 1e-12
    # hydrostatic stress never yields under either criterion
    assert von_mises(np.diag([-500.0, -500.0, -500.0])) < 1e-9 and tresca(np.diag([-500.0, -500.0, -500.0])) < 1e-9
    print("hydrostatic pressure of 500 MPa: equivalent stress 0 (no distortion, no yield)")
```

:::exercise A pressurised tube
A thin steel tube has hoop stress 180 MPa, axial stress 90 MPa and negligible radial stress. It's made of S355. Find both equivalent stresses and the safety factors.
---solution---
1. Principal stresses: $\sigma_1 = 180$, $\sigma_2 = 90$, $\sigma_3 = 0$ MPa.
2. Tresca: $180 - 0 = 180$ MPa, factor 1.97.
3. von Mises: $\sqrt{\tfrac12[90^2 + 90^2 + 180^2]} = \sqrt{24300} = 155.9$ MPa, factor 2.28.
4. Note the third principal stress (zero, radial) matters for Tresca: the largest shear here is out of the plane.
:::
