A stretched spring, a bent bow and a wound clock spring all store energy. So does every loaded structure: the work done by the loads goes into deforming the material, and it comes back when the load is removed. This strain energy gives a second way into structural problems, and the next lesson turns it into a powerful method for deflections.

## Energy in a bar

Load a bar slowly from zero to $P$. The force grows linearly with the stretch, so the work done is the area under a straight line: $W = \tfrac12 P\delta$. With $\delta = PL/(EA)$ (06.04):

$$
U = \frac{P\delta}{2} = \frac{P^2 L}{2EA} .
$$

Locally, each unit volume stores $u = \tfrac12\sigma\varepsilon = \sigma^2/(2E)$, and $U = \int u\,dV$.

## Beams and shafts

Apply the same idea to bending and torsion:

$$
U_{bending} = \int_0^L \frac{M^2}{2EI}\,dx, \qquad U_{torsion} = \int_0^L \frac{T^2}{2GJ}\,dx .
$$

Each one follows from integrating $\sigma^2/(2E)$ or $\tau^2/(2G)$ over the cross-section. Energy is quadratic in load, so energies from different loads **don't** simply add (unlike stresses), unless the loads act on independent deformations.

## Using material well

A uniformly stressed bar stores the most energy per unit volume, because every bit of material sits at the peak stress. In a tip-loaded rectangular cantilever with the same peak stress, the stress falls off along the length and towards the neutral axis. It stores only one ninth as much per unit volume. That's why leaf springs taper and archery bows are shaped to spread the stress evenly.

:::example Energy in three members
1. A 20 mm steel tie, 2 m long, at 30 kN: $U = P^2L/(2EA) = 13.64$ J.
2. The 1.8 m balcony cantilever with 5 kN at the tip: $U = \int M^2/(2EI)\,dx = P^2L^3/(6EI) = 6.27$ J, equal to $\tfrac12\cdot5000\cdot2.508$ mm.
3. The propeller shaft at 1.2 kN m: $U = T^2L/(2GJ) = 40.90$ J $= \tfrac12 T\varphi$.
4. The code integrates $\sigma^2/(2E)$ over a 3D grid for a cantilever and confirms the 1/9 ratio to 4 decimals.
:::

```python file=strain_energy.py
"""Strain energy U = integral of sigma epsilon / 2 over the volume, for a bar, a beam and a shaft."""
import math
import numpy as np


def bar_energy(P, L, E, A):
    return P**2 * L / (2 * E * A)


def beam_energy(M_of_x, L, E, I, n=20001):
    x = np.linspace(0, L, n)
    return np.trapezoid(M_of_x(x) ** 2 / (2 * E * I), x)


def shaft_energy(T, L, G, J):
    return T**2 * L / (2 * G * J)


def energy_density_volume(sig_fn, E, xs, ys, b):
    """Integrate sigma^2 / (2E) over a rectangular beam volume (x along, y across the depth)."""
    X, Y = np.meshgrid(xs, ys, indexing="ij")
    u = sig_fn(X, Y) ** 2 / (2 * E)
    return np.trapezoid(np.trapezoid(u, ys, axis=1), xs) * b


if __name__ == "__main__":
    E, G = 210e3, 80e3
    # 1. a 20 mm steel tie, 2 m long, 30 kN
    P, L, A = 30e3, 2000.0, math.pi * 20**2 / 4
    U = bar_energy(P, L, E, A); delta = P * L / (E * A)
    print(f"bar: U = {U/1e3:.4f} J, P delta / 2 = {P*delta/2/1e3:.4f} J")
    assert abs(U - P * delta / 2) < 1e-9 * U
    # 2. the balcony cantilever from 06.11: 5 kN at the tip of 1.8 m, I = 1.8456e7 mm^4
    P, L, I = 5e3, 1800.0, 1.8456e7
    U = beam_energy(lambda x: -P * (L - x), L, E, I); delta = P * L**3 / (3 * E * I)
    print(f"beam: U = {U/1e3:.4f} J, P delta / 2 = {P*delta/2/1e3:.4f} J")
    assert abs(U - P * delta / 2) < 1e-6 * U
    # 3. the propeller shaft from 06.14
    T, L, J = 1.2e6, 1400.0, math.pi * (50**4 - 42**4) / 32
    U = shaft_energy(T, L, G, J); phi = T * L / (G * J)
    print(f"shaft: U = {U/1e3:.4f} J, T phi / 2 = {T*phi/2/1e3:.4f} J")
    assert abs(U - T * phi / 2) < 1e-9 * U
    # 4. how well each uses the material: same peak stress 200 MPa, compare energy per unit volume
    s0 = 200.0; u0 = s0**2 / (2 * E)
    b, h, L = 20.0, 40.0, 500.0
    xs, ys = np.linspace(0, L, 401), np.linspace(-h / 2, h / 2, 401)
    # cantilever with tip load, sized so the root fibre reaches s0: sigma = s0 (1 - x/L) (2y/h)
    Ub = energy_density_volume(lambda X, Y: s0 * (1 - X / L) * (2 * Y / h), E, xs, ys, b)
    ratio = Ub / (u0 * b * h * L)
    print(f"energy stored per unit volume, relative to a uniformly stressed bar: tip-loaded cantilever {ratio:.4f} (1/9 = {1/9:.4f})")
    assert abs(ratio - 1 / 9) < 1e-4
```

:::exercise How much energy can a bolt absorb?
A steel bolt, 12 mm diameter and 100 mm long, is loaded in tension up to its yield stress of 640 MPa ($E = 210$ GPa). How much energy does it store? Compare an aluminium bolt of the same size loaded to its yield stress of 280 MPa ($E = 70$ GPa).
---solution---
1. Steel: $u = \sigma^2/(2E) = 640^2/(2\cdot210000) = 0.975$ N mm/mm³ (MJ/m³).
2. Volume $= \pi\cdot6^2\cdot100 = 11{,}310$ mm³, so $U = 11.03$ J.
3. Aluminium: $u = 280^2/(2\cdot70000) = 0.56$ N mm/mm³, so $U = 6.33$ J. The low modulus helps (it stretches more), but the lower strength wins because $u$ grows with $\sigma^2$.
:::
