Rods, cables, bolts, truss members and brake cables mostly carry axial force. How much they stretch matters: a brake lever that travels too far feels spongy, a bolt that stretches loses clamping force, and a lift cable that stretches lets the cabin bounce. For a bar the answer is one formula, and this lesson extends it to stepped and tapered bars and to self-weight.

## The bar formula

A bar of length $L$, area $A$ and modulus $E$ carrying an axial force $N$ has stress $\sigma = N/A$ and strain $\varepsilon = \sigma/E$. Uniform strain means the elongation is

$$
\delta = \varepsilon L = \frac{NL}{EA} .
$$

$EA$ is the **axial stiffness** of the section and $EA/L$ the spring stiffness of the bar. When $N$, $A$ or $E$ change along the bar, add up the pieces, or integrate:

$$
\delta = \sum_i \frac{N_iL_i}{E_iA_i}, \qquad \delta = \int_0^L \frac{N(x)}{E\,A(x)}\,dx .
$$

Find each segment's internal force $N_i$ by a cut and an FBD (lesson 05.06), then add the stretches.

## Self-weight and tapers

A cable of density $\rho$ hanging under its own weight carries $N(x) = \rho gA(L - x)$, so $\delta = \rho gL^2/(2E)$, independent of the area. A round bar tapering linearly from radius $r_1$ to $r_2$ gives $\delta = PL/(E\pi r_1r_2)$.

:::example A brake cable and a stepped bar
1. The brake cable from lesson 05.12: steel, 1.5 mm diameter, 1.8 m, 168 N. $A = 1.767$ mm², $\sigma = 95.1$ MPa, $\delta = 168\cdot1800/(200000\cdot1.767) = 0.856$ mm. (Stranded cable is softer than solid wire, so the real figure is larger.)
2. Stepped bar fixed at a wall: steel 400 mm × Ø20 next to the wall, aluminium 300 mm × Ø30 beyond it. 30 kN pulls at the free end, 10 kN pushes back at the joint. Cuts give $N = 20$ kN in the steel and 30 kN in the aluminium.
3. $\delta = 20000\cdot400/(210000\cdot314.2) + 30000\cdot300/(70000\cdot706.9) = 0.1213 + 0.1819 = 0.3032$ mm.
4. A 1 km steel cable hanging free stretches $7850\cdot9.81\cdot1000^2/(2\cdot210\times10^9) = 0.183$ m under its own weight.
:::

```python file=axial.py
"""Axial bars: delta = sum N L / (E A) for stepped bars, plus self-weight and a tapered bar by integration."""
import math
import numpy as np


def stepped(segments):
    """segments: (L, A, E, N) with N the internal axial force in that segment (tension positive). Returns total stretch."""
    return sum(N * L / (E * A) for L, A, E, N in segments)


def integrate_stretch(N, A, E, L, n=20001):
    """delta = int_0^L N(x) / (E A(x)) dx by Simpson's rule."""
    x = np.linspace(0, L, n); f = N(x) / (E * A(x))
    w = np.ones(n); w[1:-1:2] = 4; w[2:-1:2] = 2
    return L / (n - 1) / 3 * (w @ f)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # the brake cable from lesson 05.12: steel, 1.5 mm diameter, 1.8 m long, 168 N
    A = math.pi * 0.75**2
    d = stepped([(1800, A, 200e3, 168)])
    print(f"brake cable stretch {d:.4f} mm (stress {168 / A:.2f} MPa)")
    # stepped bar: steel rod 400 mm of 20 mm diameter then aluminium 300 mm of 30 mm diameter;
    # 30 kN pulls at the free end and 10 kN pushes back at the joint
    As, Aa = math.pi * 10**2, math.pi * 15**2
    segs = [(400, As, 210e3, 30e3 - 10e3), (300, Aa, 70e3, 30e3)]   # steel next to the wall carries 20 kN, aluminium 30 kN
    d = stepped(segs)
    print(f"stepped bar: steel {segs[0][3] * 400 / (210e3 * As):.4f} mm + aluminium {30e3 * 300 / (70e3 * Aa):.4f} mm = {d:.4f} mm")
    # check by integrating the aluminium piece numerically
    num = 20e3 * 400 / (210e3 * As) + integrate_stretch(lambda x: 30e3 + 0 * x, lambda x: Aa + 0 * x, 70e3, 300)
    assert abs(num - d) < 1e-9
    # a hanging steel cable under its own weight: N(x) = rho g A (L - x), delta = rho g L^2 / (2 E)
    L = 1000e3                                 # mm
    rg = 7850 * 9.81 * 1e-9                    # rho g in N/mm^3
    d_self = integrate_stretch(lambda x: rg * 1.0 * (L - x), lambda x: 1.0 + 0 * x, 210e3, L)
    print(f"1 km steel cable hanging under its own weight stretches {d_self:.3f} mm (rho g L^2 / 2E = {rg * L**2 / (2 * 210e3):.3f})")
    assert abs(d_self - rg * L**2 / (2 * 210e3)) < 1e-6
    # tapered round bar (radius r1 -> r2 linearly): closed form delta = P L / (E pi r1 r2)
    P, Lt, r1, r2 = 50e3, 500.0, 15.0, 10.0
    d_t = integrate_stretch(lambda x: P + 0 * x, lambda x: math.pi * (r1 + (r2 - r1) * x / Lt) ** 2, 210e3, Lt)
    print(f"tapered bar: {d_t:.6f} mm, closed form {P * Lt / (210e3 * math.pi * r1 * r2):.6f} mm")
    assert abs(d_t - P * Lt / (210e3 * math.pi * r1 * r2)) < 1e-9
```

:::exercise A lift rod
A 6 m steel hanger rod of 24 mm diameter carries a 45 kN floor load at its lower end. Find the stress and the stretch. What diameter keeps the stretch under 2 mm?
---solution---
1. $A = \pi\cdot12^2 = 452.4$ mm², $\sigma = 45000/452.4 = 99.5$ MPa.
2. $\delta = 45000\cdot6000/(210000\cdot452.4) = 2.84$ mm.
3. For $\delta \le 2$ mm: $A \ge 45000\cdot6000/(210000\cdot2) = 642.9$ mm², so $d \ge 28.6$ mm. Use 30 mm.
:::
