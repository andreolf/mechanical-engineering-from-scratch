Watch a river flow past a boulder. Upstream the water hugs the rock; behind it, the flow breaks away into a swirling, slack wake where leaves circle. That break-away is **separation**, and it decides most of the drag on blunt bodies. It happens because the boundary layer, already slowed by friction, can't push uphill against rising pressure.

## The adverse pressure gradient

Outside the boundary layer, Bernoulli links pressure and speed: where the outer flow $U_e(x)$ speeds up, pressure falls (a **favourable** gradient); where it slows down, pressure rises (an **adverse** gradient). Across the thin layer the pressure is the same as outside, so the slow fluid near the wall feels the same push. At the wall itself the momentum equation gives

$$
\mu\left.\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}\right|_{\text{wall}} = \frac{dp}{dx} .
$$

With $dp/dx > 0$ the profile curves the wrong way near the wall, develops an inflection point, and eventually the wall shear $\tau_w = \mu\,\partial u/\partial y$ falls to zero. Beyond that point the near-wall fluid flows backwards and the layer lifts off: separation.

## Thwaites' method

Integrating the boundary-layer equations across the layer gives the **momentum integral equation**. Thwaites noticed that, for laminar layers, it can be solved almost exactly in closed form:

$$
\theta^2(x) = \frac{0.45\,\nu}{U_e^6}\int_0^x U_e^5\,dx', \qquad \lambda = \frac{\theta^2}{\nu}\frac{dU_e}{dx} .
$$

$\theta$ is the momentum thickness and $\lambda$ a pressure-gradient parameter. The layer separates when $\lambda \approx -0.09$. All you need is the outer velocity $U_e(x)$, and the result doesn't depend on the Reynolds number: $\nu$ cancels out of $\lambda$.

## The cylinder

With the ideal-flow outer velocity $U_e = 2U\sin\theta$, Thwaites predicts separation at 103.1°. The exact laminar boundary-layer solution for this velocity gives 104.5°, and Pohlhausen's older method 109.5°, so the literature range is 104–110° and Thwaites lands 1.4° below the exact value. In real flows separation happens much earlier, near 80°, because the wake itself changes the pressure field. Feeding Thwaites the measured pressures (Hiemenz's fit) reproduces that.

:::example Where the flow leaves the cylinder
1. At the front stagnation point $\lambda = 0.0750$ (Thwaites' exact limit). At 60°, $\lambda = 0.0589$; past 90° the gradient turns adverse, and at 120° $\lambda = -0.5100$.
2. With $U_e = 2U\sin\theta$ on 200 001 points, $\lambda$ reaches $-0.09$ at **103.11°**, 1.4° short of the exact 104.5°.
3. A cylinder of a different size, speed and fluid (R = 0.2 m, 7 m/s, $\nu = 1.5\times10^{-5}$) gives the same angle to $10^{-6}$ degrees: Re drops out.
4. With the Hiemenz fit to measured pressures, separation moves to 78.62°, close to the 80° seen in experiments.
5. For a boulder 0.3 m across in a 1 m/s river, the momentum thickness at 90° is only 0.1342 mm.
:::

```python file=thwaites.py
"""Thwaites' method for a laminar boundary layer, used to find where the flow separates from a cylinder."""
import math
import numpy as np


def thwaites(xs, Ue, nu):
    """Momentum thickness theta^2 = 0.45 nu / Ue^6 * integral Ue^5 dx, and lambda = theta^2 Ue' / nu."""
    Ue5 = Ue ** 5
    integral = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(0.5 * (Ue5[1:] + Ue5[:-1]) * np.diff(xs))])
    with np.errstate(divide="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
        th2 = 0.45 * nu * integral / Ue ** 6
    dUe = np.gradient(Ue, xs)
    th2[0] = 0.075 * nu / dUe[0]                      # stagnation-point limit
    return th2, th2 * dUe / nu


def separation_angle(ue_of_angle, R=1.0, U=1.0, nu=1e-5, n=200001):
    """Angle (degrees from the front stagnation point) where lambda first falls to -0.09."""
    ang = np.linspace(1e-6, math.radians(170), n)
    xs = ang * R
    Ue = U * ue_of_angle(ang)
    _, lam = thwaites(xs, Ue, nu)
    i = np.argmax(lam < -0.09)
    a = ang[i - 1] + (-0.09 - lam[i - 1]) * (ang[i] - ang[i - 1]) / (lam[i] - lam[i - 1])
    return math.degrees(a), ang, lam


if __name__ == "__main__":
    ideal = lambda a: 2 * np.sin(a)
    hiemenz = lambda a: 1.814 * a - 0.271 * a ** 3 - 0.0471 * a ** 5          # fit to measured pressures, subcritical cylinder
    s1, ang, lam = separation_angle(ideal)
    print(f"lambda at the stagnation point {lam[0]:.4f}; at 60 deg {np.interp(math.pi/3, ang, lam):.4f}, at 120 deg {np.interp(2*math.pi/3, ang, lam):.4f}")
    print(f"ideal-flow pressure distribution: separation at {s1:.2f} deg")
    # literature: 104.5 deg for the exact boundary-layer solution (Terrill), 109.5 deg from Pohlhausen's method
    assert abs(s1 - 104.5) < 2.0                                             # Thwaites lands within 2 degrees of the exact value
    s2, _, _ = separation_angle(hiemenz)
    print(f"measured (Hiemenz) pressure distribution: separation at {s2:.2f} deg (experiments: about 80 deg)")
    # the result doesn't depend on Re or the size: theta scales out of lambda
    s3, _, _ = separation_angle(ideal, R=0.2, U=7.0, nu=1.5e-5)
    assert abs(s3 - s1) < 1e-6
    # momentum thickness on a 0.3 m boulder in a 1 m/s river at 90 degrees
    R, U, nu = 0.15, 1.0, 1e-6
    ang = np.linspace(1e-6, math.pi / 2, 20001)
    th2, _ = thwaites(ang * R, U * 2 * np.sin(ang), nu)
    print(f"boulder: momentum thickness at 90 deg = {math.sqrt(th2[-1])*1000:.4f} mm")
```

:::exercise Is the layer still attached?
On an ideal-flow cylinder, $\lambda = 0.45\cos\theta\,\int_0^\theta\sin^5 t\,dt\,/\sin^6\theta$. With $\int_0^\theta\sin^5 t\,dt = \tfrac{8}{15} - \cos\theta + \tfrac23\cos^3\theta - \tfrac15\cos^5\theta$, evaluate $\lambda$ at 100°. Is the layer attached there?
---solution---
1. $\cos100° = -0.17365$, $\sin100° = 0.98481$.
2. The integral: $0.53333 + 0.17365 - 0.00349 + 0.00003 = 0.70352$.
3. $\lambda = 0.45\cdot(-0.17365)\cdot0.70352/0.98481^6 = -0.05497/0.91224 = -0.06026$.
4. $-0.06026 > -0.09$: adverse, but the layer is still attached. It separates about 3° further on.
:::
