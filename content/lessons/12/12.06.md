A kite tugging on its line over Sanur beach and an airliner wing work the same way. A popular myth says the air over the top must "catch up" with the air underneath, so it goes faster. It doesn't: in reality the upper flow arrives at the trailing edge well before the lower flow. The real explanation is circulation, set by a sharp trailing edge, and the Kutta-Joukowski theorem from lesson 11.16.

## The Kutta condition

Potential flow around an airfoil has a solution for every value of circulation $\Gamma$. Most of them whip the flow around the sharp trailing edge at infinite speed, which a viscous fluid won't tolerate. When a wing starts moving, a **starting vortex** is shed from the trailing edge, and by Kelvin's theorem an equal and opposite circulation stays bound to the wing. It settles at the one value for which the flow leaves the trailing edge smoothly: the **Kutta condition**. Then $L' = \rho U\Gamma$.

## Thin airfoil theory

Model the airfoil as its camber line $z(x)$, with a sheet of vortices along it. Requiring the camber line to be a streamline at angle of attack $\alpha$, and substituting $x = \tfrac c2(1 - \cos\theta)$, the vortex strength becomes a Fourier series with coefficients

$$
A_0 = \alpha - \frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\frac{dz}{dx}\,d\theta, \qquad A_n = \frac2\pi\int_0^\pi\frac{dz}{dx}\cos n\theta\,d\theta .
$$

The lift and the moment about the quarter chord follow:

$$
C_L = \pi(2A_0 + A_1) = 2\pi(\alpha - \alpha_{L0}), \qquad C_{m,c/4} = \frac\pi4(A_2 - A_1) .
$$

The **lift slope is $2\pi$ per radian** (0.110 per degree) for every thin airfoil. Camber shifts the zero-lift angle $\alpha_{L0}$ to a negative value, so a cambered wing lifts even at $\alpha = 0$. The theory holds until stall, typically 12–16°, when the flow separates from the upper surface.

:::example The NACA 2412
1. A flat plate at 4°: $C_L = 0.43865 = 2\pi\alpha$, and the quarter-chord moment is zero.
2. NACA 2412 (2% camber at 40% chord), Glauert integrals with 200 000 midpoints: lift slope 6.283185 per radian $= 2\pi$; zero-lift angle −2.077°; $C_{m,c/4} = -0.0531$. These match wind-tunnel values closely below stall.
3. $C_L$ = 0.2278 at 0°, 0.6664 at 4°, 1.1051 at 8°.
4. A 2.5 m² kite with this section at 6° in an 8 m/s wind ($\rho = 1.16$ kg/m³): $L = \tfrac12\rho U^2 S C_L = 82.2$ N. That's a 2D estimate; a real kite of low aspect ratio gives much less (lesson 12.07 and beyond).
:::

```python file=thinairfoil.py
"""Thin airfoil theory: lift slope, zero-lift angle and pitching moment from the camber line, via Glauert integrals."""
import math
import numpy as np


def naca4_slope(x, m, p):
    """dz/dx of a NACA 4-digit mean camber line (max camber m at chord position p)."""
    return np.where(x < p, 2 * m / p ** 2 * (p - x), 2 * m / (1 - p) ** 2 * (p - x))


def glauert(m, p, alpha, n=200000):
    """A0, A1, A2 with x = (1 - cos t)/2. Midpoint rule in t avoids the endpoints."""
    t = (np.arange(n) + 0.5) * math.pi / n; dt = math.pi / n
    dz = naca4_slope((1 - np.cos(t)) / 2, m, p) if m > 0 else np.zeros(n)
    A0 = alpha - np.sum(dz) * dt / math.pi
    A1 = 2 / math.pi * np.sum(dz * np.cos(t)) * dt
    A2 = 2 / math.pi * np.sum(dz * np.cos(2 * t)) * dt
    return A0, A1, A2


def coefficients(m, p, alpha):
    A0, A1, A2 = glauert(m, p, alpha)
    CL = math.pi * (2 * A0 + A1)
    Cm4 = math.pi / 4 * (A2 - A1)
    return CL, Cm4


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # symmetric plate: C_L = 2 pi alpha
    a = math.radians(4)
    CL, Cm = coefficients(0, 0.4, a)
    print(f"flat plate at 4 deg: C_L = {CL:.5f} (2 pi alpha = {2*math.pi*a:.5f}), C_m,c/4 = {Cm:.5f}")
    assert abs(CL - 2 * math.pi * a) < 1e-12 and abs(Cm) < 1e-12
    # NACA 2412: 2 % camber at 40 % chord
    m, p = 0.02, 0.4
    c1, _ = coefficients(m, p, math.radians(2)); c2, Cm = coefficients(m, p, math.radians(6))
    slope = (c2 - c1) / math.radians(4)
    a0 = math.radians(2) - c1 / slope
    print(f"NACA 2412: lift slope {slope:.6f} per rad (2 pi = {2*math.pi:.6f}), zero-lift angle {math.degrees(a0):.3f} deg, C_m,c/4 = {Cm:.4f}")
    assert abs(slope - 2 * math.pi) < 1e-9                                  # camber shifts the line, it doesn't change the slope
    for deg in (0, 4, 8):
        print(f"  alpha = {deg} deg: C_L = {coefficients(m, p, math.radians(deg))[0]:.4f}")
    # a kite over Bali: 2.5 m^2, 8 m/s wind, 6 deg, aspect-ratio correction left out (lesson 12.07 and beyond)
    CL = coefficients(m, p, math.radians(6))[0]
    print(f"kite lift (2D estimate): {0.5*1.16*8**2*2.5*CL:.1f} N")
```

:::exercise Landing lift
A light aircraft with NACA 2412 wings ($\alpha_{L0} = -2.077°$) lands at 30 m/s with 16 m² of wing, sea-level air ($\rho = 1.225$). In 2D theory, what angle of attack gives a lift of 9000 N?
---solution---
1. Required $C_L = 9000/(\tfrac12\cdot1.225\cdot30^2\cdot16) = 9000/8820 = 1.0204$.
2. $\alpha - \alpha_{L0} = 1.0204/(2\pi) = 0.16240$ rad $= 9.305°$.
3. $\alpha = 9.305 - 2.077 = 7.23°$. A real finite wing needs a few degrees more because of its tip vortices.
:::
