A cyclist riding along the Zürichsee at 35 km/h spends most of their effort pushing air aside. Drag on a body comes in two kinds, and which one dominates depends on the shape. For a rider, almost all of it is pressure drag from the separated wake, and that's why tucking into a lower position is worth so much.

## Friction and pressure drag

The force of the fluid on a body has two parts. **Friction drag** is the wall shear stress integrated over the surface; it dominates on streamlined bodies such as a thin plate, a fish or a glider wing. **Pressure drag** (form drag) comes from the low pressure in the separated wake behind the body; it dominates on bluff bodies such as cylinders, cars and cyclists. Both are lumped into a drag coefficient,

$$
F_D = \tfrac12\rho U^2\,C_D\,A .
$$

A flat plate side-on to the flow has $C_D \approx 0.003$ (friction only); a cylinder has $C_D \approx 1.2$; a plate facing the flow, $\approx 2$. For people and bikes, $C_D$ and the frontal area $A$ are hard to separate, so they are measured together as $C_D A$, in m².

## Power and the v³ law

Power is force times speed. In still air the aerodynamic part is

$$
P_{\text{aero}} = \tfrac12\rho\,C_D A\,v^3 ,
$$

so doubling your speed needs eight times the aerodynamic power. Add rolling resistance $C_{rr} m g v$ (linear in speed) and divide by the drivetrain efficiency. With a headwind $w$ the drag uses the air speed $v + w$, but the power still uses your ground speed $v$: $P = \tfrac12\rho C_D A (v + w)^2 v$.

:::example A ride around Zürich
Air at 96.6 kPa and 15 °C: $\rho = 1.1679$ kg/m³. An 80 kg rider and bike, $C_{rr} = 0.004$, drivetrain efficiency 97%.

1. At 200 W: upright ($C_D A = 0.50$ m²) 29.94 km/h; on the hoods (0.32) 34.46 km/h; in the drops (0.28) 35.94 km/h; time-trial position (0.22) 38.75 km/h.
2. Aero power on the hoods at 8 m/s is 98.63 W; at 16 m/s it's 789.06 W. The ratio is 8.0000: the $v^3$ law.
3. Riding 36 km/h on the hoods takes 225.0 W: 192.6 W against the air (86%) and 32.4 W rolling.
4. Into a 5 m/s Bise headwind, the same 200 W gives only 24.22 km/h.
:::

```python file=cyclist.py
"""Power to ride a bicycle: aerodynamic drag with C_D A, rolling resistance, and the speed you get for a given power."""
import math

G = 9.81


def air_density(p=96_600.0, T=288.15):
    """Ideal gas; 96.6 kPa is a typical pressure at Zurich's altitude (408 m)."""
    return p / (287.05 * T)


def power(v, CdA, m=80.0, Crr=0.004, wind=0.0, eta=0.97, rho=None):
    """Rider power (W) at ground speed v (m/s) into a headwind `wind` (m/s) on the flat."""
    rho = air_density() if rho is None else rho
    va = v + wind
    aero = 0.5 * rho * CdA * va * abs(va) * v
    roll = Crr * m * G * v
    return (aero + roll) / eta, aero / eta, roll / eta


def speed_for_power(P, CdA, **kw):
    lo, hi = 0.0, 40.0
    for _ in range(100):
        mid = (lo + hi) / 2
        lo, hi = (mid, hi) if power(mid, CdA, **kw)[0] < P else (lo, mid)
    return (lo + hi) / 2


if __name__ == "__main__":
    rho = air_density()
    print(f"air density {rho:.4f} kg/m^3")
    positions = {"upright": 0.50, "hoods": 0.32, "drops": 0.28, "time trial": 0.22}
    for name, CdA in positions.items():
        v = speed_for_power(200, CdA)
        print(f"{name:10s} C_D A = {CdA:.2f} m^2: 200 W gives {v*3.6:.2f} km/h")
    # aerodynamic power alone scales with v^3 in still air
    a1 = power(8.0, 0.32)[1]; a2 = power(16.0, 0.32)[1]
    print(f"aero power at 8 m/s {a1:.2f} W, at 16 m/s {a2:.2f} W, ratio {a2/a1:.4f}")
    assert abs(a2 / a1 - 8) < 1e-12
    P, A, R = power(10.0, 0.32)
    print(f"36 km/h on the hoods: {P:.1f} W total, {A:.1f} W aero ({100*A/P:.0f} %), {R:.1f} W rolling")
    v = speed_for_power(200, 0.32, wind=5.0)
    print(f"into a 5 m/s Bise headwind, 200 W gives {v*3.6:.2f} km/h")
```

:::exercise A tailwind home
The same rider on the hoods ($C_D A = 0.32$, $\rho = 1.1679$) rides home at 40 km/h with a 3 m/s tailwind. Find the aerodynamic power at the wheel (before drivetrain losses) and compare it with still air.
---solution---
1. $v = 11.111$ m/s; air speed $v - 3 = 8.111$ m/s.
2. $P = \tfrac12\cdot1.1679\cdot0.32\cdot8.111^2\cdot11.111 = 136.6$ W.
3. Still air: $\tfrac12\cdot1.1679\cdot0.32\cdot11.111^3 = 256.3$ W. The tailwind almost halves the aerodynamic effort.
:::
