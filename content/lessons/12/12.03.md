In 1904 Ludwig Prandtl solved a puzzle that had stalled fluid mechanics. Ideal-flow theory predicted zero drag, but every real body has drag. Prandtl's answer: viscosity matters only in a thin **boundary layer** next to the surface, where the velocity climbs from zero at the wall to the outer flow speed. Outside it, the flow is effectively inviscid. Splitting the problem in two made aerodynamics possible.

## How thick is the layer?

Momentum diffuses away from the wall a distance $\sqrt{\nu t}$ in time $t$ (lesson 12.01). Fluid travelling at $U$ reaches position $x$ after $t = x/U$, so

$$
\delta \sim \sqrt{\frac{\nu x}{U}} = \frac{x}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_x}}, \qquad \mathrm{Re}_x = \frac{U x}{\nu} .
$$

At high Re the layer is thin compared with the body, which justifies Prandtl's simplifications: the pressure is set by the outer flow and doesn't vary across the layer, and diffusion along $x$ is negligible compared with diffusion across it.

## The Blasius solution

For a flat plate with no pressure gradient, the boundary-layer equations have a similarity solution. With $\eta = y\sqrt{U/(\nu x)}$ and $u/U = f'(\eta)$, they reduce to the **Blasius equation**

$$
f''' + \tfrac12 f f'' = 0, \qquad f(0) = f'(0) = 0,\quad f'(\infty) = 1 .
$$

It's a boundary-value problem: we don't know $f''(0)$. **Shooting** guesses it, integrates outwards with RK4, and adjusts the guess until $f'$ tends to 1. The answer, $f''(0) = 0.332057$, gives everything engineers need:

$$
\frac{\delta_{99}}{x} = \frac{4.91}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_x}}, \qquad C_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac12\rho U^2} = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_x}}, \qquad C_D = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}_L}} .
$$

The layer stays laminar up to about $\mathrm{Re}_x \approx 5\times10^5$; beyond that it usually turns turbulent and thickens much faster.

1. Shooting with RK4 out to $\eta = 10$ and bisection on the wall curvature gives $f''(0) = 0.332057$. The velocity reaches 99% of $U$ at $\eta = 4.910$, and the displacement thickness is at $\eta = 1.7208$.
2. For air at 5 m/s ($\nu = 1.5\times10^{-5}$ m²/s):

| x, m | $\mathrm{Re}_x$ | $\delta_{99}$, mm | $C_f$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | $3.33\times10^4$ | 2.689 | 0.003638 |
| 0.5 | $1.67\times10^5$ | 6.013 | 0.001627 |
| 1.0 | $3.33\times10^5$ | 8.504 | 0.001150 |

3. The friction drag on one side of a 1 m × 1 m plate: $C_D = 1.328/\sqrt{3.33\times10^5} = 0.002300$, so $F = \frac12\cdot1.2\cdot5^2\cdot0.0023 = 0.03450$ N.
:::

```python file=blasius.py
"""The Blasius boundary layer: f''' + f f''/2 = 0 solved by RK4 shooting on the unknown wall curvature f''(0)."""
import math
import numpy as np


def rhs(s):
    f, fp, fpp = s
    return np.array([fp, fpp, -0.5 * f * fpp])


def integrate(fpp0, eta_max=10.0, n=2000):
    h = eta_max / n
    s = np.array([0.0, 0.0, fpp0]); out = [s.copy()]
    for _ in range(n):
        k1 = rhs(s); k2 = rhs(s + h / 2 * k1); k3 = rhs(s + h / 2 * k2); k4 = rhs(s + h * k3)
        s = s + h / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4); out.append(s.copy())
    return np.linspace(0, eta_max, n + 1), np.array(out)


def shoot(lo=0.1, hi=1.0, tol=1e-12):
    """Find f''(0) so that f'(eta_max) = 1 (bisection: f'(inf) grows with f''(0))."""
    while hi - lo > tol:
        m = (lo + hi) / 2
        _, s = integrate(m)
        lo, hi = (m, hi) if s[-1, 1] < 1 else (lo, m)
    return (lo + hi) / 2


if __name__ == "__main__":
    fpp0 = shoot()
    eta, s = integrate(fpp0)
    print(f"f''(0) = {fpp0:.6f}")
    assert abs(fpp0 - 0.332057) < 2e-6
    i99 = np.argmax(s[:, 1] >= 0.99)
    eta99 = eta[i99 - 1] + (0.99 - s[i99 - 1, 1]) * (eta[i99] - eta[i99 - 1]) / (s[i99, 1] - s[i99 - 1, 1])
    disp = eta[-1] - s[-1, 0]                                   # displacement thickness: integral of (1 - f')
    print(f"99 % thickness at eta = {eta99:.3f}; displacement thickness at eta = {disp:.4f}")
    assert abs(disp - 1.7208) < 1e-3 and abs(eta99 - 4.91) < 0.01
    # a 1 m plate in a 5 m/s air stream
    U, nu = 5.0, 1.5e-5
    for x in (0.1, 0.5, 1.0):
        Rex = U * x / nu
        print(f"x = {x:.1f} m: Re_x = {Rex:.3e}, delta99 = {eta99*math.sqrt(nu*x/U)*1000:.3f} mm, Cf = {2*fpp0/math.sqrt(Rex):.6f}")
    CD = 1.328 / math.sqrt(U * 1.0 / nu)
    print(f"friction drag on one side of the 1 m x 1 m plate: {0.5*1.2*U*U*CD:.5f} N (C_D = {CD:.6f})")
```

:::exercise A kite's skin
A kite panel 0.6 m long flies in a 6 m/s wind over Sanur beach ($\nu = 1.6\times10^{-5}$ m²/s). Find $\mathrm{Re}$ at the trailing edge, the boundary-layer thickness there, and the friction coefficient. Is the layer laminar?
---solution---
1. $\mathrm{Re}_L = 6\cdot0.6/1.6\times10^{-5} = 225\,000$.
2. $\delta_{99} = 4.91\cdot0.6/\sqrt{225\,000} = 6.211$ mm.
3. $C_f = 0.664/\sqrt{225\,000} = 0.001400$ at the trailing edge; the average over the panel is $C_D = 1.328/474.3 = 0.002800$.
4. $\mathrm{Re}_L < 5\times10^5$, so a smooth panel stays laminar (in practice, fabric seams and gusts may trip it).
:::
