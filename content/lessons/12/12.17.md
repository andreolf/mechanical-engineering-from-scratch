In the rainy season, squalls sweep across Bali and shop signs on single posts lean, bend, and sometimes come down. Wind load is a dynamic-pressure problem: the force grows with the square of the wind speed, and gusts, not the average wind, set the peak. Building codes such as Eurocode EN 1991-1-4 turn this into rules. Here we build the core of it, with the simplifications labelled.

## Dynamic pressure and force

Air stopped by a surface raises its pressure by up to the **dynamic pressure**

$$
q = \tfrac12\rho V^2 ,
$$

about 600 Pa at 32 m/s. The force on a body is $F = C_f\,q\,A$, where $C_f$ is a force coefficient from wind-tunnel tests. A flat signboard standing clear of the ground has $C_f \approx 1.8$: the stagnation pressure on the front plus the suction of the separated wake behind it.

## Gusts (a labelled simplification)

The mean wind near the ground is turbulent. The peak pressure over a few seconds is what breaks things. A simplified version of the Eurocode approach multiplies the mean pressure by a gust factor:

$$
q_p = (1 + 7I_v)\,\tfrac12\rho V_m^2, \qquad I_v = \frac{1}{\ln(z/z_0)} ,
$$

where $I_v$ is the turbulence intensity at height $z$ and $z_0$ the terrain roughness length: 0.01 m for open sea coast, 0.05 m for open lakeside country, 0.3 m for villages and suburbs. (For rougher terrain such as city centres, and very near the ground, the code uses a minimum height and this simple formula overstates the gusts.). Rough terrain slows the mean wind but makes it gustier. A real design uses the full code, including the terrain category, orography, the structural factor, partial safety factors and the national annex.

## From force to the post

On a sign whose centre is at height $z_c$ above a single post, the base moment is $M = F z_c$ and the bending stress is $\sigma = M/W$, with $W$ the section modulus. For a tube, $W = \pi(D^4 - d^4)/(32D)$. Because $F\propto V^2$, a storm only 40% stronger doubles the stress.

:::example A shop sign in Denpasar
A 3 m × 1.5 m sign, centre 4 m up, village terrain ($z_0 = 0.3$ m), mean wind 22 m/s, ρ = 1.2 kg/m³, $C_f = 1.8$.

1. Mean dynamic pressure 290.4 Pa. Turbulence intensity $1/\ln(4/0.3) = 0.3861$, so the peak pressure is 1075.2 Pa.
2. Force: $1.8\cdot1075.2\cdot4.5 = 8.709$ kN.
3. Base moment 34.84 kN m. A 168.3 × 7.1 mm steel tube has $W = 139.1$ cm³, so the stress is 250.5 MPa. S355 steel yields at 355 MPa: no margin left once a safety factor of 1.5 is applied.
4. Doubling the wind multiplies the load by exactly 4. At 15, 22, 30 and 40 m/s: 4.05, 8.71, 16.19 and 28.79 kN.
:::

```python file=signboard.py
"""Wind load on a signboard: dynamic pressure, a simplified gust factor, force, and the bending stress in its post.
Simplification, clearly labelled: peak pressure = (1 + 7 I_v) x mean pressure with I_v = 1 / ln(z / z0), in the spirit of
EN 1991-1-4. A real design follows the code in full, including the terrain, orography and the national annex."""
import math


def dynamic_pressure(V, rho=1.20):
    return 0.5 * rho * V * V


def peak_pressure(V_mean, z, z0=0.3, rho=1.20):
    Iv = 1 / math.log(z / z0)
    return (1 + 7 * Iv) * dynamic_pressure(V_mean, rho), Iv


def sign_load(V_mean, b, h, z_centre, Cf=1.8, z0=0.3):
    qp, Iv = peak_pressure(V_mean, z_centre, z0)
    return Cf * qp * b * h, qp, Iv


def tube_section_modulus(D, t):
    d = D - 2 * t
    return math.pi * (D ** 4 - d ** 4) / (32 * D)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a 3 m x 1.5 m shop sign in Denpasar on one steel post, centre 4 m up, mean wind 22 m/s at that height
    b, h, zc, V = 3.0, 1.5, 4.0, 22.0
    F, qp, Iv = sign_load(V, b, h, zc)
    print(f"mean dynamic pressure {dynamic_pressure(V):.1f} Pa; turbulence intensity {Iv:.4f}; peak pressure {qp:.1f} Pa")
    print(f"force on the sign (C_f = 1.8): {F/1000:.3f} kN")
    M = F * zc
    W = tube_section_modulus(0.1683, 0.0071)                  # 168.3 x 7.1 mm circular hollow section
    print(f"base moment {M/1000:.2f} kN m; post W = {W*1e6:.1f} cm^3; bending stress {M/W/1e6:.1f} MPa (S355 yields at 355)")
    # load scales with v^2
    F2, _, _ = sign_load(2 * V, b, h, zc)
    print(f"doubling the wind speed multiplies the load by {F2/F:.6f}")
    assert abs(F2 / F - 4) < 1e-12
    for Vx in (15, 22, 30, 40):
        Fx, _, _ = sign_load(Vx, b, h, zc)
        print(f"  {Vx} m/s: {Fx/1000:6.2f} kN, stress {Fx*zc/W/1e6:6.1f} MPa")
```

:::exercise Choosing a safe post
For the same sign and wind, the designer wants the stress below 355/1.5 = 236.7 MPa including a load factor of 1.5 on the wind. What section modulus is needed? Would a 219.1 × 6.3 mm tube do?
---solution---
1. Design moment $1.5\cdot34.84 = 52.26$ kN m.
2. Required $W = 52\,260/236.7\times10^6 = 2.208\times10^{-4}$ m³ = 220.8 cm³.
3. For 219.1 × 6.3 mm: $d = 206.5$ mm, $W = \pi(0.2191^4 - 0.2065^4)/(32\cdot0.2191) = 2.178\times10^{-4}$ m³ = 217.8 cm³. Just short, so pick the next wall thickness (219.1 × 8 mm).
:::
