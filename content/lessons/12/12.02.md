Below the Limmat's surface, fine silt can stay suspended for hours. Bacteria swim, pollen drifts and fog droplets hang in the air. At these tiny scales the Reynolds number is far below 1: viscosity overwhelms inertia. This world, called **creeping flow** or Stokes flow, behaves very differently from ours. Flow is perfectly reversible, there's no coasting, and drag is proportional to speed, not speed squared.

## Stokes flow

When $\mathrm{Re} = \rho U L/\mu \ll 1$, drop the inertia terms from Navier-Stokes:

$$
\nabla p = \mu\nabla^2\mathbf u, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf u = 0 .
$$

These equations are linear and have no time derivative. Reverse the motion of the boundaries and the flow retraces itself exactly. That's why a scallop opening and closing its shell can't swim at low Re (Purcell's scallop theorem), and why bacteria rotate corkscrew flagella instead.

## Stokes drag on a sphere

Stokes solved the flow around a sphere of radius $R$ moving at speed $U$. Integrating the pressure and viscous stresses over its surface gives

$$
F = 6\pi\mu R U ,
$$

one third from pressure and two thirds from friction. In terms of a drag coefficient, $C_D = 24/\mathrm{Re}$ with $\mathrm{Re}$ based on the diameter.

## Settling velocity

A grain sinks at constant speed when drag balances its weight minus buoyancy:

$$
6\pi\mu R U = (\rho_p - \rho)\,g\,\tfrac43\pi R^3 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad U = \frac{(\rho_p - \rho)\,g\,d^2}{18\,\mu} .
$$

The speed goes as $d^2$: a grain ten times smaller sinks a hundred times slower. Stokes' law holds for $\mathrm{Re} \lesssim 0.5$. For bigger grains inertia adds drag; a common correlation is Schiller-Naumann, $C_D = \frac{24}{\mathrm{Re}}(1 + 0.15\,\mathrm{Re}^{0.687})$, valid up to $\mathrm{Re} \approx 800$.

| Diameter | Stokes, mm/s | With drag correlation, mm/s | Re |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 µm (clay) | 0.0036 | 0.0036 | $7\times10^{-6}$ |
| 20 µm (silt) | 0.3597 | 0.3579 | 0.0072 |
| 60 µm | 3.2373 | 3.0916 | 0.19 |
| 200 µm (fine sand) | 35.970 | 24.796 | 5.0 |
| 1 mm (sand) | 899.25 | 155.10 | 155 |

At low Re the two agree within 0.5%. A 20 µm silt grain takes 2.32 h to sink through 3 m of still water; a 2 µm clay particle needs 9.7 days, which is why glacier rivers stay milky.
:::

```python file=settling.py
"""Settling of sediment grains: Stokes' law and its limit, with a drag correlation for larger grains."""
import math

G = 9.81


def stokes_velocity(d, rho_p, rho, mu):
    """Terminal velocity from 6 pi mu R U = (rho_p - rho) g (4/3) pi R^3."""
    return (rho_p - rho) * G * d * d / (18 * mu)


def cd_schiller_naumann(Re):
    return 24 / Re * (1 + 0.15 * Re ** 0.687)


def terminal_velocity(d, rho_p, rho, mu):
    """Balance weight minus buoyancy against drag with C_D(Re), by fixed-point iteration from the Stokes value."""
    U = stokes_velocity(d, rho_p, rho, mu)
    for _ in range(200):
        Re = rho * U * d / mu
        U_new = math.sqrt(4 * (rho_p - rho) * G * d / (3 * rho * cd_schiller_naumann(Re)))
        if abs(U_new - U) < 1e-14: break
        U = 0.5 * (U + U_new)
    return U


if __name__ == "__main__":
    rho_p, rho, mu = 2650.0, 1000.0, 1.0e-3        # quartz in river water at 20 C
    for d_um in (2, 20, 60, 200, 1000):
        d = d_um * 1e-6
        Us, U = stokes_velocity(d, rho_p, rho, mu), terminal_velocity(d, rho_p, rho, mu)
        print(f"d = {d_um:5d} um: Stokes {Us*1000:10.4f} mm/s, with drag correlation {U*1000:9.4f} mm/s, Re = {rho*U*d/mu:.4g}")
        if rho * U * d / mu < 0.1:
            assert abs(U / Us - 1) < 0.02                                       # Stokes' law holds at low Re
    d = 20e-6; U = stokes_velocity(d, rho_p, rho, mu)
    print(f"20 um silt settles 3 m in {3/U/3600:.2f} h; a 2 um clay particle needs {3/stokes_velocity(2e-6, rho_p, rho, mu)/86400:.1f} days")
    # check the drag: 6 pi mu R U equals the submerged weight
    R = d / 2
    assert abs(6 * math.pi * mu * R * U - (rho_p - rho) * G * 4 / 3 * math.pi * R ** 3) < 1e-20
```

:::exercise A fog droplet
A water droplet 10 µm across falls through air ($\mu = 1.8\times10^{-5}$ Pa s, $\rho = 1.2$ kg/m³). Find its terminal speed, check the Reynolds number, and estimate how long it takes to fall 100 m.
---solution---
1. $U = (1000 - 1.2)\cdot9.81\cdot(10^{-5})^2/(18\cdot1.8\times10^{-5}) = 3.024\times10^{-3}$ m/s, about 3 mm/s.
2. $\mathrm{Re} = 1.2\cdot3.024\times10^{-3}\cdot10^{-5}/1.8\times10^{-5} = 0.0020$: deep in the Stokes regime.
3. Falling 100 m takes $100/3.024\times10^{-3} = 33\,069$ s, about 9.2 h. Any updraft keeps fog aloft.
:::
