An engineer designing a weir on the Limmat doesn't care where each eddy goes. They need the mean velocity, the mean pressure and the mean forces. Osborne Reynolds' idea was to split every quantity into a mean and a fluctuation and average the equations. The result is a set of equations for the mean flow, plus new terms that describe how turbulence carries momentum, and no exact equation for those.

## Reynolds decomposition

Write each velocity component as a time mean plus a fluctuation:

$$
u = \bar u + u', \qquad \bar u = \frac1T\int_0^T u\,dt, \qquad \overline{u'} = 0 .
$$

The mean of a fluctuation is zero by definition. The mean of a product of fluctuations is not:

$$
\overline{uv} = \bar u\,\bar v + \overline{u'v'} .
$$

## RANS and the Reynolds stresses

Averaging the Navier-Stokes equations, the nonlinear term produces exactly such products. The Reynolds-averaged (RANS) momentum equation is

$$
\rho\left(\frac{\partial\bar u_i}{\partial t} + \bar u_j\frac{\partial\bar u_i}{\partial x_j}\right) = -\frac{\partial\bar p}{\partial x_i} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left(\mu\frac{\partial\bar u_i}{\partial x_j} - \rho\,\overline{u_i'u_j'}\right) .
$$

The new terms $-\rho\,\overline{u_i'u_j'}$ act like extra stresses: the **Reynolds stresses**. Near a riverbed, fluid moving up ($v' > 0$) tends to be slow ($u' < 0$), so $\overline{u'v'} < 0$ and the turbulent shear stress $-\rho\overline{u'v'}$ is positive, often hundreds of times the viscous stress. Half the trace is the **turbulent kinetic energy**, $k = \tfrac12(\overline{u'^2} + \overline{v'^2} + \overline{w'^2})$.

## The closure problem

Averaging created six new unknowns and no new equations. Writing equations for them brings in triple correlations, and so on forever: the **closure problem**. Engineering models close it with assumptions. The most common is an eddy viscosity, $-\overline{u'v'} = \nu_t\,\partial\bar u/\partial y$, with $\nu_t$ from a model such as k-ε or k-ω SST.

2. $\overline{u'u'} = 0.02246$, $\overline{v'v'} = 0.00638$, $\overline{w'w'} = 0.01002$ m²/s². Turbulence intensity 9.99%.
3. $\overline{u'v'} = -0.004796$ m²/s² (the target was $-0.4\cdot0.15\cdot0.08 = -0.0048$), so the Reynolds shear stress is $-\rho\overline{u'v'} = 4.796$ Pa.
4. $k = 0.01943$ J/kg.
5. $\overline{uv} = -0.004828 = \bar u\bar v + \overline{u'v'}$, exact to $10^{-12}$: averaging a product is not the product of averages.
:::

```python file=reynoldsavg.py
"""Reynolds decomposition of a velocity record: mean, fluctuations, Reynolds stresses and turbulent kinetic energy."""
import numpy as np


def decompose(u):
    U = u.mean()
    return U, u - U


def reynolds_stresses(u, v, w, rho=1000.0):
    _, up = decompose(u); _, vp = decompose(v); _, wp = decompose(w)
    uu, vv, ww, uv = (up * up).mean(), (vp * vp).mean(), (wp * wp).mean(), (up * vp).mean()
    return {"uu": uu, "vv": vv, "ww": ww, "uv": uv, "k": 0.5 * (uu + vv + ww), "tau_xy": -rho * uv}


def synthetic_channel(n, U=1.5, su=0.15, sv=0.08, sw=0.10, r_uv=-0.4, seed=7):
    """Gaussian fluctuations with a prescribed u'v' correlation, as near the bed of a river."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    a, b, c = rng.standard_normal((3, n))
    u = U + su * a
    v = sv * (r_uv * a + np.sqrt(1 - r_uv ** 2) * b)
    w = sw * c
    return u, v, w


if __name__ == "__main__":
    u, v, w = synthetic_channel(200_000)
    U, up = decompose(u)
    print(f"mean U = {U:.4f} m/s; mean of the fluctuation u' = {up.mean():.1e}")
    assert abs(up.mean()) < 1e-12                                # fluctuations average to zero by construction
    R = reynolds_stresses(u, v, w)
    print(f"<u'u'> = {R['uu']:.5f}, <v'v'> = {R['vv']:.5f}, <w'w'> = {R['ww']:.5f}, <u'v'> = {R['uv']:.6f} m^2/s^2")
    print(f"turbulent kinetic energy k = {R['k']:.5f} J/kg; Reynolds shear stress -rho<u'v'> = {R['tau_xy']:.3f} Pa")
    print(f"expected <u'v'> = r su sv = {-0.4*0.15*0.08:.6f}; turbulence intensity {np.sqrt(R['uu'])/U*100:.2f} %")
    # averaging a product is not the product of averages: the closure problem in one line
    uv_bar = (u * v).mean(); print(f"mean(u v) = {uv_bar:.6f} = U V + <u'v'> = {U*v.mean() + R['uv']:.6f}")
    assert abs(uv_bar - (U * v.mean() + R['uv'])) < 1e-12
```

:::exercise Turbulent versus viscous stress
At a point near the bed, $\overline{u'v'} = -0.0048$ m²/s² and the mean shear is $\partial\bar u/\partial y = 2$ s⁻¹. Find the Reynolds shear stress, the viscous stress (water, $\mu = 10^{-3}$ Pa s), and the eddy viscosity.
---solution---
1. Reynolds stress: $-1000\cdot(-0.0048) = 4.8$ Pa.
2. Viscous stress: $10^{-3}\cdot2 = 0.002$ Pa, 2400 times smaller.
3. Eddy viscosity $\nu_t = 0.0048/2 = 0.0024$ m²/s, 2400 times the molecular $\nu$.
:::
