You buy a coconut at a 30 °C roadside stall and put it in the fridge. When is it cold? If the whole coconut stayed at one uniform temperature while it cooled, the answer would be a simple exponential. Whether that is a fair assumption depends on one number, the **Biot number**.

## The lumped model

Suppose a body of volume $V$, surface area $A$, density $\rho$ and specific heat $c$ is at a single temperature $T(t)$. Its energy falls at the rate convection removes it:

$$
\rho c V \frac{dT}{dt} = -hA\,(T - T_\infty) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{T - T_\infty}{T_i - T_\infty} = e^{-t/\tau}, \qquad \tau = \frac{\rho c V}{hA} .
$$

After one time constant $\tau$ the excess temperature is down to 36.8%, after $3\tau$ to 5%. This is the same first-order law as an RC circuit charging.

## When is it allowed?

Inside the body, heat must conduct to the surface; outside, convection carries it off. The ratio of the internal conduction resistance to the external convection resistance is the Biot number, with the characteristic length $L_c = V/A$:

$$
\text{Bi} = \frac{hL_c}{k} .
$$

If $\text{Bi} < 0.1$ the inside stays within about 5% of uniform and the lumped model is accurate. A small steel part in oil passes easily. A water-filled coconut does not: water conducts poorly, so the outside cools while the centre lags.

Lumped exponential against the exact mean and centre temperatures of a sphere. Push the Biot number past 0.1 and watch the lime curve part company with the truth.
:::

:::example Coconut and bearing ball
**Assumptions.** The coconut is a sphere of water, r = 6 cm (ρ = 1000, c = 4180, k = 0.6), with the shell ignored. Still fridge air at 4 °C gives h = 10 W/(m² K). It starts at 30 °C; "cold" means 8 °C.

1. $L_c = r/3 = 0.02$ m, so $\text{Bi} = 10\cdot0.02/0.6 = 0.3333$: too big for the lumped model.
2. Lumped anyway: $\tau = \rho c L_c/h = 8360$ s $= 2.3222$ h, and the time to 8 °C is $\tau\ln(26/4) = 4.3467$ h. An RK4 integration of the ODE lands on 8 °C to $10^{-9}$.
3. The exact sphere solution (a 40-term series) at that moment: mean 9.4961 °C, centre 11.1005 °C. The mean actually reaches 8 °C after 5.2439 h and the centre after 5.9671 h. The lumped model is about 1.6 h optimistic about when the middle is cold.
4. A 10 mm steel bearing ball quenched in oil (k = 45, h = 100): $\text{Bi} = 0.0037$, $\tau = 61.10$ s, and the lumped curve stays within $8.2\times10^{-4}$ of the exact mean throughout.
:::

```python file=lumped.py
"""Lumped cooling: the exponential law, checked by RK4, and tested against the exact sphere series to see where Bi < 0.1 matters."""
import math


def rk4_cool(T0, Tinf, tau, t_end, n=2000):
    f = lambda T: -(T - Tinf) / tau
    T, dt = T0, t_end / n
    for _ in range(n):
        k1 = f(T); k2 = f(T + dt / 2 * k1); k3 = f(T + dt / 2 * k2); k4 = f(T + dt * k3)
        T += dt / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4)
    return T


def bisect(g, a, b, tol=1e-13):
    ga = g(a)
    while b - a > tol:
        c = 0.5 * (a + b); gc = g(c)
        if (ga < 0) == (gc < 0): a, ga = c, gc
        else: b = c
    return 0.5 * (a + b)


def sphere_roots(Bi, n=40):
    """Roots of 1 - z cot z = Bi (Bi on the radius), one in each interval ((k-1) pi, k pi)."""
    g = lambda z: 1 - z / math.tan(z) - Bi
    return [bisect(g, (k - 1) * math.pi + 1e-9, k * math.pi - 1e-9) for k in range(1, n + 1)]


def sphere_exact(Bi, Fo, roots):
    """Centre and volume-mean dimensionless temperature theta/theta_i of a sphere."""
    centre = mean = 0.0
    for z in roots:
        C = 4 * (math.sin(z) - z * math.cos(z)) / (2 * z - math.sin(2 * z))
        e = math.exp(-z * z * Fo)
        centre += C * e
        mean += C * e * 3 * (math.sin(z) - z * math.cos(z)) / z ** 3
    return centre, mean


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a coconut from a 30 C market stall into a 4 C fridge: treated as a water sphere, r = 6 cm, shell ignored, h = 10 (still fridge air)
    r, rho, c, k, h = 0.06, 1000.0, 4180.0, 0.6, 10.0
    Ti, Tinf, Tgoal = 30.0, 4.0, 8.0
    Lc = r / 3
    Bi = h * Lc / k
    tau = rho * c * Lc / h
    t8 = tau * math.log((Ti - Tinf) / (Tgoal - Tinf))
    print(f"coconut: Bi = hLc/k = {Bi:.4f} (> 0.1), tau = {tau:.0f} s = {tau/3600:.4f} h; lumped time to 8 C: {t8/3600:.4f} h")
    assert abs(rk4_cool(Ti, Tinf, tau, t8) - Tgoal) < 1e-9
    roots = sphere_roots(h * r / k)
    alpha = k / (rho * c)
    Fo = alpha * t8 / r ** 2
    cen, mean = sphere_exact(h * r / k, Fo, roots)
    print(f"exact sphere at that time: mean {Tinf + mean*(Ti-Tinf):.4f} C, centre {Tinf + cen*(Ti-Tinf):.4f} C")
    tm = bisect(lambda t: sphere_exact(h * r / k, alpha * t / r**2, roots)[1] - (Tgoal - Tinf) / (Ti - Tinf), 100, 1e6, 1e-3)
    tc = bisect(lambda t: sphere_exact(h * r / k, alpha * t / r**2, roots)[0] - (Tgoal - Tinf) / (Ti - Tinf), 100, 1e6, 1e-3)
    print(f"exact: mean reaches 8 C after {tm/3600:.4f} h, centre after {tc/3600:.4f} h")
    assert tc > tm > t8
    # a valid case: a 10 mm steel bearing ball quenched in oil, h = 100
    r, rho, c, k, h = 0.005, 7800.0, 470.0, 45.0, 100.0
    Bi = h * r / 3 / k; tau = rho * c * r / 3 / h
    roots = sphere_roots(h * r / k)
    worst = max(abs(math.exp(-t / tau) - sphere_exact(h * r / k, k / (rho * c) * t / r**2, roots)[1]) for t in range(5, 1001, 5))
    print(f"steel ball: Bi = {Bi:.5f}, tau = {tau:.2f} s; lumped vs exact mean, worst gap {worst:.1e}")
    assert worst < 1e-3
```

:::exercise A thermocouple bead
A thermocouple bead is a 1 mm diameter sphere (ρ = 8500, c = 400, k = 20) in an air stream with h = 150 W/(m² K). Check the Biot number, find its time constant, and how long it takes to show 99% of a sudden temperature step.
---solution---
1. $L_c = r/3 = 0.0005/3 = 1.667\times10^{-4}$ m. $\text{Bi} = 150\cdot1.667\times10^{-4}/20 = 0.00125$: lumped is fine.
2. $\tau = \rho c L_c/h = 8500\cdot400\cdot1.667\times10^{-4}/150 = 3.778$ s.
3. 99% means $e^{-t/\tau} = 0.01$, so $t = \tau\ln 100 = 17.40$ s.
:::
