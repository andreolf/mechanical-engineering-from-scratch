A solar panel on a Bali rooftop and one in Zürich see the same Sun but harvest very different amounts of energy. How much a surface collects depends on where the Sun is in the sky, which depends on latitude, the day of the year and the time of day. This geometry is the first step in sizing any solar collector or panel.

## Where is the Sun?

The Earth's axis is tilted 23.45°, so the latitude where the Sun is overhead at noon, the **declination** $\delta$, swings through the year. A good approximation (Cooper) for day number $n$:

$$
\delta = 23.45^\circ\,\sin\!\left(360^\circ\,\frac{284 + n}{365}\right) .
$$

The **hour angle** $\omega = 15^\circ$ per hour from solar noon. The Sun's elevation above the horizon $\alpha_s$ at latitude $\varphi$ is

$$
\sin\alpha_s = \sin\varphi\sin\delta + \cos\varphi\cos\delta\cos\omega .
$$

At noon ($\omega = 0$) this collapses to $\alpha_{\text{noon}} = 90^\circ - |\varphi - \delta|$. In the northern hemisphere with the Sun to the south, that is $90^\circ - \varphi + \delta$.

## How much energy arrives?

Outside the atmosphere the beam carries the solar constant, about 1361 W/m². A horizontal surface intercepts $G\sin\alpha_s$. Integrating over the day from sunrise to sunset ($\cos\omega_s = -\tan\varphi\tan\delta$) gives the daily extraterrestrial total:

$$
H_0 = \frac{24}{\pi}\,G_{sc}\left(\cos\varphi\cos\delta\sin\omega_s + \omega_s\sin\varphi\sin\delta\right) .
$$

The atmosphere absorbs and scatters more when the Sun is low, because the light crosses more air (the air mass is about $1/\sin\alpha_s$). A simple clear-sky model (Meinel) for the direct beam is $1353\cdot0.7^{\,\text{AM}^{0.678}}$ W/m², to which we add about 10% for diffuse sky light. Clouds then cut the real total well below the clear-sky figure.

:::example Bali against Zürich
Latitudes: Denpasar 8.65° S ($\varphi = -8.65^\circ$), Zürich 47.37° N.

1. On 21 June ($\delta = +23.450^\circ$), the noon Sun stands at 57.900° in Bali (in the *northern* sky) and 66.080° in Zürich. On 21 December ($\delta = -23.450^\circ$): 75.200° in Bali, only 19.180° in Zürich. A minute-by-minute search of the day confirms each noon value matches $90^\circ - |\varphi - \delta|$ to $10^{-6}$ degrees.
2. Numerically integrating $G_{sc}\sin\alpha_s$ over each day matches the analytic $H_0$ to within 1 Wh/m².
3. Clear-sky days: Zürich gets 8.090 kWh/m² on a horizontal panel in June but 1.051 in December, a ratio of 7.70. Bali gets 5.633 in June and 7.216 in December.
4. Over a clear-sky year: 2480 kWh/m² in Bali, 1621 in Zürich. A 20% efficient panel makes 496 and 324 kWh/m². Real totals are lower (clouds), but the ratio holds roughly.
:::

```python file=solar.py
"""Solar geometry and clear-sky yield: sun position from declination and hour angle, checked against the noon formula and the analytic daily extraterrestrial total."""
import math

GSC = 1361.0  # solar constant, W/m^2


def declination(n):
    """Cooper's formula, degrees, day of year n."""
    return 23.45 * math.sin(math.radians(360 * (284 + n) / 365))


def elevation(lat, n, solar_hour):
    phi, d, w = math.radians(lat), math.radians(declination(n)), math.radians(15 * (solar_hour - 12))
    return math.degrees(math.asin(math.sin(phi) * math.sin(d) + math.cos(phi) * math.cos(d) * math.cos(w)))


def daily_horizontal(lat, n, clear_sky=False, steps=1440):
    """Daily energy on a horizontal square metre, kWh, by integrating over the day (1-minute steps).
    clear_sky: beam through the atmosphere by Meinel's model, DNI = 1353 * 0.7^(AM^0.678), plus 10% diffuse."""
    total = 0.0
    for i in range(steps):
        a = elevation(lat, n, (i + 0.5) * 24 / steps)
        if a <= 0: continue
        s = math.sin(math.radians(a))
        if clear_sky:
            am = 1 / s
            total += 1.1 * 1353 * 0.7 ** (am ** 0.678) * s
        else:
            total += GSC * s
    return total * 24 / steps / 1000


def daily_extraterrestrial_exact(lat, n):
    phi, d = math.radians(lat), math.radians(declination(n))
    ws = math.acos(max(-1.0, min(1.0, -math.tan(phi) * math.tan(d))))
    return 24 / math.pi * GSC * (math.cos(phi) * math.cos(d) * math.sin(ws) + ws * math.sin(phi) * math.sin(d)) / 1000


if __name__ == "__main__":
    places = {"Bali (Denpasar)": -8.65, "Zurich": 47.37}
    for name, lat in places.items():
        for n, label in ((172, "21 June"), (355, "21 December")):
            noon = elevation(lat, n, 12.0)
            peak = max(elevation(lat, n, 6 + i / 600) for i in range(7201))
            print(f"{name}, {label}: declination {declination(n):+.3f}, noon elevation {noon:.3f} deg (90 - |lat - decl| = {90 - abs(lat - declination(n)):.3f})")
            assert abs(noon - (90 - abs(lat - declination(n)))) < 1e-9 and abs(peak - noon) < 1e-6
            assert abs(daily_horizontal(lat, n) - daily_extraterrestrial_exact(lat, n)) < 1e-3
    yearly = {}
    for name, lat in places.items():
        yearly[name] = sum(daily_horizontal(lat, n, clear_sky=True, steps=288) for n in range(1, 366))
        print(f"{name}: clear-sky yearly total on a horizontal panel {yearly[name]:.0f} kWh/m^2; a 20% panel makes {0.2*yearly[name]:.0f} kWh/m^2")
    jun, dec = daily_horizontal(47.37, 172, True), daily_horizontal(47.37, 355, True)
    print(f"Zurich clear-sky day: June {jun:.3f} kWh/m^2, December {dec:.3f} kWh/m^2 (ratio {jun/dec:.2f}); Bali June {daily_horizontal(-8.65, 172, True):.3f}, December {daily_horizontal(-8.65, 355, True):.3f}")
    assert yearly["Bali (Denpasar)"] > 1.2 * yearly["Zurich"] and jun / dec > 5
```

:::exercise Noon in Bali and Zürich at the equinox
On the March equinox ($\delta = 0$), find the noon elevation and the clear-sky direct beam at noon (Meinel, air mass $1/\sin\alpha_s$) in Bali and in Zürich.
---solution---
1. Bali: $90 - 8.65 = 81.35^\circ$. AM $= 1/\sin 81.35^\circ = 1.0115$, beam $= 1353\cdot0.7^{1.0115^{0.678}} = 944.5$ W/m².
2. Zürich: $90 - 47.37 = 42.63^\circ$. AM $= 1.4765$, beam $= 1353\cdot0.7^{1.4767^{0.678}} = 850.3$ W/m².
3. On a horizontal surface, multiply by $\sin\alpha_s$: 933.7 and 575.8 W/m².
:::
