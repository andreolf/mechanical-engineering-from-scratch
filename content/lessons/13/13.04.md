A CPU dissipates 100 W from a chip the size of a fingernail. Convection from such a small area can't carry that away. The solution is to extend the surface with **fins**: the aluminium spikes on a cooler, the ribs on a motorbike cylinder, the plates of a radiator. A fin works only as well as heat can conduct along it, so its temperature falls with distance from the base, and beyond a certain length extra metal does almost nothing.

## The fin equation

Take a fin of cross-section $A$, perimeter $P$ and conductivity $k$, in air at $T_\infty$ with coefficient $h$. On a slice $dx$, heat conducted in minus heat conducted out equals heat convected from the side, $hP\,dx\,(T - T_\infty)$. With the excess temperature $\theta = T - T_\infty$:

$$
\frac{d^2\theta}{dx^2} = m^2\theta, \qquad m^2 = \frac{hP}{kA} .
$$

With $\theta(0) = \theta_b$ at the base and a convecting tip, $-k\theta'(L) = h\theta(L)$:

$$
\frac{\theta}{\theta_b} = \frac{\cosh m(L-x) + \frac{h}{mk}\sinh m(L-x)}{\cosh mL + \frac{h}{mk}\sinh mL} .
$$

## Heat rate, efficiency, effectiveness

The heat leaving the fin equals what enters its base, $q = -kA\,\theta'(0)$:

$$
q = M\,\frac{\sinh mL + \frac{h}{mk}\cosh mL}{\cosh mL + \frac{h}{mk}\sinh mL}, \qquad M = \sqrt{hPkA}\,\theta_b .
$$

For a very long fin, $q \to M = \sqrt{hPkA}\,\theta_b$. Past $mL \approx 2.5$ the fin delivers over 98% of that, so more length is wasted. The **fin efficiency** $\eta_f = q/(hA_{\text{fin}}\theta_b)$ compares the fin with an ideal one at base temperature all over; the **effectiveness** $q/(hA\theta_b)$ compares it with no fin at all. Fins pay off when $k$ is high, $h$ is low and the fins are slender.

:::example A CPU cooler pin
Aluminium pin, d = 3 mm, L = 30 mm, k = 200 W/(m K), fan-driven h = 40 W/(m² K), base 40 K above the air.

1. $m = \sqrt{hP/(kA)} = 16.3299$ 1/m, so $mL = 0.4899$: a short, efficient fin. The tip runs at 35.4402 K above the air.
2. A finite-difference solve with 2001 nodes and a ghost-node tip condition matches the analytic profile to $4\times10^{-10}$.
3. Heat rate 0.42829 W, the same from the FD base gradient. Efficiency 0.9236; effectiveness 37.87, so the pin carries 38 times what its base area would without it.
4. Longer pins: 0.85713 W at 0.1 m, 0.92334 W at 0.3 m, 0.92344 W at 1 m $= \sqrt{hPkA}\,\theta_b$, the long-fin limit.
:::

```python file=fin.py
"""Fins: a finite-difference solve of the fin equation theta'' = m^2 theta, against the analytic profile, heat rate and efficiency."""
import math
import numpy as np


def fin_fd(L, m, hk, n=2001):
    """theta(0) = 1 at the base; convective tip: -k theta' = h theta at x = L (hk = h/k). Second-order tip via a ghost node."""
    x = np.linspace(0, L, n); dx = x[1]
    A = np.zeros((n, n)); b = np.zeros(n)
    A[0, 0] = 1.0; b[0] = 1.0
    for i in range(1, n - 1):
        A[i, i - 1] = A[i, i + 1] = 1.0; A[i, i] = -2.0 - (m * dx) ** 2
    A[-1, -2] = 2.0; A[-1, -1] = -2.0 - (m * dx) ** 2 - 2 * dx * hk  # ghost node eliminated with the tip condition
    return x, np.linalg.solve(A, b)


def fin_exact(x, L, m, hk):
    a = hk / m
    return (np.cosh(m * (L - x)) + a * np.sinh(m * (L - x))) / (math.cosh(m * L) + a * math.sinh(m * L))


def fin_heat(L, m, hk, M):
    a = hk / m
    return M * (math.sinh(m * L) + a * math.cosh(m * L)) / (math.cosh(m * L) + a * math.sinh(m * L))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # an aluminium pin fin on a CPU cooler: d = 3 mm, L = 30 mm, k = 200, fan-driven h = 40 W/(m^2 K), base 40 K above the air
    d, L, k, h, tb = 0.003, 0.030, 200.0, 40.0, 40.0
    P, A = math.pi * d, math.pi * d * d / 4
    m = math.sqrt(h * P / (k * A)); M = math.sqrt(h * P * k * A) * tb
    x, th = fin_fd(L, m, h / k)
    err = np.abs(th - fin_exact(x, L, m, h / k)).max()
    print(f"m = {m:.4f} 1/m, mL = {m*L:.4f}; tip excess temperature {th[-1]*tb:.4f} K; FD vs analytic max error {err:.1e}")
    assert err < 1e-6
    q = fin_heat(L, m, h / k, M)
    q_fd = -k * A * tb * (-3 * th[0] + 4 * th[1] - th[2]) / (2 * x[1])
    eta = q / (h * (P * L + A) * tb)
    print(f"heat rate {q:.5f} W (from the FD base gradient {q_fd:.5f} W); efficiency {eta:.4f}; effectiveness {q/(h*A*tb):.2f}")
    assert abs(q_fd - q) < 1e-4 * q
    # long-fin limit: q -> sqrt(h P k A) theta_b
    for Lx in (0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1.0):
        print(f"  L = {Lx:4.2f} m: q = {fin_heat(Lx, m, h/k, M):.5f} W (long-fin limit {M:.5f} W)")
    assert abs(fin_heat(1.0, m, h / k, M) - M) < 1e-9 * M
```

:::exercise A steel bolt as a fin
A 10 mm stainless-steel bolt (k = 15) sticks 50 mm out of a hot engine block, 80 K above the air, with h = 25 W/(m² K). Treat it as a long fin. Find m, mL, and the heat it carries away.
---solution---
1. $P = \pi\cdot0.01 = 0.031416$ m, $A = 7.854\times10^{-5}$ m². $m = \sqrt{25\cdot0.031416/(15\cdot7.854\times10^{-5})} = 25.82$ 1/m.
2. $mL = 1.291$: not really long. $\tanh(1.291) = 0.8594$, so an adiabatic-tip estimate gives 86% of the long-fin value.
3. Long-fin value: $\sqrt{25\cdot0.031416\cdot15\cdot7.854\times10^{-5}}\cdot80 = 2.433$ W. With the adiabatic tip, about $2.433\cdot0.8594 = 2.09$ W.
:::
