Wrapping a hot pipe in insulation always saves heat, right? Usually. But wrap a thin electrical wire in plastic and it actually runs *cooler* than the bare wire. The reason is geometry: in a cylinder, heat spreads through ever larger areas, and adding insulation also adds outside surface for convection to work on.

## Conduction through a cylindrical shell

In a long pipe, heat flows radially. The same heat $Q$ crosses every cylinder of radius $r$, whose area is $2\pi rL$, so Fourier's law reads $Q = -k\,2\pi rL\,dT/dr$. Integrating from $r_1$ to $r_2$:

$$
Q = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{R_{\text{cyl}}}, \qquad R_{\text{cyl}} = \frac{\ln(r_2/r_1)}{2\pi kL} .
$$

The temperature falls logarithmically with radius, not linearly. For a sphere the areas grow as $4\pi r^2$, and $R_{\text{sph}} = (1/r_1 - 1/r_2)/(4\pi k)$.

## The critical radius

Put insulation of conductivity $k$ on a pipe of radius $r_1$ and let air with coefficient $h$ carry heat away from the outer surface $r_2$. Per metre of pipe, the two resistances are in series:

$$
R'(r_2) = \frac{\ln(r_2/r_1)}{2\pi k} + \frac{1}{2\pi r_2 h} .
$$

The first term grows with $r_2$, the second shrinks. Setting $dR'/dr_2 = 0$:

$$
r_c = \frac{k}{h} \quad\text{(cylinder)}, \qquad r_c = \frac{2k}{h} \quad\text{(sphere)} .
$$

At $r_2 = r_c$ the resistance is a *minimum*, so the heat loss is a *maximum*. If the bare radius is already larger than $r_c$, any insulation helps. Hot-water pipes are always in this regime: with foam at $k = 0.04$ and still air at $h = 10$, $r_c = 4$ mm. Thin wires are not: with PVC at $k = 0.19$, $r_c = 19$ mm, so the insulation on a 2 mm wire helps keep it cool.

:::example A coated wire and a hot-water pipe
A 2 mm-radius wire, 40 K above still air (h = 10 W/(m² K)), coated in PVC (k = 0.19).

1. Bare, it loses 5.0265 W/m. Scanning $r_2$ in 0.01 mm steps, the loss peaks at 14.6871 W/m at $r_2 = 19.00$ mm, and a golden-section search puts the maximum at 19.000000 mm $= k/h$.
2. A sphere of radius 1 mm with the same coating peaks at 38.00 mm $= 2k/h$.
3. A 15 mm copper hot-water pipe ($r_1 = 7.5$ mm, 50 K above the air) with foam (k = 0.04): 23.562 W/m bare, 15.125 with 5 mm of foam, 10.466 with 13 mm, 7.906 with 25 mm. Here $r_c = 4$ mm is smaller than the pipe, so every millimetre helps.
:::

```python file=critical.py
"""Insulating a pipe or a wire: cylindrical conduction resistance, and the critical radius where heat loss peaks."""
import math
import numpy as np


def loss_per_metre(r1, r2, k, h, dT):
    """Conduction through the insulation shell in series with convection from its outer surface."""
    R = math.log(r2 / r1) / (2 * math.pi * k) + 1 / (2 * math.pi * r2 * h)
    return dT / R


def loss_sphere(r1, r2, k, h, dT):
    R = (1 / r1 - 1 / r2) / (4 * math.pi * k) + 1 / (4 * math.pi * r2 ** 2 * h)
    return dT / R


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a 2 mm-radius copper wire coated in PVC (k = 0.19), still air h = 10 W/(m^2 K), wire 40 K above the air
    r1, k, h, dT = 0.002, 0.19, 10.0, 40.0
    r2s = np.linspace(r1, 0.08, 7801)                          # 0.01 mm steps
    Q = np.array([loss_per_metre(r1, r, k, h, dT) for r in r2s])
    rq = r2s[Q.argmax()]
    print(f"bare wire: {loss_per_metre(r1, r1, k, h, dT):.4f} W/m; numerical peak {Q.max():.4f} W/m at r2 = {rq*1000:.2f} mm; k/h = {k/h*1000:.2f} mm")
    assert abs(rq - k / h) < 2e-4                                  # critical radius r_c = k / h
    # refine with the golden-section search to show it is exact
    a, b = r1, 0.08; g = (math.sqrt(5) - 1) / 2
    for _ in range(200):
        c, d = b - g * (b - a), a + g * (b - a)
        if loss_per_metre(r1, c, k, h, dT) > loss_per_metre(r1, d, k, h, dT): b = d
        else: a = c
    print(f"golden-section maximum at {(a+b)/2*1000:.6f} mm")
    assert abs((a + b) / 2 - k / h) < 1e-9
    # a 15 mm copper hot-water pipe (r1 = 7.5 mm) with foam (k = 0.04): r_c = 4 mm < r1, so insulation always helps
    r1p, kp = 0.0075, 0.04
    for t in (0, 0.005, 0.013, 0.025):
        print(f"  pipe with {t*1000:4.0f} mm foam: {loss_per_metre(r1p, r1p + t, kp, h, 50.0):.3f} W/m")
    # sphere: critical radius 2 k / h
    rs = np.linspace(0.001, 0.2, 199001)
    Qs = np.array([loss_sphere(0.001, r, k, h, dT) for r in rs])
    print(f"sphere: peak at {rs[Qs.argmax()]*1000:.2f} mm, 2k/h = {2*k/h*1000:.2f} mm")
    assert abs(rs[Qs.argmax()] - 2 * k / h) < 2e-6
```

:::exercise A steam pipe
A steel steam pipe has outer radius 30 mm and runs at 160 °C in a 20 °C plant room (h = 12 W/(m² K)). Insulation (k = 0.045) brings it to 80 mm. Find the critical radius, and the heat loss per metre bare and insulated (ignore the steel's resistance and radiation).
---solution---
1. $r_c = 0.045/12 = 3.75$ mm, far below 30 mm: insulation helps.
2. Bare: $Q' = 2\pi\cdot0.03\cdot12\cdot140 = 316.7$ W/m.
3. Insulated: $R' = \ln(80/30)/(2\pi\cdot0.045) + 1/(2\pi\cdot0.08\cdot12) = 3.4690 + 0.1658 = 3.6348$ m K/W, so $Q' = 140/3.6348 = 38.52$ W/m, an 88% cut.
:::
