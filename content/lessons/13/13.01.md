On an April afternoon in Bali the sun heats the west wall of a house to 34 °C on the outside, while the air conditioning holds the room at 26 °C. Heat creeps through the concrete from hot to cold. How much? Heat transfer has three modes: conduction through solids, convection by moving fluids, and radiation. Conduction is the simplest, and one law describes it.

## Fourier's law

In 1822 Joseph Fourier stated that the heat flux (W/m²) is proportional to the temperature gradient and flows down it:

$$
\mathbf q = -k\,\nabla T .
$$

The **thermal conductivity** $k$, in W/(m K), is a material property: about 400 for copper, 50 for steel, 1.4 for concrete, 0.13 for wood, 0.035 for mineral wool and 0.026 for still air. Through a plane wall of thickness $L$ with surface temperatures $T_1$ and $T_2$:

$$
q = k\,\frac{T_1 - T_2}{L} .
$$

## The steady heat equation

Energy balance on a thin slice of the wall says that, in steady state with no heat generated inside, what flows in equals what flows out:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\left(k\frac{dT}{dx}\right) = 0 .
$$

So $k\,dT/dx$ is constant through the wall: the flux is the same at every depth. If $k$ is uniform, $dT/dx$ is constant and the temperature profile is a **straight line**. Where $k$ changes, the slope changes in inverse proportion, but the flux stays the same.

A finite-difference solver makes this concrete. Divide the wall into nodes, write the flux across each face as $-k_{\text{face}}(T_{i+1} - T_i)/\Delta x$, and require equal fluxes into and out of every node. That gives a tridiagonal linear system.

:::example The Bali wall
A 20 cm concrete wall, k = 1.4 W/(m K), outside surface 34 °C, inside 26 °C.

1. Fourier: $q = 1.4\cdot8/0.2 = 56.0000$ W/m².
2. The finite-difference solve with 201 nodes gives the same flux at every face (spread $6.5\times10^{-11}$) and departs from a straight line by less than $10^{-12}$ K. Mid-wall it's 30.0000 °C.
3. Through 12 m² of wall, 672.0 W enter the room, which the air conditioner must remove.
4. If the outer half is damp (k = 2.0) and the inner half dry (k = 1.0), the flux is $8/(0.1/2 + 0.1/1) = 53.3333$ W/m², still uniform. The joint sits at 31.3333 °C: the profile bends at the joint, with a steeper slope in the less conductive half.
:::

```python file=wall.py
"""Steady 1D conduction through a wall: a finite-difference solve of d/dx(k dT/dx) = 0, checked against Fourier's law."""
import numpy as np


def solve_wall(L, T_left, T_right, k_of_x, n=201):
    """Conservative FD: cell faces carry flux -k_face (T[i+1] - T[i]) / dx. Returns x, T and the flux at every face."""
    x = np.linspace(0, L, n); dx = x[1]
    kf = k_of_x((x[:-1] + x[1:]) / 2)                        # conductivity at the faces between nodes
    A = np.zeros((n, n)); b = np.zeros(n)
    A[0, 0] = A[-1, -1] = 1.0; b[0], b[-1] = T_left, T_right
    for i in range(1, n - 1):
        A[i, i - 1] = kf[i - 1]; A[i, i] = -(kf[i - 1] + kf[i]); A[i, i + 1] = kf[i]
    T = np.linalg.solve(A, b)
    q = -kf * np.diff(T) / dx
    return x, T, q


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a 20 cm concrete wall of a Bali house: 34 C outside in the afternoon sun, 26 C in the air-conditioned room
    L, k = 0.20, 1.4
    x, T, q = solve_wall(L, 34.0, 26.0, lambda s: np.full_like(s, k))
    print(f"heat flux {q.mean():.4f} W/m^2 (Fourier k dT/L = {k*8/L:.4f}); spread along the wall {np.ptp(q):.1e}")
    lin = 34.0 + (26.0 - 34.0) * x / L
    print(f"largest departure from a straight line: {np.abs(T - lin).max():.1e} K; mid-wall {T[100]:.4f} C")
    assert np.abs(T - lin).max() < 1e-10 and np.ptp(q) < 1e-9          # homogeneous wall: linear profile, constant flux
    print(f"through 12 m^2 of wall: {q.mean()*12:.1f} W into the room")
    # a wall whose conductivity varies (damp outer half): the flux is still constant, the profile is not straight
    x, T, q = solve_wall(L, 34.0, 26.0, lambda s: np.where(s < 0.1, 2.0, 1.0))
    R = 0.1 / 2.0 + 0.1 / 1.0
    print(f"two-zone wall: flux {q.mean():.4f} W/m^2 = dT / sum(L/k) = {8/R:.4f}; temperature at the joint {T[100]:.4f} C")
    assert np.ptp(q) < 1e-9 and abs(q.mean() - 8 / R) < 1e-9
```

:::exercise A brick wall in Zürich
A 25 cm brick wall (k = 0.7 W/(m K)) has an inside surface at 18 °C and an outside surface at 2 °C. Find the heat flux and the temperature 10 cm from the inside. How much heat crosses 30 m²?
---solution---
1. $q = 0.7\cdot16/0.25 = 44.8$ W/m².
2. The profile is linear: $T = 18 - 16\cdot(0.10/0.25) = 11.6$ °C.
3. Through 30 m²: $44.8\cdot30 = 1344$ W, a small radiator running nonstop.
:::
