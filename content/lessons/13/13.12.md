A car radiator, an oil cooler, the condenser on an air conditioner: a heat exchanger moves heat from one flowing fluid to another through a wall. The design question is usually "how much area do I need?", and the answer depends heavily on which way the two fluids flow.

## The basic balance

With the hot stream's capacity rate $C_h = \dot m_h c_h$ (W/K) and the cold stream's $C_c$, energy conservation gives

$$
Q = C_h\,(T_{h,\text{in}} - T_{h,\text{out}}) = C_c\,(T_{c,\text{out}} - T_{c,\text{in}}) .
$$

Through the wall, heat flows at $U\,dA\,(T_h - T_c)$ locally, where $U$ is the overall heat transfer coefficient (a resistance network, as in 13.02). The temperature difference changes along the exchanger, so we need the right average.

## Log mean temperature difference

Integrating the two stream equations along the exchanger gives exactly

$$
Q = UA\,\Delta T_{\text{lm}}, \qquad \Delta T_{\text{lm}} = \frac{\Delta T_1 - \Delta T_2}{\ln(\Delta T_1/\Delta T_2)},
$$

where $\Delta T_1$ and $\Delta T_2$ are the hot-minus-cold differences at the two ends. In **parallel flow** both fluids enter at the same end: $\Delta T_1 = T_{h,\text{in}} - T_{c,\text{in}}$, $\Delta T_2 = T_{h,\text{out}} - T_{c,\text{out}}$. In **counterflow** they enter at opposite ends: $\Delta T_1 = T_{h,\text{in}} - T_{c,\text{out}}$, $\Delta T_2 = T_{h,\text{out}} - T_{c,\text{in}}$.

For the same inlet and outlet temperatures, the counterflow LMTD is always larger, so it needs less area. Counterflow can even heat the cold stream above the hot stream's outlet temperature, which parallel flow never can.

:::example An oil cooler
Engine oil enters at 110 °C with $C_h = 0.2\cdot2100 = 420$ W/K; cooling water enters at 25 °C with $C_c = 0.1\cdot4180 = 418$ W/K. $UA = 300$ W/K.

1. Marching both streams along the area with RK4 (parallel), and shooting on the water outlet (counterflow):
   - Parallel: oil out 77.7276 °C, water out 57.4268 °C, $Q = 13554.397$ W. $UA\,\Delta T_{\text{lm}}$ with $\Delta T_{\text{lm}} = 45.1813$ K gives the same 13554.397 W.
   - Counter: oil out 74.6186 °C, water out 60.5507 °C, $Q = 14860.179$ W, $\Delta T_{\text{lm}} = 49.5339$ K, again matching.
2. Fixed duty: cool the oil to 80 °C ($Q = 12600$ W), so the water leaves at 55.1435 °C. Parallel needs $UA = 257.59$ W/K; counterflow 229.39 W/K, 10.9% less area. Marching the counterflow exchanger with that UA brings the oil to 80 °C to $10^{-4}$ K.
:::

```python file=lmtd.py
"""Heat exchangers by LMTD: march the two streams along the exchanger numerically and confirm Q = U A LMTD, for parallel flow and counterflow."""
import math


def lmtd(dT1, dT2):
    return dT1 if abs(dT1 - dT2) < 1e-12 else (dT1 - dT2) / math.log(dT1 / dT2)


def march_parallel(Th_in, Tc_in, Ch, Cc, UA, n=20000):
    """Both streams enter at x = 0. dTh = -U dA (Th - Tc)/Ch, dTc = +U dA (Th - Tc)/Cc. RK4 along the area."""
    f = lambda th, tc: (-(th - tc) / Ch, (th - tc) / Cc)
    th, tc, dA = Th_in, Tc_in, UA / n
    for _ in range(n):
        a1, b1 = f(th, tc); a2, b2 = f(th + dA / 2 * a1, tc + dA / 2 * b1); a3, b3 = f(th + dA / 2 * a2, tc + dA / 2 * b2); a4, b4 = f(th + dA * a3, tc + dA * b3)
        th += dA / 6 * (a1 + 2 * a2 + 2 * a3 + a4); tc += dA / 6 * (b1 + 2 * b2 + 2 * b3 + b4)
    return th, tc


def march_counter(Th_in, Tc_in, Ch, Cc, UA, n=2000):
    """Cold enters at the far end. Shoot on the unknown cold outlet temperature at x = 0 so the cold stream arrives at Tc_in at x = L."""
    def cold_at_end(tc_out):
        f = lambda th, tc: (-(th - tc) / Ch, -(th - tc) / Cc)   # cold flows the other way, so it falls in +x
        th, tc, dA = Th_in, tc_out, UA / n
        for _ in range(n):
            a1, b1 = f(th, tc); a2, b2 = f(th + dA / 2 * a1, tc + dA / 2 * b1); a3, b3 = f(th + dA / 2 * a2, tc + dA / 2 * b2); a4, b4 = f(th + dA * a3, tc + dA * b3)
            th += dA / 6 * (a1 + 2 * a2 + 2 * a3 + a4); tc += dA / 6 * (b1 + 2 * b2 + 2 * b3 + b4)
        return th, tc
    lo, hi = Tc_in, Th_in
    for _ in range(60):
        mid = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
        lo, hi = (mid, hi) if cold_at_end(mid)[1] < Tc_in else (lo, mid)   # arriving too cold: the guess was too low
    tc_out = 0.5 * (lo + hi)
    return cold_at_end(tc_out)[0], tc_out


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # an oil cooler: engine oil 110 -> ? C (C_h = 0.2 kg/s x 2100 = 420 W/K) cooled by water at 25 C (C_c = 0.1 kg/s x 4180 = 418 W/K), UA = 300 W/K
    Th_in, Tc_in, Ch, Cc, UA = 110.0, 25.0, 420.0, 418.0, 300.0
    for name, fn in (("parallel", march_parallel), ("counter", march_counter)):
        th, tc = fn(Th_in, Tc_in, Ch, Cc, UA)
        Q = Ch * (Th_in - th)
        dT1, dT2 = (Th_in - Tc_in, th - tc) if name == "parallel" else (Th_in - tc, th - Tc_in)
        print(f"{name}: oil out {th:.4f} C, water out {tc:.4f} C, Q = {Q:.3f} W; U A LMTD = {UA*lmtd(dT1, dT2):.3f} W (LMTD {lmtd(dT1, dT2):.4f} K)")
        assert abs(Q - Cc * (tc - Tc_in)) < 1e-6 * Q and abs(Q - UA * lmtd(dT1, dT2)) < 1e-5 * Q
    # area for a fixed duty: cool the oil to 80 C (Q = 12600 W)
    Q = Ch * 30; tco = Tc_in + Q / Cc
    UA_par = Q / lmtd(Th_in - Tc_in, 80 - tco)
    UA_cnt = Q / lmtd(Th_in - tco, 80 - Tc_in)
    print(f"duty 12.6 kW: water leaves at {tco:.4f} C; UA needed {UA_par:.2f} W/K parallel, {UA_cnt:.2f} W/K counter ({100*(1-UA_cnt/UA_par):.1f}% less area)")
    assert UA_cnt < UA_par
    th, _ = march_counter(Th_in, Tc_in, Ch, Cc, UA_cnt)
    assert abs(th - 80) < 1e-4
```

:::exercise Sizing a water heater
A counterflow exchanger heats 0.05 kg/s of water (c = 4180) from 15 °C to 55 °C using hot water entering at 85 °C and leaving at 50 °C. U = 800 W/(m² K). Find Q, the LMTD and the area. What area would parallel flow need?
---solution---
1. $Q = 0.05\cdot4180\cdot40 = 8360$ W.
2. Counter: $\Delta T_1 = 85 - 55 = 30$, $\Delta T_2 = 50 - 15 = 35$. $\Delta T_{\text{lm}} = 5/\ln(35/30) = 32.44$ K. $A = 8360/(800\cdot32.44) = 0.3222$ m².
3. Parallel: $\Delta T_1 = 85 - 15 = 70$, $\Delta T_2 = 50 - 55 = -5$. The cold outlet would have to exceed the hot outlet: impossible in parallel flow.
:::
