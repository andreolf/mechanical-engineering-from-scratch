For a thousand years Balinese farmers have shared water through the **subak**, a cooperative system of canals, tunnels and terraces run from water temples. Water from a river weir is divided among channels by notched timber or stone dividers (*tektek*), then flows from terrace to terrace down the hillside, each one spilling over a low notch into the next. You can play with a full model in the [subak lab](https://andreolf.github.io/subak-lab/). The hydraulics behind it is the weir.

## Weirs from critical flow

A **broad-crested weir** is a raised sill long enough that the flow over it becomes critical. With upstream head $H$ above the crest (approach velocity neglected), the specific energy over the crest is $H$, so the depth there is $y_c = \tfrac23 H$ and $q = \sqrt{g y_c^3}$. For crest width $b$:

$$
Q = C_d\, b \left(\tfrac23\right)^{3/2}\sqrt{g}\,H^{3/2} \approx 1.705\,C_d\,b\,H^{3/2}\ \text{(SI)} .
$$

A **sharp-crested weir** is a thin plate. Integrating Torricelli's $\sqrt{2gh}$ over the depth of the nappe gives

$$
Q = C_d\,\tfrac23\sqrt{2g}\,b\,H^{3/2}, \qquad C_d \approx 0.62 .
$$

Either way the discharge grows as $H^{3/2}$, so measuring the head is measuring the flow. That's why weirs are used as flow meters, and why a subak divider with notches of set widths splits the flow in proportion to those widths: every notch sees the same head.

## A cascade of terraces

Treat each terrace as a tank of area $A$ holding water up to the notch crest, then spilling into the next:

$$
A\frac{dh_i}{dt} = Q_{i-1} - Q_i, \qquad Q_i = k\,(h_i - h_{\text{crest}})^{3/2} .
$$

The first terrace fills, starts spilling, and the wave of water moves down the hill. At steady state every notch passes the full inflow, so every terrace holds the same head.

:::example A notch and a cascade
1. A broad-crested notch 0.3 m wide passes 1.45 L/s at 2 cm head, 5.72 at 5 cm, 16.17 at 10 cm and 45.75 at 20 cm. A log-log fit has slope 1.500000.
2. A sharp-crested weir of the same width at 10 cm head passes 17.37 L/s.
3. Five terraces of 400 m², each holding 5 cm before it spills, with 0.3 m notches and 10 L/s from the canal. The first starts spilling after $400\cdot0.05/0.010 = 2000$ s (0.57 h on a one-minute record); the others follow at 1.82, 3.17, 4.52 and 5.88 h.
4. After 12 h every terrace holds 12.26 cm: 5 cm plus a steady head of 7.258 cm. The outflow is 9.999 L/s, back to the inflow.
:::

```python file=subak.py
"""Weirs from critical flow, and a cascade of Balinese rice terraces (subak) each spilling over a weir into the next."""
import math
import numpy as np

G = 9.81


def broad_crested(b, H, Cd=1.0):
    """Critical depth forms on the crest: Q = Cd b (2/3)^(3/2) sqrt(g) H^(3/2)."""
    return Cd * b * (2 / 3) ** 1.5 * math.sqrt(G) * max(H, 0.0) ** 1.5


def sharp_crested(b, H, Cd=0.62):
    """Integrating sqrt(2 g h) over the nappe: Q = Cd (2/3) sqrt(2 g) b H^(3/2)."""
    return Cd * 2 / 3 * math.sqrt(2 * G) * b * max(H, 0.0) ** 1.5


def cascade(n, area, crest, b, inflow, t_end, dt=1.0):
    """Water depth in n terraces in series. Each holds water up to its weir crest, then spills over a broad-crested
    notch of width b into the next. Returns times and depths (explicit Euler, small step)."""
    h = np.zeros(n); out_hist, h_hist, ts = [], [], []
    for k in range(int(t_end / dt)):
        Qin = inflow
        dh = np.zeros(n)
        for i in range(n):
            Qout = broad_crested(b, h[i] - crest)
            dh[i] = (Qin - Qout) / area
            Qin = Qout
        h += dh * dt
        if k % 60 == 0:
            ts.append(k * dt); h_hist.append(h.copy()); out_hist.append(Qin)
    return np.array(ts), np.array(h_hist), np.array(out_hist)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    Hs = np.array([0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2])
    Q = np.array([broad_crested(0.3, H) for H in Hs])
    slope = np.polyfit(np.log(Hs), np.log(Q), 1)[0]
    print("broad-crested notch 0.3 m wide: " + ", ".join(f"H {H*100:.0f} cm -> {q*1000:.2f} L/s" for H, q in zip(Hs, Q)))
    print(f"log-log slope {slope:.6f}")
    assert abs(slope - 1.5) < 1e-9                                        # discharge scales with head^(3/2)
    print(f"sharp-crested, same width, 10 cm head: {sharp_crested(0.3, 0.1)*1000:.2f} L/s")
    # five terraces of 400 m^2, water held 5 cm deep before spilling, 0.3 m notches, 10 L/s from the canal
    ts, h, out = cascade(5, 400.0, 0.05, 0.3, 0.010, 12 * 3600)
    Hss = (0.010 / (0.3 * (2 / 3) ** 1.5 * math.sqrt(G))) ** (2 / 3)
    print(f"steady head over each notch {Hss*100:.3f} cm; after 12 h the depths are {np.round(h[-1]*100, 2)} cm and the outflow {out[-1]*1000:.3f} L/s")
    first = [ts[np.argmax(h[:, i] > 0.05)] / 3600 for i in range(5)]
    print("terrace starts spilling after (h): " + ", ".join(f"{x:.2f}" for x in first))
    assert abs(out[-1] - 0.010) < 1e-4                                    # mass balance: outflow returns to inflow
```

:::exercise Sharing water fairly
A tektek divides a canal carrying 60 L/s into two notches, 0.2 m and 0.4 m wide, both broad-crested ($C_d = 1$) at the same crest height. Find the head and each share.
---solution---
1. Total width 0.6 m: $H = \left(\dfrac{0.060}{0.6\cdot1.705}\right)^{2/3}$. With $1.705 = (2/3)^{3/2}\sqrt{9.81} = 1.7049$: $H = (0.05866)^{2/3} = 0.1510$ m.
2. The same head acts on both notches, so the shares split by width: 20 L/s and 40 L/s.
3. Width is what sets each farmer's share, not the head. That's what makes the divider fair.
:::
