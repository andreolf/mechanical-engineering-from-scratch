A fire hose bucks in your hands; a pipe bend in a hydropower plant needs a concrete anchor block weighing tonnes; a jet of water knocks a plate backwards. You don't need to know the details of the flow inside to get those forces. Draw a control volume around the device and count the momentum going in and out.

## The control-volume momentum balance

Apply the Reynolds transport theorem to momentum: the sum of forces on the fluid in a fixed control volume equals the rate of change of momentum inside plus the net momentum outflow. For steady flow with uniform inlets and outlets,

$$
\sum\mathbf F = \sum_\text{out}\dot m\,\mathbf V - \sum_\text{in}\dot m\,\mathbf V .
$$

The forces include pressure on the inlet and outlet faces ($pA$, pushing inwards), the force from the walls, and gravity. It's a vector equation, so work component by component, and draw the control volume first.

## Jets on vanes

A free jet is at atmospheric pressure everywhere, so only the vane pushes on it. A jet of mass flow $\dot m$ and speed $V$ turned by an angle $\theta$ pushes the vane with

$$
F_x = \dot m V(1 - \cos\theta) .
$$

A flat plate ($\theta = 90°$) feels $\dot m V$; turning the jet right back ($180°$) doubles it.

## Pipe bends

In a pipe the pressure terms usually dominate. For a horizontal bend turning the flow by $\theta$, the force the fluid puts on the bend (and the anchor must resist) is

$$
\mathbf F = (p_1A_1 + \dot mV_1)\,\mathbf e_1 - (p_2A_2 + \dot mV_2)\,\mathbf e_2 ,
$$

where $\mathbf e_1$ and $\mathbf e_2$ are the inlet and outlet flow directions.

:::widget jetvane
A jet turned by a vane. Change the angle and let the vane move away.
:::

:::example Jets and a bend
1. Water jet, 30 mm at 20 m/s: $\dot m = 14.109$ kg/s. Force on a vane turning it 90°: 282.2 N; 135°: 481.7 N; 180°: 564.4 N, exactly $2\dot mV$.
2. 90° reducing bend, 200 L/s, 300 → 200 mm, inlet 150 kPa gauge. $V_1 = 2.829$ m/s, $V_2 = 6.366$ m/s, $p_2 = 133.77$ kPa by Bernoulli.
3. Force on the bend: $F_x = 11\,168$ N, $F_y = -5473$ N, magnitude 12 437 N, about 1.3 tonnes, mostly from pressure.
4. With the flow stopped, only pressure acts: $pA$ along both axes, which the code checks.
:::

```python file=momentum.py
"""Control-volume momentum: force of a jet on plates and vanes, and the anchor force on a pipe bend."""
import math
import numpy as np

RHO = 998.0


def jet_on_vane(V, A, theta_deg, u_vane=0.0):
    """Force (N) on a vane turning a free jet by theta, vane moving away at u_vane. Returns (Fx, Fy) exerted on the vane."""
    Vr = V - u_vane
    mdot = RHO * A * Vr                                   # mass flow meeting a single moving vane
    th = math.radians(theta_deg)
    return mdot * Vr * (1 - math.cos(th)), -mdot * Vr * math.sin(th)     # jet leaves along (cos, sin) relative to the vane


def bend_force(Q, D1, D2, p1, theta_deg, p2=None):
    """Force (N) the fluid exerts on a horizontal reducing bend; inlet along +x, outlet turned by theta. p in Pa gauge."""
    A1, A2 = math.pi * D1 ** 2 / 4, math.pi * D2 ** 2 / 4
    V1, V2 = Q / A1, Q / A2
    if p2 is None:
        p2 = p1 + 0.5 * RHO * (V1 ** 2 - V2 ** 2)         # Bernoulli across the bend (losses neglected)
    th = math.radians(theta_deg)
    m = RHO * Q
    # momentum balance on the fluid: F_wall_on_fluid + p1 A1 n1 + p2 A2 n2 = m (V2 - V1)
    e1, e2 = np.array([1.0, 0.0]), np.array([math.cos(th), math.sin(th)])
    F_on_fluid = m * (V2 * e2 - V1 * e1) - p1 * A1 * e1 + p2 * A2 * e2
    return -F_on_fluid, V1, V2, p2


if __name__ == "__main__":
    V, d = 20.0, 0.03
    A = math.pi * d * d / 4
    print(f"jet 30 mm at 20 m/s: mass flow {RHO*A*V:.3f} kg/s")
    for th in (90, 135, 180):
        Fx, _ = jet_on_vane(V, A, th)
        print(f"  turned by {th} deg: Fx = {Fx:.1f} N")
    assert abs(jet_on_vane(V, A, 90)[0] - RHO * A * V * V) < 1e-9          # flat plate: F = mdot V
    assert abs(jet_on_vane(V, A, 180)[0] - 2 * RHO * A * V * V) < 1e-9     # full reversal doubles it
    F, V1, V2, p2 = bend_force(0.2, 0.30, 0.20, 150e3, 90)
    print(f"90 deg reducing bend, 200 L/s, 300 -> 200 mm, 150 kPa: V1 {V1:.3f}, V2 {V2:.3f} m/s, p2 {p2/1000:.2f} kPa")
    print(f"  force on the bend: Fx = {F[0]:.0f} N, Fy = {F[1]:.0f} N, magnitude {np.hypot(*F):.0f} N")
    # with no flow only pressure acts: a 90 deg bend with equal ends feels p A along +x and along -y
    F0, *_ = bend_force(0.0, 0.3, 0.3, 150e3, 90)
    A1 = math.pi * 0.09 / 4
    assert np.allclose(F0, [150e3 * A1, -150e3 * A1])
```

:::exercise A fire nozzle
A fire hose of 65 mm diameter ends in a 25 mm nozzle discharging 10 L/s to the atmosphere. Find the gauge pressure at the nozzle inlet (Bernoulli) and the force in the coupling that holds the nozzle onto the hose.
---solution---
1. $V_1 = 0.01/(\pi\cdot0.065^2/4) = 3.014$ m/s; $V_2 = 20.37$ m/s.
2. $p_1 = \tfrac12\cdot998\cdot(20.37^2 - 3.014^2) = 202.6$ kPa gauge.
3. Momentum on the nozzle's fluid, along the axis: $F_\text{nozzle on fluid} + p_1A_1 = \dot m(V_2 - V_1)$, so $F = 9.98\cdot17.36 - 202\,559\cdot3.318\times10^{-3} = 173.3 - 672.2 = -498.9$ N.
4. The fluid pushes the nozzle forward with 498.9 N, and the coupling must carry that in tension. The firefighter feels the jet reaction $\dot mV_2 = 203$ N.
:::
