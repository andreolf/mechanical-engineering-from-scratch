Shut a tap fast and the pipes bang. In a hydropower penstock, the same effect can burst steel. Water is nearly incompressible, but not quite. When a valve stops a long moving column, the water next to the valve stops first and is squeezed, and a pressure wave runs back up the pipe at roughly the speed of sound. This is **water hammer**.

## The Joukowsky jump

Stop a slice of water moving at $\Delta v$ in the time a wave crosses it. Momentum gives the pressure rise

$$
\Delta p = \rho\,a\,\Delta v, \qquad \Delta H = \frac{a\,\Delta v}{g} .
$$

With $a \approx 1000$ m/s, stopping water moving at only 1 m/s raises the pressure by about 10 bar. The wave speed is lower than sound in open water (1483 m/s) because the pipe wall stretches:

$$
a = \sqrt{\frac{K/\rho}{1 + \dfrac{K D}{E e}}} ,
$$

with $K$ the bulk modulus of water, $D$ the diameter, $e$ the wall thickness and $E$ the wall's Young's modulus.

## Closure time and the method of characteristics

The pressure wave reflects at the reservoir as a relief wave and returns after $2L/a$. If the valve closes faster than that, the full Joukowsky rise appears. Slower closures are softer, because relief waves arrive while the valve is still moving. For a velocity ramped down linearly over $t_c > 2L/a$, the peak rise is Michaud's

$$
\Delta H \approx \frac{2 L V_0}{g\,t_c} .
$$

To simulate the full transient, write the 1D continuity and momentum equations along the two **characteristics** $dx/dt = \pm a$. On them the PDEs become ordinary relations:

$$
C^\pm:\quad H_P = H_{A/B} \mp \frac{a}{g}\left(V_P - V_{A/B}\right) - \text{friction} .
$$

March in time with $\Delta t = \Delta x/a$ and apply boundary conditions: fixed head at the reservoir, and the orifice law $V = \tau V_0\sqrt{H/H_0}$ at a valve whose relative opening $\tau$ falls from 1 to 0.

:::example A penstock valve
A 600 m penstock from a reservoir with 100 m of head carries water at 2 m/s. Take $a = 1000$ m/s. (A 0.5 m steel pipe with a 10 mm wall gives 1191.4 m/s by the formula above.)

1. Joukowsky: $\Delta H = 1000\cdot2/9.81 = 203.874$ m, about 20 bar. Round trip $2L/a = 1.20$ s.
2. Method of characteristics with 40 reaches, instant closure: peak head 303.874 m, a rise of exactly 203.874 m. It holds for 1.200 s, then the wave reverses and the head swings down to −103.874 m. A real pipe can't go below vapour pressure, so the water column would separate and cavitate.
3. Closures in 0.6 s and 1.2 s also give the full 203.87 m.
4. A linear velocity ramp over 3, 6 and 12 s gives 81.55, 40.77 and 20.39 m: Michaud's formula exactly. Closing the valve area linearly gives 56.06, 23.66 and 10.93 m, because most of the flow reduction happens near the end, when relief waves are already arriving.
:::

```python file=hammer.py
"""Water hammer in a penstock: the Joukowsky jump and a method-of-characteristics simulation of valve closure."""
import math
import numpy as np

G = 9.81


def wave_speed(K, rho, D, e, E):
    """Pressure wave speed in a liquid-filled elastic pipe."""
    return math.sqrt(K / rho / (1 + K * D / (E * e)))


def moc(L, a, V0, H0, t_close, t_end, N=40, f=0.0, D=0.5, law='area'):
    """Reservoir (fixed head H0) at x = 0, valve at x = L. Valve area falls linearly to zero over t_close.
    Characteristics C+ and C-: H = H_P -/+ (a/g)(V - V_P), with friction f. Returns times and valve head."""
    dx = L / N; dt = dx / a; B = a / G; R = f * dt / (2 * D)
    H = np.full(N + 1, H0) - (f * L / D * V0 * V0 / (2 * G)) * np.linspace(0, 1, N + 1)
    V = np.full(N + 1, V0)
    Hv0 = H[-1]; ts, hv = [0.0], [H[-1]]
    t = 0.0
    while t < t_end:
        t += dt
        Hn, Vn = H.copy(), V.copy()
        # interior points
        CP = H[:-2] + B * V[:-2] - B * R * V[:-2] * np.abs(V[:-2])
        CM = H[2:] - B * V[2:] + B * R * V[2:] * np.abs(V[2:])
        Hn[1:-1] = (CP + CM) / 2; Vn[1:-1] = (CP - CM) / (2 * B)
        # reservoir
        Hn[0] = H0; CM0 = H[1] - B * V[1] + B * R * V[1] * abs(V[1]); Vn[0] = (H0 - CM0) / B
        # valve: V = tau V0 sqrt(H / Hv0)
        tau = max(0.0, 1 - t / t_close) if t_close > 0 else 0.0
        CPv = H[-2] + B * V[-2] - B * R * V[-2] * abs(V[-2])
        if tau == 0: Vn[-1] = 0.0
        elif law == 'velocity': Vn[-1] = tau * V0
        else:   # V = tau V0 sqrt(H/Hv0) with H = CPv - B V: a quadratic in V
            Cv = (tau * V0) ** 2 / Hv0
            Vn[-1] = (-B * Cv + math.sqrt((B * Cv) ** 2 + 4 * Cv * CPv)) / 2
        Hn[-1] = CPv - B * Vn[-1]
        H, V = Hn, Vn
        ts.append(t); hv.append(H[-1])
    return np.array(ts), np.array(hv)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    a = wave_speed(2.2e9, 1000, 0.5, 0.01, 200e9)
    print(f"wave speed in a 0.5 m steel penstock, 10 mm wall: {a:.1f} m/s (rigid pipe: {math.sqrt(2.2e9/1000):.1f})")
    a = 1000.0; L = 600.0; V0 = 2.0; H0 = 100.0
    dH = a * V0 / G
    print(f"Joukowsky head rise a V0 / g = {dH:.3f} m ({1000*G*dH/1e5:.2f} bar); round trip 2L/a = {2*L/a:.2f} s")
    ts, hv = moc(L, a, V0, H0, 0.0, 6.0)
    print(f"MOC, instant closure: peak head {hv.max():.3f} m (rise {hv.max()-H0:.3f}), minimum {hv.min():.3f} m (would cavitate)")
    assert abs(hv.max() - H0 - dH) < 1e-6                                 # matches Joukowsky exactly
    # the head stays high for 2L/a, then the wave reverses
    dt = ts[1] - ts[0]
    n_high = np.count_nonzero((hv > H0 + dH / 2) & (ts < 2.0))
    print(f"high-pressure phase lasts {n_high * dt:.3f} s")
    assert abs(n_high * dt - 2 * L / a) < 1e-9
    for tc in (0.6, 1.2, 3.0, 6.0, 12.0):
        _, hl = moc(L, a, V0, H0, tc, tc + 8.0, law='velocity')
        _, hv = moc(L, a, V0, H0, tc, tc + 8.0)
        mich = 2 * L * V0 / (G * tc)
        print(f"closure over {tc:5.1f} s: linear velocity ramp {hl.max()-H0:7.2f} m, linear valve area {hv.max()-H0:7.2f} m" + (f", Michaud 2 L V0/(g tc) = {mich:.2f}" if tc > 2 * L / a else ""))
        if tc > 2 * L / a: assert abs(hl.max() - H0 - mich) < 1e-6            # the velocity ramp reproduces Michaud
        else: assert abs(hl.max() - H0 - dH) < 1e-6                          # closing within 2L/a gives the full Joukowsky rise
```

:::exercise A closing time limit
For the same penstock, the pipe is rated for a head rise of at most 30 m. Using Michaud, how slowly must the valve close? What happens if a worker slams it shut in 1 s?
---solution---
1. $t_c \ge 2LV_0/(g\,\Delta H) = 2\cdot600\cdot2/(9.81\cdot30) = 8.155$ s.
2. 1 s is less than $2L/a = 1.2$ s, so the full Joukowsky rise of 203.9 m appears: about seven times the rating.
3. That's why large valves have slow-closing actuators, and penstocks have surge tanks.
:::
