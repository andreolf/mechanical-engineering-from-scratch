Water flowing steadily through a nozzle speeds up as the nozzle narrows. At every fixed point nothing changes in time, yet each parcel of water accelerates, and Newton's second law is about parcels. The **material derivative** is the bridge: the rate of change seen by someone riding with the fluid, written in Eulerian variables.

## Following a parcel

Let $q(\mathbf x, t)$ be any field: temperature, a velocity component, a dye concentration. A parcel at $\mathbf x_p(t)$ sees $q(\mathbf x_p(t), t)$, and by the chain rule

$$
\frac{Dq}{Dt} = \frac{\partial q}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial q}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial q}{\partial y} + w\frac{\partial q}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial q}{\partial t} + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)q .
$$

The first term is the **local** change at a fixed point; the second is the **convective** change from moving to a place where $q$ is different. Applied to velocity it gives the acceleration of a fluid particle:

$$
\mathbf a = \frac{D\mathbf u}{Dt} = \frac{\partial\mathbf u}{\partial t} + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u .
$$

The convective term is nonlinear in $\mathbf u$. That's the source of most of the difficulty, and the richness, of fluid mechanics, turbulence included.

## Two examples

- **Stagnation-point flow** $u = ax$, $v = -ay$, like water hitting a wall: steady, yet $\mathbf a = (a^2x, a^2y)$. Parcels accelerate away from the wall along it and decelerate as they approach.
- **Solid-body rotation** $u = -\Omega y$, $v = \Omega x$: steady, and $\mathbf a = -\Omega^2(x, y)$, the centripetal acceleration of circular motion.

:::example A strengthening stagnation flow
$u = a(t)x$, $v = -a(t)y$ with $a = 0.5(1 + 0.2t)$ s⁻¹, at (2, 1) m and $t = 3$ s.

1. Local acceleration (central differences in time): (0.2, −0.1) m/s².
2. Convective: (1.28, 0.64) m/s². Total (1.48, 0.54), matching the exact $(\dot a x + a^2x,\ -\dot a y + a^2y)$.
3. Following the exact particle path and differentiating its velocity gives the same (1.48, 0.54).
4. With $a$ constant at 0.5, the local part vanishes but the convective acceleration at (2, 1) is still (0.5, 0.25) m/s².
:::

```python file=material.py
"""The material derivative Du/Dt = du/dt + (u . grad) u, computed by finite differences and checked two ways."""
import numpy as np

A, B = 0.5, 0.2      # stagnation-point flow whose strength grows in time: u = a(t) x, v = -a(t) y with a = A (1 + B t)


def field(x, y, t):
    a = A * (1 + B * t)
    return np.array([a * x, -a * y])


def material_acc(f, x, y, t, h=1e-5):
    """Local plus convective acceleration from central differences."""
    dudt = (f(x, y, t + h) - f(x, y, t - h)) / (2 * h)
    dudx = (f(x + h, y, t) - f(x - h, y, t)) / (2 * h)
    dudy = (f(x, y + h, t) - f(x, y - h, t)) / (2 * h)
    u, v = f(x, y, t)
    return dudt, u * dudx + v * dudy


def exact_acc(x, y, t):
    a, adot = A * (1 + B * t), A * B
    return np.array([adot * x + a * a * x, -adot * y + a * a * y])


if __name__ == "__main__":
    x, y, t = 2.0, 1.0, 3.0
    local, conv = material_acc(field, x, y, t)
    total = local + conv
    print(f"at ({x}, {y}), t = {t}: local {local.round(6)}, convective {conv.round(6)}, total {total.round(6)}")
    print(f"exact: {exact_acc(x, y, t).round(6)}")
    assert np.allclose(total, exact_acc(x, y, t), atol=1e-7)
    # second check: follow the particle and differentiate its velocity along the path
    dt = 1e-4; vels = []
    for k in range(-2, 3):
        # exact particle path for this field: x(t) = x0 exp(int a dt)
        tt = t + k * dt; I = A * ((tt - t) + B * (tt * tt - t * t) / 2)
        p = np.array([x * np.exp(I), y * np.exp(-I)])
        vels.append(field(*p, tt))
    acc_path = (-vels[4] + 8 * vels[3] - 8 * vels[1] + vels[0]) / (12 * dt)
    print(f"following the particle: {acc_path.round(6)}")
    assert np.allclose(acc_path, total, atol=1e-6)
    # steady flow still accelerates: at t = 0 with B = 0 the local part vanishes but the convective part does not
    print(f"steady version at (2, 1): convective acceleration {np.array([A*A*2, A*A*1])} m/s^2 even though nothing changes in time")
```

:::exercise A nozzle
In a nozzle the centreline speed is $u = U_0(1 + x/L)$ with $U_0 = 2$ m/s and $L = 0.5$ m. Find the acceleration of a water parcel at $x = 0.25$ m. Then let the supply ramp up so that $U_0 = 2 + t$ (m/s, t in s) and find it again at $t = 0$.
---solution---
1. $u = 2(1 + 0.5) = 3$ m/s and $\partial u/\partial x = U_0/L = 4$ s⁻¹.
2. Steady: $a = u\,\partial u/\partial x = 3\cdot4 = 12$ m/s², over a g, with nothing changing in time.
3. Ramping: the local term is $\dot U_0(1 + x/L) = 1\cdot1.5 = 1.5$ m/s², so $a = 13.5$ m/s².
:::
