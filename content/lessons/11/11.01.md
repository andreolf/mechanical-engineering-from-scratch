Push a block of rubber sideways and it deforms a little, then stops: it resists shear with a stress that depends on how far you've pushed. Push the surface of the Limmat and the water keeps moving as long as you push. A **fluid** is a material that deforms continuously under any shear stress, however small. What it resists isn't the amount of deformation but its **rate**, and the property measuring that resistance is **viscosity**.

## The continuum

Water is made of molecules, but a cubic micrometre still holds about $3\times10^{10}$ of them. So we treat velocity, pressure and density as smooth fields $\mathbf u(\mathbf x, t)$, $p(\mathbf x, t)$, $\rho(\mathbf x, t)$. That's the **continuum hypothesis**. It fails only when the flow's length scale approaches the mean free path, as in rarefied gas at high altitude or nanochannels.

## Newton's law of viscosity

Put fluid between two parallel plates a gap $h$ apart, fix the bottom one and slide the top one at speed $U$. The fluid sticks to both walls (the **no-slip condition**), and the shear stress needed is

$$
\tau = \mu\frac{du}{dy} .
$$

$\mu$ is the **dynamic viscosity** in Pa s: about $1.0\times10^{-3}$ for water at 20 °C, $1.8\times10^{-5}$ for air, 0.29 for SAE 30 oil and around 10 for honey. The **kinematic viscosity** $\nu = \mu/\rho$, in m²/s, appears whenever inertia competes with friction. Fluids obeying this linear law are **Newtonian**. Ketchup, paint, blood and toothpaste aren't.

## Couette flow

With no pressure gradient, the momentum balance on a thin slab of fluid reduces to $\mu\,d^2u/dy^2 = 0$, so the velocity is linear:

$$
u(y) = U\frac{y}{h}, \qquad \tau = \mu\frac{U}{h}\ \text{everywhere} .
$$

Add a pressure gradient and the equation becomes $\mu u'' = dp/dx$, which adds a parabola. This is the basis of lubrication: a thin oil film in a bearing carries the load while shearing at enormous rates.

:::widget couette
Fluid between a fixed plate and a moving one. Change the fluid, speed and gap; add a pressure gradient to bend the profile.
:::

:::example Shearing a thin film
Top plate at 2 m/s, gap 1 mm, plate area 0.5 m².

1. Water: $\tau = 10^{-3}\cdot2/0.001 = 2.0$ Pa, a force of 1.0 N.
2. SAE 30 oil: 580 Pa, 290 N. Honey: 20 000 Pa, 10 kN.
3. The finite-difference solution of $\mu u'' = 0$ is linear to machine precision, with a constant shear rate of 2000 s⁻¹.
4. With $dp/dx = -2$ MPa/m in the oil, the centre speed rises from 1.0 to 1.8621 m/s. The flow rate per metre width goes from 1.0000 to 1.5745 L/s (exact: 1.5747).
:::

```python file=couette.py
"""Newton's law of viscosity and Couette flow: the finite-difference velocity profile between a fixed and a moving plate."""
import numpy as np


def couette_fd(U, h, mu, dpdx=0.0, n=51):
    """Solve mu u'' = dp/dx on 0 <= y <= h with u(0) = 0, u(h) = U by central differences."""
    y = np.linspace(0, h, n); dy = y[1] - y[0]
    A = np.zeros((n, n)); b = np.full(n, dpdx / mu * dy * dy)
    A[0, 0] = A[-1, -1] = 1; b[0], b[-1] = 0.0, U
    for i in range(1, n - 1):
        A[i, i - 1], A[i, i], A[i, i + 1] = 1, -2, 1
    return y, np.linalg.solve(A, b)


def couette_exact(y, U, h, mu, dpdx=0.0):
    return U * y / h + dpdx / (2 * mu) * (y * y - h * y)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    U, h = 2.0, 1e-3
    for name, mu in (("water, 20 C", 1.0e-3), ("SAE 30 oil, 20 C", 0.29), ("honey, 20 C", 10.0)):
        tau = mu * U / h
        print(f"{name}: mu = {mu} Pa s, shear stress {tau:.1f} Pa, force on a 0.5 m^2 plate {tau*0.5:.1f} N")
    y, u = couette_fd(U, h, 0.29)
    assert np.allclose(u, U * y / h, atol=1e-12)                      # Newtonian, no pressure gradient: linear
    assert np.allclose(np.gradient(u, y), U / h)                       # constant shear rate
    y, u = couette_fd(U, h, 0.29, dpdx=-2e6)
    assert np.allclose(u, couette_exact(y, U, h, 0.29, -2e6), atol=1e-9)
    print(f"with dp/dx = -2 MPa/m the profile bulges: centre speed {u[len(u)//2]:.4f} m/s instead of {U/2:.1f}")
    print(f"flow rate per metre width: linear {U*h/2*1000:.4f} L/s, with gradient {float(np.sum((u[1:] + u[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(y)))*1000:.4f} L/s (exact {(U*h/2 + 2e6*h**3/(12*0.29))*1000:.4f})")
```

:::exercise A journal bearing
A 50 mm shaft turns at 3000 rpm in a 40 mm long bearing with a radial clearance of 0.05 mm, filled with oil of $\mu = 0.03$ Pa s. Treating the thin film as Couette flow, find the friction torque and the power lost.
---solution---
1. Surface speed $U = \pi Dn/60 = \pi\cdot0.05\cdot3000/60 = 7.854$ m/s.
2. Shear stress $\tau = 0.03\cdot7.854/(5\times10^{-5}) = 4712$ Pa.
3. Wetted area $\pi DL = 6.283\times10^{-3}$ m², so the friction force is 29.61 N.
4. Torque $29.61\cdot0.025 = 0.740$ N m; power $T\omega = 0.740\cdot314.16 = 232.5$ W, which ends up as heat in the oil. This is Petroff's equation.
:::
