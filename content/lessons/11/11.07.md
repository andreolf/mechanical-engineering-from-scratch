Bernoulli's equation is the most used and most misused result in fluid mechanics. It explains why a venturi meter measures flow, why a garden sprayer sucks up fertiliser, and why the pressure drops where a river speeds up between bridge piers. It also gets quoted for things it doesn't explain. To use it properly, derive it and keep the assumptions in view.

## Derivation along a streamline

Apply Newton's second law to a fluid particle moving along a streamline, with coordinate $s$. Ignoring viscosity, the only forces are pressure and gravity:

$$
\rho\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + V\frac{\partial V}{\partial s}\right) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial s} - \rho g\frac{\partial z}{\partial s} .
$$

For steady, incompressible flow, $V\,\partial V/\partial s = \partial(V^2/2)/\partial s$, and integrating along the streamline gives

$$
p + \tfrac12\rho V^2 + \rho g z = \text{constant along a streamline} .
$$

The terms are static pressure, dynamic pressure and hydrostatic pressure. Divided by $\rho g$ they become heads in metres.

## When it lies

The assumptions: **steady**, **incompressible** (Mach number below about 0.3), **inviscid** (no friction losses), and **along one streamline**. The constant can differ between streamlines unless the flow is also irrotational. It fails across pumps or turbines (they add or remove energy), in long pipes (friction), in separated wakes, and through hydraulic jumps. The popular "equal transit time" explanation of wing lift is not Bernoulli; it's just wrong.

## The venturi meter

Combining Bernoulli with continuity, $V_1A_1 = V_2A_2$, between an inlet of diameter $D$ and a throat of diameter $d$ gives

$$
Q = C_d A_2\sqrt{\frac{2\,\Delta p}{\rho\,(1 - \beta^4)}}, \qquad \beta = \frac dD .
$$

The discharge coefficient $C_d \approx 0.98$ absorbs the small friction losses.

:::widget venturipipe
Pressure along a venturi. Narrow the throat or raise the flow and watch the pressure dip.
:::

:::example A 100/50 mm venturi
1. A mercury manometer under water reads 150 mm, so $\Delta p = (13\,546 - 998)\cdot9.81\cdot0.150 = 18.464$ kPa.
2. Throat area $A_2 = 1.9635\times10^{-3}$ m² and $\beta^4 = 0.0625$, so the ideal throat speed is $\sqrt{2\cdot18\,464/(998\cdot0.9375)} = 6.282$ m/s and $Q = 0.98\cdot A_2\cdot6.282 = 12.089$ L/s (ideal 12.336).
3. Integrating the 1D Euler equation $\rho u\,du/dx = -dp/dx$ along a smooth contraction carrying the ideal flow gives a pressure drop of 18.4644 kPa: the same as Bernoulli and the manometer.
4. Speed rises from 1.571 m/s at the inlet to 6.282 m/s in the throat.
:::

```python file=venturi.py
"""Bernoulli and the venturi meter: flow rate from a pressure difference, checked against a numerical integration of the 1D Euler equation."""
import math
import numpy as np

RHO = 998.0


def venturi_Q(dp, D, d, Cd=0.98):
    """Volume flow (m^3/s) from the pressure drop between inlet (D) and throat (d)."""
    A2, beta = math.pi * d * d / 4, d / D
    return Cd * A2 * math.sqrt(2 * dp / (RHO * (1 - beta ** 4)))


def euler_1d(Q, x, D_of_x, p0):
    """March rho u du/dx = -dp/dx along a duct of varying diameter (inviscid, steady, 1D)."""
    A = np.pi * D_of_x(x) ** 2 / 4; u = Q / A
    dudx = np.gradient(u, x)
    dpdx = -RHO * u * dudx
    p = p0 + np.concatenate([[0], np.cumsum((dpdx[1:] + dpdx[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(x))])
    return u, p


if __name__ == "__main__":
    D, d = 0.100, 0.050
    dp = (13546 - RHO) * 9.81 * 0.150                       # mercury manometer reading 150 mm
    Q = venturi_Q(dp, D, d)
    print(f"manometer 150 mm Hg -> dp = {dp/1000:.3f} kPa -> Q = {Q*1000:.3f} L/s (ideal {venturi_Q(dp, D, d, 1.0)*1000:.3f} L/s)")
    # inviscid check: integrate Euler along a smooth contraction and compare with Bernoulli
    x = np.linspace(0, 0.3, 30001)
    Dx = lambda x: D - (D - d) * 0.5 * (1 - np.cos(np.pi * np.clip(x / 0.3, 0, 1)))
    Qi = venturi_Q(dp, D, d, 1.0)
    u, p = euler_1d(Qi, x, Dx, 200e3)
    bern = 200e3 + 0.5 * RHO * (u[0] ** 2 - u ** 2)
    print(f"Euler integration: pressure drop {(p[0]-p[-1])/1000:.4f} kPa; Bernoulli {(bern[0]-bern[-1])/1000:.4f} kPa; manometer {dp/1000:.4f} kPa")
    assert np.max(np.abs(p - bern)) < 5.0 and abs((p[0] - p[-1]) - dp) < 5.0
    print(f"inlet speed {u[0]:.3f} m/s, throat speed {u[-1]:.3f} m/s")
```

:::exercise What will the manometer read?
The same venturi will carry 10 L/s of water. What mercury manometer reading should you expect (ideal flow)?
---solution---
1. $V_1 = 0.01/(\pi\cdot0.1^2/4) = 1.273$ m/s; $V_2 = 4V_1 = 5.093$ m/s.
2. $\Delta p = \tfrac12\cdot998\cdot(5.093^2 - 1.273^2) = 12.134$ kPa.
3. $h = 12\,134/((13\,546 - 998)\cdot9.81) = 98.6$ mm of mercury.
:::
