Olive oil running through a narrow pipe, blood in small arteries, water in a drip-irrigation line in a Bali rice field: when the flow is slow, narrow or viscous enough, it moves in orderly concentric layers. Then Navier-Stokes has an exact solution, found experimentally by Hagen in 1839 and Poiseuille in 1840. Its most striking feature: the flow rate goes with the fourth power of the radius.

## The profile

Steady, fully developed flow in a round pipe has only an axial velocity $u(r)$. Navier-Stokes in cylindrical coordinates reduces to

$$
\frac{\mu}{r}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r\frac{du}{dr}\right) = \frac{dp}{dx} ,
$$

with $u(R) = 0$ at the wall and a finite value on the axis. Integrating twice:

$$
u(r) = \frac{-dp/dx}{4\mu}\,(R^2 - r^2) ,
$$

a paraboloid. Integrating over the cross-section gives the **Hagen-Poiseuille law**

$$
Q = \frac{\pi R^4\,\Delta p}{8\mu L}, \qquad \bar u = \frac{u_\text{max}}{2} .
$$

Halve the radius and, at the same pressure drop, the flow falls to 1/16. That's why narrowing arteries are so dangerous, and why viscometers use thin capillaries.

## When it applies

Only for **laminar** flow, $Re = \rho\bar uD/\mu \lesssim 2300$, and only once the profile has developed, after an entrance length of roughly $0.06\,Re\,D$. Above that Reynolds number, disturbances grow and the flow becomes turbulent, with a flatter profile and far larger losses (lesson 12.10). A force balance on a cylinder of fluid also gives the wall shear stress, $\tau_w = R\,\Delta p/(2L)$.

The laminar velocity profile in a pipe. Change the pressure gradient, diameter and fluid, and watch the Reynolds number.
:::

:::example Olive oil in a 20 mm pipe
$\mu = 0.084$ Pa s, $\rho = 910$ kg/m³, 5 m long, 2 kPa pressure difference.

1. $Q = \pi\cdot0.01^4\cdot2000/(8\cdot0.084\cdot5) = 18.70$ mL/s.
2. Centre speed 0.1190 m/s, mean 0.0595 m/s: ratio 0.5000.
3. $Re = 12.9$, comfortably laminar.
4. Wall shear stress $0.01\cdot2000/(2\cdot5) = 2.000$ Pa.
5. A finite-volume solution of the radial equation reproduces the profile to $10^{-6}$ of the centre speed, and gives the same 18.70 mL/s.
:::

```python file=poiseuille.py
"""Hagen-Poiseuille flow in a round pipe: the parabolic profile, flow rate, and a finite-difference check in r."""
import math
import numpy as np


def profile(r, R, dpdx, mu):
    """u(r) = (-dp/dx) (R^2 - r^2) / (4 mu)."""
    return -dpdx * (R * R - r * r) / (4 * mu)


def flow_rate(R, dp, L, mu):
    return math.pi * R ** 4 * dp / (8 * mu * L)


def fd_profile(R, dpdx, mu, n=201):
    """Solve (1/r) d/dr (r du/dr) = (1/mu) dp/dx with du/dr = 0 at r = 0 and u(R) = 0, finite volumes on a radial grid."""
    r = np.linspace(0, R, n); dr = r[1] - r[0]
    A = np.zeros((n, n)); b = np.full(n, dpdx / mu)
    A[0, 0], A[0, 1] = -4 / dr ** 2, 4 / dr ** 2                 # axis: limit r -> 0 gives 2 u''
    for i in range(1, n - 1):
        rp, rm = r[i] + dr / 2, r[i] - dr / 2
        A[i, i - 1], A[i, i], A[i, i + 1] = rm / (r[i] * dr * dr), -(rp + rm) / (r[i] * dr * dr), rp / (r[i] * dr * dr)
    A[-1, -1] = 1; b[-1] = 0
    return r, np.linalg.solve(A, b)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # olive oil (mu 0.084 Pa s, rho 910) in a 20 mm pipe, 5 m long, 2 kPa pressure difference
    R, L, dp, mu, rho = 0.010, 5.0, 2000.0, 0.084, 910.0
    Q = flow_rate(R, dp, L, mu)
    umax = profile(0, R, -dp / L, mu); umean = Q / (math.pi * R * R)
    print(f"Q = {Q*1e6:.2f} mL/s; centre speed {umax:.4f} m/s; mean {umean:.4f} m/s; ratio {umean/umax:.4f}")
    assert abs(umean / umax - 0.5) < 1e-12
    Re = rho * umean * 2 * R / mu
    print(f"Reynolds number {Re:.1f}: laminar (below about 2300), so the formula applies")
    wall_tau = dp * R / (2 * L)                                     # force balance on the fluid cylinder
    print(f"wall shear stress {wall_tau:.3f} Pa = R dp / (2L)")
    r, u = fd_profile(R, -dp / L, mu)
    assert np.max(np.abs(u - profile(r, R, -dp / L, mu))) < 1e-6 * umax
    Q_fd = float(np.sum((2 * np.pi * r[1:] * u[1:] + 2 * np.pi * r[:-1] * u[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(r)))
    print(f"finite differences: centre {u[0]:.4f} m/s, Q {Q_fd*1e6:.2f} mL/s")
    print(f"halving the radius cuts Q by a factor of {flow_rate(R, dp, L, mu)/flow_rate(R/2, dp, L, mu):.0f}")
```

:::exercise A syringe needle
Water ($\mu = 1.0\times10^{-3}$ Pa s) is pushed at 1 mL/s through a needle of 0.6 mm inner diameter and 25 mm length. Find the pressure needed across the needle, the mean speed and the Reynolds number.
---solution---
1. $\Delta p = 8\mu LQ/(\pi R^4) = 8\cdot10^{-3}\cdot0.025\cdot10^{-6}/(\pi\cdot(0.3\times10^{-3})^4) = 7860$ Pa, about 0.08 bar.
2. Mean speed $Q/(\pi R^2) = 3.537$ m/s.
3. $Re = 3.537\cdot0.0006/10^{-6} = 2122$: still just laminar. Push faster and the formula stops applying.
:::
