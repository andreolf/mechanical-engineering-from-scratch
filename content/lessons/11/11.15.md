Behind a boulder in a Bali river, a coconut can circle in an eddy for minutes before it escapes. You can explore that in the [coconut lab](https://andreolf.github.io/coconut-lab/). To describe such swirling flows, fluid mechanics uses two related quantities: **vorticity**, the local spin of each fluid parcel, and **circulation**, the total swirl around a loop. A surprise: in most of an eddy the water goes round without spinning at all.

## Vorticity

Vorticity is the curl of the velocity,

$$
\boldsymbol\omega = \nabla\times\mathbf u, \qquad \omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\ \text{in 2D} .
$$

It equals twice the local angular velocity of a fluid parcel. A tiny paddle wheel carried by the flow would spin at $\omega/2$. Flows with $\boldsymbol\omega = 0$ everywhere are **irrotational**, and the next lesson builds them from potentials.

## Circulation and Kelvin's theorem

Circulation is the line integral of velocity around a closed curve:

$$
\Gamma = \oint\mathbf u\cdot d\mathbf l = \iint\omega_z\,dA
$$

by Stokes' theorem: the total vorticity inside. **Kelvin's circulation theorem** says that in an inviscid, barotropic fluid with conservative body forces, the circulation around a loop moving with the fluid stays constant. Vorticity can't be created inside such a flow; it comes from walls and is spread by viscosity. That's why eddies are shed from boulders, bridge piers and wing tips.

## The Rankine vortex

A simple model of an eddy: inside a core of radius $a$ the fluid rotates like a solid body, $u_\theta = \Omega r$; outside it's a **free vortex**, $u_\theta = \Gamma/(2\pi r)$. The core carries uniform vorticity $2\Omega$; outside, the vorticity is zero, so parcels orbit without spinning about their own axes. The circulation is $\Gamma$ for every loop enclosing the core.

:::example An eddy behind a boulder
$\Gamma = 2$ m²/s, core radius 0.4 m.

1. Core angular velocity $\Omega = \Gamma/(2\pi a^2) = 1.9894$ rad/s.
2. Finite-difference vorticity on a 241 × 241 grid: 3.9789 in the core $= 2\Omega$; outside, zero up to a truncation error of $10^{-3}$.
3. Circulation by line integral: 0.50000 m²/s around r = 0.2 m (vorticity × area), and 2.00000 around r = 0.4, 0.8 and 1.6 m.
4. Peak swirl 0.7958 m/s at the core edge. A coconut at r = 1 m circles once every 19.7 s.
:::

```python file=vorticity.py
"""Vorticity and circulation of a Rankine vortex: finite-difference curl, line integrals, and the solid-body core."""
import numpy as np


def rankine(x, y, Gamma, a):
    """Velocity of a Rankine vortex: solid-body rotation inside radius a, free (irrotational) vortex outside."""
    r = np.hypot(x, y) + 1e-300
    ut = np.where(r < a, Gamma * r / (2 * np.pi * a * a), Gamma / (2 * np.pi * r))
    return -ut * y / r, ut * x / r


def vorticity(u, v, dx):
    """omega_z = dv/dx - du/dy by central differences (arrays indexed [y, x])."""
    return (v[1:-1, 2:] - v[1:-1, :-2]) / (2 * dx) - (u[2:, 1:-1] - u[:-2, 1:-1]) / (2 * dx)


def circulation(Gamma, a, R, n=4000):
    """Line integral of u . dl around a circle of radius R."""
    th = np.linspace(0, 2 * np.pi, n + 1)
    x, y = R * np.cos(th), R * np.sin(th)
    u, v = rankine(x, y, Gamma, a)
    return float(np.sum(((u[1:] + u[:-1]) * np.diff(x) + (v[1:] + v[:-1]) * np.diff(y)) / 2))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # an eddy behind a boulder in a river: circulation 2 m^2/s, core radius 0.4 m
    Gamma, a = 2.0, 0.4
    x = np.linspace(-1.2, 1.2, 241); dx = x[1] - x[0]; X, Y = np.meshgrid(x, x)
    u, v = rankine(X, Y, Gamma, a)
    w = vorticity(u, v, dx); R = np.hypot(X, Y)[1:-1, 1:-1]
    Omega = Gamma / (2 * np.pi * a * a)
    core, outer = w[R < 0.3], w[R > 0.5]
    print(f"core angular velocity {Omega:.4f} rad/s; vorticity in the core {core.mean():.4f} (2 Omega = {2*Omega:.4f}), outside {np.abs(outer).max():.2e}")
    assert np.allclose(core, 2 * Omega, rtol=1e-9) and np.abs(outer).max() < 1e-3 * 2 * Omega   # outside: zero up to truncation error
    for Rc in (0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6):
        c = circulation(Gamma, a, Rc)
        print(f"  circulation around r = {Rc}: {c:.5f} m^2/s (enclosed vorticity x area: {2*Omega*np.pi*min(Rc, a)**2:.5f})")
        assert abs(c - 2 * Omega * np.pi * min(Rc, a) ** 2) < 1e-5
    print(f"peak swirl speed at the core edge {Gamma/(2*np.pi*a):.4f} m/s; a coconut at r = 1 m circles once every {2*np.pi*1/(Gamma/(2*np.pi*1)):.1f} s")
```

:::exercise Inside and outside the core
For the same eddy, how long does a coconut take to circle at r = 0.2 m and at r = 0.3 m? What's the vorticity there, and the circulation around a 0.3 m loop?
---solution---
1. Both radii are inside the core, where the fluid turns like a solid body: the period is $2\pi/\Omega = 2\pi/1.9894 = 3.158$ s for both.
2. Vorticity $2\Omega = 3.979$ s⁻¹ at both.
3. Circulation around r = 0.3 m: $2\Omega\cdot\pi\cdot0.3^2 = 1.125$ m²/s, less than the full 2 m²/s because the loop encloses only part of the core.
:::
