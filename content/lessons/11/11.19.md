At the foot of a spillway, water arrives thin and very fast. A little further on, the river is deep and slow. The change isn't gradual: the surface leaps up in a churning, foaming roller called a **hydraulic jump**. You can see one in a kitchen sink, where the thin fast sheet under the tap suddenly thickens into a ring. Engineers build stilling basins to make jumps on purpose, because a jump destroys energy that would otherwise scour the riverbed.

## Momentum, not energy

Energy isn't conserved across a jump: turbulence turns it into heat. Momentum is the conserved quantity. For a short horizontal stretch of rectangular channel, the only horizontal forces are the hydrostatic pressures on the two ends, so the **momentum function** per unit width,

$$
M = \frac{y^2}{2} + \frac{q^2}{g y} ,
$$

has the same value before and after. Solving $M(y_1) = M(y_2)$ for the downstream depth gives the **Belanger equation**:

$$
\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8\,\mathrm{Fr}_1^2} - 1\right) .
$$

$y_1$ and $y_2$ are **conjugate depths**. The jump always goes from supercritical ($\mathrm{Fr}_1 > 1$) to subcritical.

## The energy lost

The specific energy drops by

$$
\Delta E = E_1 - E_2 = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4\,y_1 y_2} > 0 .
$$

This is positive only when $y_2 > y_1$. A reverse "jump" from deep to shallow would have to create energy, so it never happens. The stronger the jump, the larger the share of energy destroyed: about 2% at $\mathrm{Fr}_1 = 1.5$ and over 70% at $\mathrm{Fr}_1 = 10$. Designers aim for $4.5 < \mathrm{Fr}_1 < 9$, where the jump is steady and efficient.

:::example A stilling basin
Water leaves a spillway apron 0.25 m deep with $q = 2$ m²/s.

1. $V_1 = 8.000$ m/s and $\mathrm{Fr}_1 = 8/\sqrt{9.81\cdot0.25} = 5.1084$.
2. Bisection on $M(y) = M(y_1)$ gives $y_2 = 1.68542$ m, matching Belanger to 9 digits. Downstream, $\mathrm{Fr}_2 = 0.2918$.
3. $E_1 = 3.5120$ m and $E_2 = 1.7572$ m, so the jump destroys 1.7548 m of head, the same as $(y_2 - y_1)^3/(4y_1y_2)$.
4. That's $\rho g q\,\Delta E = 34.43$ kW dissipated in every metre width of the basin.
:::

```python file=jump.py
"""Hydraulic jump: conjugate depths from momentum, and the energy it destroys."""
import math

G = 9.81


def momentum_function(y, q):
    """Force plus momentum flux per unit width, divided by rho g: M = y^2/2 + q^2/(g y)."""
    return y * y / 2 + q * q / (G * y)


def conjugate_bisect(y1, q):
    """The other depth with the same M, found numerically on the opposite branch of the curve."""
    yc = (q * q / G) ** (1 / 3)
    M1 = momentum_function(y1, q)
    lo, hi = (yc, 50.0) if y1 < yc else (1e-6, yc)
    for _ in range(200):
        m = (lo + hi) / 2
        if (momentum_function(m, q) - M1) * (momentum_function(lo, q) - M1) <= 0: hi = m
        else: lo = m
    return (lo + hi) / 2


def belanger(y1, Fr1):
    return y1 * (math.sqrt(1 + 8 * Fr1 * Fr1) - 1) / 2


def energy(y, q):
    return y + q * q / (2 * G * y * y)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # water leaves a spillway apron 0.25 m deep carrying q = 2 m^2/s
    y1, q = 0.25, 2.0
    V1 = q / y1; Fr1 = V1 / math.sqrt(G * y1)
    y2 = conjugate_bisect(y1, q)
    print(f"V1 = {V1:.3f} m/s, Fr1 = {Fr1:.4f}; conjugate depth {y2:.5f} m (Belanger {belanger(y1, Fr1):.5f})")
    assert abs(y2 - belanger(y1, Fr1)) < 1e-9
    dE = energy(y1, q) - energy(y2, q)
    print(f"E1 = {energy(y1, q):.4f} m, E2 = {energy(y2, q):.4f} m, loss {dE:.4f} m = (y2-y1)^3/(4 y1 y2) = {(y2-y1)**3/(4*y1*y2):.4f}")
    print(f"power dissipated per metre width: {1000*G*q*dE/1000:.2f} kW; Fr2 = {q/y2/math.sqrt(G*y2):.4f}")
    assert dE > 0 and abs(dE - (y2 - y1) ** 3 / (4 * y1 * y2)) < 1e-9
    # a 'jump' from deep to shallow would need energy to be created: forbidden
    y_back = conjugate_bisect(y2, q)
    assert abs(y_back - y1) < 1e-9 and energy(y2, q) - energy(y_back, q) < 0
    for F in (1.5, 3, 6, 10):
        r = (math.sqrt(1 + 8 * F * F) - 1) / 2
        print(f"Fr1 = {F:4.1f}: y2/y1 = {r:.3f}, fraction of E1 lost {((r-1)**3/(4*r)) / (1 + F*F/2):.3f}")
```

:::exercise A sluice gate
Water shoots under a sluice gate 0.1 m deep at 5 m/s. Find $\mathrm{Fr}_1$, the conjugate depth, and the head lost in the jump.
---solution---
1. $\mathrm{Fr}_1 = 5/\sqrt{9.81\cdot0.1} = 5.0482$.
2. $y_2 = 0.1\cdot(\sqrt{1 + 8\cdot5.0482^2} - 1)/2 = 0.6657$ m.
3. $\Delta E = (0.6657 - 0.1)^3/(4\cdot0.1\cdot0.6657) = 0.6798$ m. Check with energies: $q = 0.5$ m²/s, $E_1 = 0.1 + 25/19.62 = 1.3742$ m, $E_2 = 0.6657 + 0.5^2/(19.62\cdot0.6657^2) = 0.6944$ m, difference 0.680 m.
:::
