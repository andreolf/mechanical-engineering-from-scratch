Every flow in this course, the Limmat, the air over a cyclist, oil in a bearing, obeys one set of equations, written down by Navier in 1822 and Stokes in 1845. They're Newton's second law for a fluid parcel with the viscous stresses spelled out. Simple to write and famously hard to solve (proving that smooth solutions always exist in 3D is one of the Millennium Prize problems), they still have a handful of exact solutions that anchor everything else.

## From Cauchy to Navier-Stokes

Newton's law per unit volume for a fluid parcel is the **Cauchy momentum equation**:

$$
\rho\frac{D\mathbf u}{Dt} = \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma + \rho\mathbf g ,
$$

where $\boldsymbol\sigma$ is the stress tensor. For a **Newtonian** fluid it's pressure plus viscous stress proportional to the strain rate: $\sigma_{ij} = -p\,\delta_{ij} + \mu\left(\partial u_i/\partial x_j + \partial u_j/\partial x_i\right)$, the 3D version of $\tau = \mu\,du/dy$. Substituting, and using $\nabla\cdot\mathbf u = 0$ for incompressible flow:

$$
\rho\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf u}{\partial t} + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\right) = -\nabla p + \mu\nabla^2\mathbf u + \rho\mathbf g, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf u = 0 .
$$

Four equations (three momentum components and continuity) for four unknowns ($u$, $v$, $w$, $p$).

## Reading the terms

- **Unsteady** $\rho\,\partial\mathbf u/\partial t$: local acceleration.
- **Convective** $\rho(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u$: nonlinear; the source of turbulence.
- **Pressure gradient** $-\nabla p$: in incompressible flow, pressure is whatever it needs to be to keep $\nabla\cdot\mathbf u = 0$.
- **Viscous** $\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u$: diffusion of momentum.

## Exact solutions

When the geometry makes the convective term vanish, the equations become linear and solvable. Between parallel plates with a pressure gradient $dp/dx = -G$, the flow is $u = \frac{G}{2\mu}(h^2 - y^2)$: **plane Poiseuille flow**. A rare unsteady solution with full nonlinearity is the **Taylor-Green vortex**, whose decay rate is set entirely by viscosity.

The x-momentum terms at a point, for three candidate flows. A genuine solution sums to zero everywhere and at all times.
:::

:::example Symbolic verification
SymPy substitutes each field into continuity and both momentum equations and simplifies.

1. Plane Poiseuille, $u = \frac{G}{2\mu}(h^2 - y^2)$, $v = 0$, $p = -Gx$: residuals [0, 0, 0].
2. Taylor-Green, $u = U\sin x\cos y\,e^{-2\nu t}$, $v = -U\cos x\sin y\,e^{-2\nu t}$, $p = \frac{\rho U^2}{4}(\cos2x + \cos2y)e^{-4\nu t}$: residuals [0, 0, 0].
3. The same velocity with $p = 0$ leaves $\tfrac12\rho U^2e^{-4\mu t/\rho}\sin2x$ in the x-momentum equation: not a solution.
4. Plane Poiseuille carries $2Gh^3/(3\mu)$ per unit width; mean/max = 2/3.
:::

```python file=nsverify.py
"""Navier-Stokes: symbolic check that plane Poiseuille flow and the Taylor-Green vortex are exact solutions, and that a wrong field is not."""
import sympy as sp

x, y, t = sp.symbols("x y t", real=True)
rho, mu, G, h, U = sp.symbols("rho mu G h U", positive=True)


def residuals(u, v, p):
    """Continuity and the two momentum equations, each rearranged to equal zero."""
    cont = sp.diff(u, x) + sp.diff(v, y)
    lap = lambda f: sp.diff(f, x, 2) + sp.diff(f, y, 2)
    mx = rho * (sp.diff(u, t) + u * sp.diff(u, x) + v * sp.diff(u, y)) + sp.diff(p, x) - mu * lap(u)
    my = rho * (sp.diff(v, t) + u * sp.diff(v, x) + v * sp.diff(v, y)) + sp.diff(p, y) - mu * lap(v)
    return [sp.simplify(e) for e in (cont, mx, my)]


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # plane Poiseuille flow between plates at y = +-h driven by a pressure gradient dp/dx = -G
    u = G / (2 * mu) * (h ** 2 - y ** 2); v = sp.Integer(0); p = -G * x
    print("Poiseuille residuals:", residuals(u, v, p))
    assert residuals(u, v, p) == [0, 0, 0]
    # Taylor-Green vortex, decaying in time
    nu = mu / rho; F = sp.exp(-2 * nu * t)
    u = U * sp.sin(x) * sp.cos(y) * F; v = -U * sp.cos(x) * sp.sin(y) * F
    p = rho * U ** 2 / 4 * (sp.cos(2 * x) + sp.cos(2 * y)) * F ** 2
    print("Taylor-Green residuals:", residuals(u, v, p))
    assert residuals(u, v, p) == [0, 0, 0]
    # a field that conserves mass but has the wrong pressure fails the momentum equation
    print("Taylor-Green with p = 0:", residuals(u, v, sp.Integer(0))[1])
    assert residuals(u, v, sp.Integer(0))[1] != 0
    # the plane Poiseuille flow rate per unit width and mean speed
    Q = sp.integrate(G / (2 * mu) * (h ** 2 - y ** 2), (y, -h, h))
    print("flow per unit width:", sp.simplify(Q), "; mean / max:", sp.simplify(Q / (2 * h) / (G * h ** 2 / (2 * mu))))
```

:::exercise A narrow channel
Water ($\mu = 1.0\times10^{-3}$ Pa s) flows between two plates 2 mm apart under a pressure gradient of 1000 Pa/m. Find the flow per metre width and the centre speed, and confirm by substitution that Couette flow $u = Uy/h$, $v = 0$, $p$ constant also solves the equations.
---solution---
1. Half-gap $h = 1$ mm. $q = 2Gh^3/(3\mu) = 2\cdot1000\cdot10^{-9}/(3\times10^{-3}) = 6.667\times10^{-4}$ m²/s, about 0.67 L/s per metre.
2. Centre speed $Gh^2/(2\mu) = 1000\cdot10^{-6}/0.002 = 0.5$ m/s; mean $= \tfrac23\cdot0.5 = 0.333$ m/s.
3. Couette: $\partial u/\partial t = 0$; $(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)u = u\,\partial u/\partial x = 0$ since $u$ depends only on $y$; $\nabla p = 0$; $\nabla^2u = \partial^2(Uy/h)/\partial y^2 = 0$. Every term vanishes, and $\partial u/\partial x + \partial v/\partial y = 0$.
:::
