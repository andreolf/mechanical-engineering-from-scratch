High in the Valais, water falls almost two kilometres through steel penstocks and leaves a nozzle at nearly 190 m/s, faster than a racing car. It hits the spoon-shaped buckets of a **Pelton wheel**, which turn it almost right round and send it dropping gently into the tailrace. How fast should the buckets move to grab the most energy? The angular momentum version of the control-volume balance answers that, and the same equation designs every pump, fan and turbine.

## Euler's turbomachinery equation

Apply the moment of momentum to a control volume around a rotor. In steady flow, the torque the fluid exerts on the rotor equals the angular momentum it loses:

$$
T = \dot m\,(r_1c_{u1} - r_2c_{u2}) ,
$$

where $c_u$ is the tangential (swirl) component of the absolute velocity at inlet 1 and outlet 2. Power is $P = T\omega$, and with blade speed $u = \omega r$,

$$
P = \dot m\,(u_1c_{u1} - u_2c_{u2}) .
$$

It's pure kinematics and momentum: no assumptions about viscosity or the flow inside the blade passage.

## The Pelton wheel

For a Pelton, $r_1 = r_2$ and $u_1 = u_2 = u$. The jet arrives at $c_{u1} = V$. Relative to the bucket it enters at $V - u$, is turned through an angle $\beta$ (about 165°, so the water clears the next bucket), and leaves with absolute swirl $c_{u2} = u - (V - u)\cos(180° - \beta)$. Then

$$
P = \dot m\,u\,(V - u)\,\bigl(1 + \cos(180° - \beta)\bigr) ,
$$

a parabola in $u$ with its maximum at $u = V/2$. With a 180° bucket at $u = V/2$ the water leaves with zero absolute speed: all its kinetic energy goes to the runner. Real Peltons reach 90–92 %.

:::widget peltonrun
Pelton efficiency against bucket speed. The best point sits at half the jet speed.
:::

:::example An 800 m Pelton plant
Assumed: 800 m net head, 2 m³/s, nozzle velocity coefficient 0.98, 165° buckets.

1. Jet speed $0.98\sqrt{2\cdot9.81\cdot800} = 122.78$ m/s; hydraulic power $\rho gQH = 15.70$ MW.
2. Sweeping the bucket speed, the best is 61.39 m/s $= 0.500V$, giving 14.818 MW, an efficiency of 0.9440 before bucket friction, windage and bearings.
3. An ideal 180° bucket at $V/2$ captures exactly the jet's kinetic power, 15.074 MW.
4. As a torque: a runner with 2.4 m pitch diameter turns at 489 rpm with 289.6 kN m, the same 14.818 MW.
:::

```python file=pelton.py
"""Euler's turbine equation applied to a Pelton wheel: power against bucket speed, for an Alpine high-head plant."""
import math
import numpy as np

RHO, G = 1000.0, 9.81


def euler_torque(mdot, r1, cu1, r2, cu2):
    """Torque the fluid gives the runner: mdot (r1 cu1 - r2 cu2)."""
    return mdot * (r1 * cu1 - r2 * cu2)


def pelton_power(Q, V, u, beta_deg=165.0, k=1.0):
    """Power (W) from a jet of speed V on buckets moving at u; the bucket turns the relative flow by beta,
    k is the relative-speed loss factor across the bucket."""
    w1 = V - u                                     # relative speed into the bucket
    cu2 = u - k * w1 * math.cos(math.radians(180 - beta_deg))     # absolute tangential speed leaving
    return RHO * Q * u * (V - cu2)                 # Euler with r1 = r2: P = mdot u (cu1 - cu2)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    H, Q, Cv = 800.0, 2.0, 0.98                    # assumed plant: 800 m net head, 2 m^3/s
    V = Cv * math.sqrt(2 * G * H)
    print(f"jet speed {V:.2f} m/s; hydraulic power rho g Q H = {RHO*G*Q*H/1e6:.2f} MW")
    us = np.linspace(0, V, 2001)
    P = np.array([pelton_power(Q, V, u) for u in us])
    i = P.argmax()
    print(f"best bucket speed {us[i]:.2f} m/s = {us[i]/V:.3f} V; power {P[i]/1e6:.3f} MW; efficiency {P[i]/(RHO*G*Q*H):.4f}")
    assert abs(us[i] / V - 0.5) < 1e-3                 # ideal: optimum at u = V/2
    # ideal bucket turning the flow by 180 degrees with no losses captures all the jet's kinetic energy
    Pid = pelton_power(Q, V, V / 2, beta_deg=180.0)
    assert abs(Pid - 0.5 * RHO * Q * V ** 2) < 1e-6
    print(f"180 deg ideal bucket at V/2: {Pid/1e6:.3f} MW = jet kinetic power {0.5*RHO*Q*V**2/1e6:.3f} MW")
    # the same result from Euler's equation as a torque, for a 2.4 m pitch diameter runner
    r = 1.2; u = V / 2; w1 = V - u; cu2 = u - w1 * math.cos(math.radians(15))
    T = euler_torque(RHO * Q, r, V, r, cu2); n = u / r * 60 / (2 * math.pi)
    print(f"runner 2.4 m pitch diameter: {n:.0f} rpm, torque {T/1e3:.1f} kN m, power {T*u/r/1e6:.3f} MW")
    assert abs(T * u / r - pelton_power(Q, V, u)) < 1e-3
```

:::exercise Sizing a runner
A Pelton runner under 1883 m of head (an extreme Alpine scheme) drives a generator synchronised to 50 Hz at 428.6 rpm. With $C_v = 0.98$, what jet speed results, and what pitch diameter puts the buckets at half the jet speed?
---solution---
1. $V = 0.98\sqrt{2\cdot9.81\cdot1883} = 188.37$ m/s.
2. Best bucket speed $u = 94.18$ m/s.
3. $D = 60u/(\pi n) = 60\cdot94.18/(\pi\cdot428.6) = 4.20$ m. At this head, speed rather than size limits the runner: the buckets see enormous centrifugal and impact loads.
:::
