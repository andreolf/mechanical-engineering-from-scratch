Rivers, irrigation canals and storm drains have a free surface open to the air. The water level is no longer fixed by a pipe wall: it adjusts, and that gives open-channel flow a behaviour pipes don't have. The same discharge can flow deep and slow or shallow and fast, and the switch between them happens at one special depth.

## Specific energy

Measure energy per unit weight from the channel bed. For a rectangular channel carrying discharge $q$ per metre of width at depth $y$, the mean velocity is $V = q/y$ and the **specific energy** is

$$
E = y + \frac{V^2}{2g} = y + \frac{q^2}{2 g y^2} .
$$

For fixed $q$, $E$ is large both when the flow is very deep (the first term) and when it is very shallow (the second). In between there is a minimum.

## Critical depth and the Froude number

Setting $dE/dy = 1 - q^2/(g y^3) = 0$ gives the **critical depth**

$$
y_c = \left(\frac{q^2}{g}\right)^{1/3}, \qquad E_{\min} = \tfrac32\,y_c .
$$

At that depth $V^2 = g y$, so the **Froude number** $\mathrm{Fr} = V/\sqrt{g y}$ equals 1. $\sqrt{gy}$ is the speed of a small surface wave, so Fr compares the flow speed with the wave speed:

- **Subcritical**, Fr < 1: deep and slow. Waves can travel upstream, so downstream conditions (a gate, a weir) control the level.
- **Supercritical**, Fr > 1: shallow and fast, like water on a spillway. Disturbances are swept downstream.

Every energy above $E_{\min}$ has two **alternate depths**, one on each branch.

:::example A canal over a bed hump
A canal 3 m wide carries 6 m³/s, so $q = 2$ m²/s, at a depth of 1.5 m.

1. Critical depth $y_c = (2^2/9.81)^{1/3} = 0.7415$ m. A numerical search over 280 001 depths finds the minimum of $E$ at 0.7415 m, with $E_{\min} = 1.1123$ m $= 1.5\,y_c$ and Fr = 1.0000 there.
2. At 1.5 m: V = 1.333 m/s, Fr = 0.3476 (subcritical), E = 1.5906 m.
3. The alternate supercritical depth with the same energy is 0.4167 m, with V = 4.799 m/s and Fr = 2.3735.
4. A smooth hump on the bed lowers the available specific energy by its height. The largest hump the flow can pass without backing up (choking) is $1.5906 - 1.1123 = 0.4783$ m.
:::

```python file=channel.py
"""Open-channel flow: specific energy, critical depth and the Froude number in a rectangular channel."""
import math
import numpy as np

G = 9.81


def specific_energy(y, q):
    return y + q * q / (2 * G * y * y)


def critical_depth(q):
    return (q * q / G) ** (1 / 3)


def alternate_depths(E, q):
    """The subcritical and supercritical depths with the same specific energy (bisection on each branch)."""
    yc = critical_depth(q)
    def solve(lo, hi):
        for _ in range(200):
            m = (lo + hi) / 2
            if (specific_energy(m, q) - E) * (specific_energy(lo, q) - E) <= 0: hi = m
            else: lo = m
        return (lo + hi) / 2
    return solve(yc, 10 * E), solve(1e-6, yc)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # a canal 3 m wide carrying 6 m^3/s: q = 2 m^2/s per metre width
    q = 6.0 / 3.0
    yc = critical_depth(q)
    ys = np.linspace(0.2, 3, 280001)
    E = specific_energy(ys, q)
    y_min = ys[E.argmin()]
    print(f"critical depth {yc:.4f} m; numerical minimum of E at {y_min:.4f} m; E_min = {E.min():.4f} m (1.5 yc = {1.5*yc:.4f})")
    Fr = lambda y: q / (y * math.sqrt(G * y))
    print(f"Froude number at the minimum: {Fr(y_min):.4f}")
    assert abs(y_min - yc) < 1e-4 and abs(Fr(yc) - 1) < 1e-12              # minimum specific energy at Fr = 1
    y1 = 1.5
    Es = specific_energy(y1, q)
    sub, sup = alternate_depths(Es, q)
    print(f"flow 1.5 m deep: V = {q/y1:.3f} m/s, Fr = {Fr(y1):.4f} (subcritical), E = {Es:.4f} m")
    print(f"  alternate supercritical depth {sup:.4f} m, V = {q/sup:.3f} m/s, Fr = {Fr(sup):.4f}")
    # a smooth hump on the bed lowers the available energy: the largest hump before the flow chokes
    print(f"largest bed hump before choking: {Es - 1.5*yc:.4f} m")
```

:::exercise A rice-field drain
A drain 0.5 m wide carries 0.08 m³/s. Find the critical depth and minimum specific energy. The water flows 0.3 m deep: what are V, Fr and the regime?
---solution---
1. $q = 0.08/0.5 = 0.16$ m²/s; $y_c = (0.16^2/9.81)^{1/3} = 0.1377$ m.
2. $E_{\min} = 1.5\cdot0.1377 = 0.2066$ m.
3. At 0.3 m: $V = 0.16/0.3 = 0.5333$ m/s and $\mathrm{Fr} = 0.5333/\sqrt{9.81\cdot0.3} = 0.3109$: subcritical.
:::
