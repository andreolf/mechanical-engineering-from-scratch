Before asking what forces drive a flow, we need ways to describe the motion itself. Drop a coconut into the Limmat and follow it, or stand on a bridge and watch the water passing one spot. Those are the two viewpoints of fluid mechanics. Then there are three different "lines" you can draw through a flow, and they only agree when nothing changes in time.

## Lagrangian and Eulerian descriptions

- **Lagrangian:** follow individual fluid particles, $\mathbf x_p(t)$. Natural for a coconut, a drifting buoy or a smoke particle.
- **Eulerian:** describe the velocity at every fixed point, $\mathbf u(\mathbf x, t)$. Natural for measurements at a bridge pier, and what almost all fluid equations use.

The link is $d\mathbf x_p/dt = \mathbf u(\mathbf x_p, t)$: integrating the Eulerian field gives particle paths.

## Three kinds of line

- A **streamline** is everywhere tangent to the velocity at one instant: $d\mathbf x/ds \parallel \mathbf u(\mathbf x, t_0)$ with time frozen.
- A **pathline** is the trajectory of one particle over time.
- A **streakline** joins all particles that passed through one fixed point, like dye from a nozzle or smoke from a chimney.

In **steady** flow the field doesn't change, so a particle simply runs along its streamline and all three coincide. In unsteady flow they differ, sometimes dramatically. A smoke trail in gusty wind is a streakline, and it doesn't show you where the air is going now.

## A worked field

Take $u = U_0$, $v = V_0\sin\omega t$: uniform in space, but the cross-flow oscillates. At any instant the streamlines are straight parallel lines with slope $V_0\sin(\omega t)/U_0$. A particle released at the origin at $t = 0$ follows $x = U_0 t$, $y = V_0(1 - \cos\omega t)/\omega$, a wave entirely above the axis. The streakline at $t = 2\pi/\omega$ is the mirror image, entirely below.

:::example Tracing with RK4
$U_0 = 1$ m/s, $V_0 = 0.5$ m/s, $\omega = 1$ rad/s.

1. Pathline from the origin over one period: it ends at (6.2832, 0) and peaks at $y = 1.0000 = 2V_0/\omega$, as the exact solution says.
2. Streamline at $t = \pi/2$: straight with slope 0.5000 $= V_0/U_0$.
3. Streakline at $t = 2\pi$: $y$ from −1.0000 to 0, the mirror image of the pathline.
4. For a steady field $u = 1$, $v = 0.3$, all three lines lie on $y = 0.3x$ to machine precision.
:::

```python file=kinematics.py
"""Streamlines, pathlines and streaklines in the unsteady field u = U0, v = V0 sin(omega t), traced with RK4."""
import numpy as np

U0, V0 = 1.0, 0.5


def oscillating(omega):
    return lambda x, y, t: np.array([U0, V0 * np.sin(omega * t)])


def rk4(f, s, t0, t1, n):
    """Integrate ds/dt = f(s, t) from t0 to t1 in n steps; returns all states."""
    h = (t1 - t0) / n; out = [s.copy()]; t = t0
    for _ in range(n):
        k1 = f(s, t); k2 = f(s + h / 2 * k1, t + h / 2); k3 = f(s + h / 2 * k2, t + h / 2); k4 = f(s + h * k3, t + h)
        s = s + h / 6 * (k1 + 2 * k2 + 2 * k3 + k4); t += h; out.append(s.copy())
    return np.array(out)


def pathline(field, x0, t0, T, n=400):
    """Track one particle released at x0 at time t0."""
    return rk4(lambda s, t: field(*s, t), np.array(x0, float), t0, t0 + T, n)


def streamline(field, x0, t_frozen, L, n=400):
    """Curve tangent to the velocity field frozen at one instant."""
    return rk4(lambda s, _: field(*s, t_frozen), np.array(x0, float), 0, L, n)


def streakline(field, x0, t_now, n_release=60, n=200):
    """Positions at t_now of all particles released from x0 at earlier times."""
    return np.array([pathline(field, x0, t0, t_now - t0, n)[-1] for t0 in np.linspace(0, t_now, n_release, endpoint=False)])


if __name__ == "__main__":
    T = 2 * np.pi
    f = oscillating(1.0)
    p = pathline(f, [0, 0], 0, T); s = streamline(f, [0, 0], np.pi / 2, T); k = streakline(f, [0, 0], T)
    print(f"unsteady: pathline from the origin ends at ({p[-1,0]:.4f}, {p[-1,1]:.4f}); peak y {p[:,1].max():.4f} (exact 2 V0/omega = {2*V0:.4f})")
    print(f"  streamline at t = pi/2: straight, slope {s[-1,1]/s[-1,0]:.4f} (= V0/U0)")
    print(f"  streakline at t = 2 pi: y from {k[:,1].min():.4f} to {k[:,1].max():.4f}, a mirror image of the pathline")
    assert abs(p[:, 1].max() - 2 * V0) < 1e-4                     # y(t) = V0 (1 - cos t) / omega
    assert abs(s[-1, 1] / s[-1, 0] - V0 / U0) < 1e-12
    steady = lambda x, y, t: np.array([U0, 0.3])                  # steady field: all three coincide
    for pts in (pathline(steady, [0, 0], 0, 3), streamline(steady, [0, 0], 0, 3), streakline(steady, [0, 0], 3)):
        assert np.max(np.abs(pts[:, 1] - 0.3 * pts[:, 0])) < 1e-12
    print("steady field: pathline, streamline and streakline all lie on y = 0.3 x")
```

:::exercise One more particle
In the same field, where is a particle released from the origin at $t_0 = \pi$ when $t = 2\pi$? Is that point on the pathline of the particle released at $t = 0$, on the streakline at $t = 2\pi$, or both?
---solution---
1. $x = U_0(t - t_0) = \pi = 3.1416$ m.
2. $y = V_0(\cos\omega t_0 - \cos\omega t)/\omega = 0.5(\cos\pi - \cos2\pi) = -1.0$ m.
3. It's on the streakline at $2\pi$ (all particles from the origin, by definition), at its lowest point. The pathline from $t = 0$ at $x = \pi$ is at $y = +1.0$, so the point isn't on that pathline.
:::
