A deckhand stops a drifting ship by wrapping a rope a few times round a bollard and holding the free end with one hand. Climbers lower each other with a rope round a friction device; belts drive alternators and pumps. All of this rests on one striking result: friction on a curved surface grows exponentially with the wrap angle.

## The Euler–Eytelwein formula

Take a rope wrapped round a fixed drum, about to slip, with tension $T_1$ on the slack side and $T_2$ on the tight side. Look at a short piece spanning angle $d\theta$:

- the two end tensions, $T$ and $T + dT$, are tangent to the drum;
- radially they press the rope on with $dN = T\,d\theta$;
- at the point of slipping, friction $\mu\,dN$ makes up the difference: $dT = \mu T\,d\theta$.

Integrate over the wrap angle $\beta$ (in radians):

$$
\frac{dT}{T} = \mu\,d\theta \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{T_2}{T_1} = e^{\mu\beta} .
$$

The drum's radius drops out. With $\mu = 0.3$, one turn gives a ratio of 6.6, two turns 43, three turns 286. Each extra turn multiplies by the same factor.

## Belt drives and V-belts

A belt transmits torque $(T_1 - T_2)\,r$ through the friction on its pulley, and slips when $T_1/T_2$ reaches $e^{\mu\beta}$ on the pulley with the **smaller** wrap. A V-belt wedges into its groove, which multiplies the normal force: with groove angle $2\alpha$ the effective friction coefficient is $\mu/\sin\alpha$, about three times larger for a 40° groove.

:::widget bollard
Rope round a bollard. Change the wrap and the friction coefficient. Note the log scale: the holding ratio is a straight line in the number of turns.
:::

:::example A bollard and a belt drive
1. Three turns ($\beta = 6\pi$) with $\mu = 0.3$: $T_2/T_1 = e^{5.655} = 285.7$.
2. The code checks the formula by stacking many short arcs, each in exact limiting equilibrium. 10 arcs give 6518 (far off), 1000 arcs give 285.73, 10000 give 285.68: the exponential is the limit.
3. A flat belt on a 160 mm pulley with 160° wrap, $\mu = 0.35$: ratio $e^{0.35\cdot2.793} = 2.657$. With 1.2 kN on the tight side, the slack side carries 0.452 kN.
4. Torque $(1.2 - 0.452)\cdot0.08 = 59.9$ N m; at 1450 rpm that's 9.09 kW.
5. As a V-belt in a 40° groove: $\mu_{eff} = 0.35/\sin20° = 1.023$ and the ratio becomes 17.4.
:::

```python file=belt.py
"""Belt and rope friction: T2 / T1 = exp(mu beta), checked by stacking many small slipping segments."""
import math
import numpy as np


def capstan_ratio(mu, beta):
    return math.exp(mu * beta)


def segments_ratio(mu, beta, n):
    """Split the wrap into n small arcs; each arc d(beta) is in limiting equilibrium:
    the normal force is ~ T d(beta) and friction mu N makes up the tension difference."""
    T = 1.0; db = beta / n
    for _ in range(n):
        # exact equilibrium of a short arc with entry tension T and exit tension T' (both tangent):
        # radial: N = (T + T') sin(db/2); tangential: (T' - T) cos(db/2) = mu N
        T = T * (math.cos(db / 2) + mu * math.sin(db / 2)) / (math.cos(db / 2) - mu * math.sin(db / 2))
    return T


if __name__ == "__main__":
    mu = 0.3
    for turns in (0.5, 1, 2, 3):
        beta = 2 * math.pi * turns
        print(f"{turns:3} turns round a bollard: holding ratio {capstan_ratio(mu, beta):9.2f}")
    beta = 6 * math.pi
    errs = []
    for n in (10, 100, 1000, 10000):
        r = segments_ratio(mu, beta, n); errs.append(abs(r / capstan_ratio(mu, beta) - 1))
        print(f"  {n:5d} segments: {r:.4f}")
    assert errs[-1] < 1e-5 and all(e1 > e2 for e1, e2 in zip(errs, errs[1:]))       # converges to the exponential
    # exponential in the wrap angle: each extra half turn multiplies the ratio by the same factor
    r = [capstan_ratio(mu, b) for b in np.arange(1, 6) * math.pi]
    assert np.allclose(np.array(r[1:]) / np.array(r[:-1]), math.exp(mu * math.pi))
    # a flat belt drive: 160 deg wrap on the small pulley, mu = 0.35, belt limited to 1.2 kN tight side
    beta, T1 = math.radians(160), 1.2
    T2 = T1 / capstan_ratio(0.35, beta)
    r_pulley, rpm = 0.08, 1450
    torque = (T1 - T2) * r_pulley; power = torque * rpm * 2 * math.pi / 60
    print(f"flat belt: slack side {T2:.4f} kN, torque {torque * 1000:.2f} N m, power {power:.3f} kW at {rpm} rpm")
    # V-belt with 40 deg groove: effective mu = mu / sin(20 deg)
    mu_v = 0.35 / math.sin(math.radians(20))
    print(f"V-belt: effective mu {mu_v:.4f}, ratio {capstan_ratio(mu_v, beta):.3f} vs flat {capstan_ratio(0.35, beta):.3f}")
```

:::exercise Stopping a boat
A boat drifts with a rope load of 50 kN. A deckhand can hold 250 N. The rope on a steel bollard has $\mu = 0.25$. How many turns are needed?
---solution---
1. Required ratio $50000/250 = 200$.
2. $\beta = \ln200/0.25 = 5.298/0.25 = 21.19$ rad $= 3.37$ turns.
3. In practice use at least 3.5 turns, plus a margin for a wet rope. Each extra turn multiplies the holding ratio by $e^{2\pi\cdot0.25} = 4.8$.
:::
