Weight is spread through a body, but for statics you can treat it as one force at the **centre of gravity**. Where that point sits decides whether a crane tips, how a beam section bends (its centroid is where bending stress is zero) and how a bike handles. This lesson shows how to find it for composite shapes, checks the result against a general polygon formula, and adds a neat shortcut for volumes of revolution.

## Definitions

For a body of mass $m$, the centre of mass is the mass-weighted average position:

$$
\bar{\mathbf r} = \frac{1}{m}\int \mathbf r\,dm .
$$

In a uniform gravity field it is also the centre of gravity. For a flat shape of uniform thickness, it is the **centroid** of the area: $\bar x = \frac{1}{A}\int x\,dA$, and similarly for $\bar y$. A symmetry axis always contains the centroid.

## Composite shapes

Most engineering shapes are built from rectangles, triangles and circles. Use the known centroid of each part and take the weighted average:

$$
\bar x = \frac{\sum A_i\,\bar x_i}{\sum A_i}, \qquad \bar y = \frac{\sum A_i\,\bar y_i}{\sum A_i} .
$$

A hole is a part with **negative** area. For any polygon, the shoelace formula gives the same answer straight from the vertices, which makes a good cross-check:

$$
A = \tfrac12\sum (x_iy_{i+1} - x_{i+1}y_i), \qquad \bar x = \frac{1}{6A}\sum (x_i + x_{i+1})(x_iy_{i+1} - x_{i+1}y_i).
$$

## Pappus–Guldinus

Revolve a plane area $A$ about an axis in its plane that doesn't cut it. The volume swept is the area times the distance its centroid travels:

$$
V = 2\pi\,\bar r\,A .
$$

The same holds for surface areas swept by a curve. It turns tyre, O-ring and pipe-bend volumes into one-line calculations.

:::example A T-section and an L-bracket
1. T-section: flange 200 × 30 mm on a 30 × 170 mm web. Areas 6000 and 5100 mm², centroids at 185 and 85 mm. $\bar y = (6000\cdot185 + 5100\cdot85)/11100 = 139.05$ mm above the bottom. The polygon formula on the 8 vertices gives the same.
2. L-bracket: 100 × 10 base plus 10 × 70 upright on its left end. $\bar x = (1000\cdot50 + 700\cdot5)/1700 = 31.47$ mm, $\bar y = (1000\cdot5 + 700\cdot45)/1700 = 21.47$ mm.
3. A 300 × 200 plate with an 80 mm hole centred at $x = 220$ mm: $\bar x = (60000\cdot150 - 5026.5\cdot220)/54973.5 = 143.60$ mm. It moves away from the hole.
4. Pappus: a cone made by revolving a right triangle (base 0.5 m, height 1.2 m) has $V = 2\pi\cdot(0.5/3)\cdot0.3 = 0.3142$ m³ $= \pi r^2h/3$.
:::

```python file=centroid.py
"""Centroids: composite shapes (add and subtract parts), the polygon (shoelace) formula, and Pappus-Guldinus."""
import math
import numpy as np


def composite(parts):
    """parts: (area, xc, yc); holes have negative area. Returns (A, x_bar, y_bar)."""
    A = sum(a for a, _, _ in parts)
    return A, sum(a * x for a, x, _ in parts) / A, sum(a * y for a, _, y in parts) / A


def polygon_centroid(pts):
    """Shoelace formula for a simple polygon with vertices in order."""
    p = np.asarray(pts, float); x, y = p[:, 0], p[:, 1]; xn, yn = np.roll(x, -1), np.roll(y, -1)
    cr = x * yn - xn * y
    A = cr.sum() / 2
    return A, ((x + xn) * cr).sum() / (6 * A), ((y + yn) * cr).sum() / (6 * A)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # T-section of a footbridge beam (mm): flange 200 x 30 on top of a 30 x 170 web
    A, xb, yb = composite([(200 * 30, 0, 170 + 15), (30 * 170, 0, 85)])
    Ap, xp, yp = polygon_centroid([(-15, 0), (15, 0), (15, 170), (100, 170), (100, 200), (-100, 200), (-100, 170), (-15, 170)])
    print(f"T-section: A = {A:.0f} mm^2, centroid {yb:.4f} mm above the bottom (polygon: {yp:.4f})")
    assert abs(A - Ap) < 1e-9 and abs(yb - yp) < 1e-9 and abs(xp) < 1e-12
    # L-bracket: a 100 x 10 base and a 10 x 70 upright
    A, xb, yb = composite([(100 * 10, 50, 5), (10 * 70, 5, 45)])
    Ap, xp, yp = polygon_centroid([(0, 0), (100, 0), (100, 10), (10, 10), (10, 80), (0, 80)])
    print(f"L-bracket: centroid ({xb:.4f}, {yb:.4f}) mm (polygon: ({xp:.4f}, {yp:.4f}))")
    assert np.allclose([xb, yb], [xp, yp])
    # a plate with a circular hole: subtract the hole as negative area
    A, xb, yb = composite([(300 * 200, 150, 100), (-math.pi * 40**2, 220, 100)])
    print(f"plate 300 x 200 with an 80 mm hole at x = 220: centroid x = {xb:.4f} mm (shifts away from the hole)")
    assert xb < 150
    # Pappus-Guldinus: volume = 2 pi x_bar A. A bike-tyre-like torus: tube radius 0.02 m at radius 0.33 m
    r, R = 0.02, 0.33
    V = 2 * math.pi * R * math.pi * r**2
    print(f"torus volume by Pappus: {V * 1e6:.3f} cm^3")
    # check Pappus on a cone (right triangle of base r, height h revolved about the h side): V = pi r^2 h / 3
    rc, hc = 0.5, 1.2
    Vc = 2 * math.pi * (rc / 3) * (rc * hc / 2)
    assert abs(Vc - math.pi * rc**2 * hc / 3) < 1e-12
    print(f"cone by Pappus {Vc:.6f} = pi r^2 h / 3 = {math.pi * rc**2 * hc / 3:.6f} m^3")
```

:::exercise An inner-tube volume
A bicycle inner tube is roughly a torus: tube radius 20 mm, centre-line radius 330 mm. Find its volume with Pappus.
---solution---
1. The revolved area is a circle: $A = \pi\cdot0.02^2 = 1.257\times10^{-3}$ m², centroid at $\bar r = 0.33$ m.
2. $V = 2\pi\cdot0.33\cdot1.257\times10^{-3} = 2.606\times10^{-3}$ m³, about 2.6 litres.
:::
