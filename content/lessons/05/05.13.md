Reactions tell you what the supports feel. To size a beam you need what the beam feels inside: at every section, how hard it is being sheared and bent. Those internal forces, plotted along the beam, are the shear force and bending moment diagrams. Every beam, shaft, floor joist and crane boom is designed from them.

## Cutting a beam

Cut the beam at position $x$ and look at the left part. To keep it in equilibrium, the right part must act on it with

- an **axial force** $N(x)$ along the beam,
- a **shear force** $V(x)$ across it,
- a **bending moment** $M(x)$.

With the usual sign convention (positive $M$ sags the beam, bottom in tension), equilibrium of the left part gives $V$ as the sum of the upward forces to the left, and $M$ as the sum of their moments about the cut.

## The differential relations

Balance a short slice $dx$ carrying a distributed load $q$ (downward positive):

$$
\frac{dV}{dx} = -q, \qquad \frac{dM}{dx} = V .
$$

These two lines are the whole method:

- With no distributed load, $V$ is constant and $M$ is linear between point loads.
- Under a uniform load, $V$ is linear and $M$ is a parabola.
- A point load makes $V$ jump by its size; $M$ gets a kink.
- $M$ is largest where $V$ crosses zero.

Two results worth remembering: a central point load on a simply supported span gives $M_{max} = PL/4$, and a uniform load gives $M_{max} = qL^2/8$.

:::example A footbridge span
6 m simply supported, 20 kN at 2 m plus 5 kN/m along the whole span.

1. Reactions: $R_b = (20\cdot2 + 30\cdot3)/6 = 21.667$ kN, $R_a = 50 - 21.667 = 28.333$ kN.
2. Shear just left of the point load: $28.333 - 5\cdot2 = 18.333$ kN. Just right: $18.333 - 20 = -1.667$ kN. It changes sign at $x = 2$ m.
3. So $M_{max} = M(2) = 28.333\cdot2 - 5\cdot2^2/2 = 46.667$ kN m.
4. Checks in the code: a central 30 kN load on 6 m gives $PL/4 = 45$ kN m; 8 kN/m gives $qL^2/8 = 36$ kN m; numerically $dM/dx = V$ and $dV/dx = -q$ everywhere.
:::

```python file=beam_diagrams.py
"""Shear force and bending moment diagrams by integrating dV/dx = -q and dM/dx = V along the beam."""
import numpy as np


def vm_simply_supported(L, points=(), q=0.0, n=6001):
    """Pin at 0, roller at L. Point loads (a, P) downward, uniform load q downward. Returns x, V, M."""
    x = np.linspace(0, L, n)
    Rb = (sum(P * a for a, P in points) + q * L * L / 2) / L
    Ra = sum(P for _, P in points) + q * L - Rb
    # equilibrium of the part to the left of a cut at x
    V = Ra - q * x - sum(P * (x > a) for a, P in points)
    M = Ra * x - q * x**2 / 2 - sum(P * np.maximum(x - a, 0) for a, P in points)
    return x, V, M, Ra, Rb


def vm_cantilever(L, P_tip=0.0, q=0.0, n=6001):
    """Clamped at x = 0, free at L. Closed form from equilibrium of the part to the right of x."""
    x = np.linspace(0, L, n)
    V = P_tip + q * (L - x)
    M = -P_tip * (L - x) - q * (L - x) ** 2 / 2
    return x, V, M


if __name__ == "__main__":
    L, P = 6.0, 30.0
    x, V, M, Ra, Rb = vm_simply_supported(L, points=[(L / 2, P)])
    print(f"central point load: Ra = {Ra:.1f}, Rb = {Rb:.1f} kN, M_max = {M.max():.4f} kN m (PL/4 = {P * L / 4:.1f})")
    assert abs(M.max() - P * L / 4) < 1e-6 and abs(M[-1]) < 1e-6        # M returns to zero at the roller
    q = 8.0
    x, V, M, Ra, Rb = vm_simply_supported(L, q=q)
    print(f"uniform load: M_max = {M.max():.4f} kN m at x = {x[M.argmax()]:.3f} m (qL^2/8 = {q * L * L / 8:.1f})")
    assert abs(M.max() - q * L * L / 8) < 1e-6
    # the slope of M is V (dM/dx = V) and the slope of V is -q: check both by finite differences
    assert np.allclose(np.gradient(M, x, edge_order=2), V, atol=1e-8) and np.allclose(np.gradient(V, x, edge_order=2), -q, atol=1e-8)
    # and integrating V from 0 gives M back (trapezoid rule)
    Mi = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum((V[1:] + V[:-1]) / 2 * np.diff(x))])
    assert np.allclose(Mi, M, atol=1e-9)
    # footbridge: 20 kN at 2 m plus 5 kN/m: where is the largest moment? where V crosses zero
    x, V, M, Ra, Rb = vm_simply_supported(L, points=[(2.0, 20.0)], q=5.0)
    k = M.argmax()
    print(f"mixed loads: Ra = {Ra:.4f}, Rb = {Rb:.4f} kN, M_max = {M[k]:.4f} kN m at x = {x[k]:.4f} m (V changes sign there)")
    assert abs(Rb - (20 * 2 + 5 * 18) / 6) < 1e-12 and V[k - 2] > 0 > V[k + 2]
    # cantilever balcony: 2 kN/m plus 1.5 kN at the tip, 1.8 m long
    x, V, M = vm_cantilever(1.8, P_tip=1.5, q=2.0)
    print(f"cantilever: root shear {V[0]:.2f} kN, root moment {M[0]:.3f} kN m")
    assert abs(M[0] + (1.5 * 1.8 + 2 * 1.8 ** 2 / 2)) < 1e-12
```

:::exercise A balcony cantilever
A balcony beam sticks out 1.8 m from a wall. It carries 2 kN/m (deck and people) and a 1.5 kN planter at the tip. Find the shear and moment at the wall.
---solution---
1. Take the part to the right of a cut at the wall. Shear: $1.5 + 2\cdot1.8 = 5.1$ kN.
2. Moment: $-(1.5\cdot1.8 + 2\cdot1.8^2/2) = -(2.7 + 3.24) = -5.94$ kN m. It is negative (hogging): the top of the beam is in tension, so a concrete balcony needs its steel near the top.
:::
