Switzerland stores a large share of its electricity as water behind alpine dams. The force that water puts on a dam wall or a gate follows from statics alone, because still water can't carry shear: it only pushes, perpendicular to every surface, with a pressure that grows with depth. This lesson computes the force on submerged plane surfaces and where it acts.

## Pressure in still water

Take a small vertical column of water. Its weight must be carried by the pressure difference between its bottom and top, so

$$
\frac{dp}{dz} = -\rho g \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p = \rho g h ,
$$

where $h$ is the depth below the free surface (gauge pressure, ignoring the air above). At 50 m that's about 490 kPa, nearly five atmospheres. Pressure at a point is the same in every direction.

## Force on a plane surface

For a flat gate of area $A$, the total force is the integral of the pressure, which comes out as **the pressure at the centroid times the area**:

$$
F = \int p\,dA = \rho g\,h_c\,A .
$$

It doesn't act at the centroid, though. Pressure is larger lower down, so the **centre of pressure** sits below the centroid. Measured along the plane of the gate (slant distance $s$ from the surface line),

$$
s_{cp} = s_c + \frac{I_c}{s_c\,A},
$$

with $I_c$ the second moment of area of the gate about its own horizontal centroidal axis ($bL^3/12$ for a rectangle). For a vertical rectangle reaching up to the surface, this gives the classic result: $F = \rho g b h^2/2$, acting at $2h/3$ below the surface. For a deep gate, $I_c/(s_cA)$ is tiny and the centre of pressure almost coincides with the centroid.

:::widget dam
A gravity dam section. Raise the reservoir. The amber triangle is the pressure distribution; the lime arrow is its resultant, at one third of the water depth above the base. The force grows with $h^2$ and its overturning moment with $h^3$.
:::

:::example Gates and a dam
1. Vertical gate, 3 m wide, from the surface to 4 m deep: $F = 1000\cdot9.81\cdot3\cdot4^2/2 = 235.4$ kN at $2\cdot4/3 = 2.667$ m depth.
2. Bottom outlet gate, 2 m wide and 3 m tall, centred 50 m deep: $F = 1000\cdot9.81\cdot50\cdot6 = 2.943$ MN. $I_c = 2\cdot3^3/12 = 4.5$ m⁴, so the centre of pressure is $4.5/(50\cdot6) = 0.015$ m below the centroid.
3. Inclined gate at 60°, 2 m wide, slant length 5 m from the surface: $F = 1000\cdot9.81\cdot(2.5\sin60°)\cdot10 = 212.4$ kN at slant distance 3.333 m.
4. A 60 m deep reservoir pushes $\rho g h^2/2 = 17.66$ MN on each metre of dam, 20 m above the base: an overturning moment of 353 MN m per metre that the dam's weight must resist. The code checks each closed form by integrating the pressure.
:::

```python file=hydrostatics.py
"""Hydrostatic force on a plane gate and its centre of pressure: closed form against direct integration."""
import math
import numpy as np

RHO, G = 1000.0, 9.81


def gate_force(b, s1, s2, alpha_deg=90.0):
    """Rectangular gate of width b, from slant distance s1 to s2 below the surface along a plane
    inclined at alpha to the horizontal. Returns (F in N, slant position of the centre of pressure)."""
    sa = math.sin(math.radians(alpha_deg))
    L = s2 - s1; A = b * L; sc = (s1 + s2) / 2
    F = RHO * G * (sc * sa) * A                              # pressure at the centroid times the area
    I = b * L**3 / 12
    return F, sc + I / (sc * A)                              # the centre of pressure lies below the centroid


def gate_force_numeric(b, s1, s2, alpha_deg=90.0, n=20001):
    sa = math.sin(math.radians(alpha_deg)); s = np.linspace(s1, s2, n); p = RHO * G * s * sa
    w = np.ones(n); w[1:-1:2] = 4; w[2:-1:2] = 2; w *= (s2 - s1) / (n - 1) / 3
    F = b * (w @ p); return F, b * (w @ (p * s)) / F


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # vertical gate from the surface to depth h: F = rho g b h^2 / 2 acting at 2h/3
    b, h = 3.0, 4.0
    F, scp = gate_force(b, 0, h)
    print(f"surface gate {b} m wide, {h} m deep: F = {F / 1e3:.2f} kN at depth {scp:.4f} m (2h/3 = {2 * h / 3:.4f})")
    assert abs(scp - 2 * h / 3) < 1e-12 and abs(F - RHO * G * b * h**2 / 2) < 1e-6
    # bottom outlet gate of an alpine dam: 2 m wide, 3 m tall, top edge 48.5 m below the surface
    F, scp = gate_force(2.0, 48.5, 51.5)
    Fn, sn = gate_force_numeric(2.0, 48.5, 51.5)
    print(f"outlet gate: F = {F / 1e6:.4f} MN, centre of pressure {scp:.4f} m deep (centroid 50 m): numeric {Fn / 1e6:.4f} MN at {sn:.4f} m")
    assert abs(F - Fn) < 1e-6 * F and abs(scp - sn) < 1e-9
    # inclined gate at 60 deg, slant 0 to 5 m
    F, scp = gate_force(2.0, 0, 5, 60)
    Fn, sn = gate_force_numeric(2.0, 0, 5, 60)
    print(f"inclined gate: F = {F / 1e3:.3f} kN at slant {scp:.4f} m (numeric {sn:.4f})")
    assert abs(F - Fn) < 1e-6 * F and abs(scp - sn) < 1e-9
    # overturning of a 1 m strip of a 60 m dam: resultant rho g h^2 / 2 at h/3 above the base
    H = 60.0
    P = RHO * G * H**2 / 2
    print(f"dam (per metre of crest): water push {P / 1e6:.3f} MN acting {H / 3:.0f} m above the base, overturning moment {P * H / 3 / 1e6:.2f} MN m")
```

:::exercise A sluice gate
A vertical sluice gate 1.5 m wide and 2 m tall has its top edge 1 m below the water surface. Find the force and the depth of the centre of pressure.
---solution---
1. Centroid depth $h_c = 1 + 1 = 2$ m, area 3 m². $F = 1000\cdot9.81\cdot2\cdot3 = 58.86$ kN.
2. $I_c = 1.5\cdot2^3/12 = 1$ m⁴. $s_{cp} = 2 + 1/(2\cdot3) = 2.167$ m deep.
3. The hinge or seal design uses this point: a hinge placed exactly at the centre of pressure would leave the gate balanced.
:::
