With a clean free-body diagram, equilibrium is three equations in the plane and six in space. This lesson turns them into a solver for beams with any mix of point loads, distributed loads and couples. It also shows a 3D case, a hung platform, where the same idea gives three useful equations out of six.

## The equations

A rigid body is in equilibrium when the resultant force and the resultant moment both vanish:

$$
\sum \mathbf F = \mathbf 0, \qquad \sum \mathbf M_O = \mathbf 0 .
$$

In the plane that is $\sum F_x = \sum F_y = \sum M_O = 0$. You can swap a force equation for a second moment equation about a different point, as long as the line between the two points isn't perpendicular to the remaining force direction. Picking moment points at unknown reactions is the classic trick: each moment equation then has only one unknown.

## Beams

For a beam on a pin at $a$ and a roller at $b$, with vertical loads only, the horizontal pin reaction is zero and

$$
R_b = \frac{\sum P_i (x_i - a) - \sum M_j}{b - a}, \qquad R_a = \sum P_i - R_b ,
$$

with distributed loads replaced by their resultants (05.03) and couples $M_j$ counter-clockwise positive. Check the answer with a moment equation about the *other* support: if it doesn't balance, something is wrong.

## In 3D

A body in space has six equations: three forces, three moments. Special load cases need fewer. A horizontal platform hung from vertical cables only has $\sum F_z$, $\sum M_x$ and $\sum M_y$ left; the other three are automatically zero. So three cables are determinate, and a fourth would be one too many.

:::widget beamload
A footbridge beam: pin at 0, roller at 6 m, overhang to 8 m. Move the load and change its size, add a tip load and switch the uniform load on and off. The reactions update live; push enough load onto the overhang and the pin has to pull down.
:::

:::example Overhanging footbridge beam
Pin at 0, roller at 6 m, beam to 8 m. Loads: 20 kN at 2 m, 5 kN/m over 0 to 6 m, 10 kN at the tip.

1. The UDL becomes 30 kN at 3 m.
2. $\sum M_A = 0$: $6R_b = 20\cdot2 + 30\cdot3 + 10\cdot8 = 210$, so $R_b = 35$ kN.
3. $\sum F_y = 0$: $R_a = 20 + 30 + 10 - 35 = 25$ kN.
4. Check, moments about B: $-25\cdot6 + 20\cdot4 + 30\cdot3 - 10\cdot2 = 0$. Correct.
5. A lone 15 kN m couple on a 5 m span gives $R_a = 3$ kN up and $R_b = 3$ kN down, wherever the couple sits.
:::

```python file=beam_reactions.py
"""Support reactions of a pin-roller beam with point loads, distributed loads and couples, plus a 3D platform."""
import numpy as np


def beam_reactions(a, b, points=(), udls=(), couples=()):
    """Pin at x = a, roller at x = b. Loads positive downward (kN), couples counter-clockwise (kN m).
    points: (x, P); udls: (x1, x2, q); couples: (x, M). Returns (Ra, Rb), both positive upward."""
    loads = list(points) + [((x1 + x2) / 2, q * (x2 - x1)) for x1, x2, q in udls]   # each UDL becomes its resultant
    # unknowns [Ra, Rb]: sum Fy = 0 and sum M about the pin = 0 (horizontal reaction is zero for vertical loads)
    A = np.array([[1.0, 1.0], [0.0, b - a]])
    rhs = np.array([sum(P for _, P in loads), sum(P * (x - a) for x, P in loads) - sum(M for _, M in couples)])
    return np.linalg.solve(A, rhs)


def platform(cables, weights):
    """Rigid horizontal platform hung from three vertical cables at (x, y); weights (x, y, W). Returns tensions."""
    A = np.array([[1, 1, 1], [y for _, y in cables], [x for x, _ in cables]], float)
    rhs = np.array([sum(W for *_, W in weights), sum(W * y for _, y, W in weights), sum(W * x for x, _, W in weights)])
    return np.linalg.solve(A, rhs)       # rows: sum Fz = 0, sum Mx = 0, sum My = 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # footbridge beam: pin at 0, roller at 6 m, overhang to 8 m
    Ra, Rb = beam_reactions(0, 6, points=[(2, 20), (8, 10)], udls=[(0, 6, 5)])
    print(f"overhanging beam: Ra = {Ra:.4f} kN, Rb = {Rb:.4f} kN")
    # hand: moments about A: Rb*6 = 20*2 + 30*3 + 10*8 = 210 -> Rb = 35, Ra = 60 - 35 = 25
    assert abs(Rb - 35) < 1e-12 and abs(Ra - 25) < 1e-12
    # check sum of moments about the roller too
    assert abs(-Ra * 6 + 20 * 4 + 30 * 3 - 10 * 2) < 1e-12
    # a couple only: reactions form an equal and opposite couple
    Ra2, Rb2 = beam_reactions(0, 5, couples=[(2.0, 15.0)])
    print(f"15 kN m couple on a 5 m span: Ra = {Ra2:.1f}, Rb = {Rb2:.1f} kN (position of the couple doesn't matter)")
    assert abs(Ra2 - 3) < 1e-12 and abs(Rb2 + 3) < 1e-12
    # 3D: a 2 m x 1.2 m stage platform on three cables
    cables = [(0, 0), (2, 0), (1, 1.2)]
    T = platform(cables, [(1.0, 0.6, 3.0), (1.6, 0.3, 1.5)])
    print(f"stage platform cable tensions: {np.round(T, 4)} kN")
    assert abs(T.sum() - 4.5) < 1e-12
    assert abs(T @ [y for _, y in cables] - (3 * 0.6 + 1.5 * 0.3)) < 1e-12
```

:::exercise A stage platform on three cables
A 2 m × 1.2 m platform hangs from cables at $(0, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(1, 1.2)$ m. It carries 3 kN at its centre $(1, 0.6)$ and 1.5 kN of equipment at $(1.6, 0.3)$. Find the cable tensions.
---solution---
1. $\sum F_z$: $T_1 + T_2 + T_3 = 4.5$.
2. $\sum M_x$ (moments of $y$): $1.2\,T_3 = 3\cdot0.6 + 1.5\cdot0.3 = 2.25$, so $T_3 = 1.875$ kN.
3. $\sum M_y$ (moments of $x$): $2T_2 + 1\cdot T_3 = 3\cdot1 + 1.5\cdot1.6 = 5.4$, so $T_2 = 1.7625$ kN.
4. $T_1 = 4.5 - 1.875 - 1.7625 = 0.8625$ kN. All positive, so no cable goes slack.
:::
