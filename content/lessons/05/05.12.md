Pliers, bike brakes, excavator arms and car suspensions are not trusses: their members are bent or carry loads along their length, so member forces aren't along the members. These are **frames** (built to stand still) and **machines** (built to transmit force). You analyse them by taking them apart and drawing an FBD of each piece.

## Taking a frame apart

1. Look at the whole structure first. Its FBD often gives some support reactions directly.
2. Separate the members at their pins. At each internal pin, draw the force pair as two unknown components, equal and opposite on the two members (Newton's third law).
3. Spot any two-force members (straight or not, loaded only at two pins). Their force is along the line joining the pins, which saves an unknown.
4. Write three equations per member and solve.

A cable or chain between two bodies acts the same way: one tension, pulling both ends.

## Mechanical advantage

A machine turns an input force into a larger (or faster) output. For a lever pivoted at $O$ with input $F_{in}$ at distance $a$ and output at distance $b$, moments about $O$ give

$$
F_{out} = F_{in}\,\frac{a}{b}.
$$

Chain several levers and the advantages multiply. Energy is conserved, though: the output moves $a/b$ times less. That trade appears again in the virtual work method (05.19).

:::widget brake
A rim brake: hand lever, cable, brake arm, pad. Squeeze harder, move the cable anchor on the lever and move the pad on the arm. The mechanical advantage is the product of the two arm ratios.
:::

:::example A bicycle rim brake
Finger force 60 N at 70 mm from the lever pivot; cable anchor 25 mm from the pivot. On the caliper, the cable pulls 100 mm from the arm pivot; the pad sits 40 mm from it.

1. Lever, moments about its pivot: $T\cdot25 = 60\cdot70$, so $T = 168$ N. The pivot carries $168 - 60 = 108$ N.
2. Arm, moments about its pivot: $N\cdot40 = 168\cdot100$, so $N = 420$ N. Its pivot carries $420 - 168 = 252$ N.
3. Overall: $420/60 = 7 = (70/25)(100/40)$.
4. Two pads with $\mu = 0.6$ give $2\cdot0.6\cdot420 = 504$ N of friction on the rim.
5. If the cable pulls 20° off perpendicular, its lever arm shrinks by $\cos20°$ and the tension rises to 178.8 N for the same squeeze.
:::

```python file=brake.py
"""A bicycle rim brake as a frame: two bodies (hand lever, brake arm) linked by the cable; solve each FBD."""
import math
import numpy as np


def solve_body(pivot, known, unknown_dirs):
    """Equilibrium of one body. pivot: point of the pin (2 unknowns); known: list of (point, F);
    unknown_dirs: list of (point, unit direction) for extra unknown force magnitudes. Returns [Rx, Ry, *magnitudes]."""
    cols = [((pivot), (1.0, 0.0)), ((pivot), (0.0, 1.0))] + list(unknown_dirs)
    A = np.array([[d[0] for _, d in cols], [d[1] for _, d in cols], [p[0] * d[1] - p[1] * d[0] for p, d in cols]])
    b = -np.array([sum(F[0] for _, F in known), sum(F[1] for _, F in known), sum(p[0] * F[1] - p[1] * F[0] for p, F in known)])
    return np.linalg.solve(A, b)


def unit(deg):
    return (math.cos(math.radians(deg)), math.sin(math.radians(deg)))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    F_hand = 60.0                      # newtons, one finger squeeze
    # hand lever: pivot at origin, finger force at 70 mm (perpendicular to the lever), cable anchor at 25 mm
    lever = solve_body((0, 0), [((0.070, 0), (0, -F_hand))], [((0.025, 0), (0, 1))])   # cable pulls along +y here
    T = lever[2]
    print(f"lever: pivot reaction ({lever[0]:.2f}, {lever[1]:.2f}) N, cable tension {T:.2f} N (ratio {T / F_hand:.3f} = 70/25)")
    assert abs(T / F_hand - 70 / 25) < 1e-12
    # brake arm: pivot at origin, cable pulls at 100 mm above the pivot horizontally, pad 40 mm above the pivot pushes back
    arm = solve_body((0, 0), [((0, 0.100), (-T, 0))], [((0, 0.040), (1, 0))])
    N = arm[2]
    print(f"brake arm: pivot reaction ({arm[0]:.2f}, {arm[1]:.2f}) N, pad force on the arm {N:.2f} N")
    assert abs(N / T - 100 / 40) < 1e-12
    print(f"overall mechanical advantage {N / F_hand:.2f} (= 70/25 x 100/40 = {70 / 25 * 100 / 40:.2f})")
    # braking force on the rim: two pads, rubber on aluminium mu ~ 0.6 (dry)
    mu = 0.6
    print(f"rim friction from two pads: {2 * mu * N:.1f} N")
    # a cable that pulls at 20 degrees off perpendicular loses a cos factor
    lever2 = solve_body((0, 0), [((0.070, 0), (0, -F_hand))], [((0.025, 0), unit(70))])
    print(f"cable at 20 deg off perpendicular: tension {lever2[2]:.2f} N (= {T:.2f} / cos 20)")
    assert abs(lever2[2] - T / math.cos(math.radians(20))) < 1e-9
```

:::exercise Pliers
Pliers have handles 110 mm long from the pivot and jaws 22 mm from the pivot. You squeeze each handle with 150 N at its end. What is the gripping force, and what force does the pivot pin carry?
---solution---
1. One half of the pliers, moments about the pin: $F_{jaw}\cdot22 = 150\cdot110$, so $F_{jaw} = 750$ N.
2. Force balance on that half (the hand and jaw forces are parallel): the pin carries $750 + 150 = 900$ N.
3. Mechanical advantage 5. The pin carries more than either the grip or the squeeze, which is why it is the part that wears.
:::
