Look at an old railway bridge, a roof over a sports hall or a crane boom and you'll see triangles. A truss is a set of straight bars joined at their ends so that every bar only pulls or pushes along its own axis. That makes trusses light, stiff and easy to analyse. This lesson builds a solver that handles any plane truss and shows what happens when you remove a member.

## Two-force members

An ideal truss assumes pinned joints, loads only at joints and weightless bars. Each bar then has forces at its two ends only, and equilibrium forces them to be equal, opposite and **along the bar**. One number per bar describes it: the axial force $N$, positive in tension.

## The method of joints

Every joint is a particle, so it gives two equations, $\sum F_x = \sum F_y = 0$. A bar from joint $i$ to joint $j$ with unit vector $\mathbf e_{ij}$ pulls joint $i$ with $N\,\mathbf e_{ij}$ when in tension. With $j$ joints, $m$ bars and $r$ reactions,

$$
2j = m + r
$$

is the counting condition for a determinate truss (and the rank must also be full). Built from triangles, a simple truss has $m = 2j - 3$ bars, which with 3 reactions is exactly right. By hand you start at a joint with only two unknowns and walk across the truss. By computer you assemble all $2j$ equations and solve them at once.

**Zero-force members** are worth spotting by eye. At an unloaded joint where two bars are collinear and a third isn't, the third carries nothing. They aren't useless: they brace long compression bars against buckling and carry other load cases.

:::example A three-panel Warren truss
Equilateral triangles with 4 m sides, pin at joint 0, roller at joint 3, 10 kN at each of the two inner bottom joints. 7 joints, 11 bars, 3 reactions: $14 = 11 + 3$.

1. By symmetry, both reactions are 10 kN up.
2. Joint 0: the diagonal 0–4 at 60° has $N\sin60° + 10 = 0$, so $N_{04} = -11.547$ kN (compression).
3. $N_{01} + N_{04}\cos60° = 0$, so $N_{01} = 5.774$ kN (tension).
4. The full solve also gives the middle bottom chord at 11.547 kN tension, the top chord at 11.547 kN compression, and zero force in the two middle diagonals: with symmetric loads, no shear crosses the middle panel.
5. Delete diagonal 4–1 and the joint equations become singular: a mechanism.
:::

```python file=truss_joints.py
"""Plane truss by the method of joints: assemble 2 equations per joint and solve for all member forces at once."""
import math
import numpy as np


def solve_truss(nodes, members, supports, loads):
    """nodes: (x, y); members: (i, j); supports: (node, (dx, dy)) unit reaction directions;
    loads: {node: (Fx, Fy)}. Returns member forces (tension positive) and reactions."""
    nodes = np.asarray(nodes, float)
    n, m, r = len(nodes), len(members), len(supports)
    A = np.zeros((2 * n, m + r)); b = np.zeros(2 * n)
    for k, (i, j) in enumerate(members):
        e = (nodes[j] - nodes[i]) / np.linalg.norm(nodes[j] - nodes[i])
        A[2 * i:2 * i + 2, k] += e          # a tension member pulls joint i towards j
        A[2 * j:2 * j + 2, k] -= e
    for k, (node, d) in enumerate(supports):
        A[2 * node:2 * node + 2, m + k] += d
    for node, F in loads.items():
        b[2 * node:2 * node + 2] -= F
    if np.linalg.matrix_rank(A) < m + r or 2 * n != m + r:
        raise ValueError("not a determinate truss (mechanism or redundant members)")
    x = np.linalg.solve(A, b)
    return x[:m], x[m:]


def warren(panels=3, a=4.0, P=10.0):
    """Warren truss of equilateral triangles: bottom chord nodes 0..panels, top nodes above the mid-points."""
    h = a * math.sin(math.radians(60))
    bot = [(i * a, 0.0) for i in range(panels + 1)]
    top = [((i + 0.5) * a, h) for i in range(panels)]
    nodes = bot + top
    nb = panels + 1
    members = [(i, i + 1) for i in range(panels)]                          # bottom chord
    members += [(nb + i, nb + i + 1) for i in range(panels - 1)]           # top chord
    for i in range(panels):
        members += [(i, nb + i), (nb + i, i + 1)]                          # diagonals
    supports = [(0, (1, 0)), (0, (0, 1)), (panels, (0, 1))]               # pin left, roller right
    loads = {i: (0.0, -P) for i in range(1, panels)}
    return nodes, members, supports, loads


if __name__ == "__main__":
    nodes, members, supports, loads = warren()
    F, R = solve_truss(nodes, members, supports, loads)
    for (i, j), f in zip(members, F):
        print(f"member {i}-{j}: {f:8.4f} kN {'tension' if f > 1e-9 else 'compression' if f < -1e-9 else 'zero'}")
    print("reactions", np.round(R, 4))
    # hand solution at joint 0: Ay = 10, diagonal 0-4 at 60 deg: F sin60 + 10 = 0, bottom: F01 + F cos60 = 0
    k04 = members.index((0, 4)); k01 = members.index((0, 1))
    assert abs(F[k04] + 10 / math.sin(math.radians(60))) < 1e-9 and abs(F[k01] - 10 / math.tan(math.radians(60))) < 1e-9
    assert np.allclose(R, [0, 10, 10])
    assert len(members) == 2 * len(nodes) - 3
    # break the bridge: delete a diagonal and the truss becomes a mechanism
    try:
        solve_truss(nodes, [mb for mb in members if mb != (4, 1)], supports, loads)
        raise AssertionError("should have failed")
    except ValueError as err:
        print("without member 4-1:", err)
```

:::exercise Joint 1 by hand
At joint 1 (bottom, carrying 10 kN), check the code's diagonal forces from the two joint equations, using $N_{01} = 5.774$ kN and $N_{12} = 11.547$ kN.
---solution---
1. Bars at joint 1: 1–0 (pointing left), 1–2 (right), 1–4 (up-left at 120°), 1–5 (up-right at 60°).
2. $\sum F_y$: $N_{14}\sin60° + N_{15}\sin60° - 10 = 0$.
3. $\sum F_x$: $-5.774 + 11.547 - 0.5N_{14} + 0.5N_{15} = 0$.
4. From these, $N_{14} + N_{15} = 11.547$ and $N_{14} - N_{15} = 11.547$, so $N_{14} = 11.547$ kN (tension) and $N_{15} = 0$. That matches the solver.
:::
