Real loads are messy: wind spread up a scaffold, people spread along a footbridge, soil pressing on a wall. For finding support reactions you can replace any load system with a much simpler one that has the same resultant force and the same moment about every point. This lesson shows how to make that swap and when it's legitimate.

## Force–couple systems

A force $\mathbf F$ at point $A$ can be moved to any point $B$ if you add a couple equal to the moment it had about $B$:

$$
\mathbf F \text{ at } A \;\equiv\; \mathbf F \text{ at } B \;+\; \mathbf M_B = \mathbf r_{BA}\times\mathbf F .
$$

Doing this for every force reduces any system to one resultant $\mathbf R = \sum\mathbf F_i$ at a chosen point plus one couple $\mathbf M_O = \sum \mathbf r_i\times\mathbf F_i$. Two systems are **equivalent** when both quantities agree. In the plane (and whenever $\mathbf R\perp\mathbf M_O$) you can go one step further: slide $\mathbf R$ sideways until its own moment equals $\mathbf M_O$, and the couple disappears. For parallel loads along a beam, that line of action is

$$
\bar x = \frac{\sum x_iF_i}{\sum F_i}.
$$

## Distributed loads

A line load $q(x)$ (kN/m) is the continuous version of the same sum:

$$
R = \int_a^b q(x)\,dx, \qquad \bar x = \frac{1}{R}\int_a^b x\,q(x)\,dx .
$$

The resultant is the **area** under the load curve and it acts through the **centroid** of that area. Two shapes cover most cases: a uniform load $q$ over $L$ gives $qL$ at mid-span, and a triangle rising to $q$ gives $qL/2$ at $L/3$ from the high end. A trapezoid is a rectangle plus a triangle.

**A warning.** The equivalent load gives the right reactions for a rigid body. It doesn't give the right internal forces or deflections: a beam carrying a spread load bends differently from one with a single point load. You'll see the difference in lesson 05.13.

:::example Footbridge deck and scaffold wind
1. Trapezoid from 2 to 5 kN/m over 6 m: rectangle $2\cdot6 = 12$ kN at 3 m plus triangle $3\cdot6/2 = 9$ kN at 4 m. $R = 21$ kN at $\bar x = (12\cdot3 + 9\cdot4)/21 = 3.4286$ m.
2. Wind on a 12 m scaffold, $q = 0.9\,(z/12)^{2/7}$ kN/m: $R = 0.9\cdot12\cdot\tfrac{7}{9} = 8.4$ kN acting $\tfrac{9}{16}\cdot12 = 6.75$ m up. The code gets both by Simpson's rule.
:::

```python file=equivalent_loads.py
"""Reduce a distributed load to one resultant force at one location, by numerical integration."""
import numpy as np


def reduce_load(q, a, b, n=20001):
    """Resultant R = int q dx and its line of action x_bar = int x q dx / R (Simpson's rule)."""
    x = np.linspace(a, b, n); y = q(x); h = (b - a) / (n - 1)
    w = np.ones(n); w[1:-1:2] = 4; w[2:-1:2] = 2; w *= h / 3
    R = w @ y; M = w @ (x * y)
    return R, M / R


def reduce_system(forces, about=0.0):
    """Point loads (x, F) in 2D: equivalent force and moment at a chosen point, then the single-force position."""
    R = sum(F for _, F in forces); M = sum(F * (x - about) for x, F in forces)
    return R, M, about + M / R


if __name__ == "__main__":
    L = 6.0
    # triangular load rising from 0 to 4 kN/m: resultant w L / 2 at 2L/3 from the zero end, i.e. L/3 from the peak
    R, xb = reduce_load(lambda x: 4.0 * x / L, 0, L)
    print(f"triangle: R = {R:.6f} kN at x = {xb:.6f} m (L/3 from the high end = {L - L / 3:.1f} m)")
    assert abs(R - 12) < 1e-9 and abs(xb - 2 * L / 3) < 1e-9
    # trapezoid on a footbridge deck: 2 kN/m to 5 kN/m over 6 m = rectangle + triangle
    R, xb = reduce_load(lambda x: 2 + 3 * x / L, 0, L)
    R_hand = 2 * L + 3 * L / 2; x_hand = (2 * L * L / 2 + 3 * L / 2 * 2 * L / 3) / R_hand
    print(f"trapezoid: R = {R:.4f} kN at x = {xb:.4f} m (hand: {R_hand:.1f} kN at {x_hand:.4f} m)")
    assert abs(R - R_hand) < 1e-9 and abs(xb - x_hand) < 1e-9
    # wind on a bamboo scaffold: q = q0 (z / H)^(2/7) power-law profile, H = 12 m, q0 = 0.9 kN/m
    H, q0 = 12.0, 0.9
    R, zb = reduce_load(lambda z: q0 * (z / H) ** (2 / 7), 0, H)
    print(f"wind on scaffold: R = {R:.4f} kN acting {zb:.4f} m up (exact {q0 * H * 7 / 9:.4f} kN at {H * 9 / 16:.4f} m)")
    assert abs(R - q0 * H * 7 / 9) < 1e-4 and abs(zb - 9 * H / 16) < 1e-4    # z^(2/7) has an infinite slope at z = 0, so Simpson converges slowly there
    # several point loads on a beam: one force and its line of action
    R, M0, x = reduce_system([(1.0, 10.0), (2.5, 4.0), (4.0, -3.0), (5.0, 8.0)])
    print(f"point loads: R = {R:.1f} kN, moment about x = 0: {M0:.1f} kN m, single force at x = {x:.4f} m")
```

:::exercise Four point loads
A beam carries 10 kN at 1 m, 4 kN at 2.5 m, an uplift of 3 kN at 4 m and 8 kN at 5 m. Reduce this to a force and couple at $x = 0$, then to a single force.
---solution---
1. $R = 10 + 4 - 3 + 8 = 19$ kN (downward).
2. $M_0 = 10\cdot1 + 4\cdot2.5 - 3\cdot4 + 8\cdot5 = 48$ kN m.
3. Single force: $\bar x = 48/19 = 2.5263$ m. Any support calculation gives the same reactions with this one force as with the four.
:::
