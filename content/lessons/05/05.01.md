Statics begins with one idea: a force is a vector with a point of application. Get that right and everything else in this phase (moments, equilibrium, trusses, friction) is bookkeeping. Get it wrong and a scaffold that "adds up" on paper falls over. This lesson sets up the notation and the code you'll reuse for the rest of the phase.

## What a force is

A force has a **magnitude** (newtons), a **direction** and a **line of action**. In a rigid body you may slide a force along its line of action without changing its effect (the principle of transmissibility), but you may not move it sideways: that changes its turning effect, as the next lesson shows. So a force is a *bound* (or sliding) vector, unlike a free vector such as a displacement.

## Components

In the plane, a force of magnitude $F$ at angle $\theta$ to the $x$ axis has components

$$
F_x = F\cos\theta, \qquad F_y = F\sin\theta, \qquad F = \sqrt{F_x^2 + F_y^2}, \qquad \theta = \operatorname{atan2}(F_y, F_x).
$$

Use `atan2`, not `atan`: it gets the quadrant right. In 3D, a force of magnitude $F$ along a cable from point $A$ to point $B$ is

$$
\mathbf F = F\,\frac{\mathbf r_B - \mathbf r_A}{|\mathbf r_B - \mathbf r_A|},
$$

the magnitude times a unit vector. That one formula handles every guy wire, cable and strut you'll meet.

## The resultant

Forces acting at one point (a **concurrent** system) combine into a single resultant, the vector sum:

$$
\mathbf R = \sum_i \mathbf F_i, \qquad R_x = \sum F_{x,i}, \quad R_y = \sum F_{y,i}.
$$

Graphically, lay the arrows tip to tail; the resultant closes the polygon. If the polygon closes by itself, $\mathbf R = \mathbf 0$ and the point is in equilibrium.

:::

:::example Three cables on a bamboo scaffold pole
Cables pull on a pole head with 2.0 kN at 30°, 3.0 kN at 135° and 1.5 kN at 250°.

1. Components: $(1.7321, 1.0000)$, $(-2.1213, 2.1213)$ and $(-0.5130, -1.4095)$ kN.
2. Sum: $R_x = -0.9023$ kN, $R_y = 1.7118$ kN.
3. $R = \sqrt{0.9023^2 + 1.7118^2} = 1.9350$ kN at $\operatorname{atan2}(1.7118, -0.9023) = 117.79°$.
4. A fourth lashing of 1.9350 kN at $-62.21°$ would balance the head exactly.
:::

```python file=forces.py
"""Forces as vectors: a small Force class, components, and the resultant of a concurrent system."""
import math
import numpy as np


class Force:
    """A bound vector: magnitude and direction plus the point it acts at (needed later for moments)."""

    def __init__(self, fx, fy, fz=0.0, at=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)):
        self.v = np.array([fx, fy, fz], float)
        self.at = np.array(at, float)

    @classmethod
    def polar(cls, magnitude, angle_deg, at=(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)):
        a = math.radians(angle_deg)
        return cls(magnitude * math.cos(a), magnitude * math.sin(a), 0.0, at)

    @classmethod
    def along(cls, magnitude, start, end):
        """A force of given magnitude pulling from start towards end, e.g. a cable."""
        d = np.array(end, float) - np.array(start, float)
        return cls(*(magnitude * d / np.linalg.norm(d)), at=start)

    @property
    def magnitude(self):
        return float(np.linalg.norm(self.v))

    @property
    def angle_deg(self):
        return math.degrees(math.atan2(self.v[1], self.v[0]))

    def __add__(self, other):
        return Force(*(self.v + other.v), at=self.at)


def resultant(forces):
    out = Force(0, 0, 0, at=forces[0].at)
    for f in forces:
        out = out + f
    return out


if __name__ == "__main__":
    # three cables pulling on the head of a scaffold pole (kN, angles from the x axis)
    F = [Force.polar(2.0, 30), Force.polar(3.0, 135), Force.polar(1.5, 250)]
    R = resultant(F)
    print("components (kN):", ", ".join(f"({f.v[0]:.4f}, {f.v[1]:.4f})" for f in F))
    print(f"resultant {R.magnitude:.4f} kN at {R.angle_deg:.2f} deg")
    assert np.allclose(R.v, sum(f.v for f in F))                       # component-wise sum
    assert abs(R.magnitude - math.hypot(*sum(f.v for f in F)[:2])) < 1e-12
    # a 3D guy wire: 12 kN from the top of a 20 m mast to an anchor 8 m east and 6 m north of its base
    T = Force.along(12.0, (0, 0, 20), (8, 6, 0))
    L = math.sqrt(8**2 + 6**2 + 20**2)
    print(f"guy wire length {L:.3f} m, components {np.round(T.v, 4)} kN")
    assert np.allclose(T.v, 12 * np.array([8, 6, -20]) / L) and abs(T.magnitude - 12) < 1e-12
    # a fourth cable that brings the pole head to equilibrium is just -R
    F4 = Force(*(-R.v))
    assert resultant(F + [F4]).magnitude < 1e-12
    print(f"balancing force {F4.magnitude:.4f} kN at {F4.angle_deg:.2f} deg")
```

:::exercise A guy wire in 3D
A 20 m mast is held by a guy wire anchored 8 m east and 6 m north of its base, tensioned to 12 kN. Find the force components on the mast top and the angle the wire makes with the ground.
---solution---
1. Vector from top $(0, 0, 20)$ to anchor $(8, 6, 0)$: $(8, 6, -20)$ m, length $\sqrt{64 + 36 + 400} = 22.361$ m.
2. $\mathbf F = 12\cdot(8, 6, -20)/22.361 = (4.293, 3.220, -10.733)$ kN.
3. Horizontal part $\sqrt{4.293^2 + 3.220^2} = 5.367$ kN, so the angle to the ground is $\arctan(10.733/5.367) = 63.4°$.
:::
