Bali's subak system has shared river water between rice terraces for about a thousand years. A weir in the river feeds a canal, **proportional dividers** split the canal between groups of farmers, and the water then steps down from field to field over small outlet weirs. This capstone models a small network for a year and asks the question every farmer asks in August: does the water reach my field? You can explore the real system in the [Subak lab](https://andreolf.github.io/subak-lab/).

## Weirs

A sharp-crested rectangular weir of width $b$ with head $H$ above the crest passes

$$
Q = \frac{2}{3}\,C_d\,b\,\sqrt{2g}\,H^{3/2},
$$

with $C_d \approx 0.62$. A **proportional divider** puts several notches at the same crest level. They all see the same head, so each passes a flow in proportion to its width, whatever the total:

$$
\frac{Q_i}{\sum_k Q_k} = \frac{b_i}{\sum_k b_k} .
$$

That is why the subak cuts the notch widths in proportion to each group's land: the share stays fair in a flood and in a drought.

## Water balance of one terrace

Each field of area $A$ holds a ponding depth $h$. Over a time step $\Delta t$,

$$
A\,\Delta h = \left(Q_\text{in} - Q_\text{out}\right)\Delta t + P A\,\Delta t - V_\text{ET} - V_\text{perc},
$$

where $Q_\text{out}$ is the outlet weir flow with head $h - h_c$ above the crest $h_c$, $P$ is rain, and evapotranspiration and percolation take about 5 mm and 3 mm a day. The outflow of one terrace is the inflow of the next one down.

The update is written so that every cubic metre is booked exactly once. Each loss is capped at what the field actually holds, so the depth can never go negative. The test is then strict: the ledger must close at **every** terrace to rounding error, and the whole network must close as well.

:::example A year on a 1.9 ha network
Three branches of four terraces with 8400, 6600 and 4200 m² of rice. The outlet crests are 5 cm high and 30 cm wide. The river flow, rain and season are **assumed**, not measured: 60 to 80 L/s in the wet season (November to April) with afternoon showers, and 3 L/s in the dry season. The intake takes half the river, up to 25 L/s.

1. **Divider.** Notches of 200, 157.1 and 100 mm. At 6 cm head they pass 5.382, 4.228 and 2.691 L/s, shares of 0.4375, 0.3438 and 0.2188, exactly the land shares.
2. **Mass balance.** The worst terrace balance error over the year is about $10^{-13}$ of the water that passed through it. The network takes in 438 871 m³ of canal water and rain, and the outflow to the river, the losses and the change in storage add up to the same number.
3. **Dry-season demand.** $8\ \text{mm/day} \times 19\,200\ \text{m}^2$ is 1.778 L/s, and the intake delivers 1.5 L/s, 84 % of the demand.
4. **Proportional split.** The top three terraces of every branch keep standing water all dry season. The tail-end terraces have it only 33 to 35 % of the time, because each field fills to its crest before it passes anything on.
5. **Rotation.** Sending the whole flow to one branch for 5 days at a time spreads the shortage: the worst terrace is still near 35 %, but branch 3's tail-end field rises from 33 % to 65 %. The mean over all fields falls from 0.836 to 0.660, because fields that dry out between turns lose water to re-wetting.

So rotation is fairer to the tail, at a cost in total. In practice the subak's water temple schedule also staggers planting so that not every field needs full water at once, which this model does not include.
:::

```python file=subak_network.py
"""A subak irrigation network: a river intake, a proportional divider weir that splits the canal
into three branches, and chains of terraced fields that spill from one to the next over outlet
weirs. Hourly water balance for a year with assumed seasonal flows, rain, evapotranspiration and
percolation. The mass balance must close at every terrace and for the whole network. numpy only."""
import numpy as np

g = 9.81
def weir(H, b, Cd=0.62):
    """Sharp-crested rectangular weir: Q = (2/3) Cd b sqrt(2g) H^1.5 (H = head over the crest)."""
    return 2 / 3 * Cd * b * np.sqrt(2 * g) * np.clip(H, 0, None) ** 1.5

# proportional divider: three notches at one crest level see the same head, so Q_i ~ b_i
A = np.array([[2400, 2200, 2000, 1800], [1800, 1700, 1600, 1500], [1200, 1100, 1000, 900]], float)   # field areas, m^2
b_div = 0.20 * A.sum(1) / A.sum(1)[0]                    # notch widths in proportion to each branch's land
Q = weir(0.06, b_div)
print(f"notches {np.round(b_div*1e3, 1)} mm; at 6 cm head: {np.round(Q*1e3, 3)} L/s, shares {np.round(Q/Q.sum(), 4)} = land shares {np.round(A.sum(1)/A.sum(), 4)}")
assert np.allclose(Q / Q.sum(), A.sum(1) / A.sum())

nb, nt = 3, 4                                            # branches, terraces per branch
hc, Lo = 0.05, 0.30                                      # field outlet crest 5 cm, 30 cm wide
ET, perc = 5e-3 / 86400, 3e-3 / 86400                    # m/s
dt = 3600.0
hours = np.arange(365 * 24)
day = hours / 24
wet = (day < 120) | (day >= 305)                         # Bali: wet season November to April (day 0 = 1 Jan)
rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
storm = np.zeros(len(hours))
for d in np.where(rng.random(365) < np.where(np.arange(365) < 120, 0.45, np.where(np.arange(365) >= 305, 0.45, 0.08)))[0]:
    storm[d * 24 + 14: d * 24 + 17] = rng.uniform(5, 25) / 3 / 1000 / 3600   # afternoon showers, mm over 3 h -> m/s
Q_river = np.where(wet, 0.060 + 0.02 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * (day - 30) / 365) ** 2, 0.0030)   # assumed river flow at the intake, m^3/s
intake_cap = 0.025

def simulate(rotation=False):
    h = np.zeros((nb, nt)); h[:] = 0.03
    h0 = h.copy()
    led = {k: np.zeros((nb, nt)) for k in ("in", "rain", "out", "et", "perc")}
    wetted = np.zeros((nb, nt)); dry_hours = 0
    for k in hours:
        Qin = min(intake_cap, 0.5 * Q_river[k])          # the intake takes half the river, up to its capacity
        share = b_div / b_div.sum()
        if rotation and not wet[k]:                       # dry-season rotation: the whole flow to one branch for 5 days at a time
            share = np.eye(nb)[int(day[k] // 5) % nb]
        inflow = Qin * share                              # m^3/s into the top terrace of each branch
        for j in range(nt):
            qin = inflow if j == 0 else qout_prev
            qout = np.minimum(weir(h[:, j] - hc, Lo), (h[:, j] - hc).clip(0) * A[:, j] / dt)   # cannot spill more than is above the crest
            vet = np.minimum(ET * A[:, j] * dt, h[:, j] * A[:, j])                            # cannot evaporate more than is there
            vpe = np.where(h[:, j] > 0, np.minimum(perc * A[:, j] * dt, h[:, j] * A[:, j] - vet), 0)
            vr = storm[k] * A[:, j] * dt
            dV = qin * dt + vr - qout * dt - vet - vpe
            h[:, j] += dV / A[:, j]
            for key, val in (("in", qin * dt), ("rain", vr), ("out", qout * dt), ("et", vet), ("perc", vpe)): led[key][:, j] += val
            qout_prev = qout
            if not wet[k]: wetted[:, j] += h[:, j] > 0.01
        dry_hours += not wet[k]
    return h0, h, led, wetted / dry_hours, Qin

for rot in (False, True):
    h0, h, led, frac, _ = simulate(rot)
    dS = (h - h0) * A
    bal = led["in"] + led["rain"] - led["out"] - led["et"] - led["perc"] - dS
    rel = np.abs(bal) / (led["in"] + led["rain"])
    assert rel.max() < 1e-10                                  # mass balance closes at every terrace
    assert np.all(h >= -1e-12)
    for i in range(nb):                                       # and the outflow of one terrace is the inflow of the next
        assert np.allclose(led["out"][i, :-1], led["in"][i, 1:])
    net_in = led["in"][:, 0].sum() + led["rain"].sum()
    net_out = led["out"][:, -1].sum() + led["et"].sum() + led["perc"].sum() + dS.sum()
    print(f"\n{'rotation in the dry season' if rot else 'proportional split all year'}: worst terrace balance error {rel.max():.1e}; network: in {net_in:,.0f} m^3, out + stored {net_out:,.0f} m^3")
    assert abs(net_in - net_out) / net_in < 1e-12
    print("fraction of dry-season hours with standing water (>1 cm), rows = branches, top terrace first:")
    print(np.round(frac, 2))
    if not rot: frac_prop = frac
    else: frac_rot = frac
for name, f in (("proportional", frac_prop), ("rotation", frac_rot)):
    print(f"{name:>12}: mean {f.mean():.3f}, worst terrace {f.min():.3f}, spread (max - min) {f.max() - f.min():.3f}")
assert frac_rot.min() > frac_prop.min() and frac_rot.mean() < frac_prop.mean()   # rotation is fairer to the tail, at a cost in total
print(f"tail-end terrace of branch 3: {frac_prop[2, -1]:.2f} with a proportional split, {frac_rot[2, -1]:.2f} with rotation")
```

:::exercise Fill a field
A dry 2000 m² terrace with a 5 cm outlet crest receives 5 L/s from the canal while losing 8 mm a day. How long does it take before water starts to spill to the next field? And once full, how long would it stay wet if the canal stopped?
---solution---
1. Volume to the crest: $2000 \times 0.05 = 100$ m³.
2. Losses: $0.008 \times 2000 / 86\,400 = 1.85 \times 10^{-4}$ m³/s, so the net inflow is $4.815 \times 10^{-3}$ m³/s.
3. Time to fill: $100 / 4.815 \times 10^{-3} = 20\,770$ s, about 5.77 h.
4. With no inflow the 5 cm lasts $50 / 8 = 6.25$ days, which is why a 5-day rotation keeps most fields wet.
:::
