A village solar grid makes too much power at noon and none in the evening. This capstone stores the surplus by pumping water 50 m up a hill into a tank and runs it back through a turbine after dark. Two things matter: an energy ledger that accounts for every kilowatt-hour, and the pressure surge when a valve in the 300 m penstock closes.

## The energy ledger

Pumping a flow $Q$ against the static head $H_s$ through a pipe with friction head $k_f Q^2$ needs hydraulic power $\rho g Q (H_s + k_f Q^2)$. Generating gives $\rho g Q (H_s - k_f Q^2)$. Every joule of solar surplus ends up in one of: the evening load, the pump and turbine losses, pipe friction, or potential energy in the tank,

$$
E_\text{in} = E_\text{out} + E_\text{pump loss} + E_\text{turbine loss} + E_\text{friction} + \Delta E_p .
$$

The code evaluates the head at the middle of each step's slab of water, $\rho g A\,\Delta z\,(z_0 + z + \Delta z/2)$, so the potential energy of a prism is exact and the ledger closes to rounding error. The round-trip efficiency is

$$
\eta_\text{rt} = \eta_p\,\eta_t\,\frac{H_s - h_{f,t}}{H_s + h_{f,p}} .
$$

## Water hammer

Stopping a flow suddenly sends a pressure wave up the pipe at the wave speed

$$
a = \sqrt{\frac{K/\rho}{1 + K D/(E e)}},
$$

where $K$ is the water's bulk modulus and the pipe wall of thickness $e$ and modulus $E$ stretches a little. The rise for a sudden stop is Joukowsky's $\Delta H = a\,\Delta V/g$. Any closure faster than the round trip $2L/a$ counts as sudden. Slower closures let the reflected wave relieve the valve, and Michaud's estimate is $\Delta H \approx 2LV/(g T_c)$. The **method of characteristics** solves the full wave equations along the lines $dx/dt = \pm a$, with the reservoir and the closing valve as boundaries.

:::widget hammer
Head at the valve after it starts to close, by the method of characteristics. The dashed line is the full Joukowsky rise. Close faster than 2L/a and the valve sees it all.
:::

:::example A day of storage and a valve closure
Assumed: tank floor 50 m above the pond, 2000 m² tank, 300 m steel penstock of 300 mm with $f = 0.015$, pump 78 % and turbine 82 % efficient including their electric machines. Solar surplus up to 40 kW from 08:00 to 16:00, and a 15 kW evening load from 18:00 to 23:00.

1. **Ledger.** 241.978 kWh in. 75.000 kWh out to the village, 53.235 kWh pump losses, 16.463 kWh turbine losses, 1.830 kWh pipe friction, and 95.450 kWh left in the tank. The sum matches the input to $10^{-15}$.
2. **Levels.** The tank rises from 1.000 m to 1.669 m and ends the night at 1.342 m, so the evening demand is fully served and the tank is more than big enough.
3. **Round trip.** At mid level, pumping 30 kW lifts 45.6 L/s with 0.318 m of friction, and generating 15 kW takes 36.0 L/s with 0.198 m. The round-trip efficiency is 0.6333, barely below the machines' 0.6396: in a pipe this size, friction hardly matters.
4. **Wave speed.** A 6 mm steel wall gives $a = 1191.4$ m/s and $2L/a = 0.504$ s.
5. **Joukowsky.** The generating flow moves at 0.509 m/s, so a sudden stop adds $aV/g = 61.85$ m, more than doubling the head. The MOC gives 62.04 m for an instant closure and 61.96 m for a 0.504 s closure.
6. **Slow closure.** Closing over 10 s keeps the rise to 2.00 m, 3 % of the sudden surge. Michaud predicts 3.11 m, so here it is conservative, because with a linear closure the flow falls mostly at the end. The penstock must still be rated for about 114 m, in case a valve is slammed or the power fails and the guide vanes trip.
:::

```python file=pumped_storage.py
"""Small pumped storage for a village solar grid: a day of pumping and generating with an energy
ledger that closes exactly, then water hammer in the penstock by the method of characteristics,
checked against Joukowsky and the critical closure time. numpy only."""
import numpy as np

rho, g = 1000.0, 9.81
Lp, D, f = 300.0, 0.30, 0.015                      # penstock length, diameter, friction factor
Ap = np.pi * D**2 / 4
kf = f * Lp / D / (2 * g * Ap**2)                  # friction head = kf Q^2
z0, Ares, zmax = 50.0, 2000.0, 4.0                 # upper tank floor 50 m above the pond, area, depth
eta_p, eta_t = 0.78, 0.82                          # pump and turbine, each including the motor or generator

def flow_for_power(Ph, Hs, sign):
    """Hydraulic power at the machine Ph = rho g Q (Hs + sign*kf Q^2); sign +1 pumping, -1 generating."""
    Q = Ph / (rho * g * Hs)
    for _ in range(60): Q = Q - (rho * g * Q * (Hs + sign * kf * Q**2) - Ph) / (rho * g * (Hs + 3 * sign * kf * Q**2))
    return Q

dt = 60.0
t = np.arange(0, 24 * 3600, dt)
hr = t / 3600
P_surplus = np.clip(40e3 * np.sin(np.pi * (hr - 7) / 10), 0, None) * ((hr > 8) & (hr < 16))    # solar surplus, W (assumed)
P_demand = np.where((hr >= 18) & (hr < 23), 15e3, 0.0)                                         # evening load, W
z = 1.0                                                                                       # start 1 m deep
led = dict(E_in=0.0, E_out=0.0, pump_loss=0.0, turb_loss=0.0, fric=0.0, dPE=0.0, unserved=0.0, spilled=0.0)
zs = []
for k in range(len(t)):
    if P_surplus[k] > 0 and z < zmax:
        Pe = P_surplus[k]; Ph = eta_p * Pe; Q = 0.0
        for _ in range(30):                                   # head at the middle of this step's slab, so the PE ledger is exact
            dz = Q * dt / Ares; Hs = z0 + z + dz / 2; Q = flow_for_power(Ph, Hs, +1)
        dz = Q * dt / Ares; Hs = z0 + z + dz / 2
        led["E_in"] += Pe * dt; led["pump_loss"] += (Pe - Ph) * dt; led["fric"] += rho * g * kf * Q**3 * dt
        led["dPE"] += rho * g * Ares * dz * Hs; z += dz
    elif P_surplus[k] > 0: led["spilled"] += P_surplus[k] * dt
    if P_demand[k] > 0 and z > 0.05:
        Pe = P_demand[k]; Ph = Pe / eta_t; Q = 0.0
        for _ in range(30):
            dz = Q * dt / Ares; Hs = z0 + z - dz / 2; Q = flow_for_power(Ph, Hs, -1)
        dz = Q * dt / Ares; Hs = z0 + z - dz / 2
        led["E_out"] += Pe * dt; led["turb_loss"] += (Ph - Pe) * dt; led["fric"] += rho * g * kf * Q**3 * dt
        led["dPE"] -= rho * g * Ares * dz * Hs; z -= dz
    elif P_demand[k] > 0: led["unserved"] += P_demand[k] * dt
    zs.append(z)
kWh = {k: v / 3.6e6 for k, v in led.items()}
print("energy ledger, kWh: " + ", ".join(f"{k} {v:.3f}" for k, v in kWh.items()))
lhs = led["E_in"]
rhs = led["E_out"] + led["pump_loss"] + led["turb_loss"] + led["fric"] + led["dPE"]
print(f"in {lhs/3.6e6:.4f} kWh = out + losses + stored {rhs/3.6e6:.4f} kWh; relative error {abs(lhs-rhs)/lhs:.1e}")
assert abs(lhs - rhs) / lhs < 1e-9
zs = np.array(zs)
print(f"tank level: start 1.000 m, max {zs.max():.3f} m, end {zs[-1]:.3f} m; evening demand served {kWh['E_out']:.2f} of 75.00 kWh")
Q_turb = flow_for_power(15e3 / eta_t, z0 + 2.0, -1)
Q_pump = flow_for_power(eta_p * 30e3, z0 + 2.0, +1)
hp, ht = kf * Q_pump**2, kf * Q_turb**2
rt = eta_p * eta_t * (z0 + 2.0 - ht) / (z0 + 2.0 + hp)
print(f"at mid level: pumping 30 kW lifts {Q_pump*1e3:.1f} L/s with {hp:.3f} m friction; generating 15 kW takes {Q_turb*1e3:.1f} L/s with {ht:.3f} m")
print(f"round-trip efficiency {rt:.4f} (machines alone {eta_p*eta_t:.4f})")

# water hammer: reservoir upstream, valve at the powerhouse, steel pipe 6 mm wall
K, Es, e = 2.2e9, 200e9, 0.006
a = np.sqrt(K / rho / (1 + K * D / (Es * e)))
Tcrit = 2 * Lp / a
print(f"\nwave speed {a:.1f} m/s, 2L/a = {Tcrit:.3f} s")

def moc(Q0, Tc, H0=z0 + 2.0, T=8.0, nx=60):
    """Method of characteristics. Valve: Q = Cv tau sqrt(H), tau falls linearly to 0 over Tc."""
    dx = Lp / nx; dtm = dx / a; B = a / (g * Ap); R = f * dx / (2 * g * D * Ap**2)
    H = H0 - kf * Q0**2 * np.linspace(0, 1, nx + 1); Q = np.full(nx + 1, Q0)
    Cv = Q0 / np.sqrt(H[-1]); Hmax = H[-1]; tt = 0.0; Hv = []
    while tt < T:
        tt += dtm
        CP = H[:-1] + B * Q[:-1] - R * Q[:-1] * np.abs(Q[:-1]); CM = H[1:] - B * Q[1:] + R * Q[1:] * np.abs(Q[1:])
        Hn = np.empty_like(H); Qn = np.empty_like(Q)
        Hn[1:-1] = (CP[:-1] + CM[1:]) / 2; Qn[1:-1] = (CP[:-1] - CM[1:]) / (2 * B)
        Hn[0] = H0; Qn[0] = (H0 - CM[0]) / B                                   # reservoir
        tau = max(0.0, 1 - tt / Tc) if Tc > 0 else 0.0
        cv = Cv * tau
        if cv == 0: Qn[-1] = 0.0; Hn[-1] = CP[-1]
        else:                                                                 # Q = cv sqrt(H) with H = CP - B Q
            s = (-B * cv**2 + np.sqrt((B * cv**2) ** 2 + 4 * cv**2 * CP[-1])) / 2; Qn[-1] = s; Hn[-1] = CP[-1] - B * s
        H, Q = Hn, Qn; Hmax = max(Hmax, H[-1]); Hv.append(H[-1])
    return Hmax, np.array(Hv)

V0 = Q_turb / Ap
dHj = a * V0 / g
H_inst, _ = moc(Q_turb, 0.0)
H_valve0 = z0 + 2.0 - kf * Q_turb**2
print(f"pipe velocity {V0:.3f} m/s; Joukowsky rise aV/g = {dHj:.2f} m; MOC instant closure rise {H_inst - H_valve0:.2f} m")
assert abs((H_inst - H_valve0) / dHj - 1) < 0.03
print("closure time s   peak head m   rise m   Michaud 2LV/(g Tc) m")
for Tc in (0.2, Tcrit, 2.0, 5.0, 10.0, 20.0):
    Hm, _ = moc(Q_turb, Tc, T=Tc + 6)
    mich = 2 * Lp * V0 / (g * Tc)
    print(f"   {Tc:6.3f}       {Hm:7.2f}    {Hm-H_valve0:6.2f}    {min(mich, dHj):6.2f}")
    if Tc <= Tcrit: assert abs((Hm - H_valve0) / dHj - 1) < 0.03                # closing faster than 2L/a gives the full Joukowsky rise
    if Tc == 10.0: H10 = Hm - H_valve0
assert H10 < 0.3 * dHj
print(f"a 10 s closure keeps the rise to {H10:.2f} m, {H10/dHj*100:.0f} % of the instant-closure surge")
```

:::exercise HDPE instead of steel
Replace the steel penstock with HDPE of 300 mm and 18 mm wall, $E = 1.0$ GPa. Find the wave speed, $2L/a$ and the Joukowsky rise for $V = 0.509$ m/s. Which pipe is kinder to the valve, and what is the catch?
---solution---
1. The wall term is
$$
\frac{K D}{E e} = \frac{2.2\times10^9 \times 0.3}{1.0\times10^9 \times 0.018} = 36.7 .
$$
2. $a = \sqrt{2.2\times10^6/37.7} = 241.6$ m/s, and $2L/a = 2.48$ s.
3. $\Delta H = 241.6 \times 0.509/9.81 = 12.5$ m, about a fifth of the steel value.
4. HDPE is kinder to the valve, because the flexible wall absorbs the surge. The catch is that it is weaker and creeps, so its pressure rating at 114 m is far harder to meet, and it must be buried or shaded from the sun.
:::
