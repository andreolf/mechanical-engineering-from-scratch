Clouds over a village solar grid can drop the output for half a minute at a time. A steel flywheel can carry the load through: it spins up on surplus and gives the energy back through a motor-generator. This capstone sizes the wheel and then asks the safety question first: how far is it from bursting? Spin one up in the [Spinning flywheel lab](./#lab-flywheel).

## Energy

A disc of radius $b$, thickness $t$ and density $\rho$ has $I = \tfrac12 \rho\pi b^4 t$ and stores $\tfrac12 I\omega^2$. Running between $\omega_\text{max}$ and $\omega_\text{max}/2$ releases 75 % of it. Per kilogram a flat disc stores

$$
\frac{E}{m} = \frac{(\omega b)^2}{4},
$$

so the **rim speed** sets the specific energy, not the size.

## Stresses in a rotating disc

Radial equilibrium of a thin disc in plane stress leads to

$$
\frac{d^2u}{dr^2} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{du}{dr} - \frac{u}{r^2} = -\frac{(1-\nu^2)\rho\omega^2 r}{E},
$$

with $\sigma_r = 0$ at free edges. For a solid disc the closed form peaks at the centre,

$$
\sigma_r = \sigma_\theta = \frac{3+\nu}{8}\rho\omega^2 b^2 .
$$

A **bore**, however small, makes the hoop stress at its edge

$$
\sigma_\theta(a) = \frac{3+\nu}{4}\rho\omega^2\left(b^2 + \frac{1-\nu}{3+\nu}a^2\right),
$$

about **twice** the solid value. All stresses grow as $\omega^2$, so the disc bursts at $\omega_\text{max}\sqrt{\sigma_u/\sigma_\text{max}}$. The code solves the equation by finite differences and checks it against both closed forms.

:::example Size and check the wheel
Assumed: 4340 steel, $\rho = 7850$ kg/m³, $\nu = 0.29$, yield 860 MPa, ultimate 1000 MPa. The wheel must give 10 kW for 30 s, 300 kJ usable, and has a 0.30 m radius.

1. **Stress-limited speed.** Putting half of yield at the centre gives 11 617 rpm, a 365 m/s rim and a disc only 5.4 mm thick weighing 12.0 kg at 33.30 kJ/kg. In air that is a saw blade, not an energy store: drag, balance and the bearings rule it out.
2. **Rim-speed-limited.** At 150 m/s the wheel runs at 4775 rpm. With $I = 300\,000/(0.5 \times 500^2 \times 0.75) = 3.2$ kg m², it is 32.0 mm thick, weighs 71.1 kg and stores 5.625 kJ/kg, exactly $150^2/4$ J/kg.
3. **Centre stress.** Finite differences give 72.637 MPa, the closed form 72.637 MPa. The yield margin is 11.84 and the disc would burst at 3.710 times full speed, 17 716 rpm.
4. **The bore.** With a 40 mm bore the hoop stress at the bore is 145.44 MPa (closed form 145.41), 2.002 times the solid value. The burst speed falls to 2.622 times full speed, 12 520 rpm.
5. **Overspeed.** A governor trip at 1.15 times full speed puts 192.3 MPa at the bore, still 4.47 times below yield.
6. **Containment.** At full speed the wheel holds 400 kJ, about the kinetic energy of a 1 t car at 100 km/h (386 kJ). The margins above are about stress; a real installation still needs a steel or concrete pit, because a crack, a bad weld or a bearing seizure does not read the margin.
:::

```python file=flywheel_store.py
"""Steel flywheel to ride a village solar grid through 30 s cloud dips: size the disc, check the
rotating-disc stresses with a finite-difference solve against the closed form, and find the burst
margin, with and without a shaft bore. numpy only."""
import numpy as np

rho, E, nu = 7850.0, 205e9, 0.29               # 4340 steel, quenched and tempered (assumed properties)
sy, su = 860e6, 1000e6                          # yield and ultimate
P, t_ride = 10e3, 30.0                          # bridge 10 kW for 30 s
E_use = P * t_ride
print(f"usable energy {E_use/1e3:.0f} kJ")

def disc_fd(a, b, w, n=1501):
    """Radial displacement of a rotating disc in plane stress, finite differences.
    u'' + u'/r - u/r^2 = -(1 - nu^2) rho w^2 r / E, with sigma_r = 0 at free edges (and u(0) = 0 if solid)."""
    r = np.linspace(a if a > 0 else 0.0, b, n); h = r[1] - r[0]
    A = np.zeros((n, n)); rhs = -(1 - nu**2) * rho * w**2 * r / E
    for i in range(1, n - 1):
        A[i, i - 1] = 1 / h**2 - 1 / (2 * h * r[i]); A[i, i] = -2 / h**2 - 1 / r[i] ** 2; A[i, i + 1] = 1 / h**2 + 1 / (2 * h * r[i])
    def free(i, sgn):                            # sigma_r ~ u' + nu u / r = 0, second-order one-sided difference
        idx = [i, i + sgn, i + 2 * sgn]; c = np.array([-3, 4, -1]) / (2 * h) * sgn
        A[i, :] = 0; A[i, idx] = c; A[i, i] += nu / r[i]; rhs[i] = 0
    if a > 0: free(0, +1)
    else: A[0, :] = 0; A[0, 0] = 1; rhs[0] = 0
    free(n - 1, -1)
    u = np.linalg.solve(A, rhs)
    du = np.gradient(u, h, edge_order=2)
    with np.errstate(divide="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
        ur = np.where(r > 0, u / np.where(r > 0, r, 1), du)
    sr = E / (1 - nu**2) * (du + nu * ur); st = E / (1 - nu**2) * (ur + nu * du)
    return r, sr, st

def disc_exact(a, b, w, r):
    k = (3 + nu) / 8 * rho * w**2
    if a == 0:
        return k * (b**2 - r**2), k * (b**2 - (1 + 3 * nu) / (3 + nu) * r**2)
    return k * (a**2 + b**2 - a**2 * b**2 / r**2 - r**2), k * (a**2 + b**2 + a**2 * b**2 / r**2 - (1 + 3 * nu) / (3 + nu) * r**2)

# two ways to set the speed of a solid 0.6 m disc that runs between w_max and w_max/2 (75 % of the energy usable)
b = 0.30
def size(w_max, a=0.0):
    I = E_use / (0.5 * w_max**2 * 0.75)
    th = I / (0.5 * rho * np.pi * (b**4 - a**4))
    return th, rho * np.pi * (b**2 - a**2) * th, 0.5 * I * w_max**2
w_stress = np.sqrt((sy / 2) / ((3 + nu) / 8 * rho * b**2))      # centre stress at half of yield
w_rim = 150.0 / b                                                # rim speed 150 m/s: air drag, bearings, balance, a shop that can make it
for name, w in (("stress-limited", w_stress), ("rim-speed-limited", w_rim)):
    th, mass, Ef = size(w)
    print(f"{name:>18}: {w*60/2/np.pi:6.0f} rpm, rim {w*b:5.0f} m/s, {th*1e3:5.1f} mm thick, {mass:5.1f} kg, {Ef/mass/1e3:5.2f} kJ/kg")
    assert np.isclose(Ef / mass, 0.25 * (w * b) ** 2, rtol=1e-9)          # E/m = (rim speed)^2 / 4 for a flat disc
w_max = w_rim
th, mass, E_full = size(w_max)
print(f"choose the rim-speed limit: a 5.4 mm disc spinning at 11 600 rpm in air is a saw blade, not a store")

r, sr, st = disc_fd(0.0, b, w_max)
er, et = disc_exact(0.0, b, w_max, r)
print(f"\ncentre stress: finite differences {st[0]/1e6:.3f} MPa, closed form {et[0]/1e6:.3f} MPa")
assert np.max(np.abs(st - et)) / et[0] < 2e-3 and np.max(np.abs(sr - er)) / et[0] < 2e-3
burst = np.sqrt(su / st.max())                                   # stress grows as w^2
print(f"solid: peak {st.max()/1e6:.2f} MPa, yield margin {sy/st.max():.2f}, burst at {burst:.3f} x full speed ({w_max*burst*60/2/np.pi:.0f} rpm)")

# a 40 mm shaft bore: the hoop stress at the bore is about twice the solid-disc centre stress
a = 0.020
r2, sr2, st2 = disc_fd(a, b, w_max)
er2, et2 = disc_exact(a, b, w_max, r2)
assert np.max(np.abs(st2 - et2)) / et2[0] < 2e-3
burst2 = np.sqrt(su / st2.max())
th2, m2, _ = size(w_max, a)
print(f"with a {2*a*1e3:.0f} mm bore: bore hoop stress {st2[0]/1e6:.2f} MPa (closed form {et2[0]/1e6:.2f}), {st2[0]/st[0]:.3f} x the solid centre")
print(f"  {th2*1e3:.1f} mm thick, {m2:.1f} kg; burst at {burst2:.3f} x full speed ({w_max*burst2*60/2/np.pi:.0f} rpm)")
assert st2[0] / st[0] > 1.95 and burst2 > 2.0 and burst > 3.0
# overspeed: if the controller fails and the grid drives the motor, the governor must trip well below burst
trip = 1.15
print(f"overspeed trip at {trip:.2f} x: stress {st2[0]*trip**2/1e6:.1f} MPa at the bore, still {sy/(st2[0]*trip**2):.2f} x below yield")
assert sy / (st2[0] * trip**2) > 2
```

:::exercise An aluminium wheel
Make the same 0.30 m wheel from 7075-T6 aluminium ($\rho = 2810$ kg/m³, $\nu = 0.33$, yield 500 MPa) at the same 150 m/s rim speed with a 40 mm bore. Find the bore hoop stress, the yield margin, and the mass for 300 kJ usable.
---solution---
1. $\omega = 500$ rad/s. The bore stress is
$$
\sigma_\theta(a) = \frac{3.33}{4}\times 2810 \times 500^2\left(0.09 + \frac{0.67}{3.33}\times 0.0004\right) = 52.7\ \text{MPa}.
$$
2. Yield margin $500/52.7 = 9.5$.
3. Specific energy depends only on the rim speed, 5.625 kJ/kg, so the full 400 kJ again needs about 71 kg of metal. Aluminium is lighter per cubic metre, so the disc is 2.8 times thicker, about 89 mm, for the same mass. Its advantage would only show at a higher rim speed, which this design rules out.
:::
