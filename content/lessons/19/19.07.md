An e-bike for the hills of Bali has to do two things that pull against each other: reach the 25 km/h assist limit on the flat, and climb long 8 % roads without cooking its motor. This capstone sizes the gear ratio of a mid-drive using the DC motor of Phase 14, the vehicle dynamics of Phase 06 and the thermal RC model of Phase 08.

## Motor and road

A brushed or field-oriented brushless motor behaves as

$$
V = K\omega_m + I R,\qquad \tau_m = K I,
$$

with the same constant $K$ for back-EMF and torque in SI units. The controller limits the current to $I_\text{max}$, and it cannot push more current than the back-EMF leaves room for. Through a reduction $G$ with efficiency $\eta_g$, the wheel of radius $r$ gets

$$
F_w = \frac{\eta_g G K I}{r} + \frac{P_\text{rider}}{v},\qquad \omega_m = \frac{G v}{r} .
$$

The road takes $F_r = mg(C_{rr}\cos\theta + \sin\theta) + \tfrac12\rho C_dA\,v^2$. A tall gear (small $G$) spins the motor slowly and gives a high top speed but little force; a low gear (large $G$) climbs well but runs out of back-EMF headroom before 25 km/h.

## Heat

On a long climb at the current limit, the copper loss $I^2R$ heats the winding through a thermal RC,

$$
C_\text{th}\frac{dT}{dt} = I^2 R - \frac{T - T_\text{amb}}{R_\text{th}},
$$

which tends to $T_\text{amb} + I^2 R R_\text{th}$ with time constant $R_\text{th}C_\text{th}$. A slow climb lasts many time constants and gets there.

## Energy ledger

With ideal PWM the battery supplies $(K\omega_m + IR)I$. That plus the rider's work goes into copper loss, gear loss, rolling and air drag, potential energy and kinetic energy. Booking every force at the step's mid-speed makes the work equal the change in kinetic energy, and the ledger closes up to the time-step error.

:::example Choose the ratio
Assumed: 100 kg of bike and rider, $C_{rr} = 0.008$, $C_dA = 0.55$ m², 0.35 m wheel, a 36 V motor with $R = 0.25\ \Omega$ and $K = 0.08$ N m/A, 15 A limit, $\eta_g = 0.92$, a rider giving 100 W, and a winding with $C_\text{th} = 400$ J/K and $R_\text{th} = 1.8$ K/W in 30 °C air. The test climb is 3 km at 8 %, 240 m up.

| Ratio | Flat, km/h | On 8 %, km/h | Climb time | Winding peak | Battery |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 25.0 | 6.5 | 27.8 min | 121.3 °C | 54.6 Wh |
| 16 | 25.0 | 9.5 | 19.0 min | 110.4 °C | 63.5 Wh |
| 22 | 25.0 | 15.7 | 11.6 min | 92.6 °C | 73.7 Wh |
| 26 | 21.7 | 19.7 | 9.3 min | 78.7 °C | 78.1 Wh |

1. **Ratio 10** creeps up the hill at the current limit for 27.8 minutes, nearly three thermal time constants of 720 s. The winding reaches 121.3 °C, over its 120 °C limit, although it uses the least battery.
2. **Ratio 26** climbs fastest and coolest, but its no-load speed is below 25 km/h, so it never reaches the assist limit on the flat.
3. **Ratio 22** is the largest that still reaches 25.0 km/h: the motor's no-load speed of $V/K = 450$ rad/s maps to 25.8 km/h at the wheel. It climbs at 15.7 km/h and peaks at 92.6 °C.
4. **Check at 15.7 km/h.** The motor gives $0.92 \times 22 \times 0.08 \times 15/0.35 = 69.4$ N and the rider 22.9 N, against 86.05 N of grade and rolling plus 6.2 N of drag.
5. **Ledger for ratio 22.** 73.69 Wh from the battery and 19.26 Wh from the rider go into 10.83 Wh copper, 5.03 Wh gear, 11.63 Wh rolling and drag, 65.19 Wh potential energy and 0.26 Wh kinetic energy. The relative mismatch is $4\times10^{-6}$, the time-step error.
6. **Range.** The climb uses 24.6 Wh/km, so a 500 Wh pack covers about 20 km of it.
:::

```python file=ebike_drive.py
"""E-bike mid-drive: DC motor model, gear ratio choice against top speed and hill climbing, an
energy ledger from battery to road, and winding temperature on a long tropical climb. numpy only."""
import numpy as np

g, rho_air = 9.81, 1.18
m, Crr, CdA, rw = 100.0, 0.008, 0.55, 0.35          # rider and bike, rolling, drag area, wheel radius
Vb, R, Kt = 36.0, 0.25, 0.08                        # battery, winding resistance, torque constant (= back-EMF constant)
I_max, eta_g = 15.0, 0.92                           # controller current limit, gearbox and chain
P_rider, v_cut = 100.0, 25 / 3.6                    # the rider's own pedalling, EU assist cut-off speed
Cth, Rth, T_amb, T_lim = 400.0, 1.8, 30.0, 120.0    # winding heat capacity J/K, to ambient K/W, Bali afternoon, insulation limit

def road(v, grade):
    th = np.arctan(grade)
    return m * g * (Crr * np.cos(th) + np.sin(th)) + 0.5 * rho_air * CdA * v * abs(v)

def motor_current(v, G):
    """Full throttle below the cut-off: current limited by the controller and by the back-EMF."""
    if v >= v_cut: return 0.0
    wm = G * v / rw
    return float(np.clip((Vb - Kt * wm) / R, 0, I_max))

def wheel_force(v, G):
    I = motor_current(v, G)
    return eta_g * G * Kt * I / rw + P_rider / max(v, 1.0), I

def ride(G, grade, dist, dt=0.01, v0=0.5):
    v, x, T, t = v0, 0.0, T_amb, 0.0
    L = dict(batt=0.0, rider=0.0, copper=0.0, gear=0.0, roll_aero=0.0, PE=0.0, KE=-0.5 * m * v0**2)
    Tmax = T
    while x < dist:
        Fw, I = wheel_force(v, G)
        Fr = road(v, grade)
        v1 = max(v + (Fw - Fr) / m * dt, 0.05)
        vm = 0.5 * (v + v1)
        wm = G * v / rw
        L["batt"] += (Kt * wm + I * R) * I * dt                 # ideal PWM: the battery supplies back-EMF plus IR at the motor current
        L["copper"] += I**2 * R * dt
        L["gear"] += (1 - eta_g) * Kt * I * wm * dt
        L["rider"] += P_rider / max(v, 1.0) * vm * dt
        th = np.arctan(grade)
        L["PE"] += m * g * np.sin(th) * vm * dt
        L["roll_aero"] += (Fr - m * g * np.sin(th)) * vm * dt
        T += ((I**2 * R) - (T - T_amb) / Rth) / Cth * dt; Tmax = max(Tmax, T)
        x += vm * dt; v = v1; t += dt
    L["KE"] += 0.5 * m * v**2
    return t, v, Tmax, L

def top_speed(G, grade):
    vs = np.linspace(0.5, 15, 14501)
    net = np.array([wheel_force(v, G)[0] - road(v, grade) for v in vs])
    return vs[np.argmax(net < 0)] if np.any(net < 0) else np.nan

def max_grade(G):
    gr = np.linspace(0, 0.4, 4001)
    v = 2.0                                                          # walking pace with full current
    F = wheel_force(v, G)[0]
    return gr[np.argmax(np.array([road(v, s) for s in gr]) > F)]

print("ratio  flat speed km/h  8 % speed km/h  max grade at 7 km/h   3 km at 8 %: time  winding peak C  battery Wh")
res = {}
for G in (10, 16, 22, 26):
    vf, vh = top_speed(G, 0) * 3.6, top_speed(G, 0.08) * 3.6
    gmax = max_grade(G)
    t, vend, Tmax, L = ride(G, 0.08, 3000)
    res[G] = (vf, vh, gmax, t, Tmax, L)
    print(f"  {G:3d}       {vf:5.1f}            {vh:5.1f}         {gmax*100:5.1f} %              {t/60:5.1f} min     {Tmax:6.1f}      {L['batt']/3600:6.1f}")
G = 22
t, vend, Tmax, L = ride(G, 0.08, 3000)
ins = L["batt"] + L["rider"]
outs = L["copper"] + L["gear"] + L["roll_aero"] + L["PE"] + L["KE"]
print("\nledger for G = 22, Wh: " + ", ".join(f"{k} {v/3600:.2f}" for k, v in L.items()))
print(f"in {ins/3600:.3f} Wh, out {outs/3600:.3f} Wh, relative mismatch {abs(ins-outs)/ins:.1e} (time-step error)")
assert abs(ins - outs) / ins < 2e-3
assert np.isclose(L["PE"], m * g * np.sin(np.arctan(0.08)) * 3000, rtol=2e-3)
# the choices
assert res[10][4] > T_lim                       # tall gear: the motor stalls into its current limit on the hill and cooks
assert res[22][0] >= 24.9 and res[22][4] < T_lim
assert res[26][0] < 24.0                             # too low a gear spins the motor out before the cut-off speed
print(f"G = 22: reaches {res[22][0]:.1f} km/h on the flat, climbs 8 % at {res[22][1]:.1f} km/h, winding peaks at {res[22][4]:.1f} C")
Wh_km = (res[22][5]["batt"] / 3600) / 3.0
print(f"battery use on the climb {Wh_km:.1f} Wh/km; a 500 Wh pack covers {500/Wh_km:.0f} km of such climbing")
```

:::exercise Steady winding temperature
On the ratio 10 climb the motor sits at the 15 A limit. Find the copper loss, the steady winding temperature, and the time constant. How long would the climb have to be for the winding to stay under 120 °C?
---solution---
1. Copper loss $15^2 \times 0.25 = 56.25$ W.
2. Steady temperature $30 + 56.25 \times 1.8 = 131.3$ °C, over the limit.
3. Time constant $R_\text{th}C_\text{th} = 720$ s.
4. From $T = T_\infty - (T_\infty - T_0)e^{-t/\tau}$ with $T_0 = 30$ °C: $e^{-t/720} = 11.25/101.25$, so $t = 720 \ln 9 = 1582$ s, about 26 minutes. The 27.8 minute climb is just too long, which matches the simulation.
:::
