Where does gravity act on a bracket? Where is the neutral axis of an I-beam? Where does a dam's water pressure push? Each answer is a centroid: an average position, weighted by area or by force. This lesson defines it as a ratio of integrals, computes it for curved shapes, and then for any polygon from a list of corner points, which is how CAD programs do it.

## Centroid as a weighted average

For a plane region $A$, the centroid is the average of $x$ and $y$ over the area:

$$
\bar x = \frac{\int_A x\,dA}{\int_A dA}, \qquad \bar y = \frac{\int_A y\,dA}{\int_A dA} .
$$

The numerators are **first moments of area**. For a uniform thin plate the centroid is the centre of gravity, and in Phase 06 it's where the neutral axis of a beam passes.

## A semicircle by strips

Take a semicircle of radius $r$ above the $x$ axis. By symmetry $\bar x = 0$. Slice it into horizontal strips of height $dy$ and width $2\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}$:

$$
\int_A y\,dA = \int_0^r y \cdot 2\sqrt{r^2 - y^2}\,dy = \left[-\tfrac23 (r^2 - y^2)^{3/2}\right]_0^r = \tfrac23 r^3 .
$$

Divide by the area $\pi r^2/2$:

$$
\bar y = \frac{2r^3/3}{\pi r^2/2} = \frac{4r}{3\pi} \approx 0.424\,r .
$$

## Composite shapes

Integrals add over pieces, so for a shape made of simple parts with areas $A_i$ and centroids $\bar y_i$:

$$
\bar y = \frac{\sum A_i \bar y_i}{\sum A_i} .
$$

Holes count as negative area.

## Any polygon from its corners

For a closed polygon with corners $(x_0, y_0), \dots, (x_{n-1}, y_{n-1})$ in counter-clockwise order, Green's theorem turns the area integrals into sums over edges. With $c_i = x_i y_{i+1} - x_{i+1} y_i$:

$$
A = \tfrac12\sum c_i, \qquad \bar x = \frac{1}{6A}\sum (x_i + x_{i+1})\,c_i, \qquad \bar y = \frac{1}{6A}\sum (y_i + y_{i+1})\,c_i .
$$

This "shoelace" formula is exact for polygons, and curved outlines are handled by approximating them with many points.

:::example Centroid of a T-section
A T-beam has a flange $100 \times 20\ \mathrm{mm}$ on top of a web $20 \times 80\ \mathrm{mm}$. Total height $100\ \mathrm{mm}$; measure $y$ from the bottom of the web.

1. Web: $A_1 = 20 \times 80 = 1600\ \mathrm{mm^2}$, centroid at $y = 40\ \mathrm{mm}$.
2. Flange: $A_2 = 100 \times 20 = 2000\ \mathrm{mm^2}$, centroid at $y = 80 + 10 = 90\ \mathrm{mm}$.
3. $\bar y = \dfrac{1600 \times 40 + 2000 \times 90}{3600} = \dfrac{244\,000}{3600} = 67.8\ \mathrm{mm}$.

The neutral axis sits $67.8\ \mathrm{mm}$ up, well above mid-height, because most of the material is in the flange. Under bending, the bottom of the web is farthest from it and sees the highest stress.
:::

```python file=centroid.py
"""Area and centroid of any simple polygon by the shoelace formula."""
import math


def polygon_centroid(pts):
    A = cx = cy = 0.0
    n = len(pts)
    for i in range(n):
        x0, y0 = pts[i]
        x1, y1 = pts[(i + 1) % n]
        c = x0 * y1 - x1 * y0
        A += c
        cx += (x0 + x1) * c
        cy += (y0 + y1) * c
    A *= 0.5
    if abs(A) < 1e-15:
        raise ValueError("degenerate polygon")
    return A, cx / (6 * A), cy / (6 * A)   # a clockwise outline gives negative A, same centroid


def semicircle(r, n):
    return [(r * math.cos(math.pi * k / n), r * math.sin(math.pi * k / n)) for k in range(n + 1)]


if __name__ == "__main__":
    r = 1.0
    for n in (16, 256, 4096):
        A, x, y = polygon_centroid(semicircle(r, n))
        print(f"n={n:5d}: area {A:.6f}, y_c {y:.6f}  (4r/3pi = {4 * r / (3 * math.pi):.6f})")
    assert abs(y - 4 * r / (3 * math.pi)) < 1e-6 and abs(x) < 1e-12   # the lesson's test

    T = [(40, 0), (60, 0), (60, 80), (100, 80), (100, 100), (0, 100), (0, 80), (40, 80)]
    A, x, y = polygon_centroid(T)
    assert abs(A - 3600) < 1e-9 and abs(x - 50) < 1e-9 and abs(y - 67.7778) < 1e-4
    print(f"T-section: area {A:.0f} mm^2, centroid y = {y:.2f} mm")
```

:::exercise A plate with a hole
A $200 \times 100\ \mathrm{mm}$ plate lies with its corner at the origin. A hole of diameter $40\ \mathrm{mm}$ is centred at $(150, 50)$. Find the centroid.
---solution---
1. Plate: $A_1 = 20\,000\ \mathrm{mm^2}$ at $(100, 50)$.
2. Hole: $A_2 = -\pi \times 20^2 = -1256.6\ \mathrm{mm^2}$ at $(150, 50)$.
3. $\bar x = \dfrac{20\,000 \times 100 - 1256.6 \times 150}{20\,000 - 1256.6} = \dfrac{2\,000\,000 - 188\,496}{18\,743.4} = 96.65\ \mathrm{mm}$.
4. $\bar y = 50\ \mathrm{mm}$ by symmetry. Removing material on the right shifts the centroid left by $3.35\ \mathrm{mm}$.
:::
