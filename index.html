Each lab runs a real (simplified) model in your browser. Delete parts, stop the spin, choke the flow, and watch exactly what fails. Every model is labelled with its assumptions: these are educational approximations, not design tools.

FIG_TRS · Phase 05 Statics · Phase 16 FEM

Break the bridge

A 12 m steel truss bridge carries a truck. Tap any member to delete it. The direct stiffness method re-solves instantly: cyan is tension, orange compression. Remove a redundant brace and the load finds another path. Remove the wrong one and the bridge becomes a mechanism.

FIG_GYR · Phase 07 Dynamics

Why the spinning wheel doesn't fall

A 2 kg flywheel hangs off a pivot by one end of its axle. Gravity's torque should tip it over. With enough spin it swings sideways instead, because torque changes angular momentum (lime arrow) in the direction of the torque (red arrow). Stop the spin and watch it drop.

FIG_VEN · Phase 11 Fluid Dynamics 1

Choke the pipe

Water flows through a venturi. Squeeze the throat or open the valve: particles speed up where the pipe narrows and the pressure columns fall. Push too far and Bernoulli predicts pressure below vapour pressure, so the water would cavitate.

Gauge column height is proportional to absolute pressure head; red means below vapour pressure.

How this works

One loop, 356 times

Engineering material usually arrives in pieces: a formula sheet here, a CAD tutorial there, a simulation button you're told to trust. Here every lesson runs the same loop, from Analysis 1 to a working FEM and CFD solver, and only then the open-source pro tool.

  1. 01 DeriveWork the math by hand: free-body diagram, balance law, assumptions written down.
  2. 02 CodeImplement it from scratch in Python/NumPy (sometimes JS). No black boxes.
  3. 03 TestA test proves it: analytic limit, conservation law or benchmark.
  4. 04 Keep the artifactCommit the solver, plot or lab. Then meet the pro tool it maps to: FreeCAD, CalculiX, FEniCSx, OpenFOAM.
