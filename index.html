How a lesson works

Every lesson follows the same four steps, so you always know what comes next and what “done” means.

  1. Derive

    Work the model out by hand with the assumptions written down. Equations are typeset so you can follow every line.

  2. Code

    Write it in Python from nothing, without black boxes. Every code file is downloadable and tests itself.

  3. Test

    Prove it against a hand calculation, an analytic limit or a conservation law. Then check yourself with the quiz.

  4. Keep

    Commit the result to your own repository and meet the open-source tool it maps to, from FreeCAD to OpenFOAM.

Break the machine

Each lab runs a simplified physical model in your browser. Remove parts, stop the spin or choke the flow, and see what fails. Every lab states its assumptions; these are teaching models, not design tools.

Break the bridge

A 12 m steel truss carries a truck. Tap a member to remove it and the forces are solved again at once: blue members are in tension, orange in compression. Remove a redundant brace and the load finds another path; remove the wrong one and the bridge folds.

Why the spinning wheel doesn’t fall

A 2 kg flywheel hangs from a pivot by one end of its axle. Gravity’s torque should tip it over, yet with enough spin it swings sideways instead: the torque (red arrow) turns the angular momentum (lime arrow) rather than tipping it. Stop the spin and it drops.

Choke the pipe

Water flows through a venturi. Narrow the throat or open the valve: the water speeds up where the pipe narrows and the pressure columns fall. Push too far and the model predicts pressure below the vapour pressure, where real water would boil into bubbles.

The coconut that never escapes

A coconut trapped in the eddy behind a boulder in a Bali river: separation and recirculation you can watch. Used in phases 11 and 17.

The course

Twenty phases that follow the ETH Zurich mechanical engineering bachelor, plus practice. Open a phase to see its lessons: all 356 are written in full, each with derivations, from-scratch code, a widget and a quiz.

Finish a phase, earn a certificate

Pass a phase test with 80% or more and your browser generates a certificate of completion with your name, the date and a verification code. Lessons and quizzes earn XP, and a daily streak keeps you coming back.

It’s a free certificate of completion, not an accredited qualification, and it isn’t affiliated with or endorsed by ETH Zurich.
